# Decision lenses: choose form from function

These lenses stimulate design judgment. They are not templates, mandatory page orders or interchangeable skins. Select the one that best fits the visitor's primary question, then intentionally break or combine it when necessary.

| Visitor job | Candidate visual language | Primary interaction / evidence | Common trap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What is happening now? | Bold time-stamped verdict, trend, evidence ladder | Window or place selector changes sourced verdict | Big number shown as a total when it is only one report |
| Where should I go? | Geographic canvas with concise selected-place dossier | Map and list coordinate by stable ID; verified arrival/source links | Decorative map with unverified parking or blank tiles |
| What will I see or experience? | Photo-led interpretive field guide or chaptered landscape | Select a real place to reveal exact-subject image, story and access | Giant irrelevant hero and dozens of identical cards |
| Which is better for me? | Comparison bench or decision matrix | Change priorities; honest differences, tradeoffs and unknowns | Arbitrary scores or fabricated travel times |
| When will it happen? | Temporal strip, season wheel or event progression | Time changes observed/forecast states with evidence and clear age | False continuity from sparse samples |
| How does it work? | Annotated cutaway, sequence, measured diagram or optional interactive model | Controls demonstrate a sourced mechanism with static equivalent | Fancy 3D or animation that teaches nothing |
| How should I complete the journey? | Stop sequence, route story, day sheet or itinerary workbench | Change duration/stops and update validated route/order | A line on a map with no real public approach |
| How do I follow a changing event? | Live event desk, timeline, trace and next-action channel | Select event, latest verified observation and useful follow-up | Repeated unverified LIVE claims and stalled spinners |

## Composition judgment

1. **Content precedence:** The most consequential answer and next action should be discoverable in the first phone screen. That does not dictate a fixed hero size or an always-top map.
2. **Data to visual form:** Rank magnitude with bars only when comparable numeric magnitudes exist. Use diagrams for mechanics, maps for consequential geography, chronology for real changes over time, and real photography for exact-place recognition or storytelling. An illustration never becomes source evidence.
3. **Typography and density:** Use readable hierarchy and a few intentional strong anchors, not every number at 56px or every section in a rounded card. Distinguish estimates, observations, forecasts and unverified states.
4. **Color and material:** Let meaning, landscape and accessibility drive palette. Reuse compatible tokens where useful, but avoid forcing blue snow accents, navy bands, gray 1px box grids, gradients, or shadows on every section. WCAG AA remains mandatory.
5. **Interaction and motion:** Only build an interaction when visitor input changes something interpretable; always support keyboard, reduced motion, and truthful no-JS alternatives. Optional 3D and camera feeds come after the useful answer.
6. **Story and interpretation:** Visitor-friendly park-guide language suits landscapes, plants and historic infrastructure when grounded in sources. Operational weather, toll and emergency-status tools may need more concise informational voice.
7. **Constraint fit:** A strictly visual refresh should leave data pipelines, APIs, ads, canonical/SEO, indexing and build steps unchanged. A proposal for a new product may require real engineering, but only where the job demands it.

## Three illustrative differences

- **Michigan Snow Totals:** Reported inches, selected time window and ranked places naturally support prominent tabular numerals, an inline magnitude comparison and a geographic report map. Never call an individual report a statewide accumulation total.
- **Rideau Canal:** Visitors may need to understand lock operations and where to watch. A sourced lock sequence, annotated working model and validated public vantage points may outperform a row of status cards. Use interpretive text and distinguish live camera availability from an imagined feed.
- **UP Waterfalls:** Visitors need to recognize a specific waterfall and find a responsible public approach. Real credited photography, location-led wayfinding, access provenance and meaningful regional groupings are more useful than a snow-style dashboard.

The final concept must look, read and behave differently for a reason. At least two dimensions must differ meaningfully from the two closest existing tools: answer/decision, composition, interaction/navigation, and editorial/visual character. Relabeling a shared structure fails.
