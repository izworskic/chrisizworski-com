Stewardship

The cedar sweepers and woody cover keep the Au Sable cold and hold fish. Anglers of the Au Sable run the annual river cleanup, the Cedars for the Au Sable replanting, in-river habitat work, and brown-trout redd surveys across all three branches; you can volunteer or join. Help keep the water clean: drain and dry your boat, waders and gear between rivers so you do not carry invasive species in. The Huron-Manistee National Forest posts current conditions for the lower river.

Birding

The jack-pine plains between Grayling and Mio are the world stronghold of the Kirtland’s warbler. Nesting areas are closed and posted from spring into summer, so the way to see one is a guided tour: Michigan Audubon from Hartwick Pines near Grayling, the U.S. Forest Service from Mio (Mio Ranger District, 989-826-3252), and AuSable Valley Audubon from Oscoda. The Kirtland’s Warbler Festival falls on the first Saturday of June.

The river access points double as eBird hotspots, with Stephan Bridge Road, Chase Bridge and the South Branch Road sites among the best. See recent sightings on eBird for Crawford County, the Grayling reaches and the branches, and Iosco County, the lower river and the mouth.

A field reference by Chris Izworski

The Au Sable River, mapped honestly

This is a working map of the Au Sable River in Michigan, built and maintained by Chris Izworski. It exists because most Au Sable maps online are wrong in small, frustrating ways: guessed pins, missing regulation lines, basemaps that fail to load, and no sense of how far it really is from one landing to the next. Every point here carries a verified coordinate, or an honest note when it does not, and every mainstem access point carries a real river mile measured along the surveyed channel from the Grayling public access down to the mouth at Lake Huron near Oscoda.

The river runs about 120 miles from Grayling to Lake Huron, the same distance raced each July in the Au Sable River Canoe Marathon. It is the birthplace of Michigan trout fishing and the home water of Trout Unlimited, and it carries some of the most storied fly water in the country.

The five reaches of the Au Sable

The map splits the river the way anglers and paddlers actually think about it. The Holy Water is the flies-only, catch-and-release mainstream from Burton's Landing down to Wakeley Bridge, about nine miles of legendary trout water. The Mainstream below it runs general regulations toward Mio. The South Branch holds the Mason Tract, a second flies-only stretch from Chase Bridge to the Lower High Banks. The North Branch threads through Lovells. The Lower river and dams begins at Mio Pond and steps down a chain of six hydro impoundments, Mio, Alcona, Loud, Five Channels, Cooke and Foote, before the last free run to the lake below Foote Dam.

Fishing the Au Sable

The Au Sable is Michigan’s most storied trout river and the birthplace of Trout Unlimited, founded on these banks in 1959. It holds wild brown, brook, and rainbow trout in cold, spring-fed water, with steelhead and salmon running the lower river below Foote Dam.

Regulations change from one section to the next, so always confirm the stretch you are fishing in the current Michigan DNR Fishing Guide. The well-known special-regulation water, both drawn in gold on the map:

  • Holy Water, Burton’s Landing to Wakeley Bridge: flies only, catch-and-release, open year-round. About eight miles of wadeable dry-fly water near Grayling.
  • Mason Tract on the South Branch, Chase Bridge to Lower High Banks: flies only, no-kill (the gold reach on the upper South Branch). Narrower and more intimate, and a quieter place to fish the Hex.
  • Below Mio Dam: Great Lakes tributary rules for steelhead and salmon.

The hatches the river is known for, in rough order through the season:

  • May: Hendricksons.
  • Late May into June: Sulphurs and the Brown Drake.
  • Late June into July: the Hex (Hexagenia limbata), emerging at dusk and after dark. The river’s signature night hatch.
  • Summer: terrestrials, ants, beetles, and hoppers.
  • Fall: streamers for big browns.

Fly shops and guides are marked on the map: Gates Au Sable Lodge and the Old Au Sable Fly Shop near Grayling, Drake’s, and the North Branch Outing Club at Lovells. Tap a marker for contacts, or use the Fish filter on the Places tab to show angler access, wade water, and shops. For daily conditions and hatch tracking, see Michigan Trout Report.

Guided float trips. The classic way to fish the Au Sable is a guided float, with the guide running a handcrafted Au Sable riverboat or a drift boat while you cast to the banks. Gates Au Sable Lodge is the river’s best-known full-service operation and floats every branch and the lower river; the Old Au Sable Fly Shop and Fuller’s North Branch Outing Club at Lovells run guided floats in the same boats, and independent guides include Au Sable River Guide Service and Betts Guide Service. Hex week in late June books months out, so reserve early.

Public land and wading access. Much of the corridor runs through the Au Sable State Forest and the Huron-Manistee National Forest. To see what is public, switch the basemap to Terrain: the USGS topographic tiles shade national forest and state land. In Michigan the public can generally wade and fish a navigable river like the Au Sable even where it flows past private land, but you cannot cross private property to reach the water or to carry around an obstacle, and where a bank is posted you should stay in the stream. For exact ownership boundaries, check the Michigan DNR land maps. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

More ways to explore the Au Sable

Multi-day trips and camping. The river is gentle enough for overnight and multi-day floats, with riverside state forest and federal campgrounds along the way. Use the Camp filter on the Places tab to show them. From Grayling to Mio the riverside campgrounds are state run and first come, first served; the federal sites farther downstream are the ones that take reservations. On the lower river below Mio, plan around the six hydro dams: each is a portage, and the water below them runs bigger and colder.

Paddling the dam ponds. Below Mio the river backs up behind six hydro dams into long flatwater ponds. The three to four miles above each dam, on Cooke, Foote, Loud, Alcona and Five Channels ponds, carry little or no current, so on a windy day they paddle like open lakes and a headwind can stop you cold. Check the forecast before you launch on the lower river with the Au Sable wind tool, give yourself extra time across the ponds, and never approach a dam from the water: take out early and use the marked portage.

The AuSable River Canoe Marathon. The 120-mile non-stop race from Grayling to Oscoda, run since 1947 on the last full weekend of July, is billed as the world’s toughest spectator race. It starts at 9 p.m. with a sprint through downtown Grayling and finishes in Oscoda the next afternoon, with crews following all night. The bridges on this map double as spectator stops; for checkpoint times see the official spectator guide.

Birding and the Kirtland’s warbler. The jack-pine country around Grayling and Mio is the world stronghold of the Kirtland’s warbler, one of conservation’s great comeback stories. Nesting areas are closed in summer, so the way to see one is a guided tour from the Mio Ranger Station, Hartwick Pines near Grayling, or AuSable Valley Audubon out of Oscoda, with the Kirtland’s Warbler Festival held the first Saturday of June. Wakely Lake, Tuttle Marsh, and the river overlooks are good general birding, and the river access points are eBird hotspots in their own right. More Michigan birding at Michigan Birding Report.

The River Road byway and landmarks. Along the lower river near Oscoda, the River Road National Scenic Byway passes Lumberman’s Monument, Iargo Springs, and the Highbanks overlooks, all on the map under the Hike and scenic filters.

Au Sable float times and distances

Each mainstem access point has a river mile, so the planner can give you a distance and an estimated time between any put-in and take-out. Estimates assume roughly 3.2 miles per hour on free-flowing river and 2.2 on the flatwater ponds, plus a portage at each dam. Below 4001 Bridge Canoe Landing, the last take-out on the National Scenic stretch, camping shifts to the federal dispersed system: 102 individually designated sites that require a reservation from May 15 to September 30 through recreation.gov. Developed Forest Service campgrounds at Gabions, Rollways and Monument, plus Old Orchard Park on Foote Pond, give vehicle-accessible options.

Plan your start time. Match your put-in to the float length and the daylight left. Liveries send their last boats out early in the afternoon for the longer Grayling-area runs, around noon for a float finishing at Wakeley and mid-afternoon for shorter ones, and on overnight trips they collect gear near midday, so start in the morning for anything past a few hours.

About the data

The river channel and the impoundment shapes are drawn from OpenStreetMap survey geometry. Access coordinates are verified individually rather than scraped, and the few approximate pins are labeled as such. River miles are computed by projecting each access point onto the stitched channel, which is why the mouth lands at about 119.6 miles, within a rounding error of the marathon distance.

Car spotting and shuttles

Every access point on this map has parking, so a two-car self-shuttle always works. If you would rather run your own boat and have your car waiting at the take-out, several liveries offer a car-spot or shuttle. Call ahead.

Facilities. Every access point has parking. Vault toilets are at the developed campgrounds and at the River Road day-use sites, including Lumberman’s Monument, Iargo Springs and the Highbanks overlooks; drinking water is generally only at the developed Forest Service and state campgrounds. Many upper-river accesses are carry-in or shallow gravel landings, while the lower pond launches have hard-surface boat ramps. The smaller launches and bridge accesses are primitive, so pack out what you bring in.

  • Upper river and Holy Water, Grayling: Carlisle, Borchers, and Penrod’s.
  • South Branch, Roscommon: Campbell’s and PaddleBrave.
  • Mio: Hinchman Acres and Rainbow Resort.
  • Lower river, Glennie to Oscoda: Alcona Canoe Rental and Oscoda Canoe Rental.

Au Sable River planner FAQ

How accurate are the float times on this map?

The planner estimates time on the water at about 3.2 miles per hour on free-flowing river and 2.2 on the flat impoundment ponds, and it adds a portage at each dam. These are planning estimates, not guarantees: real paddling time shifts with flow, wind, how often you stop, and how hard you paddle. For segment-by-segment detail, see the full Au Sable River guide.

What do the colors and markers mean?

Each reach of the Au Sable carries its own color: the Holy Water, the mainstream, the South Branch and Mason Tract, the North Branch, and the lower river and dams. Markers show the access type, such as public launches, bridges, liveries, campgrounds, overlooks, and dams, and every mainstem access point carries a measured river mile.

Where does the live river flow come from?

Flow is pulled live from the U.S. Geological Survey gauges on the Au Sable, near Grayling and below Mio, and shown in cubic feet per second. The map notes how current each reading is so you can judge conditions before you drive.

Can I plan a trip on the South Branch or North Branch?

Yes. The planner covers the mainstem from Grayling to Lake Huron, the South Branch through the Mason Tract, and the North Branch through Lovells. Choose any put-in and take-out on a branch and the map returns the distance and an estimated float time.

Can I camp along the Au Sable River?

Yes. Riverside state forest and federal campgrounds let you turn a float into an overnight or multi-day trip; use the Camp filter to show them. The state campgrounds from Grayling to Mio are first come, first served, while the federal sites farther down take reservations. On the lower river below Mio, six hydro dams mean portages and bigger, colder water, so plan those stretches carefully.

Where can I see the Kirtland’s warbler near the Au Sable?

In the jack-pine country around Grayling and Mio, the bird’s world stronghold. Nesting areas are closed in summer, so join a guided tour from the Mio Ranger Station or Hartwick Pines near Grayling, both marked on the map.

What is the AuSable River Canoe Marathon?

A 120-mile non-stop canoe race from Grayling to Oscoda, run since 1947 on the last full weekend of July. It starts at 9 p.m. with a sprint through Grayling and finishes in Oscoda the next afternoon, and the bridges on this map make good spectator stops.

Can I rent a canoe or kayak on the Au Sable River?

Yes. Liveries run the length of the river: Borchers and Penrod’s near Grayling, Rainbow Resort and Hinchman Acres at Mio, Alcona Canoe Rental near Glennie, and Oscoda Canoe Rental at the mouth. Most rent both canoes and kayaks and offer a shuttle or car-spot for a one-way float. Call ahead, especially during Hex week in late June.

Is the Au Sable good for kayaking, or only canoeing?

Both. The river has no whitewater, so a kayak handles every reach as easily as a canoe, and most liveries rent either. Kayaks track a little straighter than a canoe on the flatwater ponds behind the six lower dams, where a stiff headwind can otherwise stall you.

Do I need to register a canoe or kayak in Michigan?

No, not if it is paddle-only. Michigan exempts non-motorized canoes and kayaks from registration regardless of length, as long as they are not used for rental or commercial purposes. Add any motor, even a small electric trolling motor, and it must be registered with the Secretary of State. This is general guidance, not legal advice.
