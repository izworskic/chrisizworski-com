## Evidence and corrections

### Chipmunk Falls — #17

**What can be said:** This is the Iron County Chipmunk Falls on the Net River near Amasa, not the differently named Alger County site. World Waterfall Database disqualifies this as a waterfall under its own size standard and describes a drop below three feet. Its reported place-name displacement and competing directions make a saved map pin unreliable.

**Do not recommend:** Do not plan a dedicated waterfall visit or follow a generic logging-road or map-pin route.

**Evidence required to reconsider:** Reconcile reported river rapid against GIS and independent field evidence, then identify land tenure, legal public roadside stopping and a safe public riverbank approach, if any.

**Source links:** [Michigan waterfall GIS natural source](https://gisp.mcgi.state.mi.us/arcgis/rest/services/BaseMap/General_Geology/MapServer/7) · [World Waterfall Database: tiny Net River rapid](https://www.worldwaterfalldatabase.com/waterfall/Chipmunk-Falls-13118) · [UPtravel Chipmunk Falls near Amasa](https://www.uptravel.com/listing/chipmunk-falls-amasa/723/) · [Competing Iron County listing](https://michiganupperpeninsula.com/places-to-visit/waterfalls/)

### Copper Falls — #144

**What can be said:** Copper Falls is a series of different drops on Owl Creek near Eagle Harbor. The upper falls, main creek cascades and lower mine-adit flow are separate features. Historic mine workings also alter the stream. A specialist source's reader discussion identifies Copper Falls Loop as private, with an older purported owner comment permitting some foot visits while discouraging vehicles. That is not verified, current, property-specific visitor authorization.

**Do not recommend:** Do not route drivers down Copper Falls Loop, into an old pit or to mine shafts. Treat the older private-land comment as context, not permission.

**Evidence required to reconsider:** Obtain authoritative present parcel ownership, legal public road stopping, property-specific permission and safe walk to a named drop. Differentiate Copper Falls from Upper Copper and Copper Adit Falls.

**Source links:** [Michigan waterfall GIS natural source](https://gisp.mcgi.state.mi.us/arcgis/rest/services/BaseMap/General_Geology/MapServer/7) · [Owl Creek Copper Falls field report and access discussion](https://www.waterfallsofthekeweenaw.com/owl-creek/copper-falls/) · [Upper Copper Falls mine-adit distinction](https://www.waterfallsofthekeweenaw.com/owl-creek/upper-copper-falls/) · [Copper Adit Falls separate feature](https://www.waterfallsofthekeweenaw.com/owl-creek/copper-adit-falls/) · [Copper Country historic visitor directions](https://www.coppercountry.com/destinations/waterfalls/copper-falls/)

### Cataract Escanaba Falls — #18

**What can be said:** This state GIS feature appears to correspond to Cataract Dam Falls below the Cataract impoundment near Gwinn. Travel Marquette describes the site as a waterfall outing. More importantly, the Michigan DNR's 2024 compartment review says adjacent Upper Peninsula Power Company lands were open for access along the river. That 2024 statement is stronger than a generic travel blog, but does not certify the exact safe lot, the protected dam boundary, a continuous trail to lower drops or current operating restrictions.

**Do not recommend:** Do not use dam-service roads, spillways or river-edge shortcuts as a default map route. General access in an older management report is not a safe, signed waterfall trail.

**Evidence required to reconsider:** Confirm present operator public-recreation rules at Cataract, the exact approved public parking location, marked walk below dam, current safety exclusion zones and 2026 gate status.

**Source links:** [Michigan waterfall GIS natural source](https://gisp.mcgi.state.mi.us/arcgis/rest/services/BaseMap/General_Geology/MapServer/7) · [Michigan DNR 2024 Gwinn Compartment 32032, pages 1–2](https://www.dnr.state.mi.us/publications/pdfs/ForestsLandWater/Cmpt_Reviews/Gwinn/2024/gwi032_comp_info.pdf) · [Travel Marquette official regional visitor guide](https://www.travelmarquette.com/outdoor-adventure/waterfalls/) · [Cataract Dam Falls independent natural feature account](https://www.waterfallsofthekeweenaw.com/middle-branch-escanaba-river/cataract-dam-falls/) · [Cataract plant ownership reference](https://hydro.org/map/hydro/existing-hydropower/?state=MI)

### Cliff Falls — #116

**What can be said:** Cliff Falls is also called Forty Foot Falls, a broad cascade on the Cliff River in northern Marquette County. Independent field descriptions place it at the state GIS point, but one commercial roundup assigns the same name a Keweenaw Cliff Drive destination far to the north. Other accounts describe an unsigned rough forest-road approach and an informal clearing, yet no official current parking or property right is established by those accounts.

**Do not recommend:** Do not use the unrelated Keweenaw coordinates or assume that an unsigned campsite/pull-off is legal public parking.

**Evidence required to reconsider:** Resolve parcel title and public forest road status on the Cliff River, inspect road/parking status from an authoritative manager and verify the complete lawful walking access before offering a driving pin.

**Source links:** [Michigan waterfall GIS natural source](https://gisp.mcgi.state.mi.us/arcgis/rest/services/BaseMap/General_Geology/MapServer/7) · [Cliff Falls field and visitorship account](https://upper-michigan.com/guide/waterfalls/cliff-falls.html) · [Forty Foot Falls independent waterfall photography and account](https://gowaterfalling.com/waterfalls/fortyfoot.shtml) · [Conflicting Keweenaw Cliff Drive waterfall list](https://michiganupperpeninsula.com/places-to-visit/waterfalls/)

### Mountain Stream Falls — #127

**What can be said:** The feature lies in the Huron Mountains between Mountain Lake and Pine Lake. The World Waterfall Database labels the waterfall Posted and closed to general access, while regional descriptions identify private Huron Mountain Club land. Michigan GIS and a separate USGS-derived hydro feature independently locate the same stream. A broad waterfall directory misplaces an alleged public hike at Sugarloaf Mountain far from this feature.

**Do not recommend:** Do not attempt to hike to Mountain Stream Falls or adopt the inaccurate Sugarloaf/M-550 directions.

**Evidence required to reconsider:** Keep as restricted geographic research. Reconsider only if the relevant owner or land manager publishes a specific lawful public access corridor and clear visitor rules.

**Source links:** [Michigan waterfall GIS natural source](https://gisp.mcgi.state.mi.us/arcgis/rest/services/BaseMap/General_Geology/MapServer/7) · [World Waterfall Database: Posted/private access](https://www.worldwaterfalldatabase.com/waterfall/Mountain-Stream-Falls-13230) · [Regional Mountain Stream Falls private land description](https://upper-michigan.com/guide/waterfalls/mountain-stream-falls.html) · [Conflicting Sugarloaf Mountain directions](https://michiganupperpeninsula.com/places-to-visit/waterfalls/)

### 80-Foot Falls — #148

**What can be said:** The mapped Eighty Foot Falls, also known as Ives Falls, lies on the River Styx in remote Huron Mountain country. World Waterfall Database explicitly marks the waterfall Unconfirmed, based on one-metre lidar suggesting an approximately 105-foot flume. Some lists call it a roadside view, while other sources describe private Huron Mountain Club land with no public trail. Neither the fall's height nor a legal roadside overlook has been established.

**Do not recommend:** Do not drive to a supposed Ives Lake roadside overlook or cross club land to search for an unverified flume.

**Evidence required to reconsider:** Obtain reliable field confirmation of the actual flume, reconcile its GIS/GNIS position and independently establish parcel ownership, authorized public viewpoint and safe signed parking, if any.

**Source links:** [Michigan waterfall GIS natural source](https://gisp.mcgi.state.mi.us/arcgis/rest/services/BaseMap/General_Geology/MapServer/7) · [World Waterfall Database: Eighty Foot/Ives Unconfirmed](https://www.worldwaterfalldatabase.com/waterfall/Ives-Falls-13171) · [Private-land and historic-name research](https://upper-michigan.com/guide/waterfalls/private-land-falls-marquette-alger.html) · [Conflicting roadside-view claim](https://michiganupperpeninsula.com/places-to-visit/waterfalls/)


## Important source hierarchy

A specialist field report can document waterfall character but not grant property permission. A tourism itinerary is not a present-day legal parking survey. Michigan DNR's Compartment 32032 report (2024) explicitly described general public river access on adjoining UPPCO Cataract lands; that statement does not identify a current dam visitor lot or safe route below the plant. The World Waterfall Database's private/posting and disqualified statuses are useful negative evidence; a third-party 'easy roadside' claim is not enough to overturn them. GIS, USGS/GNIS-derived place names, lidar, and exact natural coordinates are not driving directions.
