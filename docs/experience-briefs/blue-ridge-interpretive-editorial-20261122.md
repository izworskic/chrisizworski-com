# Blue Ridge Parkway interpretive editorial pass
Date: November 22, 2026
Owning repository: izworskic/chrisizworski-com

## Visitor job

A visitor wants to know **which drive fits the time and current conditions** and **what they will see, understand and do at each stop**. Practical results remain first. Interpretive writing follows the selected place and its relevant landscape, not an extra full-screen introduction.

## Voice and coverage

Write as a knowledgeable, thoughtful park interpreter without speaking as or impersonating National Park Service staff. A useful field note identifies a real feature, explains why it matters, and invites the visitor to notice it while respecting access and weather constraints. Avoid interchangeable superlatives, long preambles, fabricated observations and promises of open facilities.

- Main planner: concise landscape orientation, readable result and map captions, condition/weather meaning, and visitor-facing route options.
- Four gateway pages: Asheville (Craggy heath and Pisgah forest/water); Boone (Cone carriage roads, Rough Ridge, Linn Cove engineering); Roanoke (Sharp Top / Abbott Lake and Mabry Mill working history); Cherokee (Waterrock and spruce-fir country).
- Five separate search-intent pages: closures, weather, best stops, itineraries, milepost map. Maintain each page's particular decision, not generic duplicate text.
- All 28 catalog stops and 16 round-trip routes use distinctive grounded language. The live field note, stop cards, snapshot fallbacks, and deterministic/optional fact-sealed route brief keep a consistent interpretation style.

## Editorial choice

Reject: "An efficient high-value overlook with great photo opportunities."

Prefer: "Notice how the Linn Cove Viaduct curves around Grandfather Mountain rather than cutting into its fragile slopes. Its engineering was shaped by the land."

The second gives visitors something observable, explains its significance and avoids an unverified experience guarantee.
