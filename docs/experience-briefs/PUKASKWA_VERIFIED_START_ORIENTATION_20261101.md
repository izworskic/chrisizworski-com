## Verified official evidence
1. Parks Canada day hiking: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/activ/experience/randonnees-hiking . All eight published lengths and hiking durations are returns from the Hattie Cove Visitor Centre. The Combination Hike specifically goes Visitor Centre -> Southern Headland -> Horseshoe Beach -> Beach Trail -> North Loop Campground -> Halfway Lake -> road back to the Visitor Centre.
2. Parking: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/visit/services/boutique . Official day-use parking is at the Visitor Centre, not a verified precise signed trail entrance.
3. Original illustrated trail map: https://pcweb2.azureedge.net/-/media/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/WET4/activ/maps/Carte-map-HCCAT.jpg . Official illustration is not a navigation-grade coordinate survey.
4. Seasonal facilities: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/visit/heures-hours . Visitor Centre regular season ends September 15, kiosk October 15; November staffing and road access are not assured.
5. Official public condition notices: https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/activ/experience/randonnees-hiking/etat-sentiers-trail-conditions . As reviewed Nov 1 the White River bridge notice remains closed dated October 22; ordinary day-trail open observations are still dated October 12 and thus stale.
6. Prior verified partial OpenStreetMap geometry: https://chrisizworski.com/pukaskwa-national-park/trail-centerlines.geojson . The 14 named segments across five trails do NOT reach all signed starts, mapped junctions or complete returns. ODbL rights: https://www.openstreetmap.org/copyright . Seven public Parks Canada GIS places include day-use parking but are not trailhead GPS coordinates.

## Alternative concepts and decision
A. Trace missing parking-to-path connectors by aligning the colourful official illustrated map with OpenStreetMap. Rejected: the diagram is not georeferenced trailhead evidence, and joining paths would imply reliable navigation and access where neither is established.
B. Connect the documented official Visitor Centre origin, named path and any ordered intermediate places to the user's selected hike, while keeping GIS points separate and failing closed. Implemented. No fabricated GPS coordinates, hiking connectors, complete route, GPX, permit or opening claims.

## November 1 additional source gate: legal walking surfaces (no guessed connectors)

Official Pukaskwa rules, https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/visit/reglement-rules-and-regulations (page modified May 12, 2025), explicitly prohibit going off trail or failing to use established hiking/walking trails and roadways, as well as entering Zone 1 preservation areas. Parks Canada's responsible-visit guide https://parks.canada.ca/voyage-travel/conseils-tips/guide explicitly says to use roadways and paths around campground facilities and not to cut through neighbouring campsites. This governs how the official Combination Hike's "walk past campsites" must be interpreted; it is not permission to shortcut through a campsite or to infer a mapped connector. Verified against official live webpages November 1, 2026.

**Editorial / interaction decision:** Every selected trail now surfaces a source-linked pedestrian access rule and one of three evidence states: *Visitor Centre origin but unverified GPS connector* (six regular day trails), *official named circuit but unverified GPS connector* (Combination Hike), or *closed — no walking approach* (White River Bridge). Source links are the park's rules and responsible-visit guidance. These states do not create any new coordinates, signed trailhead points, GPS downloads, route leg joins or current opening claims. They do not make a road traversable simply because OSM labels its geometry `highway=service`. The corridor and OSM junction evidence remain optional research context.

**Verified public route scope:** Parks Canada's day-hiking page gives the official named sequence Visitor Centre → Southern Headland → Horseshoe Beach → Beach Trail → North Loop Campground → Halfway Lake → park road return. It does not publish exact crossing geometry for the signed trail starts. The official GIS point database has Visitor Centre day-use parking, not certified trailhead points. The two public Trails APCA layers previously yielded no Hattie Cove linework. No sourced park-issued turn-by-turn parking-to-trail GPS route was established in this review, so the map remains explicitly non-navigational. Current seasonal public access and 2026 winter road conditions remain unverified.

**Release gate:** Test eight hike classifications, no trailhead coordinate promotion, sources present in no-script HTML, selected-hike synchronization, closed bridge override (including `last-known-closed`), and preserve original source attribution, SEO, ads, and map fallbacks. Before a later GPS-route release require a licensed Parks Canada entrance survey or separately verified signed trailheads PLUS documented authorized walking connections and current on-site access. Simply tying visible OSM ways into a continuous line is not acceptable.

## November 1: exact published campground drawing and label-based wayfinding
The agency also hosts a more detailed 1000 × 761 Hattie Cove campground map on https://parks.canada.ca/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/activ/camping/avant-front (original image https://pcweb2.azureedge.net/-/media/pn-np/on/pukaskwa/activ/2020/Campground_Map_FOR_PRINT_OTENTIKS-ADDITION.jpg). Its key distinguishes black solid public campground roads from dotted walking trails, with day-use Parking and Visitor Centre on the east of Hattie Cove. It explicitly labels Beach Trail, Boardwalk Beach Trail, Southern Headland, Bimose and Fire Walk/Coastal Hiking Trail. The broader official map shows Manito Miikana. The Parks Canada day hiking guide independently specifies the named Combination Hike road return.

**What this resolves:** visitors now get a second, close-up official campground drawing, tied to their chosen hike, with a source-linked guide to which printed label to find and how to identify established paths/roads. Six supported selected trails share five published printed-name references (Combination starts with Southern Headland). Manito remains on the broader park map. The White River closure disables approach highlighting.

**Important source limitation:** The blue hiker symbols in labelled boxes are map keys/route names and must *not* be promoted to verified GPS coordinates of signed physical entrances. The 1000×761 x/y annotations intentionally circle printed names, not entrances. Neither of these official drawings publishes a coordinate-accurate walkable GPS route from the GIS parking pin to each trail. Walking directions remain from the established published path and road network shown on Parks Canada's original map, not synthetic GPX or guessed lines. Road access and trail conditions must still be checked seasonally.
