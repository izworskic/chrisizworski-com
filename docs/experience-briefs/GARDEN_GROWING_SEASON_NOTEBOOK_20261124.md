## Visitor decision and alternatives
A gardener wants to know how the same crop and planting date might develop in their region and which upcoming nights need attention. Three opportunities: regional historical replay (existing validated weather records, low operational cost), current cold-night reference (existing NWS mode, stale/partial risk), live crop progress (blocked by incomplete daily observation data and independent field validation). Deliver first two and withhold third. Two credible concepts: an always-visible daily conditions board (quick weather but crowds the existing monthly jobs/planting interface and invites forecast overclaim); a compact, optional seasonal notebook (first screen anchor, date/cultivar/location action, historical timeline plus separate weather, reversible and no initial data traffic). Choose notebook. Unlike existing frost probability chart (historical station risk, date chosen for safety) and saved garden plan (crop timing by frost station), this tests cultivar heat accumulation against five actual gridded weather seasons and compares packet-day reference; a separate explicitly requested forecast uses a complete overnight interval. No generic copied dashboard, extra hero, imagery or animation.

External references checked 2026-11-24: Oregon State Croptime (https://extension.oregonstate.edu/impact/croptime-helps-vegetable-growers-fine-tune-planting-harvest-schedules) connects variety and planting date to heat models; local validation is not established. UMN Extension (https://extension.umn.edu/vegetable-growing-guides-farmers/crop-and-field-planning-tools-vegetable-farmers) separates crop scheduling from harvest windows, useful for packet comparison. Neither design or proprietary content is copied. Source register and limitations in GARDEN_WEATHER_EVIDENCE_V1.md.
