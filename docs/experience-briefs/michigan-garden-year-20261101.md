# Michigan gardening: your garden through the year

Experience OS 1.2.1. Canonical owner: `/michigan-gardening/`. Michigan is the jurisdiction, not an implied national recommendation. The question is: what does the year look like for the plants I chose, near my town? The existing local engine remains the source of dates, conditions and source limits.

## Research and comparison

Checked November 1, 2026: [University of Minnesota's Upper Midwest home garden calendar](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/upper-midwest-home-garden-care-calendar) organizes actual garden activities throughout the year; [Almanac's location-based planting calendar](https://www.almanac.com/gardening/planting-calendar) personalizes a crop schedule. Neither is the source of this tool's dates. MSU and Michigan climatologist sources remain visible on the page. A date is a planning reference, not a forecast or harvest promise.

Compare (A) another all-crop chart requiring the gardener to scan dozens of rows, and (B) a compact twelve-month notebook for the plants they selected, with a selectable month and an immediate list of actual engine references. Choose B: useful on a phone, visibly connected to the gardener's own choices, and no fabricated dates for an empty month. Unlike the site's existing frost-odds tool, it starts from plants and their actions; unlike the broader planting calendar's all-crop table, it begins with a personal month-by-month notebook. The design is a working garden journal, not a shared adventure-tool template.

## State and truth

Stable selected month is `YYYY-MM` or `all`; crop IDs and packet days remain the existing saved garden state. Month counts include engine windows overlapping that month, not invented days or harvest forecasts. Changed selections update the month list and pressed control together. Returning A→B→A restores the corresponding answer. Empty month says no dated note, not no garden work. Missing fall frost suppresses the fall reference; unknown crops continue to show limitations. No map, images, tracking or external service is required. No JS retains the existing gardening guides and explanatory fallback.
