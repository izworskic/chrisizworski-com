## North-star question
A nervous or curious gardener should finish one visit knowing: (1) why leafcutter bees are beneficial, (2) why they are unlikely to sting when left alone, (3) what to do now for the next generation, (4) how seasons differ for nesting and purchased cocoons, and (5) what, if anything, deserves a purchase.

## Discovery and scientific findings
1. **What the name covers:** “leafcutter bee” is a group, not one managed species. MSU E-3337 discusses native Michigan Megachile along with other cavity-nesting bees. Habitat guidance must not assume every species has the same incubation and winter lifecycle. The commercially sold *Megachile rotundata* is a species introduced from Eurasia, confirmed by USGS, UF/IFAS and USDA/SARE.
2. **Why they are useful:** women/females carry pollen under the abdomen, construct consecutive leaf-lined brood cells in cavities and visit summer flowers. Some are important crop pollinators, especially the alfalfa leafcutter for alfalfa seed. But “100x honeybees” and “triple vegetable production” are unqualified promotional claims; don't put them on the site. In particular, SARE says leafcutters and mason bees cannot buzz-pollinate tomatoes. UMN says tomatoes and peppers are often self-pollinating; cucumber/squash/pumpkin need insect transfer, with several other important bee groups. Build a mixed-pollinator garden, not a monoculture.
3. **Stings:** Solitary females rarely sting unless trapped or handled; not stingless. Do not tell visitors they cannot sting, or guarantee safety for allergic persons, children or pets. There are no hive-defense swarms/honey stores.
4. **Leaf cutting:** round/scalloped leaf-edge cuts often cosmetic on otherwise healthy plants (USU/UC IPM). They are building material, not leaf consumption. Avoid pesticides to treat cosmetic cuts.
5. **Habitat before hotels:** Xerces prioritizes native forage, natural stems, dead wood, nesting areas and no pesticides; not every bee hotel is beneficial. MSU emphasizes adequate rain shelter, southeast morning exposure, rigid mount, ~4–5 feet up, clean tunnel construction, 5–8 inch hollow stems, varied tunnel sizes (1/16–1/2 inches), cleanable replaceable nest material, seasonal pest monitoring. Small hotels support a subset of cavity nesters, not the ~70% nesting in ground.
6. **Timing:** MSU suggests setting out a house in Michigan late March to mid-April, leaving through late October. Nesting of most Michigan leafcutters generally completes July–August. These are **nest-house** management dates, NOT purchased cocoon release dates. Crown Bees recommends ~75°F sustained daytime temperatures and open blooms for incubated commercial *M. rotundata* cocoons. This is labeled vendor-specific, not a wild-bee species threshold.
7. **USDA hardiness zone caveat:** zone represents extreme winter low, not a blossom calendar nor a real-time bee-flight measure. The interface lets users select 3a–10b to receive a qualitative zone context, but it never transforms that into a precise emergence date. Actual supplied blooms and day-temperature observation drive managed-bee decisions. No geolocation and no unverified live weather.
8. **Autumn/winter:** Native/mixed occupied tubes should be protected and left intact; rotate new tunnels and let old ones emerge safely in spring/summer. MSU E-3337 describes an emergence box for occupied old tubes, winter protected outdoor or unheated storage, optional refrigerator 36–39°F for appropriate stored brood, cleaning/replacing material and guarding against ants, parasites, birds and moisture. Crown Bees advises not putting **ready-to-emerge purchased leafcutter cocoons** in a fridge and describes mature dormant summer-leaf brood in cool protected storage, with species/product-specific spring harvesting. Site distinguishes these states to avoid mixing conflicting protocols.
9. **Introduced bees risk:** USGS / USDA show *M. rotundata* was introduced and naturalized; buying commercially raised insects should not be equated to rescuing native bees. Default is to welcome local wild bees and support natural habitats. No broad recommendation to transport cocoons across regions; ask species, origin, pathogen screening and local regulations first.
10. **Realistic outcomes:** Nest occupancy is not guaranteed; pollination gains depend on local species, crop and ecosystem. No invented flight forecasts, dates, yields, stock or live prices.

## Release-and-dormancy deep research addendum — November 1, 2026
### Biological states — do not conflate
1. **Young larvae in fresh occupied nesting tubes.** They are still feeding and building the cocoon. Being leaf-capped does not prove maturity. Moving tubes too early can interrupt growth and food access. USU recommends late-autumn removal after flights end and gentle capped-end-up storage.
2. **Fully developed dormant prepupae (diapause).** For `Megachile rotundata`, the 5th instar spins a leaf-lined cocoon and enters prepupal diapause, often for the winter. This is the state producers cold store, sometimes under highly controlled refrigeration. Other local Megachile species can overwinter at different stages and some are bivoltine. A user's ZIP/USDA zone cannot reveal state.
3. **Pre-incubated almost-emerging adults.** Crown Bees explicitly ships `M. rotundata` near emergence. They cannot be treated as still dormant, returned to a refrigerator, or delayed until arbitrary later flower dates. Delivery week should align with dependable warmth, open flowers and ready nest.
### Summer release criteria
- Crown Bees: consistent daytime ~75°F (24°C) and abundant blossoms; choose same delivery week target crop is flowering; immediately place ready shipments. This is **manufacturer advice** not universal native bee biology. If heat >90°F (32°C) during incubation, avoid hot enclosures; seek seller guidance on emergers in breathable bag and early-morning release.
- MSU: set out bee hotel late March–mid April in Michigan, keep through late October; most summer leafcutters finish nesting July–August. This is **nest setup timing**, not a Michigan calendar-based date for releasing purchased cocoon shipments.
- Scientific commercial field guide: over-wintered prepupae typically incubated ~21–25 days at 28–30°C before peak bloom; cited Kemp/Bosch 2001 suggests 27–32 days at 29°C for male/female diapausing bees, whereas vendor calendar gives ~20 days at 84°F/30°C or ~42 days at 70°F/21°C. Different protocols produce different figures, so no universal countdown for household users.
- Zone provides only winter climate context. Release advisory is a **conditional**, not date forecast: sustained suitable local daytime warmth, adequate pollen/nectar and compatible dry nesting cavity all verified by the gardener.
### End of season and winter protection
- With home wild nest tubes, maintain the house through nesting. When female activity has ceased in late autumn/after first frosts and larvae have had adequate development, move gently capped-end up to dry unheated shed/garage, well-ventilated and pest-protected, or protect in place if truly dry, as USU and MSU recommend. Do not treat one night at 60°F as a switch. Crown Bees uses ~60°F as an autumn activity clue; prioritize observations over a single reading. A fall mesh bag keeps out pests; avoid airtight sealed plastic.
- Spring: rotate used tubes into a shaded/dark emergence box with exit opening and fresh nearby tubes, let prior-year residents emerge at their natural time, then discard/clean original tubes after emergence. Unknown species are **not** to be opened, warmed, or harvested via commercial procedures.
- **Why sources seem to disagree about refrigeration:** Utah State Extension and Crown Bees discourage putting intact leafy **home bee-hotel tubes** in a household refrigerator (mould/moisture risk). By contrast, Canadian Food Inspection Agency recommends a commercial `M. rotundata` facility store mature isolated brood at **4–10°C = 39–50°F** with controlled airflow, humidity and hygiene. UF/IFAS describes professionally extracted brood at **5–8°C**. SARE discusses 35°F for ~150–210 days under professional management. These are context-specific, not contradictory biological facts.
- **Pre-cooling development hazard:** 2024 Alberta research guidance says young nests need to complete larval feeding and cocoon formation before full winter cold storage; one industrial protocol suggests about **three weeks near 20°C / 68°F**, subject to developmental stage, or less at higher temperatures. Do NOT apply as a simple 'keep all wild nests at 68°F in your heated house for 3 weeks' rule.
- Mature commercial prepupae do not need to be 'put to sleep' each fall by a gardener; diapause is a naturally occurring developmental state. Never offer instructions to force already-emerged adult bees back into dormancy.

### Data providers and architecture
- New official NWS forecast endpoint `/api/leafcutter-release-weather?site=bay-city`; `?mode=locations` supplies 31 Michigan city references already defined in `lib/maple-sap-engine.cjs`. These are CITY REFERENCE FORECASTS, **not GPS or property-level measurements**. Zero location permission requests or user-account data.

No API credentials, new datastore, Redis, scheduled tasks or scraping. Only whitelisted NWS hourly URL host/patterns accepted, with timeout, public 15-minute edge cache, noindex and error fallback.
- New `lib/leafcutter-release.cjs` converts official hourly `isDaytime` readings 09:00–18:00 at the provider's local timezone into 7-day forecast **daytime-high approximations**; a day needs six available daytime hours. Two consecutive adequately covered days with high ≥75°F is a *planning signal only*, inspired by Crown Bees seller shipping threshold, never a validated native-bee emergence rule.
- Source issuance time from NWS `generatedAt` or `updateTime`; issued >18 hours earlier, unparseable or incomplete cannot make a favorable readiness judgment. Every result labels source, issue time, check time and optional maximum daytime chance of precipitation.
- The daily card is real forecast data, not observed flight or live local temperature. The browser explicitly requests a forecast after the user selects a location, rather than doing unlimited weather reads for every page visitor. NWS outages yield a no-claim status; the planner avoids any positive forecast recommendation after a failed attempt.
- Forecast can influence the personal decision ONLY for currently selected Michigan month; no inserting a current November forecast into a user-selected July planning scenario. Selecting another city invalidates the old forecast. Wild/nonnative bee distinction and commercial-brood-stage guards remain stronger than any weather signal.
- The candidate release decision also requires user-reported flowers and clean nest occupancy/material readiness; without flowers or available nest, a favorable forecast is NOT green-light clearance. Actual purchase/species and shipment window still require supplier approval.
- Forecaster station and gardener's lakeshore property can diverge. Map pin / GPS coordinates are not claimed to represent an exact garden. NWS status has an evidentiary qualifier: official forecast; gardener bloom/nest report is user observation; no current verified bee activity.

## November 1, 2026 — ZIP area weather matching and geospatial confidence

**Why:** The previous 31-place Michigan forecast list included city-center forecast grid locations that could differ materially from users along Saginaw Bay or in long rural ZIP areas. A selected ZIP should *change the actual official NWS forecast point*, not merely produce a city label.

**Source choice:** 2020 Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs) accessible via the U.S. Census Bureau's official TIGERweb ArcGIS REST service, layer 1:
https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov/arcgis/rest/services/TIGERweb/PUMA_TAD_TAZ_UGA_ZCTA/MapServer/1

**Scientific/geographic limitations:** A USPS ZIP Code is an address delivery-route identifier, not a guaranteed polygon. ZCTAs are Census approximations made from 2020 blocks; not all valid USPS ZIPs have ZCTA mappings, especially P.O. boxes and business-only codes. A Census `INTPTLAT/INTPTLON` is one **representative interior point** of a statistical area, **not** the user's home, garden, nearest weather sensor or ZIP delivery boundary. This source is deliberately described as approximate. Michigan validation requires five ASCII numeric digits beginning in 48 or 49, exact matching ZCTA5 in Census response, only one feature, and a valid Michigan-bounded point. It fails closed when no ZCTA is published. There is no automatic substitution of Bay City/another city when a ZIP fails.
Official documentation: https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/geography/guidance/geo-areas/zctas.html

**Execution:** New `lib/leafcutter-zip.cjs` resolves `ZIP 48706` through a fixed-host, fixed-endpoint Census TIGERweb query that permits only a strictly validated five-digit MI ZIP. It requests `ZCTA5,INTPTLAT,INTPTLON`, `returnGeometry=false`, `f=json`. In `api/leafcutter-release-weather.js` the optional `zip=48706` parameter takes precedence over any `site` parameter, and the returned Census point goes directly to NWS `/points/43.****,-83.****`, then to its returned official hourly forecast URL. This is not reverse geolocation and never requests a device GPS permission. Users may instead leave the ZIP blank and use the existing 31 named reference cities.

**UI:** ZIP input is first-class, with a clearly secondary city fallback. Entering/editing a ZIP invalidates earlier forecast cards, prevents stale city data from being misapplied, and rechecks the current ZIP on the next explicit click. The result identifies `ZIP 48706 · Census 2020 statistical ZIP-area reference point` with approximate N/W coordinates and attribution; does not claim precise property data. Invalid/outside-Michigan ZIP is rejected before network; missing mapped ZCTA gives a visible explanation (especially P.O. boxes) and instructions to clear ZIP and choose a city. Census outages remain distinct from NWS outages. User-entered ZIP is not stored in browser storage or automatically inserted in page URL; API request necessarily includes ZIP as a server query parameter.

**Safeguards:** Source coordinates are parsed as finite numbers; match exact requested ZCTA; ignore multiple/ambiguous candidates; reject extra query/SSRF controls. Census and NWS fetches have bounded timeouts, no upstream redirects, and no new API key, Postgres table, Redis, cron job or tracking. The existing official NWS forecast is still forecast-only; typical flowers are still hypothetical and gardener-reported blooms and clean tubes remain required before any possible release planning opportunity.

**Tests:** New coverage in `tests/leafcutter-release-weather.test.js`: valid 48xxx/49xxx, rejects 90210, malformed and injection payloads, exact matched centroid, out-of-state geographic bounds, simulated 2020 Census query and point-dependent NWS request, non-ZCTA P.O. box 404 and fail-closed Census outage. Existing interactive planner tests extended to assert ZIP priority, invalidation, wrong-state rejection and fallback city. Browser QA at 390/1440 px exercises ZIP result, no-ZCTA error and return to city, including no horizontal overflow, no-JS and accessibility basics.

**Next precision layer, not part of this release:** If the site eventually collects an approximate opt-in map pin instead of ZIP, verify NWS point directly, show station/grid resolution and preserve no-GPS defaults. For broad rural ZCTAs, distance from centroid to garden can still be substantial; do not add a 'hyperlocal' label to this release.

## November 1, 2026 — Thermal-day enhancement after ZIP release

**Visitor need:** Two areas may both peak near 75°F but have different overnight cool-downs and patterns of warmth. Help readers distinguish a single fleeting warm high from accumulating daytime and overnight thermal energy, without inventing an adult emergence forecast.

### Research and decision boundary
- University of Wisconsin AgWeather explains that growing degree-days are accumulated heat above a selected **organism-/stage-specific threshold**, and that calculation method and biofix must agree with the validated model: https://agwx.soils.wisc.edu/thermal-models/degree-days .
- Classic 1992 work `Whitfield & Richards` on commercial `Megachile rotundata` immature *egg/larval stages* estimated 15°C as a developmental base and 116°C-days for **egg-to-prepupal** development; this is **not a number of degree-days to adult emergence**: https://doi.org/10.1051/apido:19920102 .
- `Kemp & Bosch (2001)` show diapausing adult emergence depends on controlled postwinter incubation temperature, stage and sex: https://academic.oup.com/aesa/article-abstract/94/2/244/187157 .
- Newly available USDA Agricultural Research Service dataset (May 22, 2026) describes a *three-phase interrupted-development model for managed adult emergence*, based on 2021–2023 lab experiments at 21–31°C and explicit cold/transfer stages. It cannot be projected directly onto uncontrolled outdoor weather or incubated vendor shipments: https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/data-from-a-three-phase-interrupted-development-model-and-web-application-for-predicting-t . Do not imply the site has implemented or validated that research model.
- Crown Bees purchased summer leafcutters are **already incubated** and ready to emerge in sustained warm bloom weeks; they cannot be cooled to restart diapause. Advice still requires source-specific readiness and actual food/nesting material: https://crownbees.zohodesk.com/portal/en/kb/articles/how-to-select-bee-ship-date .

### Model actually implemented
- `lib/leafcutter-release.cjs` exports a pure `summarizeHeat(periods, fresh, now, timeZone)` function consuming the **same official NWS hourly weather records** as the existing forecast. No new upstream, auth secrets, Redis, background task or location collection.
- For each full local day with all hour positions, compute **GDD50°F = sum(max(0, forecast hourly air temperature°F - 50°F)) / number of hourly entries**. The daily values are added over the available complete days, generally the next 5–6 days. This is a general **garden warmth context index** with a familiar base of 50°F, not a species-specific thermal or reproductive threshold; it is NOT presented as a prediction of wild bee emergence. It differs from many formal daily sine or max/min GDD calculations and is labelled explicitly as hourly approximation.
- Alongside GDD, count forecast **daylight hours from 09:00–18:00 local** at or above 75°F. The seller's 75°F guidance is a flight/shipping rule of thumb; hourly counts illustrate persistence, not sufficient conditions to place bee cocoons.
- Full-day coverage requires 00:00 through 23:00 local and 24 distinct hourly samples (or 23/25 across a legitimate daylight-saving offset change). Partially covered day and missing hours show **partial** with no invented 0 GDD; NWS forecast older than 18 hours or fewer than two complete forecast days provides **unavailable**, not a total.
- `/api/leafcutter-release-weather` adds a `heat` evidence object. Every value is **forecast**, not an observed running season total.
