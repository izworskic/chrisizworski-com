# Michigan garden engine: source-qualified fall planning

November 1, 2026. Engine owner: `public/assets/garden-tools/plant-now.js`; Node adapter `lib/plant-now/index.js`. Consumers: Michigan gardening hub and Michigan soil-temperature page (location handoff). The older zone-6a planting calendar and national planting tools use separate engines; this change does not silently replace their data or improve their calculations.

`fallReference(id)` reports the MSU E3172 table as approximate days **after germination**, not days from sowing. Head cabbage requires transplants per the bulletin. Values are generic examples, not the user's packet, nor guaranteed harvest dates. Reference: https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/files/E3172_-_Fall_Vegetable_Crops_for_your_Garden.pdf, page 2. Existing engine decisions remain source-qualified and do not borrow unsupported dates.

`fallPlan(station,{year,maturityDays,bufferDays,frostISO?})` returns a structured result. `referenceBasis` distinguishes visitor input from a station's published typical frost. Missing station data is an explicit failure; an invalid calendar date is rejected; UTC date arithmetic handles leap years and year rollover. The caller controls the buffer; no source claims that a chosen buffer guarantees success. The calculation is not a last safe sowing date, a live observation or a harvest prediction. The homepage's fall calculator now uses this common calculation.

Spring-only soil rules no longer give plant-now status before March 1. This boundary is a conservative display choice, not an MSU-specified local planting date or proof that the ground is frozen. The July 31 boundary remains a display limit. Warm-seed soil must exceed 60°F, and passing a station's 25% freeze reference does not mean frost risk is gone.

Still needed for a complete garden engine: crop-specific current MSU source verification for the plants labeled not reported; variety-specific starting, transplant, sowing and fall harvest distinctions; more complete crop-specific multi-season rules; regional validation and user testing. A neighboring state's rules or the existing 47-crop calendar offsets must not be silently promoted to verified Michigan data. No visitor outcome is claimed.

## Multi-crop contract and potatoes

`gardenPlan(station, items, {dateISO, conditions, frostISO})` accepts stable crop IDs and optional packet maturity and buffer values. It returns the existing source-qualified current status per crop, explicit errors for duplicates and unknown IDs, optional fall arithmetic and its provenance, and a generic MSU reference separately from user-supplied packet days. It never substitutes the table value for the packet. Garlic is explicitly excluded from the fall-harvest countdown because it overwinters. The home garden list consumes this contract and can store optional packet days locally; its 14-day buffer is an exposed planning assumption, not a promise.

Potatoes: [MSU Potato Breeding and Genetics, home gardening](https://www.canr.msu.edu/potatobg/Files/Potato-Home-Gardening/Grow%20Potato%20MSU_2pager.pdf) says spring planting at 45°F or above. The engine asks for a soil reading at planting depth, retains a conditional workable-bed check, and does not manufacture a station-specific opening date. Its winter-to-spring month boundary is a display convention. Soil readings from a nearby station should not be described as measurements of a visitor's own bed.

Remaining work: this release does not fully source every listed herb or flower, does not build bed layout or predict harvest, and does not merge the separate national or 47-crop calendar engines. A full source-by-source inventory is needed before expanding verified rules.

Basil: [Michigan State organic vegetable production guide, Herbs chapter](https://www.canr.msu.edu/foodsystems/uploads/files/full-pdf.pdf) describes basil as tender and recommends direct seeding or transplanting after frost danger has passed. This is advisory guidance, not a false guarantee based on the station's 25% date. Turnips: MSU's [How to Plant Vegetables](https://www.canr.msu.edu/ingham/uploads/files/VegetablePlantingGuide.pdf) names a 40°F minimum germination temperature. The engine keeps the seasonal display boundary explicit.


## Expanded coverage and calendar years

MSU's [squash guide](https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/resources/pdfs/pumpkinsquash2011.pdf) includes zucchini and winter squash; its [planting guide](https://www.canr.msu.edu/ingham/uploads/files/VegetablePlantingGuide.pdf) covers warm-season melons and squash. The tool's above-60°F check is a conservative threshold, not an ideal germination temperature for every variety. Without a soil reading, the engine now asks the gardener to check instead of declaring the bed ready. [Sweet corn](https://www.canr.msu.edu/uploads/resources/pdfs/sweetcorn2011.pdf), [dill](https://www.canr.msu.edu/nativeplants/uploads/files/Dill2013.pdf) and [cilantro](https://www.canr.msu.edu/foodsystems/uploads/files/full-pdf.pdf) get qualitative source-specific guidance without a fabricated station-specific planting deadline.

The multi-crop result now includes structured spring events with explicit ISO years and sources. Dates are calculated from the station's month-and-day reference in the actual year, including leap years; after the spring display season, events refer to the following spring. Fall events remain explicitly labeled planning estimates. Packet days can be edited or cleared within each saved plant.

## Calendar integration and regional plant guidance

The Michigan calendar retains its 47 established names and legacy links, but its visitor-visible dates and statuses now use `calendarEntry` from the same engine as the homepage. It respects typical, cautious and visitor-entered spring references without treating them as frost-free guarantees. Onion sets and seed-started onions remain distinct. Unknown fall frost at a station is shown as unavailable; garlic is not assigned a first-frost-based window. No end date is invented for open-ended sowing and transplant guidance. Static tables contain qualitative source-based advice for non-JavaScript visitors. The previous unverified offset schedule is no longer presented as an authoritative planting calendar.

All listed herbs and flowers now have source-qualified qualitative guidance. Michigan sources remain preferred; when only regional information was found, Minnesota or Iowa Extension is named explicitly, and no Michigan station-specific date is inferred. These sources describe growing practices, not a local weather observation. The regionally sourced guides are https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/growing-herbs, https://extension.umn.edu/news/where-bouquets-begin-growing-cutting-garden-seed, https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/direct-seeding-vegetables-and-annuals-preparation-and-care, and https://yardandgarden.extension.iastate.edu/how-to/how-start-petunias-seed-indoors. Variety-specific dates still depend on the packet and local conditions.

The separate national planting product remains owned by `izworskic/national-planting`. Its local instructions require explicit versioned package or HTTP contracts for cross-tool behavior. Its national weather-aware model has different geography, crop methods and uncertainty inputs; it is not safe to silently substitute Michigan station rules for that model. Its integration remains separate work.

A versioned read-only `/api/michigan-garden-plan-v1` contract exposes the existing engine's Michigan station, current crop guidance, rule/source metadata, and uncertainty to independently owned tools. It requires an explicit Michigan town or ZIP and returns a clear error for unknown locations. It does not expose live weather or calculate a national frost deadline. The national-calendar client change is a separately reviewed integration: it adds Michigan notes alongside its weather-aware national model, labels their different inputs, makes no request for non-Michigan locations, and keeps its national plan usable when the Michigan contract fails. The two models remain distinct, rather than silently replacing national calculations.
