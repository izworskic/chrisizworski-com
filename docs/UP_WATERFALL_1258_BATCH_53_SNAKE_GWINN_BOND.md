## Lower Falls (Snake Creek GIS record), GIS #152 (MARQUETTE)

**Disposition:** `snake-creek-gis-waterway-versus-salmon-trout-lower-falls-probable-identity-conflict`; noindex research. **Public access verdict:** A complete legally cleared public visitor route is not established.

Michigan GIS #152 labels Lower Falls on SNAKE CREEK at 46.815112,-87.804203. A USGS GNIS-derived Lower Falls record in the Ives Hill quad at 46.815278,-87.803611 is only about 49 metres away. The GNIS Snake Creek mouth at 46.811313,-87.812907 is about 786 metres from the state waterfall point. American Whitewater's Salmon Trout River description places Snake Creek and then Clear Creek as tributaries between Middle Falls and the downstream Lower Falls, not at the Lower Falls itself. Michigan DNR's November 2023 regulation describes Lower Falls as a Salmon-Trout River feature. These records strongly suggest the GIS #152 WATERWAY label conflicts with the actual mainstem Lower Falls. Do not merge the feature into a different waterfall record without official hydrography/alias confirmation.

American Whitewater reports the Middle/Lower Salmon Trout River falls within the Huron Mountain Club's access-restricted private property, and the club confirms gated, patrolled road access. There is no public visiting corridor, approved parking, walking route, or legal backcountry approach to this exact waterfall. A broad old tourism list placing 'Lower Falls' on Snake Creek west of Big Bay is name evidence, not public admission or permission.

**Evidence age:** No 2026 change in Huron Mountain Club access policy or verified parcel boundary permitting public sightseeing was found. River-running descriptions and DNR fisheries rules are not trail permissions.

**Decision:** Private/restricted access; research only. Do not draw a route to Snake Creek, Lower Falls, a club gate or the raw waterfall point.

**To promote:** Verify official hydrography and the 49-m GIS/GNIS probable mainstem Lower Falls alias, confirm exact present owner and public access permission, and obtain current legal parking and a continuous public trail before any promotion. Until then do not imply any club entry is permitted.

## First Falls (Gwinn), GIS #176 (MARQUETTE)

**Disposition:** `municipal-river-park-first-rapid-park-access-supported-waterfall-route-not-surveyed`; noindex research. **Public access verdict:** The municipal Farquar/Metsa Tourist Park day-use area is officially documented; this exact falls trail/parking remains unverified.

Michigan GIS #176 locates First Falls on the East Branch Escanaba River at 46.285073,-87.434620, within about 10 metres of the field account at 46.285160,-87.434570. The firsthand source describes a roughly THREE-INCH drop into a dark pool. The community tourism page explicitly names First Falls, and Forsyth Township's adopted September 2024 recreation plan identifies Farquar/Metsa Tourist Park's public day-use area and an Eagle Scout-built rustic trail heading north along the river, with views of a small waterfall. This establishes a legitimate park-level place to walk, but the township does not certify exact waterfall naming/point geometry or current site conditions.

Forsyth Township's adopted plan verifies a municipal day-use area, campground, picnic pavilion and rustic riverside trail, with accessibility grade 2 for the park. A separately accessible path links two portions of the day-use area; this MUST NOT be described as wheelchair access along the rustic waterfall trail. A 2009-era field description suggests parking near a loop/pavilion, but no current designated waterfall-specific lot, signed First Falls trail endpoint, live seasonal day-use hours or surveyed parking-to-fall polyline was found. Visitors can inquire at the park; do not advertise a safe swimming hole or promise waterfall-specific parking.

**Evidence age:** The official park description is adopted 2024, not a live November 2026 trail/park operations report. The 3-inch flow feature is not a large waterfall and could look different at changing water levels.

**Decision:** Public park experience is supported; exact First Falls navigation and current trail conditions are not certified. No automatic waterfall driving/route marker.

**To promote:** Confirm actual day-use parking and current season opening with Forsyth Township and identify the maintained or accepted river-trail endpoint for the approximately 10-matched First Falls feature. If promoting, present primarily as an honest municipal riverside walk and minor drop, not a major waterfall attraction.

## Second Falls (Gwinn), GIS #177 (MARQUETTE)

**Disposition:** `municipal-river-park-second-small-rapid-357m-gis-field-conflict`; noindex research. **Public access verdict:** The municipal Farquar/Metsa Tourist Park day-use area is officially documented; this exact falls trail/parking remains unverified.

Michigan GIS #177 locates Second Falls at 46.285692,-87.434130. A firsthand waterfall catalog locates the named Second Falls at 46.288870,-87.433460, about 357 metres farther north. State GIS First #176 and Second #177 lie just 78 metres apart, whereas the two firsthand fall points are about 421 metres apart along the river vicinity. The firsthand account calls Second Falls an upstream THREE-INCH ripple approached along a park trail past First Falls. Forsyth Township's adopted 2024–2029 recreation plan independently documents a rustic trail heading north from the Farquar/Metsa Tourist Park day-use area, but only describes views of 'a small falls' and does not certify Second Falls or either coordinate.

There is a genuine municipal public park and official riverside trail. That is a stronger access signal than a random unmarked woods fall, but its existence does not settle a 357-m name/coordinate disagreement or prove a signed endpoint to this particular Second Falls. A firsthand account mentions a rock slab and informal fishing branches. These are not an ADA route or current approved parking-to-viewpoint geometry; no route polyline or falls-point driving directions should be generated. River rocks, dark water and seasonal park restrictions require caution.

**Evidence age:** No November 2026 official park trail survey, opening notice, Second Falls sign or water-safety check was found. The township plan documents an existing trail as of its September 2024 adoption, not live conditions.

**Decision:** Public Farquar/Metsa Tourist Park is documented, but the Second Falls point and continuous waterfall-specific trail remain unresolved. No GIS-based route.

**To promote:** Reconcile GIS #177 against firsthand 357-m-upstream coordinate with township/hydrographic field evidence, confirm whether First/Second are separately signed named drops, and map only a municipally approved parking-to-viewpoint trail if one is confirmed.

## Little Falls (Bond Falls Flowage), GIS #23 (ONTONAGON)

**Disposition:** `bond-flowage-interior-creek-middle-branch-waterway-conflict-inundation-rough-track`; noindex research. **Public access verdict:** A complete legally cleared public visitor route is not established.

Michigan GIS #23 records Little Falls at 46.377188,-89.099486 with waterway attribute MID ONTONAGON RIVER. A firsthand source places the fall at 46.378580,-89.099840 on INTERIOR CREEK, about 157 metres away, and describes the main inlet to Bond Falls Flowage. Michigan DNR's Ontonagon River watershed falls table names Little Falls on the MIDDLE BRANCH Ontonagon River; its historical Bond Falls Flowage angler-study map shows Little Falls and Interior Creek in the same reservoir inlet vicinity. This is an authentic named natural feature but the precise inlet/mainstem label is unresolved. A generic tourism page instead claims 'Little Falls' on the MICHIGAMME River and maps it at 46.6903,-89.2247, about 36.1 km away; do not borrow that false label or location.

A firsthand report describes unmarked logging two-tracks, forks, wet and potentially impassable soft sections, then an informal bank path near the impoundment. American Whitewater acknowledges Little Falls as a brief Class II riverbed drop on the reach to Bond Falls Flowage, possibly buried under reservoir water; its downstream paddling take-out is not certified public hiking parking at Little Falls. Michigan DNR confirms the wider Bond Falls Flowage but does not provide a present approved trailhead, safe driving end point, precise flowage-shore public access rights, or continuous legal trail to the GIS #23 point.

**Evidence age:** Little Falls can be almost or completely submerged at high flowage pool; under drawdown a drop of roughly 1–2 feet may be visible. This is reservoir-dependent, not a live water-level forecast. No November 2026 reservoir level or legal road condition is verified.

**Decision:** Named flowage-inlet ripple; visibility and access unconfirmed. Do not supply a logging-track parking pin or guaranteed waterfall sighting.

**To promote:** Resolve state GIS Middle Branch versus firsthand Interior Creek label, establish current regulated flowage pool level and appearance, prove road ownership and legal vehicle/shoreline access, and obtain a signed or otherwise verified complete visitor foot corridor before public routing.
