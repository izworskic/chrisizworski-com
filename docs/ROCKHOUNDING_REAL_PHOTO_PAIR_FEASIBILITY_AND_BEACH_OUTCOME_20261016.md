# Rockhounding Agate Beach paired-photo feasibility: decision record (2026-10-16)

## Answer

**No verified photograph pair has been located** that depicts the same Grand Marais Agate Beach segment before and after one of the eight source-verified 2026 GLOS Spotter offshore disturbance episodes. Several research phases tried: independently dated 2026 visitor photos, public Wikimedia Commons originals/geotags, older 2014 and 2016 pictures, a weekly original media watch, and actual webcam inventories. We should stop presenting another candidate-finder as if it completes physical validation.

The visible candidate evidence is:
- July 4–5, 2026 photographs from different publishers; each is well before the July 21 monitored event, and they are not a paired before/after.
- A possibly July 24, 2026 Agate Beach social video, self-reported and not geographically/photo-clock/stable-landmark matched; at best one uncertain **post-event** image, with no close pre-event frame.
- October 5 and October 12 real large offshore wave events have no known site-matched pre/post image pair in the reviewed sources.
- Older 2014 and 2016 shoreline views have unresolved camera positions/headings; source-verified 2016 vs 2026 camera GPS is **~192 m** apart and not storm-bracketing.
- The [Chamber of Commerce Grand Marais webcam listing](https://grandmaraismichigan.com/webcams/) is Bay View, Marina View and North Street; it does not establish an archived Agate Beach shore-facing repeat camera.
- A [Sept 26 2025 Flickr photo album](https://www.flickr.com/photos/crcasemyers/albums/72177720329430559) has several dated Agate Beach scenes, but one same-day album is **not** a 2026 storm-bracketing pair.
- Similar place names matter: a July 2026 popular Agate Beach sunset photo actually refers to Stanton Township near Toivola, not Grand Marais.

**A matching pair cannot be manufactured from available data.** An unknown photo orientation, local time zone, current waterline and weather-stage confounders should never be silently filled by a model. Do not spend paid scraping credits or build more visually attractive pseudo-comparisons that lack the core requirement.

## A more useful primary-grounding discovery: storms can REMOVE available stones

Longtime Grand Marais observer Karen Brzys left first-person historical research observations:
1. [March 22, 2012: spring beach, ice and rock](https://agatelady.blogspot.com/2012/03/spring-beach-post-2-plus-magnified.html): in some shoreline segments, moving or melting ice deposited rocks, while other sections remained obstructed.
2. [November 2013 local agate field account](https://www.agatelady.com/photo-gallery-customer-agate-finds-november2013.html): recurrent storms could remove stones to the lake or bury them in sand; in early August 2013 even the **Woodland Park** shoreline had almost no exposed stones in places. This is **not exactly the same reach as Agate Beach** and not a measured 2026 event.
3. [March 29, 2014 retrospective](https://agatelady.blogspot.com/2014/03/year-to-year-differences-in-grand.html): variability in late-March snow and shore-ice cover changed how accessible local beaches and stones were from one year to the next.

These descriptions are independent historical *qualitative* observations, **not numeric calibration labels**, not a controlled photo pair, and not directly linkable to the original June–October 2026 Spotter archive. They make an important scientific point: neither higher waves nor a storm ending can logically imply newly exposed agates. Sometimes waves bury or remove a rock layer. Seasonal ice is another mechanism, not the same as open-water storms.

## Product decision and stop-loss threshold

The 2026 Spotter-based engine can credibly show:
- Measured recent/archived offshore disturbance, direction and wave decline with staleness.
- Whether elevated conditions later returned.
- Independent NWS warnings, with unknown hazards never equated to safe.
- **Unverified site stone exposure** and independent collecting/access gates.

It CANNOT yet show calibrated *best agate hunting now* probabilities or declare safe post-storm visits. Visitor-facing language should use **“Offshore waves recently disturbed the area; beach exposure unknown”** rather than **“fresh stones likely”**.

**Stop searching for an exact pair as a critical-path development task** until one of the following occurs: (i) a genuinely Agate-facing archived fixed-view camera with original date-stamped frames is verified, (ii) independent source images with georeferenced same-vantage pre/post around the *same* real event are found, or (iii) a directly documented local post-storm sediment/stone observation with clear date/site is found. The weekly existing free-source geotag watch can keep scanning quietly; no further duplicate camera-finding engines or paid subscriptions are justified.

No site visit, agency outreach, creator contact, image rights acquisition, or paid APIs were required for this research decision. The new separate historical record in `data/rockhounding/historical-local-stone-availability-observations.json` is used to protect source distinction and support the existing noindex laboratory's explanatory text, **not to rewrite model thresholds**.

## Result

- Qualified 2026 offshore wave events: 8.
- Same-beach, same-vantage 2026 before/after storm photo pairs: **0**.
- Current field-measured new-stone outcomes: **0**.
- Historical local qualitative outcome directions: **both increased and decreased accessible rock**, documented.
- Current calibrated Agate Beach discovery opportunity: **NOT AVAILABLE**.

This is an explicit evidence limit, not a reason to hold up improving the tool's measured conditions layer.
