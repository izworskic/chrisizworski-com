## Rock Bluff Falls — Michigan GIS #22

**Status:** `bluff-creek-location-discrepancy-old-us45-unsigned-access` (noindex; not cleared for public route).

Michigan GIS #22 maps Rock Bluff Falls at 46.370607,-89.183436. A Western U.P. Visitors Bureau brochure places its three-drop falls at 46.373742,-89.185312, about 377 metres from the GIS point. A distinct field entry called Upper Rock Bluff Falls is roughly 1.37 kilometres from the state point; a 2023 reader comment suggests further possible confusion with Mateson Creek. These sources cannot be silently combined into a single exact visitor waypoint.

The regional tourism brochure suggests stopping near Old US-45 and walking alongside Bluff Creek; an independent field account describes steep forest, brush, an informal two-track and an ATV track. Neither confirms signed authorized parking, current vehicle access on Old US-45, ownership/right-of-way across the creek valley or a maintained footpath. A tourism direction is not a verified legal parking or public approach authorization.

**Decision:** Research only. Do not navigate to either historical falls point as parking or assume Old US-45 provides legal public visitor access.

**To qualify:** Reconcile Rock Bluff, Upper Rock Bluff and the Mateson Creek report with independently surveyed or official geographic identity; verify parcels, road jurisdiction, signed public parking and a continuous authorized walking corridor to the correct drops.

## Wolverine Falls — Michigan GIS #28

**Status:** `cisco-branch-upstream-displacement-paddling-hazard-no-hike` (noindex; not cleared for public route).

Michigan GIS #28 records Wolverine Falls at 46.405577,-89.348732 without a river label. Michigan DNR's Ontonagon River assessment identifies the falls on the Cisco Branch, and the federal Wild and Scenic Rivers description independently recognizes Wolverine Falls as a notable feature on that branch. American Whitewater includes a 2026 first-hand report placing Wolverine farther upstream than the traditional marked position; a September 2025 solo paddling account describes a substantial Class III drop along the Cisco Branch. Exact contemporary drop-point alignment remains unresolved.

The river is a federally designated Wild and Scenic corridor, but designation does not validate every bank parcel, forest spur, roadside stop, take-out or walking path. American Whitewater's Forest Road 6930 references describe expert paddling put-ins and take-outs, not a certified public waterfall hike. No manager-approved parking-to-viewpoint trail, safe river-bank lookout or fully verified right-of-way has been established.

**Decision:** Research-only river feature, not a signed walk-to waterfall. Do not use the GIS point or a whitewater take-out as a driving destination.

**To qualify:** Resolve the correct fall location against GPS-tracked and authoritative river maps; verify Ottawa National Forest/other land ownership, current MVUM road status, parking and a continuous public walking route to a safe viewpoint if one exists. Keep skilled paddling conditions separate from sightseeing guidance.
