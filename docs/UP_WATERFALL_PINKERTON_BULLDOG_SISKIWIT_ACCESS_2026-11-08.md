## 1. Pinkerton Creek: the trail crosses the creek; do not invent the waterfall

Michigan DNR publishes [Porcupine Mountains trail descriptions](https://www.michigan.gov/recsearch/-/media/Project/Websites/recsearch/documents/MapsO-S/PMWSP-trail-descriptions.pdf). Pinkerton Trail is 2.6 miles, including **South Boundary Road → Pinkerton Creek (1.4 mi)** and **Pinkerton Creek → Lake Superior Trail (1.2 mi)**. The descriptions mention the creek but do not identify a signed Pinkerton Falls waterfall spur. The park [official page](https://www.michigan.gov/recsearch/parks/porkies) says South Boundary Road from Headquarters Road to the Pinkerton Trailhead is closed to vehicles each winter beginning **December 1** until late spring.

[UP Travel](https://www.uptravel.com/listing/unnamed-falls-pinkerton-creek/4474/) separately lists an **unnamed** waterfall on Pinkerton Creek. Unrelated private waterfall-name directories describe a named **Pinkerton Falls** (one mirrors USGS GNIS 2126322), and some list **Traders Falls** on Pinkerton Creek. The third-party coordinate descriptions conflict; a named USGS-linked geographic feature does not establish that the UP Travel unnamed drop is that feature. [GNIS guidance](https://www.usgs.gov/us-board-on-geographic-names/what-geographic-names-information-system-gnis) explicitly does not provide directions or ownership/visitor rights. Until an independent DNR, GNIS original or surveyed map establishes the correspondence, do **not** merge these as one waterfall, infer road/trail connection or create a navigation marker. The creek crossing is NOT a waterfall trail certificate.

Sources and identity checks: [Anyplace America Pinkerton Falls GNIS mirror](https://www.anyplaceamerica.com/directory/mi/gogebic-county-26053/falls/pinkerton-falls-2126322/), [Michigan Upper Peninsula waterfall directory](https://michiganupperpeninsula.com/places-to-visit/waterfalls/), [UP Travel Pinkerton listing](https://www.uptravel.com/listing/unnamed-falls-pinkerton-creek/4474/).

**Next proof threshold:** an authoritative feature-specific identity join to the unnamed drop, and land-manager published continuous legal visitor route if such a route is ever described. Existing official Pinkerton Trail can be described only as trail information, not waterfall directions.

## 2. Bulldog Falls: legal wilderness context is not a signed approach

The [federal Wild & Scenic Yellow Dog River record](https://www.fws.gov/rivers/guidance/river/yellow-dog) covers the upper river from the outlet of Bulldog Lake Dam to the Ottawa NF boundary, describing a series of cascades. The cumulative river descent does **not** describe the single height of Bulldog Falls. The [USFS McCormick Wilderness brochure](https://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/stelprdb5109821.pdf) describes the **three-mile White Deer Lake Trail** and rugged country with difficult navigation; the White Deer Lake Trail is not an official Bulldog waterfall spur. The [USFS recreation guide](https://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/fseprd567804.pdf) notes foot access, generally unsigned paths, and that animal/user traces can be misleading.

An independent [field report on Bulldog Falls](https://www.waterfallsofthekeweenaw.com/yellow-dog-river/bulldog-falls/) describes a series of cascades downstream of Bulldog Lake and a primitive route with water crossings. We use this for feature context, **not** as a public parking/route source: unmaintained logs, an old footbridge and historic forest-road descriptions are not certified public infrastructure.

Separately, the [Yellow Dog Watershed Preserve's Community Forest](https://yellowdogwatershed.org/land-protection-program/yellow-dog-river-community-forest/) provides a publicly described trail to **Hills Falls**. Those visitors should use that appropriate existing outing, not have its parking transferred upstream as a Bulldog Falls arrival. Do not confuse with any generic “Yellow Dog Falls” map listing.

**Next proof threshold:** a current Forest Service source proving the continuous start-to-viewpoint route (or explicitly characterizing off-trail travel with boundaries/constraints). Without it, general public-wilderness status proves recreational context but **not** a safe trip for a general waterfall finder.

## 3. Siskiwit Falls: a real NPS portage, but no confirmed waterfall-viewing path

NPS [The Greenstone 2026](https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/upload/Web_Optimized_2026-Greenstone.pdf), page 6, records **Malone Bay to Siskiwit Lake as a 0.3-mile canoe portage with about 40 feet of elevation change**. The NPS overview map explicitly cautions against using it for navigation. This official portage is real. It is **not** an NPS-recommended hike of 0.3 miles **to Siskiwit Falls**.

NPS [Malone Bay campgrounds and docks](https://www.nps.gov/isro/planyourvisit/lake-superior-accessible-campgrounds.htm) provide park-access context. NPS [Voyageur II service information](https://home.nps.gov/isro/planyourvisit/voyageur-ii.htm) states Malone Bay calls require arranged passenger pickup or drop-off, and should not be presented as automatic anytime transportation. NPS [operating hours](https://www.nps.gov/isro/planyourvisit/hours.htm) and current [Superintendent's Compendium](https://www.nps.gov/isro/learn/management/superintendents-compendium.htm) close all islands to public access **November 1–April 15 each year**. As of the review date **November 8, 2026**, the island is closed. This recurring policy should not be misrepresented as a fresh weather or ferry feed.

UP Travel names Siskiwit Falls in the lake-outlet area. **We did not find an NPS signed trail or official waterfall-viewpoint spur** linking the 0.3-mile portage to the named feature. The portage being nearby does not imply a legal, managed, safe final walk. Additionally, [Bayfield County Wisconsin](https://www.bayfieldcounty.wi.gov/329/Hiking) maintains a *different* Siskiwit Falls near Cornucopia, with its own parking and trail; those directions cannot be used on Isle Royale.

**Next proof threshold:** official NPS waterfall-viewpoint positioning relative to an established open-season trail/portage and a qualified full-route assessment, plus transport availability. Never promise a same-day visit via Voyageur II.

## Architecture and UI experience decision

**A. Open three destination pages with route maps. Rejected.** Source truth does not support visitor routes. New URLs would create SEO competition with the main `/upper-peninsula-waterfalls/` and expose unverified navigation pins.

**B. Add dated primary-source access dossiers to three existing indexed tourism *source records* in the noindex atlas. Chosen.** This distinguishes who has verified the trail/portage and where evidence stops, with a direct case chooser, official links, seasonal rule, exact missing proof threshold, and a no-route verdict. The existing 640 evidence records remain 640. Sources are cited separately from route authorization; all three location/parking/geometry values are null and are prohibited by Python/JS tests.

**C. Live provider API or request-time Python. Rejected.** No supported current waterfall route/closure API exists; all sources are dated public documents with known applicability. Static offline Python validation, versioned JSON and lightweight JS controls deliver the evidence with no new backend or secret.

### Distinctiveness (Experience OS v1.2.1)

Existing U.P. Waterfall Explorer answers “which legally accessible waterfall should I visit?” with a shortlist, map and travel choices. The Evidence Atlas answers “what geographic/source evidence exists and what's the unresolved identity?” through source family filters. This **access gate research addition** answers a different task: “how far does the *land-manager's documented approach* actually go and why is a waterfall itinerary still unsupported?” The three-case editorial chooser and expandable source proof ledger are not a copy of a waterfall visit card, freighter ledger or rocket launch verdict.

First screen: remains the concise source context and safety caveat. The new access section is placed after the earlier 13-name identity summary. Clicking an exact case immediately narrows to one research record and displays an evidence trail. The existing map never shows a source-only pin. On source failure the controls remain disabled and a readable fallback to the main guide remains.

### Source register and scientific boundaries

All facts come from public federal/state land-manager pages or short factual observations from independent sources with direct outbound attribution. We do not copy historical route directions, imagery or extensive third-party descriptions. The official 2026 portage guide is a dated edition, not continuously updated. The older USFS trail brochure gives stable wilderness context but cannot prove present road/safety conditions. Current seasonal status should be checked against NPS and DNR pages before future travel. Each machine record has a reviewed date, primary source references, scientific/spatial limit, complete-route decision, missing proof and null coordinates.
