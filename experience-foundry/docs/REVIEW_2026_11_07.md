## Findings and decisions

The original v0.1 branch had two individually authored preview renderers, lexical registry discovery and 11 unit tests. It did not have a usable custom-project handoff, source-rights/scope contract, deterministic time replay, populated browser acceptance or a current pilot status. Empty previews could compile while proving almost nothing about interactive behavior. Prototype-key recipe lookup, null collection members, permissive dates, truthy safety flags and symlink output traversal were concrete validation defects.

Two approaches were considered:

1. Add a universal page generator and more skins. Rejected: this would encourage a generic output, conceal source/integration gaps and duplicate established tools.
2. Complete a decision-first workbench that creates a concrete research/implementation packet, supports separately authored preview interactions, preserves source boundaries and runs repeatable acceptance. Selected: it addresses the actual build/review failures and leaves room for custom experiences.

The two recipes remain useful examples, not a compulsory design language. Agents can plan a custom experience without selecting either one. A generated planning packet contains unverified tasks, not a completed design or delegated execution.

## Experience review

The journal uses evidence-type filtering over a readable chronological list. The maritime ledger uses record selection with a synchronized dossier, endpoints and scoped source qualification. These differ in decision, composition and interaction; no shared hero/card/map template was introduced. Shared pieces are escaping, time/source contracts and review warnings.

Initial rendered review exposed unnecessary scroll before the actual evidence. Compact headings and moving the secondary source desk after the primary interaction bring the decision earlier, particularly on phones. The two interfaces retain their own typography, hierarchy and control model. Long unbroken hostile text wraps without creating horizontal overflow.

## Value and loss criteria

Prefer a complete experience that improves a documented visitor decision over expanding the number of recipes or URLs. A useful reusable pattern must demonstrate a changed answer after an input, an accurate fallback and clear evidence boundaries. Any fabricated source/access/live claim, identical generic interface, missing owner CI or broken keyboard/no-JS path blocks the relevant product release regardless of aesthetics. No numerical ROI or scientific-skill score is assigned without evidence.
