          <div class="section-heading">
            <div>
              <p class="eyebrow">The one day it closes on purpose</p>
              <h2 id="bridgeWalkHeading">Is the Mackinac Bridge Closed on Labor Day?</h2>
            </div>
          </div>
          <p class="bridge-walk__answer">Yes. The Mackinac Bridge closes to public traffic from 6:30 a.m. to noon every Labor Day for the Annual Bridge Walk, and it reopens at noon. This is the only scheduled closure on the calendar. Every other closure on this page is weather, maintenance, or an incident.</p>
          <p class="bridge-walk__date"><strong data-walk-date>The next Bridge Walk falls on Labor Day.</strong> <span data-walk-countdown></span></p>
          <p class="bridge-walk__state" data-walk-state>Northbound and southbound traffic both stop. If you are driving between the peninsulas that morning, cross before 6:30 a.m. or wait until after noon, and expect a backlog at the toll booths once it reopens.</p>
          <div class="bridge-walk__facts">
            <div>
              <h3>Walking it</h3>
              <p>Free, and no registration. Walkers start from both ends: Bridge View Park on the west side of I-75 in St. Ignace, and the Jamet Street ramp on the east side of I-75 in Mackinaw City. The walk opens at 7:00 a.m. and nobody is allowed to start after 11:30 a.m.</p>
            </div>
            <div>
              <h3>Getting back</h3>
              <p>No buses or shuttles are provided. If you walk the full five miles across, you arrange your own ride back after the bridge reopens at noon. Most people walk to the midpoint and turn around so they finish where they parked.</p>
            </div>
            <div>
              <h3>Walking it both ways</h3>
              <p>The round trip is ten miles. Walkers heading back have to cross the midpoint by 10:00 a.m. or they get turned around, because the bridge has to be clear in time to reopen.</p>
            </div>
            <div>
              <h3>Accessibility</h3>
              <p>The Bridge Authority takes requests for reasonable accommodation by phone at 906-643-7600 and asks for several days of notice. TTY users can reach the Michigan Relay Center at 800-649-3777.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
          <p class="bridge-walk__source">Start locations, timing, and the closure window are set by the Mackinac Bridge Authority and confirmed each spring. Check the official page before you go.</p>
          <a class="button button-outline" href="https://www.mackinacbridge.org/events/walk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-growth-cta="mackinac-bridge-walk">Open the official Bridge Walk page &#8599;</a>

        <section class="faq-section" aria-labelledby="faqHeading">
          <div class="section-heading">
            <div>
              <p class="eyebrow">Straight answers</p>
              <h2 id="faqHeading">Mackinac Bridge Crossing Questions</h2>
            </div>
          </div>
          <div class="faq-list">
            <details>
              <summary>Is the Mackinac Bridge closed for the Bridge Walk?</summary>
              <p>On Labor Day the bridge closes to public traffic from 6:30 a.m. to noon for the Annual Bridge Walk, then reopens. It is the only closure on the bridge calendar that is scheduled in advance. See the Bridge Walk section above for timing and start locations.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Can I drive across during the Bridge Walk?</summary>
              <p>No. The bridge closes to all traffic from 6:30 a.m. until noon every Labor Day for the Annual Bridge Walk, and both directions are shut for that whole window. There is no escort, no exception for high-profile vehicles, and no way across by road during those hours. This is the only closure that appears on the calendar in advance; every other closure is weather, maintenance, or an incident, and none of those can be predicted more than a day or two out. Plan to cross before 6:30 a.m. or after the bridge reopens at noon, and allow extra time at the toll plaza once traffic starts moving again, because several hours of held-back traffic clears at once. The walk itself runs from St. Ignace south to Mackinaw City. Ferries to Mackinac Island operate from both ends of the Straits and are not affected by the bridge closure.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Is the Mackinac Bridge closed right now?</summary>
              <p>The live panel at the top reads the current Mackinac Bridge Authority report and identifies open, advisory, escort, partial-closure, and closed conditions. If that feed is unavailable, this tool will not guess. Use the direct official-status link instead.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Is the wind shown here measured on the bridge?</summary>
              <p>No. The Bridge Authority does not publish a real-time bridge-deck wind or gust reading in the public conditions feed used by this tool. The NOAA number comes from Mackinaw City or the western Straits and is shown only as off-bridge weather context. The official bridge report always controls.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Will the Mackinac Bridge close tomorrow?</summary>
              <p>No weather forecast can guarantee a closure. The hourly cards show when forecast sustained wind approaches MDOT's published ranges, but ice, visibility, storms, maintenance, and the bridge's own readings can change the decision. Recheck immediately before leaving.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>What is the best time to cross the Mackinac Bridge?</summary>
              <p>There is no reliable public forecast for the easiest crossing window because the Bridge Authority does not publish future restriction decisions or live wait times. Compare the current official status, both cameras, NWS approach forecast, radar, and Mi Drive events here, then recheck immediately before you leave.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>What counts as a high-profile vehicle?</summary>
              <p>MDOT includes semis, RVs, campers, vans, panel trucks, enclosed trailers, open trailers carrying anything, pickups with caps, tonneau covers or cargo, and work trucks with mounted toolboxes, ladders, or equipment. Use the checker above or read the <a href="/mackinac-bridge-rv-trailer-wind-rules/">complete RV and trailer guide</a>.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Can someone drive my vehicle across for me?</summary>
              <p>Yes. The Mackinac Bridge Authority runs a driver assistance program around the clock for people who would rather not make the crossing themselves, which is common enough to be a standing service rather than a favour. An Authority driver takes your vehicle across for $10 plus the normal toll for that vehicle. You must be at least 18, and you complete the Authority's waiver and vehicle checklist before handing over the keys. It runs in both directions, 24 hours a day, including during high-wind advisories when ordinary drivers may be held or escorted. It is worth knowing about if you tow, drive a high-profile vehicle, or simply find the height and the open grating difficult, which is a more common reason than most people expect. Pickup arrangements differ slightly depending on which side you start from, so check the instructions for your direction. See the <a href="/mackinac-bridge-driver-assistance/">step-by-step pickup instructions</a> for each direction.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>How much is the Mackinac Bridge toll?</summary>
              <p>The Mackinac Bridge toll is charged per axle rather than per crossing, which catches people out when they are towing. Passenger vehicles pay $2 per axle, so an ordinary two-axle car is $4 each way. Every other vehicle class pays $5 per axle, and a trailer adds its own axles to the count: a two-axle car pulling a single-axle trailer is billed as three axles. The toll is the same in both directions and is collected at the St. Ignace plaza on the north end. Payment is taken by card, by cash, and by MacPass transponder, which regular crossers load in advance. Rates are set by the Mackinac Bridge Authority and change rarely, so confirm the figure before budgeting a large or commercial vehicle. The bridge is the only road link between Michigan's two peninsulas, so there is no toll-free alternative short of driving the long way around through Wisconsin. See the <a href="/mackinac-bridge-tolls/">full toll, towing, and payment guide</a>.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>How do restriction-change alerts work?</summary>
              <p>Enable page alerts and leave the tool open. It checks for a change in the official condition level and can send a browser notification. Close the tab or the browser and alerts stop, so treat this as a convenience and recheck the official status directly before you drive.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>What happens during falling-ice closures?</summary>
              <p>The bridge closes to public traffic, and the reopening time cannot be predicted. The Bridge Authority says falling-ice closures have averaged several hours, but individual closures vary widely. Do not attempt to cross until the official status says the bridge has reopened.</p>
            </details>

          <div class="section-heading">
            <div>
              <p class="eyebrow">The one day it closes on purpose</p>
              <h2 id="bridgeWalkHeading">Is the Mackinac Bridge Closed on Labor Day?</h2>
            </div>
          </div>
          <p class="bridge-walk__answer">Yes. The Mackinac Bridge closes to public traffic from 6:30 a.m. to noon every Labor Day for the Annual Bridge Walk, and it reopens at noon. This is the only scheduled closure on the calendar. Every other closure on this page is weather, maintenance, or an incident.</p>
          <p class="bridge-walk__date"><strong data-walk-date>The next Bridge Walk falls on Labor Day.</strong> <span data-walk-countdown></span></p>
          <p class="bridge-walk__state" data-walk-state>Northbound and southbound traffic both stop. If you are driving between the peninsulas that morning, cross before 6:30 a.m. or wait until after noon, and expect a backlog at the toll booths once it reopens.</p>
          <div class="bridge-walk__facts">
            <div>
              <h3>Walking it</h3>
              <p>Free, and no registration. Walkers start from both ends: Bridge View Park on the west side of I-75 in St. Ignace, and the Jamet Street ramp on the east side of I-75 in Mackinaw City. The walk opens at 7:00 a.m. and nobody is allowed to start after 11:30 a.m.</p>
            </div>
            <div>
              <h3>Getting back</h3>
              <p>No buses or shuttles are provided. If you walk the full five miles across, you arrange your own ride back after the bridge reopens at noon. Most people walk to the midpoint and turn around so they finish where they parked.</p>
            </div>
            <div>
              <h3>Walking it both ways</h3>
              <p>The round trip is ten miles. Walkers heading back have to cross the midpoint by 10:00 a.m. or they get turned around, because the bridge has to be clear in time to reopen.</p>
            </div>
            <div>
              <h3>Accessibility</h3>
              <p>The Bridge Authority takes requests for reasonable accommodation by phone at 906-643-7600 and asks for several days of notice. TTY users can reach the Michigan Relay Center at 800-649-3777.</p>
            </div>
          </div>
          <p class="bridge-walk__source">Start locations, timing, and the closure window are set by the Mackinac Bridge Authority and confirmed each spring. Check the official page before you go.</p>
          <a class="button button-outline" href="https://www.mackinacbridge.org/events/walk/" target="_blank" rel="noopener" data-growth-cta="mackinac-bridge-walk">Open the official Bridge Walk page &#8599;</a>

        <section class="faq-section" aria-labelledby="faqHeading">
          <div class="section-heading">
            <div>
              <p class="eyebrow">Straight answers</p>
              <h2 id="faqHeading">Mackinac Bridge Crossing Questions</h2>
            </div>
          </div>
          <div class="faq-list">
            <details>
              <summary>Is the Mackinac Bridge closed for the Bridge Walk?</summary>
              <p>On Labor Day the bridge closes to public traffic from 6:30 a.m. to noon for the Annual Bridge Walk, then reopens. It is the only closure on the bridge calendar that is scheduled in advance. See the Bridge Walk section above for timing and start locations.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Can I drive across during the Bridge Walk?</summary>
              <p>No. The bridge closes to all traffic from 6:30 a.m. until noon every Labor Day for the Annual Bridge Walk, and both directions are shut for that whole window. There is no escort, no exception for high-profile vehicles, and no way across by road during those hours. This is the only closure that appears on the calendar in advance; every other closure is weather, maintenance, or an incident, and none of those can be predicted more than a day or two out. Plan to cross before 6:30 a.m. or after the bridge reopens at noon, and allow extra time at the toll plaza once traffic starts moving again, because several hours of held-back traffic clears at once. The walk itself runs from St. Ignace south to Mackinaw City. Ferries to Mackinac Island operate from both ends of the Straits and are not affected by the bridge closure.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Is the Mackinac Bridge closed right now?</summary>
              <p>The live panel at the top reads the current Mackinac Bridge Authority report and identifies open, advisory, escort, partial-closure, and closed conditions. If that feed is unavailable, this tool will not guess. Use the direct official-status link instead.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Is the wind shown here measured on the bridge?</summary>
              <p>No. The Bridge Authority does not publish a real-time bridge-deck wind or gust reading in the public conditions feed used by this tool. The NOAA number comes from Mackinaw City or the western Straits and is shown only as off-bridge weather context. The official bridge report always controls.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Will the Mackinac Bridge close tomorrow?</summary>
              <p>No weather forecast can guarantee a closure. The hourly cards show when forecast sustained wind approaches MDOT's published ranges, but ice, visibility, storms, maintenance, and the bridge's own readings can change the decision. Recheck immediately before leaving.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>What is the best time to cross the Mackinac Bridge?</summary>
              <p>There is no reliable public forecast for the easiest crossing window because the Bridge Authority does not publish future restriction decisions or live wait times. Compare the current official status, both cameras, NWS approach forecast, radar, and Mi Drive events here, then recheck immediately before you leave.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>What counts as a high-profile vehicle?</summary>
              <p>MDOT includes semis, RVs, campers, vans, panel trucks, enclosed trailers, open trailers carrying anything, pickups with caps, tonneau covers or cargo, and work trucks with mounted toolboxes, ladders, or equipment. Use the checker above or read the <a href="/mackinac-bridge-rv-trailer-wind-rules/">complete RV and trailer guide</a>.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>Can someone drive my vehicle across for me?</summary>
              <p>Yes. The Mackinac Bridge Authority runs a driver assistance program around the clock for people who would rather not make the crossing themselves, which is common enough to be a standing service rather than a favour. An Authority driver takes your vehicle across for $10 plus the normal toll for that vehicle. You must be at least 18, and you complete the Authority's waiver and vehicle checklist before handing over the keys. It runs in both directions, 24 hours a day, including during high-wind advisories when ordinary drivers may be held or escorted. It is worth knowing about if you tow, drive a high-profile vehicle, or simply find the height and the open grating difficult, which is a more common reason than most people expect. Pickup arrangements differ slightly depending on which side you start from, so check the instructions for your direction. See the <a href="/mackinac-bridge-driver-assistance/">step-by-step pickup instructions</a> for each direction.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>How much is the Mackinac Bridge toll?</summary>
              <p>The Mackinac Bridge toll is charged per axle rather than per crossing, which catches people out when they are towing. Passenger vehicles pay $2 per axle, so an ordinary two-axle car is $4 each way. Every other vehicle class pays $5 per axle, and a trailer adds its own axles to the count: a two-axle car pulling a single-axle trailer is billed as three axles. The toll is the same in both directions and is collected at the St. Ignace plaza on the north end. Payment is taken by card, by cash, and by MacPass transponder, which regular crossers load in advance. Rates are set by the Mackinac Bridge Authority and change rarely, so confirm the figure before budgeting a large or commercial vehicle. The bridge is the only road link between Michigan's two peninsulas, so there is no toll-free alternative short of driving the long way around through Wisconsin. See the <a href="/mackinac-bridge-tolls/">full toll, towing, and payment guide</a>.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>How do restriction-change alerts work?</summary>
              <p>Enable page alerts and leave the tool open. It checks for a change in the official condition level and can send a browser notification. Close the tab or the browser and alerts stop, so treat this as a convenience and recheck the official status directly before you drive.</p>
            </details>
            <details>
              <summary>What happens during falling-ice closures?</summary>
              <p>The bridge closes to public traffic, and the reopening time cannot be predicted. The Bridge Authority says falling-ice closures have averaged several hours, but individual closures vary widely. Do not attempt to cross until the official status says the bridge has reopened.</p>
            </details>
