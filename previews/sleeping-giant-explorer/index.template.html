Meet the Giant.
Find your own adventure.

Sleeping Giant Provincial Park rises above the blue reach of Lake Superior. Beyond the shoreline lie quiet forest paths, a lake-worn stone arch, secluded sand and cliffs that seem to meet the sky. Each trail tells a different story. Come find the one you have time to hear.

Six trails to discover
01 / SHOREMODERATE

Sea Lion Trail

The forest gives way to bare rock and a pebble beach on Perry Bay. Just beyond stands the Sea Lion, an extraordinary arch of dark diabase shaped by time and the lake. Stop at the interpretive panels to learn the story held in the stone.

1.5 miOntario Parks: 2.4 km · return walk from South Kabeyun parking
Allow roughly an hour for the return walk, as Ontario Parks suggests. Leave a little time to linger.

A short walk to one of Superior’s most unusual shoreline landmarks.

02 / SUMMITDIFFICULT

Top of the Giant

First comes the long walk through the forest. Then the trail climbs toward the Giant’s broad, high summit, where sheer cliffs reveal Lake Superior on both sides of the peninsula. The views are immense, but reaching them takes patience, stamina and daylight.

13.5 miOntario Parks: 21.8 km · entire return from parking, not just the summit spur
Ontario Parks suggests 6–10 hours on foot for the full return. A combined bike-and-hike time is not published.

The permitted bike ride ends at the South Kabeyun–Talus Lake junction. The rest is on foot.

03 / SUMMITDIFFICULT

Head of the Giant

Follow the long approach toward Sawyer Bay, then begin the steepest climb in the park. Above the trees, the head of the Giant opens into a sweeping lookout. The reward is the view; the price is a demanding climb and a long return.

10.1 miOntario Parks: 16.2 km · entire return from South Kabeyun parking
Ontario Parks does not publish a full return time. Plan with care and enough daylight.

If you bring a bicycle, Ontario Parks does not identify a rack where this route becomes a walk.

04 / LOOKOUTDIFFICULT

Nanabosho Lookout

A long approach through the woods leads up the Giant’s chest. At Nanabosho Lookout, the trees fall away to reveal Sawyer Bay, Marie Louise Lake and islands scattered across the horizon. Take in how much land and water surround you.

10.6 miOntario Parks: 17.0 km · entire return from South Kabeyun parking
Ontario Parks does not publish a full return time. The distance deserves an early start.

A bicycle may shorten part of the approach, but no rack is documented at the walking transition.

05 / BEACHMODERATE

Middlebrun Bay Beach

Take a forest path to a quiet sweep of sand at Middlebrun Bay. The lake reaches the beach in clear, cold waves; this is a place to slow down, watch the water and listen to the shore. Superior can be frigid even on a sunny afternoon.

2.9 miOntario Parks: 4.6 km · return to the beach from Middlebrun trailhead
Ontario Parks lists the distance but not a complete walking time. Leave room for the beach.

This trail begins elsewhere in the park. Confirm its trailhead and parking with Ontario Parks.

06 / LOOKOUTMODERATE

Pass Lake Overlook

A short climb leads to an old sandstone height overlooking Pass Lake and Thunder Bay. Thousands of years ago, as glacial waters receded, this bluff was an island. The trail also passes Ravine Lake and returns through the cool shade of cedars.

0.6 miOntario Parks: 1.0 km · return walk from Pass Lake trailhead
Ontario Parks lists the return distance but no complete walking time.

Check the trailhead and current access with park staff before setting out.

03 BEFORE YOUR WALK

A little preparation makes room for wonder.

01

Find the right trailhead

For the Sea Lion and the long Giant climbs, Ontario Parks directs walkers to the signed South Kabeyun lot on Highway 587, about 22.7 miles (36.5 km) south along the highway. It is different from the park gatehouse. We have not independently confirmed the exact parking entrance, so follow the park’s signs, not an approximate map pin.

Park information: parking and permits ↗
02

When the trail belongs to your feet

You can cycle part of South Kabeyun, but only as far as its junction with Talus Lake Trail. Do not cycle beyond the cutoff. Ontario Parks describes a bicycle rack there. The walking transitions for Head of the Giant and Nanabosho are different, and racks are not documented at those points.

Ontario Parks guide to cycling and hiking ↗
03

Let the park have the final word

The lake can change its mood quickly, and long hikes need daylight for the return. This guide does not report live weather, trail conditions, open roads or parking. A walk that looks possible on paper may not be possible today. Always check park notices and the conditions in front of you.

There is no need to hurry a place like this.

Listen to the wind in the trees. Watch the light move over the water. Choose a trail with enough daylight for both the outward journey and the walk home. The best thing you can carry away is a memory of being here, not the feeling that you raced through it.
