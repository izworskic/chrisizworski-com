A REPEAT-VISIT DETECTION STUDY

Zero sightings are data, too.

An adult beetle sighting alone cannot tell us when beetles first appeared. The missing evidence is dated visits when you checked carefully and saw none, followed by a visit when you confirmed an adult. This notebook is designed to preserve that sequence rather than manufacture an accuracy percentage.

  1. Start before the adult window. Choose one fixed garden bed of beans, roses, berries or grapes. Begin at least two weeks before the seasonal arrival you expect.
  2. Repeat the same check. Search the same plants, with the same method and effort, for at least five minutes each visit. Check weekly early on and every 2–3 days near emergence.
  3. Record a real no, yes or uncertain. A “no” means you actually completed the inspection and saw no adults. “Uncertain” must never be counted as a no.
  4. Verify and export. Link a clear identification to your own records and consult USA-NPN's adult phenophase protocol and verify whether enrollment for this species is available at your Michigan site. The public species profile does not currently list Michigan among its displayed states. That is a Nature's Notebook observation-program listing, not a limit on the separate USA-NPN heat-based Japanese beetle forecast, whose contiguous-U.S. coverage includes Michigan; modeled stages still do not confirm beetles in a garden. FIELD MANUAL / SOURCES & LIMITATIONS

What a warning can and cannot say.

A

Seasonal reference

MSU and land-grant Extension biology tells us which plants and life stages deserve periodic scouting. A month is an approximate Michigan growing-season guide, not an arrival date.

B

Weather favorability

National Weather Service hourly forecasts can flag broad wet or hot, dry scouting conditions. These are explicitly uncalibrated cues. A weather warning does not identify a pest or diagnose a disease.

C

Biological model

Species have different degree-day algorithms, thresholds and starts. The MSU cabbage maggot model and USA-NPN Japanese beetle model cannot share a generic heat total. Our official USA-NPN Japanese beetle adult and egg-stage models are available for verified reference points. Other species' biological forecasts remain unavailable until their models and source feeds are validated. Neither model proves a local infestation.

D

Observe before treating

Check evidence, consider whether injury is meaningful, preserve beneficial insects and verify each product's Michigan registration and exact crop label before using pesticides.

Keep this checklist even without a live forecast.

For squash, inspect the base of vines and undersides of leaves. For tomatoes, check lower leaves for spots and new growth for caterpillars. For cabbage and kale, protect vulnerable young plants and turn leaves over for caterpillars. For beans, inspect leaf undersides and keep an eye out for Japanese beetles in summer. During Michigan winter, clean up badly infected crop residue, rotate crop families and protect beneficial overwintering habitat.
