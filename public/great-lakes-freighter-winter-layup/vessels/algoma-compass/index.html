GREAT LAKES / SHIP BIOGRAPHY

Algoma Compass

A ship that keeps rewriting its name—and its winters.

Built in Toledo in 1973, this River Class self-unloading freighter has sailed under three names and two national flags. Now Algoma Compass, it has returned to Owen Sound for winter layup more than once; the 2026 arrival required an icebreaker.

Chapter 1

From Roger M. Kyes to Algoma Compass

The vessel entered service as Roger M. Kyes in 1973, became Adam E. Cornelius in 1989, and took the Algoma Compass name after joining Canadian ownership. Its history shows that one ship can accumulate several identities without becoming several different vessels.

Chapter 2

The winter Owen Sound needed an icebreaker

In January 2026 the Compass waited near Leith as harbor ice blocked its way. The Canadian Coast Guard's Judy LaMarsh opened a route, enabling the freighter to reach its winter berth January 19.

Chapter 3

Why annual records matter

Owen Sound's waterfront museum also records a winter stay in 2025. Linking those separate winters shows a recurring harbor relationship without inventing a continuous route or precise berth location.
