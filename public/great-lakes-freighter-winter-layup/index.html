    04 Below the winter silence

    Why Freighters Lay Up

    Lock schedules, ice, freight demand and maintenance all shape the winter break.

    JAN 15 – MAR 25 CLOSURE

    The calendar closes the lakes

    The Welland Canal clears its last ships around the turn of the year and the Soo Locks shut on January 15 for maintenance. Nothing moves between Lake Superior and the lower lakes until March 25. Many ships that could run through ice simply have nowhere to go.

    Ride the Soo Locks ↗
    GRAVING DOCKS & FIT-OUT

    Winter is the shipyard season

    A laker runs nine months without a break, so January through March is when hulls are inspected, engines overhauled, self-unloader belts replaced and steel renewed. The quiet harbor is the busiest time of year for the yards.

    Inside winter shipyard work ↗
    NOAA ICE & USCG CUTTERS

    Watch winter change the lakes

    Ice cover decides how long the last trips can run and how hard the icebreakers work in March. The NOAA ice record gives the regional picture; it cannot tell you about the ice beside one dock.

    Where the Shipping Year Stands Right Now

    The Great Lakes fleet follows a strict four-phase operational cycle governed by the St. Lawrence Seaway and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers at Sault Ste. Marie. This guide automatically highlights the current phase in Eastern Time (America/Detroit), or you can inspect any phase below.

    Auto-detected season
    Nov 1 – Jan 15 · Closing Weeks & Final Lockages

    The race to beat the Welland & Soo Locks closures

    From November through January 15, thousand-footers sprint to build winter iron ore stockpiles at lower-lake steel mills while ocean-going salties clear the St. Lawrence Seaway before late December. The Welland Canal clears around January 10, and the last freighter clears the Poe Lock at Sault Ste. Marie before midnight on January 15.

    • Best shore watching: Duluth Ship Canal, Soo Locks Park, Blue Water Bridge (Port Huron/Sarnia), and Sturgeon Bay bridges as the first ships arrive for early graving-dock work.
    • What changes in this atlas: The new winter ledger resets November 1 (`America/Detroit`). Vessels only appear on the current-season map after a dated arrival report is published.

    Jan 16 – Feb 28 · Deep Winter Layup & Lock Dewatering

    When the thousand-footers sleep in harbor ice & dry docks

    With the Soo Locks closed and dewatered by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, Lake Superior is sealed off from the lower lakes. Nearly the entire U.S. and Canadian laker fleet sits moored in winter berths at Superior/Duluth, Sturgeon Bay, Toledo, Milwaukee, Sarnia, Owen Sound, Port Colborne, and Hamilton for five-year hull inspections, engine overhauls, and steel renewal.

    • Best shore watching: Sturgeon Bay's Michigan St. & Oregon St. bridges (viewing Fincantieri Bay Shipbuilding), Superior's Barker's Island waterfront, Owen Sound's inner harbour walls, and Toledo's Maumee Riverfront.
    • What still moves on the lakes: Tug-barge units on lower Lake Michigan, winter coal/salt shuttle runs on the Detroit & St. Clair rivers, and USCG icebreakers (Mackinaw, Bristol Bay, Neah Bay).

    04B Illustrated Port & Shipyard Field Guide

    Where to See the Winter Fleet: 8 Great Lakes Layup & Lock Hubs

    Shipyards and commercial cargo docks are private industrial facilities, never public driving destinations. However, several of the Great Lakes' most famous winter layup harbors sit directly across from public parks, bridges, and waterfront boardwalks where you can photograph laid-up thousand-footers safely from shore.

    Lake Superior · Twin Ports 6 Archive Ships (2025–26)

    Superior & Duluth, WI–MN

    Fraser Shipyards · Ogdensburg Pier · Redwood Group Elevator M

    The premier winter layup and heavy-repair hub for the thousand-foot iron ore fleet. Fraser Shipyards operates two graving docks in Superior where hulls like Lee A. Tregurtha, Mesabi Miner, Hon. James L. Oberstar, and John J. Boland undergo major winter overhauls.

    Public shore viewing & access rules: Fraser Shipyards and elevator docks are closed industrial property. View laid-up silhouettes from public vantage points at Barker's Island (Superior), Blatnik Bridge approaches, and watch January arrivals or late-March breakout departures at Duluth's Canal Park and Aerial Lift Bridge.

    Sturgeon Bay, Wisconsin

    Fincantieri Bay Shipbuilding · 1,158-ft Graving Dock

    Door County's working shipyard town hosts one of the largest winter concentrations of classic lakers and 1,000-footers on the Great Lakes, including 2025–26 winter stays by James R. Barker, Edgar B. Speer, and Manitoulin.

    Public shore viewing & access rules: Arguably the best public winter ship-viewing town on the Lakes: pedestrian walkways on the Michigan Street Bridge and Oregon Street Bridge, plus Sawyer Park and Graham Park on the West Waterfront, offer dramatic views across the channel toward the yard without entering private property.

    Sarnia North Slip · Government Dock · Blue Water Bridge

    St. Marys River · Sault Ste. Marie Closed Jan 15 – Mar 25

    Soo Locks Winter Dewatering & Icebreaking

    Poe Lock & MacArthur Lock · USCG Sector Sault Ste. Marie

    Why the upper-lakes fleet lays up: every January 15, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers closes the Soo Locks, pumps millions of gallons of water out of the chambers, and lowers heavy equipment onto the lock floor until March 25.

    Public shore viewing & access rules: Soo Locks Park and its outdoor observation decks in downtown Sault Ste. Marie remain open in winter (typically 9 a.m. to 6 p.m.) to watch USACE winter maintenance, while Rotary Park overlooks USCGC Mackinaw and St. Marys River icebreakers.

    04C Winter Shipping Reference

    Frequently Asked Questions: Winter Layup & Lock Closures

    Where do Great Lakes freighters go in the winter?

    When the Welland Canal closes around late December / January 10 and the Soo Locks close on January 15, Great Lakes freighters tie up in protected winter layup harbors across the United States and Canada. Major winter layup and shipyard ports include Superior/Duluth (Fraser Shipyards, Ogdensburg Pier, and Elevator M), Sturgeon Bay, Wisconsin (Fincantieri Bay Shipbuilding), Toledo, Ohio (Maumee River and CSX/Midwest docks), Milwaukee, Wisconsin (Jones Island and the Municipal Mooring Basin), Sarnia, Ontario, Owen Sound, Ontario, Port Colborne, and Hamilton, Ontario.

    When do the Soo Locks close and reopen for the winter season?

    Under federal regulation and the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers schedule, the Soo Locks in Sault Ste. Marie, Michigan close annually on January 15 and reopen on March 25. During that ten-week shutdown, the Poe Lock is pumped dry (dewatered) so crews can inspect and repair miter gates, Filling/Emptying valves, and chamber walls that operate nonstop for nine months of the year.

    Where can you watch laid-up Great Lakes freighters from shore?

    While shipyards and cargo docks are strictly private industrial property, several harbors offer close public vantage points: in Sturgeon Bay, WI, the Michigan Street Bridge, Oregon Street Bridge, and Sawyer Park look directly toward Fincantieri Bay Shipbuilding; in Superior/Duluth, Barker's Island and Canal Park offer views of winter docks and March departures; in Owen Sound, ON, public paths line the inner harbour walls; and in Toledo, OH, Glass City Metropark and the National Museum of the Great Lakes overlook the Maumee River.

    Why doesn't a stopped ship on live AIS prove winter layup?

    Freighters routinely anchor for winter gales, wait for unloading docks, shift berths inside a port, or power down AIS transponders after mooring. Inferring winter layup from AIS speed alone produces false reports. This atlas separates live AIS harbor traffic from the winter layup roster and only records a winter berth when a dated source report from a port authority, maritime historical society, museum, operator, or corroborated harbor observer confirms it.
