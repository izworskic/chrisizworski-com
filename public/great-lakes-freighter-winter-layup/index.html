The story behind the stillness

When the locks grow quiet, the yards get loud.

Winter layup is more than parking a ship. Welders, pipefitters and maintenance crews use the break between operating seasons for repairs, inspections and upgrades. Fraser Shipyards described January 2026 as a period of intensive winter work.

Why do freighters lay up?

Seasonal canal and lock schedules, ice, freight demand and maintenance planning shape the winter shutdown. No single factor explains every vessel's berth.

What these records mean

A harbor confirmed. A date attached. No invented precision.

The 2025–26 archive contains twelve source-dated records across Superior, Sturgeon Bay, Milwaukee, Owen Sound and Toledo: ten verified historical records and two separately identified reports. The 2024–25 season also includes Owen Sound records. Sources have different dates. None represents a live ship location. Berths may change during winter repair work.

Each map marker is an approximate harbor reference. It is not an individual berth, public ship-viewing destination or nautical waypoint. A ship's current location cannot be inferred from a past-season roster.

Photographs are genuine, dated vessel images. They are not necessarily photos from the layup season; license and provenance appear with each image. No auto-generated photos, guessed cargo histories or automatically merged vessel identities. Verified 2024–25 records are available through the season selector, and permanent vessel biographies link across years.
