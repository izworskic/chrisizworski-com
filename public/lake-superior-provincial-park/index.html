Ontario · Highway 17 · A guide to the north shore

Lake Superior Provincial Park: follow the wild shore.

Highway 17 winds between forested hills and the restless blue of Lake Superior. One turn brings you to a beach beneath a towering cliff; another leads to a river tumbling over rock. Some stops need only twenty minutes. Others invite you to spend the better part of a day. Here is how to find the part of this shore that is right for you.

Before you set out · park notice dated October 26, 2026

Superior is beautiful in every season, but the park's visiting season has rules of its own. Ontario Parks' published 2026 day-use season ended October 25. Agawa Rock Pictographs Trail closed September 11 for private Indigenous ceremonies and hazardous fall conditions; Trapper's Trail closed October 17.

In the fall, Nokomis and South Old Woman River trails lie within permitted hunting areas: wear hunter orange and keep dogs leashed. Gargantua Road is also closed until further notice, affecting several backcountry access points. Other stops are not confirmed accessible after seasonal closure. This dated snapshot is not a live opening-status service. Read today’s notices from Ontario Parks before travelling.

There is no need to rush from one trailhead to the next. If you have half an hour, stand at Old Woman Bay and take in the cliff. With an afternoon, follow a river inland or climb to a lookout. We’ll help you choose a stop that suits your time and footing. The time shown is time at the stop, not driving between them, and an easy-rated hike is not automatically accessible.

A closer look at this coast

The lake sets the pace here.

Beyond the highway, the forest opens onto granite headlands, pebble beaches, sheltered coves and rivers that have long provided routes into the interior. Look up from the water: the great rock walls and the small wind-shaped trees tell you just how exposed this shoreline can be. A calm morning can change quickly, so leave room in your plans for the weather.

People have known and travelled these shores for generations. The pictographs at Agawa Rock belong to a living Anishinaabe cultural landscape, not simply to the park’s list of attractions. Visit with the respect due to an active sacred place, and honour all posted closures.

01 / Great first stop · Hwy 17 km 1042.6

Old Woman Bay

Access unverified

At Old Woman Bay, the road gives way to a sweep of sand and pebbles beneath a cliff rising some 200 metres above the lake. Look toward the rock face for the profile that gives the bay its name. This is a place to step out of the car, listen to the surf, and remember just how big Superior is.

Allow 20–45 minRoadside day-use areaNot verified wheelchair-accessible
Where to stop and what to watch for

Begin at the designated Old Woman Bay day-use area. You can enjoy the shoreline without taking on Nokomis Trail; if you have a few hours and want the view from above, the Nokomis trailhead begins here too. Weather and wave conditions can change the feel of the beach quickly, so keep a respectful distance from rough water.

Ontario Parks lists the day-use location as 47.790313, -84.896840. That is a destination coordinate, not a surveyed parking-stall position. Parking capacity and off-season maintenance are not confirmed, and the sand-and-pebble beach is not certified wheelchair-accessible.

02 / Sacred site

Agawa Rock Pictographs

Closed for 2026

At Agawa Rock, painted figures along the lakeside cliff are part of a living Anishinaabe cultural landscape. In a normal visiting season, the rugged trail leads down toward the water and an exposed rock ledge where Superior’s waves have the final say. This is an active sacred place, not simply another photo stop. The trail is closed for the remainder of 2026: please do not enter.

Plan 45–75 minRugged and exposedNo dogs at sacred site (community request)
Why this place deserves special care

Local First Nations have asked visitors not to bring dogs to the pictographs. Ontario Parks closed the trail September 11, 2026, for private Indigenous ceremonies and hazardous fall weather. This closure is firm, even on a calm day. When the trail eventually reopens, visitors must still consider its steep rock descent, slippery surfaces and exposed lakeside ledge; it is not a low-effort or barrier-free walk.

The Ontario government GeoTour describes a signed road off Highway 17 and a parking area about 1.5 km along that road. Its mapped Agawa Rock location is not a confirmed parking entrance, so we deliberately offer no navigation pin for it.

Read Ontario Parks' Agawa Rock closure and cultural-site guidance

03 / Hwy 17 km 1090.6

Pinguisibi / Sand River — first falls

Access unverified

Follow the sound of moving water into the woods. Pinguisibi, the Ojibwe name for the Sand River, means “river of fine white sand.” For generations it was a route inland; today the trail follows the current past rapids, quieter reaches and three waterfalls. The first falls make a lovely short outing. If your afternoon is free, there is more river to discover upstream.

Short visit: allow 30–60 minFull 6 km return: 1½–3 hrEasy-rated trail, not barrier-free
How far should you follow the river?

Start from the Sand River day-use area and walk upstream. The first waterfall is reached after a short hike; our 30–60-minute visit estimate is a planning allowance, not an official first-falls trail time. For the full 6 km out-and-back, Ontario Parks suggests 1½–3 hours. Keep an eye on children near the fast water and wet rock, and return by the same trail.

The park identifies this as a day-use area. Older management documents mention paved access roads and vault toilets, but we have not independently confirmed today's parking entrance or restroom availability. The Highway 17 trail reference is km 1090.6.

04 / Lake Superior shoreline

Katherine Cove

Access unverified

Katherine Cove is where the shoreline softens. After miles of exposed rock and forested headlands, you find a broad curve of fine sand and shallow water with the hills close behind. Bring your lunch, let the children explore the beach, or simply sit awhile and watch the light move across the lake. Even here, Superior can be cold, and conditions deserve a careful look.

Allow 30–60 minDay-use areaBeach access not mobility-verified
Plan a quiet stop at the cove

Ontario Parks lists Katherine Cove among its day-use beaches. Its relatively shallow nearshore water makes it appealing to families, but a gentle-looking day is not a guarantee of safe swimming: judge the waves, temperature and beach notices before entering the water. Sand should not be confused with verified wheelchair access.

The Ontario government GeoTour identifies the day-use site at 47.443533, -84.749733. A historic park plan describes paved parking and vault toilets, but neither proves today's available parking spaces or services. The approximate km 1088 reference is for highway orientation, not an entrance pin.

Check Ontario Parks' beach and day-use information

05 / Hwy 17 km 1101.4

Awausee — first lookout

Access unverified

The Awausee Trail begins quietly on an old logging road, then turns uphill beside a ravine. Before long, the forest opens to the lower Agawa River valley and Agawa Mountain. You do not need to complete the demanding full loop to earn a view: the first lookout is a worthwhile destination in its own right. The long climb beyond it is for hikers with much more time.

First lookout: 45 min returnFull loop: 4–6 hrUphill, uneven ground
Take the first lookout, or the whole climb?

Find the trail reference at Highway 17 km 1101.4. Ontario Parks gives roughly 45 minutes for the first lookout and return; retrace your route from that viewpoint if it fits your day. Farther up, the complete 10 km loop climbs through maple forest to additional Agawa Valley lookouts and typically takes 4–6 hours.

The short choice still involves uphill walking and uneven footing. It is not a barrier-free stroll. An official highway kilometre marker identifies the approach, but we have not verified a precise parking-entrance coordinate.

See the official Awausee trail guidance

06 / Old Woman Bay trailhead

Nokomis loop

Access unverified

From the beach at Old Woman Bay, Nokomis climbs through lichen-draped boreal forest until the lake comes into view from above. The lookouts reveal the broad sweep of the bay and the immense cliff across the water. Look for the old woman’s profile in the rock; it can be easier to imagine from a higher vantage. Save your energy for the descent, which is steep.

5 km loop1½–3 hrModerate · hunting advisory
What the climb asks of you

Begin at the Old Woman Bay day-use area, the same place as the easy shoreline stop. The 5 km loop generally takes 1½–3 hours. It is a real moderate hike with uneven footing and a steep final descent, not an accessible viewpoint reached from the parking lot.

Ontario Parks identifies Nokomis and South Old Woman River trails within areas where hunting is permitted in the fall of 2026. Nokomis itself was not listed as closed in that notice; wear hunter orange, keep dogs leashed and check alerts for your visit date.

Check trail and hunting-season advisories

07 / Hwy 17 km 1080.8

Orphan Lake loop

Access unverified

Few day hikes show so many faces of this park. Orphan Lake Trail moves through hardwood and evergreen forest, crosses an area burned in 1998, and reaches a lookout over Lake Superior. Farther on, a pebble beach opens onto the big water. The return follows the Baldhead River past waterfalls before climbing toward Orphan Lake. Give this walk an unhurried afternoon.

8 km loop2–4 hrModerate
Follow the loop all the way home

Ontario Parks rates this 8 km loop moderate and suggests 2–4 hours. At the pebble beach the route meets the more demanding Coastal Trail; stay with the signed Orphan Lake loop to follow the Baldhead River and climb back toward the trailhead. The lakefront rock and shoreline can be slick in wet weather.

The park publishes a Highway 17 access reference at km 1080.8. We do not have a separately verified parking-coordinate pin for this trail, so use the official park map and on-site trail signs rather than a guessed location.

See Ontario Parks' full Orphan Lake route description

08 / Hwy 17 km 1074.3

Trapper's Trail

Closed for 2026

Along quiet Rustle Lake, Trapper’s Trail threads through wetland habitat where a floating boardwalk and viewing platforms invite you to look closely. Watch for the signs of beaver, birds along the water’s edge, or perhaps a heron standing still in the shallows. Wildlife is never guaranteed, and this is a place best appreciated slowly. The trail is closed for the 2026 season.

1.5 km loop45–90 minEasy-rated; not barrier-free verified
A wetland walk to save for another season

The 1.5 km loop is rated easy and normally takes about 45–90 minutes, with two viewing platforms and a floating boardwalk. Ontario Parks closed it October 17, 2026, because of potential hunting activity. Please respect the closure; the boardwalk is not permission to enter. A boardwalk also does not establish that the entire route is wheelchair-accessible.

The official Highway 17 trail marker is km 1074.3, but an exact parking entrance has not been verified. Check official reopening notices before adding this walk to a future itinerary.

Check the official Trapper's Trail closure

09 / Hwy 17 km 1113.8

Crescent Lake loop

Access unverified

Step away from the wide-open shore and the park grows quieter. Crescent Lake Trail wanders among yellow birch, old pine trees and three inland lakes: Crescent, MacGregor and Mudhole. It is a gentler way to experience the forest, especially with children who like to stop and look at every little thing along the path. Leave time for those discoveries.

2 km loop30–60 minRoad/parking access to verify
A quieter walk among the inland lakes

Ontario Parks describes a 2 km loop beginning at Crescent Lake Campground, passing yellow birch at least 80 years old and century-old pines. Allow 30–60 minutes for the trail itself. It is rated easy, but that does not mean the path has verified barrier-free access.

The park's trail reference is Highway 17 km 1113.8. Because the trail starts at a campground, confirm seasonal road and parking access before travelling; do not assume you can drive in after the operating season ends.

Read the official Crescent Lake trail description

10 / Agawa Bay

Agawa Bay Visitor Centre

Facility closed seasonally

Before heading down a trail, the Agawa Bay Visitor Centre can help you understand the country you are entering. Its exhibits introduce the park’s natural features and cultural history, giving meaning to the cliffs, rivers and forest you will see outside. It is an especially good beginning for a first visit. Unlike the hiking trails, the building is identified by Ontario Parks as barrier-free.

Allow 20–45 minVerified barrier-free facilityVisitor Centre closed Oct 2026
Begin with the park's bigger story

Ontario Parks identifies the Visitor Centre as a barrier-free facility. That designation is for the building, not a promise that its surrounding beaches or trails can be reached with mobility equipment. Interpretive visits work best during the centre’s published operating hours.

The Visitor Centre was listed as closed in late October 2026; check its current schedule before setting out. The image above shows nearby Agawa Bay beach, not the Visitor Centre building itself.
