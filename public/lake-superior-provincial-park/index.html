01 / Great first stop · Hwy 17 km 1042.6

Old Woman Bay

Access unverified

The high-impact roadside stop: sand and pebble shore beneath towering cliffs. Good for photos and a brief beach walk without committing to Nokomis.

Allow 20–45 minRoadside day-use areaNot verified wheelchair-accessible
Parking, access and safety

Ontario Parks lists a day-use area on Lake Superior with Nokomis trail access and publishes the day-use location at 47.790313, -84.896840. This is a destination coordinate, not a surveyed parking-stall position. Exact parking capacity and off-season maintenance are not published on the facility page. Beach terrain varies; a roadside stop is not an accessibility certification.

02 / Sacred site

Agawa Rock Pictographs

Closed for 2026

An active sacred Anishinaabe site beside the lake. The rocky descent and exposed shoreline make it unsuitable for mobility-limited visitors or high-wave conditions—even during an operating season.

Plan 45–75 minRugged and exposedNo dogs at sacred site (community request)
Respectful access, parking and safety

Ontario Parks reports the trail closed since September 11, 2026 for private Indigenous ceremonies and hazardous fall weather. Do not treat calm waves as permission to enter. Local Indigenous communities request that dogs not be taken to the pictograph site.

The Ontario government GeoTour places the signed access road about 7.5 km north of the Visitor Centre and documents a parking lot 1.5 km from Highway 17. Its Agawa Rock GPS waypoint is not specifically identified as the parking lot, so no driving pin is offered here. The 2026 closure overrides normal driving and hiking guidance.

Official closure notice

03 / Hwy 17 km 1090.6

Pinguisibi / Sand River — first falls

Access unverified

One of the park's most useful short hiking choices. Walk upstream toward the first waterfall, then decide whether to continue.

Short visit: allow 30–60 minFull 6 km return: 1½–3 hrEasy-rated trail, not barrier-free
Parking and trail detail

Ontario Parks identifies the Sand River day-use area for picnics and waterfall access. The historic management plan documents paved roads and vault toilets, not independently surveyed modern parking coordinates. The first waterfall is a short hike upstream; 30–60 minutes is a planning allowance, not an official published time for that waterfall alone.

04 / Lake Superior shoreline

Katherine Cove

Access unverified

The gentler beach choice for families, with fine sand and shallow nearshore water. Lake Superior water can still be cold.

Allow 30–60 minDay-use areaBeach access not mobility-verified
Parking and practicalities

Ontario Parks confirms Katherine Cove as a designated day-use beach. The official Ontario GeoTour publishes the day-use stop at 47.443533, -84.749733; the management plan describes paved parking and vault toilets historically. Neither proves the exact entrance, an available space, or operational facilities on your trip date. The km 1088 marker remains for approximate highway ordering only.

05 / Hwy 17 km 1101.4

Awausee — first lookout

Access unverified

A compact high-view option hidden inside a demanding trail. Ontario Parks lists 45 minutes return to the first lookout; do not accidentally route visitors onto the full climb.

First lookout: 45 min returnFull loop: 4–6 hrUphill, uneven ground
Parking and route

Trailhead at Hwy 17 km 1101.4; use the same trail back from the first lookout. The overall loop is 10 km and demanding. This shorter option remains a real hike, not low-effort or barrier-free.

06 / Old Woman Bay trailhead

Nokomis loop

Access unverified

The iconic overlook hike above Old Woman Bay. Expect steep sections, rough footing and a steep final descent. An autumn hunting-area advisory applies.

5 km loop1½–3 hrModerate · hunting advisory
Trail and seasonal advisory

Starts at the Old Woman Bay day-use area. Ontario Parks identifies Nokomis within the designated fall hunting area and recommends hunter orange. Dogs must be leashed. This is not a closure notice for the trail itself.

10 / Agawa Bay

Facility closed seasonally

    Permits, access and primary sources

    Lake Superior Provincial Park participates in Ontario Parks' advance day-use permits. During operating periods, permits can generally be obtained starting at 7 a.m. Eastern, up to five days before your visit. Check the official reservation site for availability and the exact applicable fee. A day-use permit is not a backcountry camping permit.
