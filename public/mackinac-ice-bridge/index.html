03 / Read the winter landscape

Three clues that tell a bigger story

CLUE ONE · PERSISTENT COLD

The cold builds over days

A cold afternoon is a moment; several days of subfreezing weather are a process. Follow the consecutive freezing hours, then look for warm spells that interrupt them. Even prolonged cold cannot certify strong ice across a channel.

CLUE TWO · WIND AND WATER

The Straits are not a pond

Currents, changing wind and boat traffic can open leads, move whole sheets of ice and make nearby places look completely different. That's why a smooth-looking chart cannot answer whether a snowmobile can travel.

CLUE THREE · THE VIEW FROM ABOVE

Ice concentration isn't thickness

NOAA charts estimate how much of the water surface is ice-covered. They do not sample every crack, pressure ridge or hidden current. Treat changing map colors as an ecological and seasonal signal, not a route recommendation.

04 / History and place

Why Christmas trees stand on frozen water

Visitors know Mackinac Island for its summer bicycles and horse-drawn carriages. Winter belongs to a smaller, year-round community. When the waters between the island and St. Ignace freeze, island residents have traditionally marked a snowmobile ice connection with discarded Christmas trees.

The trees are striking—a small winter forest standing where ferries travel in summer. But they are not an official highway. This tradition appears only in favorable winters, and even then the ice can change suddenly.

Mackinac State Historic Parks notes that nineteenth-century winter ice supported fishing with nets and poles; the Christmas-tree line is a much newer expression of life on the same unpredictable water. Read the Parks' historical account ↗

Ranger's field question

What changes first?

Try watching this forecast each week. Is the air holding below freezing? Does wind strengthen? Does the NOAA chart finally show ice across more of the Straits? The story is in the changes, not one green checkmark.

05 / The documented 2026 season

One winter, three milestones

These are verified historical events, not current bridge conditions.

January 20, 2026

Shipping waters restricted

The Coast Guard's Northern Great Lakes Captain of the Port closed the waters separating St. Ignace and Mackinac Island to vessel traffic to facilitate ice growth.

Coast Guard notice ↗
March 19, 2026

Navigation reopened

The Coast Guard reopened the waterway. A shipping restriction is an administrative navigation decision, never an authorization to cross ice.

Coast Guard announcement ↗
March 24, 2026

Icebreaking resumed

Coast Guard Cutter Mobile Bay was scheduled to begin breaking ice between St. Ignace and the island, a reminder of how rapidly conditions can change.

Sources, methods and limitations

Observed ice and forecast weather are different products, intentionally kept separate. Refreshes request current data; when a feed fails or is stale, the page shows unknown rather than guessing.

  • U.S. National Ice Center Great Lakes daily ice chart service — analysis polygons; local samples only summarize broad water conditions.
  • National Weather Service API — St. Ignace hourly air temperature and wind forecasts, updated as NWS publishes them.
  • Coast Guard Great Lakes port status — check official marine-navigation announcements; not polled or labeled as current ice-bridge status on this page.
  • Mackinac Island Tourism FAQ — guidance against recreational snowmobiling across the ice bridge.
