How to pick a productive walk

A practical guide to search visibility—not a map of where bucks shed.

01 · Snow cover

Let the ground reveal itself

Search when ground is bare or patchy. A fresh blanket of snow hides older sheds; shallow snow can still reveal dark beams in some light.

02 · Terrain

Walk legal edges and transitions

On permitted lands, study field and timber edges, old trails and crossings without entering active winter deer refuge areas. Get landowner permission for private property.

03 · Wildlife

Leave deer undisturbed

Deep snow and late-winter energy stress make pushing deer costly. Avoid bedding and concentration cover, especially in the Upper Peninsula. Waiting is often the better trip decision.

Field safety and access: Check property rules, seasonal closures, hunting activity, ice hazards and local conditions before traveling. Do not trespass or pursue deer. The forecast locations are representative points, not shed sites.

What this tool can—and can't—tell you

Can snow or a cold front predict when deer drop antlers?

No. Antler casting has biological and individual variation. This tool considers broad seasonal context only, while the seven-day weather outlook helps choose when existing sheds may be easier to see.

Is there a perfect day to go?

No guaranteed day. A few days of exposure can help, but new snow may cover antlers again. Terrain, access, deer disturbance and the quality of local snow information matter.

Why doesn't the tool show exactly where to search?

It does not track individual bucks or promote winter concentration locations. A regional forecast pin is not a marked public hunting area. Get site-specific permission and check area regulations.

What's the difference between snow depth and new snowfall?

Snow depth is snow already covering the ground. New snowfall is snow added by a storm. Weather forecasts alone do not accurately reconstruct snow depth. Check NOAA analysis or your own observations.
