Understand the place

The Locks make more sense when you see the system

USACE describes three core jobs here: move vessels between different water levels, hold Lake Washington and Lake Union near their operating elevation, and limit saltwater intrusion into the freshwater system.

Navigation

Two lock chambers, two scales

The small lock is 30 × 150 ft and handles much of the smaller traffic. The large lock is 80 × 825 ft and can accommodate much larger vessels and tow configurations.

Water control

Freshwater held above the Sound

The ship canal system is managed around 20–22 ft above sea level. The spillway helps control that elevation while the locks repeatedly exchange water during vessel passages.

Fish passage

21 steps around a human barrier

The fish ladder has 21 weirs that let migrating fish gain elevation gradually. The viewing room makes that migration visible when salmon are moving through the system.

Access and practical visit notes

GroundsUSACE publishes daily grounds hours of 7 AM–9 PM.
Fish ladder viewingUSACE publishes fish-ladder viewing hours of 7 AM–8:45 PM.
Wheelchair accessThe Visitor Center and Fish Ladder Viewing Room are accessible, and many garden paths and lock-wall walkways are paved or navigable by wheelchair. Conditions can vary.

The federal grounds are free to enter. The 75-minute route leaves the Locks grounds for A Salish Welcome and the National Nordic Museum/Frankie Feetsplinters; museum admission and hours are separate.
