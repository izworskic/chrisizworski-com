USACE operating information

Use this page to decide when to visit the Ballard Locks. There is no dependable public minute-by-minute schedule for every boat. The vessel map shows AIS-equipped traffic approaching and moving through the Lake Washington Ship Canal; WDFW fish counts show the salmon run; NOAA supplies tide timing; and USACE publishes access hours, water management and maintenance information. The useful part is seeing those signals together.

Traffic caveat: the AIS map does not represent every pleasure boat and is not an official lockage count. Many small recreational craft do not transmit AIS. Use the map to identify trackable commercial and larger vessels, then use the live Ship Canal camera and the on-site view for a fuller traffic picture.

Infrastructure

Why Ballard has unusual data density

The locks are simultaneously a navigation system, a freshwater-level control structure, a saltwater barrier and a salmon migration passage.

The Lake Washington Ship Canal and Hiram M. Chittenden Locks were completed in 1917. USACE identifies three core functions: move vessels between tidal Puget Sound and the freshwater lakes, maintain Lake Washington and Lake Union water levels, and limit saltwater intrusion into the freshwater system. The fish ladder supports migrating salmon.

How to use the live screen

If you mainly want ships

Start with the AIS map. A named commercial vessel in Shilshole Bay or Salmon Bay is a much stronger signal than a generic traffic forecast. Keep the camera open while you travel. Many recreational boats are invisible to AIS.

If you mainly want salmon

Use the latest species cards and the data date. Chinook typically dominate late summer, coho build into September and early fall, and sockeye are concentrated earlier in summer. Fresh high counts plus an open viewing room are the strongest fish-viewing combination.

If you want the best overall visit

The go-now score rewards fresh salmon activity, usable weather, open visitor areas, available lock chambers and an upcoming tide turn. It intentionally does not invent a vessel count from the AIS map.
