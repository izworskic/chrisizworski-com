USACE operating information

Use this page to decide when to visit the Ballard Locks. There is no dependable public minute-by-minute schedule for every boat. The vessel map below shows AIS-equipped traffic approaching and moving through the Lake Washington Ship Canal; WDFW fish counts show the salmon run; NOAA supplies tide timing; and USACE publishes access hours, water management and maintenance information. Taken together, those signals tell you considerably more than any one source by itself.

Important traffic distinction: the AIS map does not represent every pleasure boat and is not an official lockage count. Small recreational craft often do not transmit AIS. Use the map to identify trackable commercial and larger vessels, then use the camera and on-site view for the full traffic picture.

Infrastructure

Why Ballard has so much useful data

The locks are simultaneously a navigation system, a freshwater-level control structure, a saltwater barrier and a salmon migration passage.

Large lock
80 × 825 ftOverall chamber; USACE lists 760 ft available navigation length.
Small lock
30 × 150 ftOverall chamber; commonly used by smaller recreational traffic.
Spillway
235 ftSix gates help manage Lake Washington and Lake Union elevations.
Water level
20–22 ftUSACE operating range above sea level in the project datum.

The complex was completed in 1917. USACE identifies three core functions: move vessels between tidal Puget Sound and the freshwater lakes, maintain Lake Washington and Lake Union water levels, and limit saltwater intrusion into the freshwater system. The integrated fish ladder supports anadromous salmon migration.

How to use the live screen

If you mainly want ships

Start with the AIS map. A named commercial vessel in Shilshole Bay or Salmon Bay is a much stronger signal than a generic traffic forecast. Keep the camera open while you travel. Remember that many recreational boats are invisible to AIS.

If you mainly want salmon

Use the latest species cards and the data date. Chinook typically dominate late summer, coho build into September and early fall, and sockeye are concentrated earlier in summer. A high recent count plus an open fish-ladder viewing room is the strongest fish-viewing combination.

If you want the best overall visit

The go-now score rewards fresh salmon activity, usable weather, open visitor areas, available lock chambers and an upcoming tide turn. It intentionally does not invent a vessel count from the AIS map.
