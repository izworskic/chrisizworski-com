Choose your viewing spot

Whitefish Point lighthouse shoreline and public boardwalk

The Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum describes a public boardwalk to the Lake Superior shoreline. Its indoor museum closes at 5 p.m. during its May–October season; nighttime museum or bird-observatory access must not be assumed. Check posted gate and beach rules before travel.

Paradise and Tahquamenon-area legal viewpoints

Use a lawful roadside or park observation area only when explicitly open. The route north from Paradise to Whitefish Point is about 11 miles; do not park on narrow shoulders or enter wildlife closures after dark.

Nighttime access and safety

The Whitefish Point Bird Observatory says its outdoor areas operate dawn to dusk. Do not enter those managed areas after dark. Do not use drones on the protected Point. Lake Superior winds, erosion and cold water create real nighttime hazards.

Do not use a lakeshore forecast as a direct observation. Check conditions on the ground, follow posted closures and always choose legal parking and a route back in darkness.

Frequently asked questions

Can I see northern lights at Whitefish Point tonight?

Possibly, but the answer depends on the latest NOAA aurora signals, local NWS sky cover, darkness and a legal view north. This page reports unknown rather than inventing a sighting.

Is Whitefish Point open for nighttime aurora viewing?

Do not assume every Whitefish Point property is open after dark. The bird observatory says its areas are dawn-to-dusk, and the Shipwreck Museum's indoor buildings close at 5 p.m. Verify access for any public shoreline area before arriving.

Is Paradise cloud cover the same as Sault Ste. Marie?

No. Clouds can differ sharply across the eastern Upper Peninsula. The NWS cloud reading here is requested for a Whitefish Point lakeshore coordinate, separate from the regional NOAA OVATION proxy.

Does a high Kp guarantee aurora over Lake Superior?

No. Kp is a geomagnetic index and OVATION is a short-term model, not a local sighting probability. Dark skies, cloud cover, moonlight and the northern horizon matter.
