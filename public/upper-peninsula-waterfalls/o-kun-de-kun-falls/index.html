One signed North Country Trail hike visits the smaller upstream Konteka (Peanut Butter) Falls before the larger O Kun de Kun curtain at a suspension bridge.

What makes this visit worthwhile?

This is a hike-first waterfall day, not a scenic-byway roadside stop. A remote-feeling Baltimore River curtain viewed from a suspension bridge.

Trail and access reality

2026 NCTA trail ownership audit: The U.S. Forest Service owns the signed Segment 22 corridor; Ottawa National Forest and Peter Wolfe Chapter volunteers maintain it. The May 2026 chapter trail guide documents the US-45 parking, 1.4-mile route to the suspension bridge, and seasonal conditions. The map point is the fall, not a car parking destination.

About 1.4 miles one way along North Country Trail Segment 22 from the US-45 trailhead to the Baltimore River suspension bridge, passing Konteka Falls; short gravel spur, boardwalk and steeper riverbank tread.

The eastern side of the Baltimore River is not a parking access point. Do not plan to scramble behind the waterfall. A known visitor route does not guarantee that a bridge, road or overlook is accessible on your visit date.

Two waterfalls on one trail: Konteka, then O Kun de Kun

First: Konteka Falls. The smaller upstream sandstone drop is called Konteka Falls by the North Country Trail chapter, Konteko Falls by an independent local waterfall mapper, and Peanut Butter Falls by Visit Keweenaw. It is not a separate driving destination. Follow the same signed NCT from the US-45 parking area. The smaller fall is near the trail as it reaches the Baltimore River. A short steep spur offers closer river views, but descending it is optional and may be unsafe when wet or icy.

Second: O Kun de Kun Falls. Stay on the main NCT downstream to the larger waterfall and suspension bridge. The NCTA chapter describes roughly 1.4 miles one way to the bridge, not 1.4 miles to each fall. The bridge is the straightforward public viewing choice; do not scramble behind the falls or improvise a river crossing.

Map meaning: the Konteka feature reference from an independent specialist waterfall mapper is at 46.64996, -89.15506. This is an approximate natural feature coordinate, never parking or a point-to-point hiking route. Neither mapped falls pin establishes today’s trail opening, safe footing or bridge conditions.

Consult the NCTA Segment 22 source, Visit Keweenaw two-falls account, and independent feature-location source. Check NCT trail alerts before leaving.

Seasonal waterfall conditions

River volume and the landscape can change after rainfall or snowmelt. Autumn offers hardwood colors; ice can make winter approaches hazardous. This page does not provide measured real-time waterfall flow or a current parking report.
