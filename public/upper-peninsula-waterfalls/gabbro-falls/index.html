Gabbro Falls, where the river breaks through the rock

A dark, narrow Black River gorge and several striking cascades lie beside Snowriver's Black River Basin resort. Gabbro Falls, sometimes called Baker's Falls, rewards a deliberate short hike rather than an improvised cliff scramble.

Snowriver's own website explicitly encourages waterfall visits on its property, and Bessemer names the bridge trailhead by the Blackjack resort parking area. Choose the safer documented approach

Start
Snowriver Black River Basin (formerly Blackjack Ski Hill) near Bessemer. The City of Bessemer identifies a waterfall trailhead near the Black River bridge by the resort parking area.
Park
Ask Snowriver guest services where waterfall visitors may use its designated Black River Basin parking. The resort's published trail map includes parking, but does not establish free non-guest or bridge-shoulder permission. Do not park in an unsigned shoulder, block a bridge or enter a service road.
Walk
Snowriver's owner-published waterfall description states approximately 0.3 mile from the Black River bridge to Gabbro Falls. Use the trailhead described by the City and follow currently posted pedestrian signs. The exact ground track and separate parking-to-trailhead distance are not surveyed here.
Time
Allow about 45–90 minutes for a cautious stop, depending on surface, weather and chosen permitted viewpoint. This is a planning allowance, not a verified minimum walking time or a calculated drive.
Terrain
Uneven soil, mud, roots and slippery rock ledges above an unfenced, powerful river gorge. Keep well back from drop-offs. Do not descend to the plunge pool or attempt a river crossing.
Before departure
Confirm the visitor parking space, open bridge-side footpath and any temporary resort restrictions with Snowriver (guest services: 906-229-5181).

Two approaches, but only one recommended here

Bridge trail: documented visitor choice. The property operator publishes a 0.3-mile hike from the Black River bridge, and the City of Bessemer identifies a trailhead near the bridge by Blackjack parking. Taken together, they establish a published visitor approach on the resort property. That does not mean every connecting road shoulder or informal riverbank is open.

Upper-road viewpoint: permission incomplete. Snowriver says a waterfall viewing approach lies within about 20 yards of a road, but does not identify a visitor-approved upper service-road entrance, designated parking space or marked connector. Independent blogs describe driving resort service roads and wandering utility clearings. This guide does not recommend those shortcuts, and it intentionally omits a service-road route and upper viewpoint navigation pin.

Winter: not an open-now verdict. This is an operating ski area. A resort photo of the falls in snow does not establish that pedestrian access, the bridge trail, parking or any ski run is open to hikers. Follow current resort directions, not an assumed summer route.

Read the landscape

Several drops tear through dark, blocky bedrock along the Black River. The gorge is narrow enough to obscure part of the plunge from any single angle; what sounds like one broad waterfall reveals multiple channels when viewed from safe permitted positions. Water levels and visibility vary with rainfall and snowmelt. Do not predict river flow, winter ice strength or swimming safety from a distant gauge or a scenic photograph.

Snowriver supplies original photographs of Gabbro Falls. No third-party image has been copied into this guide.

Evidence behind the visit

Property/manager
Snowriver Mountain Resort explicitly identifies Gabbro on its Black River Basin property.
Visitor permission context
Snowriver's summer attraction page invites visitors to view the waterfall. This is not a guarantee of unrestricted access in every season.
Parking evidence
City of Bessemer names the Black River bridge trailhead by the Blackjack ski hill parking area. Snowriver's own ski map depicts resort parking, but does not spell out non-guest waterfall visitor entitlement or a verified bridge-side lot coordinate.
Foot access
Snowriver operator guidance describes the 0.3-mile bridge-to-falls walk. City guidance independently names the bridge trailhead.
Natural feature identity
Michigan state GIS feature #38 locates Gabbro Falls at 46.502079, -89.992485. It does NOT locate parking.
River hazard
American Whitewater describes Gabbro Falls as a Class V waterfall hazard; no safe paddling or plunge-pool swimming is recommended here.

Evidence reviewed November 2, 2026. The owner/city route is documented, not surveyed. No claim of live trail opening, posted current parking, permitted non-guest lot privileges, all-season walking, measured flow or a safe descent to river level.
