Bridge closure: the important distinction

Older Keweenaw visitor descriptions recommend a steel pedestrian bridge as the best view. Houghton Township's official attractions page describes its second historic pedestrian bridge with picnic tables as currently closed. Do not enter that bridge or work around barriers. The county recreation plan separately identifies the MDOT-owned Eagle River Bridge Park as a public waterfall viewing area.

The useful plan is to park legally in the park and use its designated viewing path if accessible on your visit. A waterfall can be viewable while a historic pedestrian bridge is closed. There is no live inspection here, and temporary construction or winter conditions may restrict the park as well.

Choose this waterfall for the right experience

Choose this stop for the signed park's waterfall view; do not confuse the public park path with the historic pedestrian bridge currently listed closed.

A dam changes the height and appearance of the river drop; the entire height is not one uninterrupted natural fall.

For a short, managed visitor experience, compare this stop to Haven Falls in its county park and Eagle River Falls at the MDOT bridge park. For a natural geology walk, Silver River Falls in the nature preserve requires more attention to road-side parking and walking surfaces.

What to check before you drive

Spring runoff or after rain; water and mist vary during dry weeks. None of these descriptions measure the present water volume. A river's visual intensity changes with rain, spring melt, drought and freezing weather. The Michigan GIS waterfall pin and USGS hydrographic identifiers do not report real-time water levels.

Access conditions: A dam changes the height and appearance of the river drop; the entire height is not one uninterrupted natural fall. Check the responsible authority and actual gates, barriers and signs for the day you travel. The Keweenaw can develop snow or ice early, and a published public walking route is not proof of current safe footing. Avoid river edges, unstable rock, dam structures and closed bridges.

Why this matters: A location can be known on a map long before its legal public visitor route is clear. This page cites parking, path, manager and waterfall coordinates separately so you can decide whether the visit makes sense before committing to the drive.
