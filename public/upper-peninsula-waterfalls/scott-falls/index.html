Lake Superior · M-28 · Au Train Township

Scott Falls

At a bend in M-28, a thin curtain of water spills from a sandstone shelf into a shallow alcove. The waterfall feels almost hidden in the trees. Across the highway, an official roadside park offers something unusual for a waterfall visit: a place to stop and look without a backcountry hike.

Verified visitor scope: view from the MDOT roadside park, without crossing M-28. The park may close seasonally. Current opening and roadside conditions are unverified. The waterfall-side footpath and the highway crossing are NOT authorized routes in this guide.

Come for the park, view the falls across the road

Where to arrive
Use MDOT's Scott Falls Roadside Park / H.J. Rathfoot State Roadside Park on the Lake Superior (north) side of M-28 near Au Train, only when the park entrance is open and the designated parking area is available. Alger County's recreation plan identifies this park and its waterfall view across the highway. Do not stop in a travel lane, on the bridge, or an undesignated shoulder. The natural waterfall GIS coordinate is NOT the roadside park entrance.
Walking
Remain on the roadside-park side of M-28 for the verified waterfall view across the highway. There is no established public pedestrian crossing in these sources, and the waterfall-side path, under-ledge grotto and sandstone rim are NOT a certified visitor trail. Do not cross busy M-28 or climb to the falls based on this guide.
Seasonal park status
MDOT's April 2026 roadside-park notice specifically names Scott Falls Roadside Park as delayed for spring snow clearance, and says seasonal roadside parks may close in late October. It does not say the park is open on your date. If it is closed, do not improvise roadside parking or turn this into a shoulder stop.
Time needed
About 5–20 minutes for a park-side look if the facility is available; more for a beach picnic when permitted.

A sandstone curtain in the northwoods

Scott Falls collects in a small creek before spilling from a layered sandstone lip. The drop is modest, but the dark alcove gives the water the look of a curtain hanging from the forest. The falls are most noticeable with snowmelt or after rain. In dry weather the stream can thin so much that the rock shelf becomes more prominent than the falling water.

Why this guide does not tell you to cross M-28

The waterfall is on the opposite side of a busy state highway from the Lake Superior roadside park. Some travel blogs describe walking across M-28 or behind the falls, but those reports do not establish an official pedestrian crossing, rights on the waterfall bank, or a maintained safe approach. A short distance is not the same as a safe crossing. Stay on the park side for this supported view; do not walk into traffic, park on an unapproved shoulder, or scramble onto wet rock.

Two sides of the road, two different experiences

The Alger County Recreation Plan describes the MDOT park as having parking, a Lake Superior beach, picnic facilities and a waterfall view across the highway. That is the supported visitor experience. The separate natural-falls coordinate in the Michigan GIS marks Scott Falls itself, not the park entrance, a pedestrian trail or a legal shoulder parking spot.

Five things verified separately

Park manager
Alger County's 2024 recreation plan identifies MDOT management of the Scott Falls Roadside Park, not ownership of the falls-side bank.
Public viewpoint
The county plan explicitly documents the waterfall view across the highway from the roadside park. It does not approve a highway crossing.
Parking
MDOT lists the Scott Falls roadside park and seasonal closing practices. Current open status remains unverified. No precise parking GPS is asserted.
Walking route
No hiking or pedestrian crossing is needed for the park-side viewpoint; there is no verified public footpath to the waterfall bank.
Natural falls location
Michigan GIS feature #196 marks the waterfall itself at 46.436999, -86.814470, separate from visitor parking.

Local Munising visitor information also places Scott Falls opposite H.J. Rathfoot State Roadside Park and describes a view from the highway. The Lake Superior Circle Tour corroborates the location and describes a possible walk behind the falls, which this guide deliberately does not recommend without crossing and land-access verification.

Make it a relaxed stop, not a risky crossing

When the park is open, the Lake Superior beach and picnic area can make this a useful break between Marquette and Munising, whether Scott Creek is flowing strongly or barely at all. For a closer waterfall viewpoint without crossing M-28, consider Wagner Falls or Laughing Whitefish Falls, both with documented approaches.
