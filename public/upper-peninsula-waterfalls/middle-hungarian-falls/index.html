Middle Hungarian Falls

The missing middle drop: follow the land-trust-mapped gorge trail from the same legal arrival as Upper and Lower Hungarian Falls, then watch Dover Creek spill over sandstone into its sheltered pool.

Official gorge trail and parking published; current conditions unverified. The upper ten acres belong to Keweenaw Land Trust and the middle/lower gorge includes Michigan DNR land. Neighboring parcels are private, and no off-trail base route or river crossing is cleared. The feature pin is independent approximate geography, never a car destination.

Before you drive

Land manager
Keweenaw Land Trust owns the public upper entry. Michigan DNR owns much of the public middle/lower gorge, with private land adjacent. Respect the land-manager-published map and stay on established public paths.
Legal public access
KLT publishes public year-round gorge walking and an illustrated Middle Falls on its red-dashed public trail network. The owner map identifies private parcels beside the state and land-trust lands.
Parking and arrival
From M-26 in Tamarack City, take 6th Street uphill to Golf Course Road. KLT identifies roadside visitor parking near its nature-area sign at 47.173159, -88.444743. This is the approved arrival reference, not the falls. The rough former forest road is for walking, not driving.
Walking route
Follow the old gated/rough woods route roughly 400 yards toward KLT's gathering area, then use the signed or published downstream gorge trail leading to the Middle Falls, a few hundred yards beyond the path split per Visit Keweenaw. The full gorge system is roughly two miles. No surveyed middle-specific hiking distance or creek crossing is claimed.
Suggested visit allowance
45–110 minutes including cautious gorge travel, an editorial planning range rather than a surveyed hike time.
Best viewing conditions
Spring thaw and the days after heavy rain. At summer low flows, this smaller watershed may show a thin cascade, not a roaring waterfall.

Two different points: waterfall and parking

Navigate to KLT's published seasonal roadside parking

The parking reference is taken directly from the land manager. It is not winter-plow confirmation, and it is not a guaranteed free space. Never drive the blocked woods road.

The waterfall pin at 47.17193, -88.44781 is an approximately geocoded natural feature, with another independent public location ~21 m away. It is not state GIS #130 or #134, a surveyed public viewpoint, or a parking waypoint.

Land trust ownership, route and parking map

What makes Middle Hungarian Falls worth the walk?

Most people know Hungarian Falls as two extremes: an upper waterfall near the historic dam and a dramatic plunge farther down the gorge. Between them is the sandstone drop that makes the trail feel like a continuous story. Dover Creek cuts through an old lava landscape, with a compact shaded pool that rewards a slower visit.

Compared with Upper Falls, the middle reach adds a downhill forest walk and more exposed roots. Compared with Lower Falls, the principal visitor experience is a less demanding trail-side look; the steep informal routes into the base are not necessary or endorsed.

The land trust's mapped trail and the tourism guide describe access, but neither establishes today's water depth, road plowing, fallen trees or rock stability. Do not improvise a river crossing or climb down an eroded cliff to reproduce a photograph.

Access decision: use the owner's map, not a stranger's pin

Parking: KLT's only precisely published visitor arrival reference is 47.173159, -88.444743 on Golf Course Road. Parking is along the road near its trail sign where the signed shoulder is available, not a dedicated paved lot or a promise of winter vehicle access.

Walking: Follow the rough old track on foot to the trail split, then keep on the mapped downstream gorge path. Visit Keweenaw describes the Middle Falls after a few hundred yards on that path. KLT's illustrated map marks the falls on public lands and identifies adjacent private property.

Feature identity: The Michigan state waterfall inventory has upper #134 and lower #130, but no separately matched Middle Hungarian Falls point. Two non-government geographic sources put the middle cascade within about 21 metres of each other; that point is an approximate *natural feature*, not a validated trailhead or car destination. The source disagreement over the positions of the lower falls does not authorize moving or conflating either state point.

What changes with the season?

November and winter: KLT warns that Golf Course Road is often unplowed; park legally where plowing ends or take a different trip. Ice over steep rock and unprotected gorge edges can make this outing inappropriate, even if the preserve remains open to pedestrian visits.

Spring: The middle plunge can become powerful after snowmelt and rain, when muddy trail edges and flooded crossings are also most hazardous. Do not cross Dover Creek.

Summer and fall: The creek may run low; trail conditions remain unverified. High-water scenery and dry-foot public access are different questions.
