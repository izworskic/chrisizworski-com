Tibbets Falls

The Sturgeon River does not make one dramatic leap here. Instead, amber water fans over a long staircase of slate, catching light between cedar trunks and hemlock boughs. The falls stretch along a river bend, and the walk to see them feels like a short piece of the great North Country Trail.

Public trail and named parking established by the North Country Trail Association. Current park and trail conditions are unverified; follow the latest chapter maps and any posted restrictions. The unmaintained sandy two-track and slippery riverside ledges are NOT part of the recommended vehicle approach.

A waterfall you meet along the trail

Public parking
From M-28 near Watton take Baraga Plains Road north, cross the Sturgeon River on the road bridge, and continue to the named Tibbets Falls / North Country Trail parking area on the west side of Plains Road. The Peter Wolfe Chapter's current segment 46 guide confirms this parking area and warns not to block the westbound sandy two-track. The parking is a trailhead, NOT the waterfall GIS coordinate. Use NCTA's current Avenza map #27 and road signs; no live winter clearing, exact driving turnoff coordinates or vehicle access down the sandy two-track is guaranteed.
Walking route
Follow the marked foot connector from the Tibbets Falls trailhead to the blue-blazed North Country Trail, then use the official Peter Wolfe Chapter/Avenza #27 map to follow the Sturgeon River section where Tibbets Falls appears along Segment 44. The waterfall is a series of slate-ledged rapids visible from the trail as it climbs the bluff. Several NCT segments and a separate white-blazed fishermen's spur meet near Plains Road; confirm the correct trail on the current chapter map. Do not drive the unmaintained sandy two-track, attempt an unsignposted river crossing, or walk out onto slick slate slabs for a closer photograph.
Walking time
Allow roughly 1–2 hours for a relaxed outing, including time near the river and route decisions. The chapter supplies segment distances rather than a certified single trailhead-to-waterfall mileage; consult Avenza map #27 for the path you plan to follow.
Trail ownership and maintenance
NCTA's Peter Wolfe Chapter April 2025 guide identifies State of Michigan ownership for Segments 44–46, with the trail maintained and marked by chapter volunteers. This does not authorize roaming through unrelated private parcels or driving on sandy side tracks.
Seasonal access
The chapter warns winter clearing of the named trailhead lot is variable. Baraga Plains Road may be plowed, but access to the parking area and trail is not confirmed for today. Unmaintained two-tracks are not an alternative.

Follow the blue blazes, not the tire tracks

The named parking area is along Baraga Plains Road, about a mile beyond the river bridge according to chapter materials. Walk the signed foot connector to the North Country Trail. Be alert to the white-blazed riverside fishermen's spur that leads toward the road bridge; the blue blazes indicate the NCT. The Tibbets cascade is associated with the Sturgeon River portion of Segment 44. Because several connectors and trail junctions meet here, download the current chapter map before arrival rather than relying on a straight line to the waterfall waypoint.

Take the view from the trail

The chapter describes Tibbets as several hundred feet of low slate drops visible from the NCT as the path ascends a bluff. The downstream rock shelves can offer an inviting photograph, but they are exceptionally slippery, especially during runoff and after rain. Do not scramble out onto the wet ledges or force a river crossing to reach a closer pool. The sound of the river carries well from the maintained trail.

What the forest is like

The trail passes riverbank cedar, soft floodplain ground and drier rises. Several sections have boardwalks and short bridges, while summer grass and berry growth can narrow the tread. Unlike a paved scenic overlook, this is a real forest trail: roots, wet ground and changing water make good footwear and navigation important.

Two distinct road approaches you should not confuse

The chapter's named Plains Road parking area is the ordinary visitor arrival. Its map also shows an unmaintained westbound sandy two-track with possible informal parking near the river. The chapter warns that it is soft, may damage vehicles and is not cleared. This guide deliberately uses the named road parking and walking trail instead. It does not recommend driving to the falls, following logging roads or parking near the Plains Road bridge bend after dark.

Natural falls location, not trailhead

The pin is Michigan GIS waterfall #44, not the Baraga Plains Road lot or the full NCT walking route. Use official NCTA Avenza map #27 for the trail and parking junctions.

Current NCTA trail alerts

Five sources of visitor confidence

Land manager
NCTA's 2025 Segments 44–46 guide reports State of Michigan ownership and volunteer-maintained trail.
Public access
The chapter expressly describes Tibbets Falls as a sight on the signed public NCT, with dedicated trailhead parking.
Vehicle parking
The named Tibbets Falls trailhead on Baraga Plains Road is the supported parking option. Avoid sandy two-tracks and blocking entrances.
Trail to waterfalls
The blazed trail on Segment 44 passes the slate cascades. This is a trail view, not a defined riverside climbing route.
Natural location
Michigan GIS feature #44 records the natural falls, while an independent field account places the same cascade nearby. Neither source certifies parking coordinates.

Check before you go: The Peter Wolfe Chapter's current maps and NCTA alerts take precedence over the dated PDF if the trail changes. Conditions and winter parking are not monitored by this page.

Continue a Sturgeon River day

Pair this quieter forest walk with Canyon Falls, which has a more developed highway-side entrance, or compare the much more remote Sturgeon Falls gorge guide. They are different experiences; Tibbets is the place for a maintained trail and a long, low river cascade.
