Trap Falls

Trap Falls is the opposite of a drive-up waterfall. Reaching the Upper Carp River is part of the day, with a sustained forest walk and a quieter river setting well beyond the developed roadside overlooks.

Before leaving the car

Land manager
Michigan DNR · Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park
Documented parking
Use the designated Government Peak Trailhead parking on M-107 inside Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park. Do not navigate to the waterfall coordinate or improvise riverbank parking.
Walking route
Take the Government Peak Trail from its signed M-107 trailhead. Michigan DNR's visitor publication describes a 3.2-mile round-trip hike to Trap Falls; other guides publish longer approaches. Confirm the current route and mileage on the DNR trail map before leaving the trailhead.

Public nonmotorized Government Peak Trail within Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park. This is a committed woodland hike, not a pullout or accessible waterfall platform.

What this waterfall feels like

The Upper Carp River crosses a rugged old-growth forest landscape. The reward is a low, concentrated river drop and the space around it, not a huge vertical cataract. Spring snowmelt and rain change its appearance, but there is no reliable real-time gauge for this specific waterfall on this page.

A remote Upper Carp River cascade reached on a sustained Government Peak Trail walk through rugged Porcupine Mountains forest. Route mileage differs among DNR and regional descriptions; this guide deliberately follows the DNR visitor publication while warning visitors to verify current signage.

Find the right public approach

Begin at the signed Government Peak Trailhead on M-107. Follow the Government Peak Trail into the wooded interior and use the current DNR map and on-trail markers to confirm the approach to Trap Falls. Do not use the state GIS pin as an entrance. The walk requires real trail time in both directions; allow for slower travel on roots, uneven ground or wet surfaces.

Choose Trap Falls for a half-day hiking destination rather than a quick scenic stop. Compare with Overlooked if your party needs a much shorter walk.

Choose the outing that fits your day

The DNR visitor publication describes a 3.2-mile round trip. Regional materials give different mileage for a Government Peak approach. Rather than promising exact distance, this guide recommends treating Trap Falls as a half-day walk and confirming the current route at the trailhead. If route signs differ from a phone map, follow park instructions and turn back when uncertain.

Travelers seeking less walking can choose Overlooked Falls from the Little Carp River lot or visit the accessible Nawadaha viewing platform at the Presque Isle River scenic area. Trap Falls is for someone deliberately choosing the trail itself.

Seasonal road and trail reality

Michigan DNR states that South Boundary Road is closed to ordinary vehicles from Headquarters Road south to Pinkerton Trailhead from December 1 through late spring. Neither a summer trailhead description nor a mapped waterfall confirms winter vehicle access.

The river corridor has roots, grades, potential mud and slippery rock. Do not attempt the falls as a casual winter stop or assume water crossings are bridged. M-107 and South Boundary Road seasonal restrictions may affect driving access.

Snow, ice, high water and trail erosion can change whether a published summer route is reasonable. This guide does not calculate today's waterfall flow, park-road status, or winter trail safety. Confirm current conditions with the Michigan DNR before setting out.

Watch the river, not just your map

This is not a paved or barrier-free viewing facility. Carry water and a paper or offline park map, and plan to return to your vehicle before daylight fades. Wet stone near any waterfall is unforgiving; view from a safe distance and do not enter a rushing channel. Confirm that M-107 and nearby park roads are open to vehicles before departing.
