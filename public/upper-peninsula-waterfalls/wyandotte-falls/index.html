Wyandotte Falls

The aptly named Misery River moves quietly out of the Twin Lakes before folding down a few small ledges between old cedar trunks. Wyandotte Falls is not a roadside monument. It rewards a short forest walk, and in spring the narrow river can sound much larger than it looks on a summer map.

Before leaving the car

Approach
From M-26 approximately 1.5 miles southwest of Twin Lakes State Park, turn west onto Poyhonen Road toward Wyandotte Hills Golf Course. The Keweenaw visitor bureau describes a parking area approximately 0.8 mile along the side road; the road and trailhead are not the raw waterfall GIS location.
Public visitor walk
The visitor bureau waterfall brochure identifies a roughly 250-yard walk along a rustic trail to the falls. Independent local descriptions place the path south from a small parking area beyond the golf course's storage shed. Use the evident trail and posted signs without entering buildings or operational golf-course property.
Difficulty
Uneven roots, cedar forest, boggy sections and mossy river rock make this a moderate-feeling, undeveloped short walk. The official regional brochure calls the fall moderate. No accessible path, handrail, hardened overlook or boardwalk is verified.
Time allowance
Allow 30–60 minutes for the out-and-back trail and viewing when the permitted lot and access road are usable; not an official posted duration.
Season
Spring melt can make the low, two-stage falls more active. During late summer, water may become a trickle. Current opening and road conditions are unverified; no guarantee of plowed winter access to the golf-course vicinity or safe trail footing is established.

Only the documented public-facing roadside or marked visitor approach is described. This guide provides no surveyed parking waypoint, no unapproved upstream scramble and no current parking availability.

Geographic falls point ≠ visitor parking

Michigan state GIS #128 identifies the named waterfall, not the highway pull-off, lot, or guaranteed walking route. Use the regional road description and posted signs to arrive safely.

The character of a quiet waterfall

The Misery River squeezes around boulders and tumbles over a modest pair of rocky steps in deep forest. This is a place for small details: wet cedar bark, tiny pools and the unusual way water disappears behind dark rock before reappearing. The scenery is intimate rather than spectacularly tall, and the falls may look very different at low flow.

Follow the correct road, not the waterfall coordinate

There are two relevant positions: the named falls on Michigan GIS #128 near 46.890025, -88.877565, and the signed road/trail arrival along Poyhonen Road. The regional brochure gives a road distance to a parking area, not the exact latitude of a legal parking bay. An independent walking description places the trailhead beyond the golf course maintenance shed. Do not drive through private maintenance areas or use the waterfall dot to generate a vehicle route.

How much walking to expect

The Keweenaw tourism brochure says 250 yards on a rustic path, whereas some later local accounts call the forest walk roughly a quarter mile. Distances may reflect different start points or rough estimates; our plan uses the brochure's 250-yard trail description with no surveyed digital route line. Follow a recognizable public trail and return the same way rather than exploring trails crossing private golf-course activity zones.

When the Misery River delivers

The river drains the Twin Lakes and surrounding bog country. Spring thaw or rain can bring surprisingly full water over low rock, but a late-summer stop can find more green moss than rushing foam. Photographers interested in soft creek falls should favor spring or immediately after rain, while planning for muddy footing.

A sensible Twin Lakes side trip

Wyandotte is easiest to understand as a Twin Lakes side trip rather than a detour across unmarked forest roads. Visit Twin Lakes State Park separately for a developed recreation area, and treat the golf course as a landmark rather than an automatic public parking entitlement. Skip the falls if there is no clear, permitted space to park.
