What kind of waterfall experience is this?

Keep this as a map research stop for now; Haven or Eagle River offers a better documented arrival and viewing route.

A tiered Silver River waterfall near an M-26 roadside park, still awaiting a documented safe walking connection from visitor parking. Research guide only. Park ownership is identified in an older county land-use plan as MDOT, while the 2025–2029 county recreation plan lists the site but gives no current crossing guidance.

This is a place to slow down and notice how streams cut across the Keweenaw's volcanic and sedimentary rock. The shape of the waterfall depends on the season and recent precipitation; no live discharge or daily water-level estimate is available for this spot.

The walking route, not just the map dot

The Keweenaw visitor brochure describes a rustic pull-off south of the M-26 Silver River bridge and a short path on the north side. That route would require an at-grade highway crossing, which has not been independently confirmed as a safe legal pedestrian approach.

Do not cross M-26 or enter the river gorge solely on these directions. Confirm legal pedestrian access and any roadwork with the responsible road authority.

Public-parking guidance and permission to reach a waterfall are different things. Use designated park surfaces or approved viewing structures. Do not assume that an old footpath, steep riverbank, waterfall lip, dam or roadside shoulder is safe or legally accessible because a waterfall appears on a map.

What changes through the year?

Spring flow over the stepped Silver River bedrock; wet rocks and ice can make streamside footing hazardous.

During spring runoff water may be strong and viewpoints slippery. Summer can bring lower flows. Autumn makes the surrounding woods part of the outing. Snow, ice and unplowed roadside lots can prevent a visit even when the geographic point is still visible on a map. No current facility-open status or forecast-based water-flow claim is made here.

County park accessibility assessments describe parts of a site, not every path. Do not infer a wheelchair-safe route without current surface and facility information.
