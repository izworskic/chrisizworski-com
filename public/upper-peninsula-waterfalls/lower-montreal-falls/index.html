Montreal Falls: two wild drops, one legal approach

At the far eastern Keweenaw, the Montreal River tumbles over volcanic rock before flowing into Lake Superior. Lower Montreal Falls is the first destination on a remote lakeshore walk; Upper Montreal Falls is a separate, more difficult upstream excursion. The adventure begins with choosing the right road.

Backcountry, not a casual roadside stop. The only published lawful visitor approach is via Mandan Road and Fish Cove. Do not use Smith Fisheries Road: that approach is private property and trespassing. Current two-track, gates, shoreline footing, winter travel and trail conditions are not verified here.

Should you go?

Best fit
Experienced hikers equipped for rough two-track driving, unreliable cell service, self-navigation along unmarked lakeshore, and a full daylight outing. This is not an accessible, maintained waterfall walk.
Land and visitor permission
The lower and upper waterfalls are on State of Michigan land. Visit Keweenaw and a notice developed with nearby landowners and Michigan DNR identify the lawful public visitor approach via Fish Cove. Public permission to the falls does not authorize driving through adjacent private holdings.
Parking arrival
From Copper Harbor continue east from the end of US-41 onto Mandan Road. After approximately 7.5 miles, turn onto Hoar Lake/Fish Cove Road and follow the signs to Fish Cove's rustic parking area. The tourism guide recommends high clearance and slow travel on unpaved two-track roads. No surveyed parking coordinate or current road-opening guarantee is published here.
Walk to Lower Montreal Falls
From Fish Cove parking walk south to the Lake Superior shore, then follow the faint, unmarked and unmaintained path west along the shoreline for approximately one mile to the lower falls. Bring an offline map and give yourself enough daylight to retrace the route. Do not mistake the lake shore for a marked public trail.
Optional Upper Montreal Falls
After the lower falls, the separate upper drop lies farther up the Montreal River. An independent waterfall specialist describes a rough west-bank path, with steeper outcrops and uncertain footing. No official distance, continuous signed footpath or safe final viewpoint is verified. Turn around at the lower falls if navigation or terrain becomes uncertain.
Time and seasonal reality
Allow roughly 2½–5+ hours as a planning buffer, not a published official round-trip hiking time. Visit Keweenaw recommends hiking after the spring melt when roads and trails firm up. Avoid assuming access in November, snow, flood, high lake waves, thunderstorms or darkness.

Two waterfall locations, not two parking pins

Lower: 47.39257, −87.84114, at the river's Lake Superior mouth. Upper: 47.39563, −87.83551, upstream. Independent specialist waterfall locations only; not NPS/DNR surveyed viewpoints or lot coordinates.

Map warning: Neither marker is a car destination. A straight line between them is not a trail. We intentionally provide no car-routing link to either waterfall and no invented path from Fish Cove parking.

Which falls will you see?

Lower Montreal Falls marks the river's final descent to Lake Superior. It spreads over dark, angular volcanic ledges, with the lake opening beyond the falls and distant Keweenaw ridges across the water. This is the destination described by Visit Keweenaw's Fish Cove route.

Upper Montreal Falls is a distinct upstream chute in a narrow bedrock corridor, where water concentrates into a deeper pool beneath steep sides. Getting closer to the upper drop can require more route-finding around the riverbank; the lower-falls visit does not obligate you to continue.

Middle Montreal Falls? Some independent directories list Middle Montreal Falls too. Its distinct physical identity, accessible viewpoint, and legal walking approach have not been independently reconciled here, so this guide intentionally does not invent a third pin or separate destination.

A crucial correction to older directions

Historic waterfall guides still recommend Smith Fisheries Road from Lac La Belle. That advice is obsolete. A Visit Keweenaw notice produced with local landowners and Michigan DNR states that reaching Montreal Falls by Smith Fisheries Road is trespassing. The legal approach is via Mandan Road and Fish Cove. Changing gate locations on private roads are not alternate permission to cross.

Do not confuse this Keweenaw Montreal River with Interstate Falls on the Michigan–Wisconsin border. Interstate Falls has also been called Montreal Falls, but it is hundreds of miles from this Keweenaw destination and has an entirely different public arrival and land manager.

Michigan DNR also describes a Montreal Corridor trail development project within the Keweenaw Point trail system. That is a construction initiative, not proof that a finished maintained waterfall trail or new parking area is open today. See the DNR project updates rather than following speculative future-route map lines.

When to choose another waterfall

Choose Manganese Falls or Haven Falls when you need a shorter Keweenaw visit with established visitor approaches. Skip Montreal Falls if your vehicle cannot safely travel the seasonal two-track, there is no legal usable parking, shoreline rocks are wet or iced, or your hiking party lacks experience navigating unmarked paths.

Expect patchy cell service, no drinking water or restrooms near the falls, and unpredictable water levels in small backcountry streams. Use boots with traction, carry food and a headlamp, and never cross slick river ledges just to reach a photograph location.
