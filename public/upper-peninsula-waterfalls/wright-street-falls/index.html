Wright Street Falls

Where the Dead River meets Marquette’s industrial history, water slips over bedrock under a towering penstock. It is a modest but rewarding short walk, not the rugged Dead River Falls gorge farther upstream.

Plan this short walkOriginal public parking guideBack to the waterfall map
Access evidence reviewed November 2, 2026 · current opening unverified. Travel Marquette describes public Wright Street dirt parking and a short river walk. Obey posted NTN conditions and all hydroelectric facility barriers. The site is not a same-day trail or water-flow feed.

Plan the actual visit

Where
Wright Street west of central Marquette, near the Board of Light and Power and Noquemanon North Trails.
Allow
15–30 minutes at the falls, an editorial short-stop allowance, not measured travel time or a guaranteed accessible path.
Park
Travel Marquette describes a dirt lot off Wright Street near the Board of Light and Power and a smaller lot closer to the falls. Choose a signed public space rather than a roadside shoulder.
On foot
Walk toward the river on the existing public path and cross the boardwalk. The tourism authority describes approximately 450 feet from its closer public parking to the cascade below the penstock.
Expect
Natural rock, uneven/possibly wet path surfaces and industrial water-control infrastructure. The cascade is approximately 10 feet in published visitor guidance; flow is influenced by hydropower operations.
Do not
Walk on the penstock, access a powerhouse or utility enclosure, climb over barriers, or follow these directions to McClure’s upstream Dead River Falls.

Map the correct waterfall

Why this falls is different

It is worth noticing how the river landscape and the electric power era meet. The bedrock ledges, flowing water, and penstock visible above the river are all part of this viewpoint. A modest cascade can be a memorable pause because its industrial setting is unlike the forest gorges farther into the U.P.

Do not confuse it with Dead River Falls. The famous multi-drop Dead River gorge near the McClure hydropower site is much farther upstream and much more difficult. The project’s separate access review found a historic FERC private-property warning along that gorge trail. Nothing about this publicly described Wright Street footpath grants permission to enter the upstream route.

What to check before departing

Review current Noquemanon North Trails notices and posted utility signage. Conditions may change with snow, ice or facility operations. The published path description is not an assessment of wheelchair accessibility or winter maintenance. Never infer water volume from a distant gauge or a season alone.
