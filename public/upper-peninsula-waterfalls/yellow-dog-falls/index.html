Yellow Dog River · Big Bay · Marquette County

Yellow Dog Falls

The Yellow Dog steps over old crystalline bedrock, cutting a chain of cascades through hemlock and northern hardwood forest. This is a walk into a protected river corridor, not a waterfall beside a highway.

Public forest trail documented. Current opening and road conditions are unverified. Follow the landowner's signed access from County Road 510. Do not use the waterfall point as a vehicle destination or interpret archived river data as today's flow.

Public access
Yellow Dog Watershed Preserve owns and protects the public 688-acre Community Forest. The established nonmotorized trail leads from the signed bridge-side entrance into the waterfall corridor. Do not cross into downstream private parcels or use river fords as an informal shortcut.
Parking arrival
Yellow Dog River Community Forest's primary public parking and trailhead immediately south of the Yellow Dog River bridge on County Road 510. From CR-550 follow CR-510, turn left after about 2.6 miles to stay on dirt CR-510 at the AAA junction, continue about 3.9 miles and cross the river; landowner directs visitors to the lot on the left.
Trail to the falls
Follow the established forest trail from the County Road 510 bridge parking area on the south side of the Yellow Dog River, downstream toward the falls. Landowner estimates about 0.75 mile to the first falls; a published geological field guide describes roughly 0.5 mile to Yellow Dog Falls. Treat mileage as approximate and return on the signed route.

How to find the right Yellow Dog trail

From Marquette, head north on County Road 550 toward Big Bay. Turn west onto County Road 510. After roughly 2.6 miles, turn left to stay on CR-510 where the road turns to dirt at County Road AAA. Go roughly 3.9 more miles, cross the Yellow Dog River bridge and use the signed parking area on the left, just south of the river. Those directions come from the landowner, not a route inferred from the waterfall map.

Start at the Community Forest sign and established river trail. The landowner describes approximately three-quarters of a mile to the first waterfall. The geological field guide describes a roughly half-mile walk to Yellow Dog Falls, also called Hills Falls. Because the river has multiple drops and visitor descriptions use different stopping points, expect an approximate walk rather than a promised tenth-of-a-mile distance. Stay with the signed trail and return to the same parking area.

Do not confuse Yellow Dog Falls with Pinnacle Falls. Pinnacle Falls Preserve has its own signed two-track parking beyond County Road AAA and a much steeper trail. It is a separate outing, not an alternative trailhead for this visit.

What makes this river different?

The Yellow Dog River Community Forest protects 688 acres and public access to a series of cascades over exposed ancient rock. The preserve's management plan notes several major drops along this corridor; the main Yellow Dog Falls is among the best known. The geological field guide describes the river flowing through Archean crystalline rocks of the Compeau Creek Gneiss region. This geology helps explain the rocky ledges, the dark plunge pools and the broken series of drops rather than one broad, man-made ledge.

This is living forest as well as a waterfall. Watch for old hemlock, wet creekside moss and changing river sound as the channel narrows. Enjoy the viewpoints without following improvised tracks toward river cliffs or crossing onto private property farther downstream. River current and slippery bedrock can be dangerous, especially after heavy precipitation.

When is it worth visiting?

Spring runoff often brings a stronger display. Autumn brings hardwood color into the river corridor. Summer can be pleasant for a forest walk even when the cascades are quieter, while winter access requires independent road and trail condition checks. County Road 510 includes an unpaved stretch; weather and maintenance can change whether that approach is practical.

No live water-flow claim. The nearby USGS Yellow Dog River gauge 04043275 does not supply current discharge data: its record ended in 2016. A historical gauge reading is not today's waterfall flow and must not be presented as real-time intelligence.

Choose the trip that fits the day

If you want a pleasant but uneven forest walk to a chain of falls, begin at the Community Forest parking area. For a short developed path near Marquette, compare Wagner Falls or Fumee Falls. If you specifically want a harder river gorge, use the separate Pinnacle Falls guide instead of leaving the signed Yellow Dog trail.

Plan for adequate daylight, traction, water and the return trip. Do not assume mobile service or an emergency evacuation route from the riverbank.
