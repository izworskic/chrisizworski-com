Pinnacle Falls

Beyond the last signed two-track, the woods fall toward a river gorge. A steep footpath takes you down beneath a tall rock pinnacle to one of the Yellow Dog River's memorable waterfalls.

Public preserve hiking approach documented. Current opening and road conditions are unverified. Do not mistake this remote, rugged trail for a roadside stop or direct a vehicle to the waterfall map point.

Start at the actual trailhead

Land manager
Yellow Dog Watershed Preserve · Pinnacle Falls Preserve (conservation easement held by The Nature Conservancy)
Legal public access
Pinnacle Falls Preserve is a 280-acre Yellow Dog Watershed Preserve protected property; the owner lists hiking as an allowed use. Stay on the established preserve foot trail and respect preserve rules. No current same-day road or trail-open guarantee.
Parking approach
Small signed Pinnacle Falls Preserve parking area at the end of the remote access two-track from County Road AAA, reached via CR-550 and CR-510 from Marquette/Big Bay. The preserve owner publishes turn-by-turn guidance; do not drive to the waterfall coordinate. Road surface, winter clearance and two-track passability must be checked before driving.
Walking route
Approximately one mile each way from the preserve parking area, on a steep, rugged foot trail descending into the Yellow Dog River gorge; use the posted trail and its mid-route interpretive sign. Expect a strenuous climb back to the car.
Typical visit allowance
90–180 min after reaching the parking area. Not a measured route time.
For whom?
Confident walkers comfortable with a steep forest tread, a remote access track, and variable footing. Not a stroller, paved-boardwalk or wheelchair route.

The waterfall isn't the parking entrance

Michigan GIS point #155 · 46.706651, -87.806989. USGS hydrography places the feature roughly 11 meters away. Neither dataset identifies a legal driving entrance.

Follow the owner’s signed parking instructions

Finding your way without guessing

The owner’s arrival instructions begin on County Road 550 toward Big Bay, then County Road 510 and County Road AAA. The final approach leaves the paved road for a signed two-track. Follow the signs for Pinnacle Falls Preserve to its small parking area. Check the preserve’s directions before departure, especially if you are unfamiliar with the forest road network.

Do not confuse two Yellow Dog River outings. The nearby Yellow Dog River Community Forest has its own trailhead by the County Road 510 bridge. That is not the same departure point as the Pinnacle Falls Preserve parking approach. Choosing the wrong lot can turn a straightforward visit into a failed search for the trail.

Travel Marquette's waterfall driving brochure offers a second published arrival description. It helps confirm the forest-road sequence, but it does not establish current road maintenance, seasonal accessibility or private-property boundaries. Don't continue on unsignposted private spurs if the correct access is unclear.

Walk down into the gorge

The preserve describes an approximately one-mile trail from the designated parking area, steep and rugged, passing an interpretive sign on the way down. Plan for around two miles of walking out and back plus any time at viewpoints. The return climb matters more than the modest waterfall height: loose soil, roots and wet rock can demand care, particularly after rain or with early-season ice.

The river drops past a large exposed pinnacle and through a deep gorge. Look from established ground rather than climbing onto unsupported rock lips. Water depth, current and falling-rock hazards are not evaluated here, and recreational permissions are not a guarantee that swimming near the drop is safe.

What makes this place different?

On land cared for by the Yellow Dog Watershed Preserve, the water runs through exposed Michigamme Formation bedrock. The preserve's account describes a roughly 25–30-foot waterfall and a wooded pinnacle rising about 150 feet over the gorge. Long before today's hiking trail, timber crews built a dam and chute here in the 1880s to help move cut logs downstream during spring runoff. Remnants survive near the falls.

The preserve now covers 280 acres across both sides of the river, including a conservation easement held by The Nature Conservancy. Visitors can hike, fish and watch birds, but motorized recreation is not permitted on preserve lands and camping must remain more than 300 feet from waterways, trails and parking areas. These rules protect the very landscape that makes the hike worth taking.

When should you go?

Spring runoff often gives the water more force; autumn brings a different texture to the red pines, maple and exposed rock. Summer can have lower flow. Nothing here is a real-time stream gauge, and the upstream weather cannot tell us whether the trailhead two-track is open or passable on a particular day.

Choose another waterfall if you need a short, easy walk or are travelling in a low-clearance vehicle during wet or icy conditions. Wagner Falls and Fumee Falls are more developed choices, though their parking and seasonal status still need checking.
