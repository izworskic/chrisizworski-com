Falls River · L’Anse · Baraga County

Power House Falls

A short, broad Falls River cascade beside a historic concrete powerhouse, with a small riverside park and upstream trail. This is a carefully researched visitor guide, not a live road or river-condition report.

Public visitor approach documented; current opening and road conditions are unverified. The Village of L'Anse describes a small park beside Power House Falls, and Baraga County visitor bureau separately lists it as a waterfall destination. Independent field directions identify the signed Power Dam Road park spur. No verified current closure notice was found; do not assert open now or winter plowing.

Before leaving the car

Public arrival and parking
Travel from US-41 south of L'Anse onto Power Dam Road. The Baraga County tourism listing and independent field guide describe the signed Power House Falls spur off Power Dam Road leading to a small park with parking and pavilion. This is NOT the downtown Falls River Trail parking and not the GIS natural falls waypoint. Present maintenance, road clearance and park opening have not been verified live.
Walking route
From the small park at Power House Falls, view the cascade from established riverside ground. The Village of L'Anse describes a roughly half-mile upstream riverside trail; this is the general path length, not a signed promise of access to every other drop. Do not enter the old powerhouse, climb its walls, use industrial structures, or scramble down the falls.
How to decide
Choose this for a short, less strenuous Falls River stop separate from the downtown Middle and Lower Falls Trail. Stay on the park side and do not use the abandoned industrial building.
Current conditions
No current winter-plowing or side-road maintenance confirmation; verify access before driving. Riverside ice, dam ruins and abandoned concrete structures are unsafe.

How to find the park

Do not confuse Power House Falls with the downtown Falls River Trail. From US-41 south of L'Anse, take Power Dam Road and follow the signed spur near its end to the small falls-side park. Independent field accounts describe parking and a pavilion near the cascade. This is the visitor arrival that exists in published sources, rather than the 46.736793,-88.44433 natural waterfall point.

History beside the water

The Falls River once powered a local generating site. An old concrete building stands near the cascade, which explains the waterfall's name. Village visitor material still describes the surviving structure and a small park. The building is a relic, not an interpretive museum or safe room to enter. View it from outside and keep children away from damaged masonry.

How far to walk

This is primarily a short waterfall stop. The village also describes a path continuing upstream for about half a mile. That reference is not a promise of a cleared route to every upstream named feature, nor is it an invitation to cross a river, railway or industrial property. Trails may be slippery and unmaintained seasonally.

A useful geographic cross-check

The state Michigan waterfall GIS identifies natural Power House Falls as feature #88 near 46.736793,-88.44433. The USGS Falls River at Old Powerplant stream site is at about 46.737155,-88.444853, and American Whitewater describes Power House Falls near 46.73680,-88.44440. Those are closely aligned geographic references. None of them is a certified visitor parking coordinate.

Do not confuse with Power Dam Falls

Power Dam Falls upstream is associated with old dam remnants. This field guide covers the distinct Power House cascade beside the park, not a guaranteed route to upstream ruins or utility facilities. For a different public walking day, follow the signed downtown Falls River Trail for Middle and Lower Falls.

Natural falls, not visitor parking

This map shows the natural waterfall's geographic feature. It does NOT certify the visitor lot, walking trail, vehicle route, legal gate or current opening. Use the published source instructions above.
