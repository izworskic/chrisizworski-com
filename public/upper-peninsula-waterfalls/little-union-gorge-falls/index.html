Is Little Union Gorge Falls right for your day?

Best short walk for combining several modest river drops with interpretive copper-mine history. It also works when a longer wilderness waterfall hike does not fit your day.

The interpretive loop is the product here: the little gorge, early copper-mining story and nearby water are all part of a useful stop. Low water can diminish the waterfall's spectacle.

The route is generally shorter than the park's backcountry trails, but wet rock and trail conditions still change after rain. Park in designated areas and stay out of river channels.

Parking is a different location from the waterfall

Use the signed Union Mine Interpretive Trail parking/trailhead on the west side of South Boundary Road, south of the Wilderness Visitor Center. Do not mistake the nearby outpost campground for the official trailhead.

Parking waypoint: No independently surveyed parking coordinate is supplied for this guide. Use the signed visitor lot described by the responsible land manager; do not drive to the waterfall coordinates.

The map dot locates the geographic waterfall feature for comparison. It is not a road-routing target. Public access belongs to the signed route. An otherwise open scenic site can still have a temporarily closed road, unstable stairs or changed flow conditions.

Trail and what to expect on foot

Walk the signed 1-mile Union Mine Interpretive Trail loop. Interpretive signs cover the 1846 copper mine; the trail passes the Little Union River gorge and several cascades. Keep to the established path instead of walking in the creek.

Public one-mile interpretive loop maintained by Michigan DNR in Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park. Road access, seasonal conditions, and park permit requirements are separate checks.

Do not use waterfall height or a straight-line map distance to estimate walking effort. Follow the posted corridor and turn back rather than attempting an improvised shortcut through dense vegetation, river beds or inaccessible structures.

South Boundary Road winter vehicle closure

Winter road decision: Michigan DNR states South Boundary Road closes to vehicles at Headquarters Road south to the Pinkerton Trailhead from December 1 through late spring. The waterfall's normal visitor parking is on the affected park road system; do not plan an ordinary drive-up visit during that closure.

Check Michigan DNR's current park road bulletin before driving. A legal public hiking trail is not proof that its summer visitor parking can be reached by vehicle in winter.
