Little Union Gorge Falls

A short waterfall-and-copper-mining-history circuit through Little Union River Gorge.

Before you leave the car

Land manager
Michigan DNR · Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park
Public access
Public interpretive hiking trail within Michigan DNR Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park. Stay on the official path; do not enter historic mine workings or leave designated overlooks.
Where to park
Union Mine Interpretive Trailhead signed parking on the west side of South Boundary Road, approximately 1.7 miles south of M-107. Opposite the Union River Outpost vicinity; use the official park map and signs.
Walking approach
Walk the signed Union Mine Interpretive Trail, approximately a 1-mile loop. The route visits historic mining features and follows Little Union River cascades; steps and uneven ground occur, and the falls are an attraction along the loop rather than the parking point.
Typical time allowance
45–90 min. An editorial estimate, not a measured route time.
Best season
Spring runoff and rich fall colors; summer foliage shades the gorge.

Waterfall versus parking

Michigan GIS feature 117 is a waterfall-location estimate. Parking is described by the DNR map; no precise driving pin has been invented. Follow park signs, not a straight-line map route.

Check parking with the park authority

Which waterfall day does this fit?

Choose this for a roughly one-mile loop and interpretive history rather than a long, waterfall-only backcountry hike. Best combined with nearby Union Mine sights.

This is a series of scenic drops along Little Union Gorge, not a promise of a single large plunge. The trail also interprets the area's nineteenth-century mining history.

Start at the right trail

Begin at the official Union Mine lot and follow the loop's signed historical stops. Enjoy cascades from the maintained viewpoints; do not follow faint gorge-edge shortcuts. The mine workings are historical hazards, not public tunnels.

Walk the signed Union Mine Interpretive Trail, approximately a 1-mile loop. The route visits historic mining features and follows Little Union River cascades; steps and uneven ground occur, and the falls are an attraction along the loop rather than the parking point.

This trip is not a drive-up waterfall. Do not confuse the GIS marker with a trail junction, and avoid crossing unmaintained riverbanks to shorten the walk.

Park access and seasonal decisions

The loop crosses South Boundary Road and uses steps near the gorge. Expect wet roots/rock and check park-season vehicle road access before arrival.

Wet stairways and gorge edges can be hazardous; winter road access and park trail conditions are not monitored live by this page.

Waterfall appearance depends on rainfall, snowmelt and upstream water. The USGS/NHD waterfall inventory is geographic background data, not a live flow reading. An open park does not guarantee that its loop, parking area or trail is open today.
