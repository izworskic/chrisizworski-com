Duppy Falls

There is a quieter face to the western U.P.'s waterfalls, away from stairways and roadside viewing decks. At Duppy Falls the Jumbo River gathers speed over dark rock in a deep northwoods hollow. Reaching it requires knowing where the forest-road approach ends and accepting that the last stretch is an informal footpath, not a maintained visitor trail.

Forest Service visitor route documented; current opening and road conditions are unverified. Forest road signs and closure orders control access. The falls geographic pin is NOT a public parking destination.

Before leaving the car

Land manager
U.S. Forest Service, Ottawa National Forest — Kenton Ranger District.
Published road approach
The U.S. Forest Service's current Duppy Falls page directs visitors south from Kenton on Forest Service Highway 16 to Forest Road 3610. Turn right and travel approximately a quarter mile to an unmarked trail. The agency does not identify a measured legal parking lot or a GPS trailhead here. Stop only where vehicle access is permitted and the road is passable, without blocking gates or traffic.
Walk to the water
From the Forest Service's described unmarked trail, walk west toward the Jumbo River. At the river, the agency says to continue upstream to the falls. This path was created by anglers; it is not a signed, hardened nature trail. No exact length or maintained bridge status is promised by the agency. The Iron County chamber and local photography descriptions report longer informal approaches, so allow extra time, carry an offline map and be willing to turn around.
Viewing area and footing
Expect a set of low, distinct cascades among forested hills and an old quarry clearing. The water and rocks can be slippery even when the river seems shallow. A fisherman-worn path is no indication of a developed overlook or accessible trail, and you do not need to ford the river.
Season and road use
The Forest Service labels the recreation site open January through December, but that is a site designation, not evidence that FR 3610 is plowed, maintained or drivable today. The approach is particularly uncertain after storms, during spring breakup and in snow. Current opening and road conditions are unverified.

No measured parking-space GPS location or live route status is supplied. Review official road and forest alerts before departure.

Geographic reference, not driving directions

Michigan GIS feature #26 places the natural waterfall, not a legal turnout or surveyed trail. Current USFS and regional road descriptions disagree about some side-road numbers; obey the Forest Service's FR 3610 approach and posted signs.

A crucial fork in the directions

Several otherwise reputable regional visitor sources give different forest-road identifiers. The Forest Service's current Duppy Falls recreation page and its 2015/2019 official waterfall brochures consistently name FR 3610 off Forest Highway 16. Other publications refer to Forest Service Trail Road 3645 or USFS 168 near the approach. Those differences are not minor when you're in the woods: do not assume the road numbers are interchangeable or that a side track is open to cars. This guide uses FR 3610 as the land-manager route and treats the other labels as unverified alternatives.

The walk is part of the destination

After the turnoff, the Forest Service describes about a quarter mile on the road before the unmarked path begins. That figure belongs to the road approach, not a surveyed trail mileage to the falls. Follow the informal path west, then work upstream only where it is clearly public and safe. The forest is dense, and the bank may have fallen trees, muddy patches, slippery rocks or stream crossings. If you cannot confidently follow the same approach back, do not continue.

A waterfall without a developed viewing deck

Duppy's appeal is how the Jumbo River drops through multiple rock ledges among evergreens and hardwoods. The Forest Service notes an abandoned state quarry and describes the foot route as created by trout fishermen. There is no published universal-access viewpoint, handrail, signed loop or managed footbridge guarantee. Photograph from stable ground rather than entering moving water or following a dangerous slope for a lower-angle shot.

The difference between 'site open' and a usable road

The Forest Service recreation listing may say Site Open and show a January–December season. That is not a current road report. Forest roads can be temporarily closed, damaged or snow-covered, and a driver's map may include roads unavailable for public motor vehicles. Consult the forest's alerts and the current motor-vehicle use map; posted signs and closures override this guide. Even if the falls are open to hikers, the road or driving turnout may not be.

Good reasons to choose something else

This is a backcountry-oriented waterfall stop. It is unsuitable for travelers who need a paved lot, a guaranteed short accessible walk or precise GPS navigation to a signed platform. For those travelers, choose a separately verified public overlook such as Quiver Falls or a developed site such as Bond Falls instead of forcing a difficult forest route.
