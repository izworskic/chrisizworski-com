Superior Falls

Follow the Montreal River toward Lake Superior, where red sandstone, moving water and a working hydroelectric plant share a narrow gorge.

Before you go

Land and facility manager
Northern States Power Company (Xcel Energy), Superior Falls Hydroelectric Project P-2587
Public access
Public access is described by a regional access inventory and Wisconsin tourism guidance on the MICHIGAN side of the Montreal River. The land is adjoining an active hydroelectric station operated by Northern States Power / Xcel Energy. Follow the signed visitor paths only; no crossing to an assumed Wisconsin-bank access is needed.
Where to park
From US-2 in Wisconsin take Highway 122 north approximately 4.2 miles, cross the state line into Michigan, continue roughly 0.5 mile and turn left onto the signed gravel access road for the utility-side parking area. The mapped waterfall is not the parking entrance.
Walking and viewing
Signed upper and lower waterfall viewing paths from the utility-side public parking area. The lower gorge path is steep, rocky or uneven and may be dangerous when wet or icy; a shorter upper overlook is the safer alternative for some visitors. Wisconsin tourism describes 0.5–1 mile of visitor walking, depending on route.
Time to allow
45–100 min; an editorial planning allowance, not a measured trip duration
Best seasonal experience
Spring runoff and autumn color; regulated hydroelectric releases mean appearance varies and cannot be forecast from a seasonal label.

The waterfall is not the trailhead

Michigan state GIS feature 50 identifies the waterfall. The USGS Falls/Rapids V2 point agrees within approximately two meters. Neither map point is verified vehicle parking.

Read the signed road approach

Why Superior Falls is a different border waterfall

The Montreal River forms the dividing line between Michigan and Wisconsin here. Superior Falls lies downstream of Interstate Falls, close to the river's final run into Lake Superior. Unlike the town-owned Wisconsin trailhead for Interstate Falls, Superior Falls has a public visitor approach on the Michigan side, next to a hydroelectric project. Keeping the two destinations separate matters: they are not interchangeable parking stops and a single generic river pin will not get you to both.

The gorge drops through layered sandstone ledges, and the viewing experience depends on which signed path you choose. The upper overlook is the practical first choice. You may also see a path that descends toward the lower gorge; the regional public access inventory explicitly warns that this slope is steep and can be dangerous when wet or icy. Do not assume that reaching the upper overlook proves the lower route is safe.

Get to the public parking area, not the dam

From US-2 on the Wisconsin side, take Highway 122 north. Published tourism directions describe approximately 4.2 miles before crossing the state border into Michigan. Continue another half mile, then turn left onto the gravel road leading to a signed visitor parking area. Watch for the site signs and current access restrictions rather than relying on a routing app pointed at the waterfall's water coordinates.

The hydroelectric complex remains a working utility facility. The public access study identifies parking, benches and walking opportunities on the utility-side property, but this is not blanket permission to explore around plant buildings, dam works, fences or river-control structures. Stay on signed visitor paths. The upper and lower viewing paths are not certified wheelchair-accessible and the lower path should never be described as an easy stair-free stroll.

What the water may look like

Spring runoff can make this a dramatic stop and autumn hardwoods frame the sandstone gorge. But Superior Falls is a regulated hydropower reach. Its appearance can shift with operations and rainfall, and this guide does not have live discharge telemetry for the waterfall. Do not infer water volume, a release schedule, a visible rainbow or a safe river crossing from the USGS waterfall inventory.

For a first visit, build in enough time to read the signs, walk to a safe overlook and decide whether the lower route is suitable. A travel companion who avoids rough footing may reasonably choose only the upper view. In winter, ice can cover steep tread and guardrails; a previously published trail is not evidence of a snow-cleared, open route today.

Which western U.P. waterfall day should you choose?

Choose Superior Falls when you want a border-river gorge with a working-hydropower story and a signed parking approach. Choose Interstate Falls for a shorter woodland walk from the Wisconsin-side Town of Kimball conservation area. If you prefer a developed Michigan state scenic-site experience, compare Bond Falls and Agate Falls instead. Those alternatives have different managers, seasonal road access and trail constraints.

Never approach the powerhouse, dam structures or fenced/posted areas. The access inventory warns the lower trail is steep and dangerous when icy or wet. Road and trail opening are not verified in real time.

Five separate source checks

Reviewed October 27, 2026. Access and geographic evidence are separate. The listed sources establish a published visitor opportunity, not a real-time opening, a parcel boundary survey or guaranteed road passability.

land Manager
FERC identifies Northern States Power as the applicant and utility operator for Superior Falls Hydroelectric Project P-2587; county public access study describes Xcel-owned adjoining property.
public Access
Bayfield County regional public access inventory describes Michigan-side visitor access, a public parking area, benches and trails near Superior Falls; utility facilities and posted restrictions remain off limits.
parking
Travel Wisconsin publishes the Highway 122 approach, Michigan state-line crossing, signed left-turn gravel road and visitor parking area.
trail
Public access study lists upper/lower scenic paths and warns the lower gorge descent is steep and potentially dangerous when wet or icy; tourism listing describes rough 0.5–1 mile walking.
location
Michigan state geographic waterfall feature 50 locates the falls, distinct from public parking. USGS Falls/Rapids V2 name-matched point lies approximately 2 m away; neither supplies a legal route.

Scientific cross-reference: USGS Falls and Rapids V2 (NHDPlusV2 COMID 1812799, two-meter name-and-location match to Michigan GIS). These hydrographic identifiers are for research, not navigation or legal access.
