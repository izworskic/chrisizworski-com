Nonesuch Falls

A quieter Little Iron River waterfall walk paired with the ruins of the Nonesuch copper-mining settlement.

Published public visitor route; current opening and road conditions are unverified. Reviewed October 26, 2026. Check Michigan DNR closures and South Boundary Road conditions before traveling. The map marks the waterfall, never a driving destination.

Before you leave the car

Land manager
Michigan DNR · Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park
Public access
Michigan DNR state park public hiking land with a signed Nonesuch trail approach. Check park road gates and current trail notices; historic mine structures and shafts must not be entered.
Where to park
Signed Nonesuch parking along South Boundary Road in Porcupine Mountains Wilderness State Park. DNR waypoint: 46.759717, -89.619900. This is a parking reference, not the waterfall.
Walking approach
From the signed Nonesuch parking / North Country Trail area on South Boundary Road, follow the marked hiking route through the historic Nonesuch Mine corridor. A regional visitor guide describes roughly 1.25 miles one way; map-based trail distances vary with chosen route.
Typical time allowance
90–180 min. An editorial estimate, not a measured route time.
Best season
Spring snowmelt and autumn foliage; the waterfall may look different after dry weather.

Waterfall versus parking

Michigan GIS feature 108 is a waterfall-location estimate. The separate parking dot comes from Michigan DNR's printed GPS waypoints. Follow park signs, not a straight-line map route.

Check parking with the park authority

Which waterfall day does this fit?

Choose this over a roadside waterfall when you want a moderate woods hike with a mining-history stop; allow enough time to return by the same public path.

Michigan GIS locates the named falls at feature 108; coordinates disagree somewhat with some regional maps. Use the signed state park trail, never a straight line to the waterfall pin.

Start at the right trail

The Nonesuch Mine landscape is part of the experience, but open ruins are not an invitation to explore shafts or unstable buildings. Take photos from the safe visitor route and return by marked trails.

From the signed Nonesuch parking / North Country Trail area on South Boundary Road, follow the marked hiking route through the historic Nonesuch Mine corridor. A regional visitor guide describes roughly 1.25 miles one way; map-based trail distances vary with chosen route.

This trip is not a drive-up waterfall. Do not confuse the GIS marker with a trail junction, and avoid crossing unmaintained riverbanks to shorten the walk.

Park access and seasonal decisions

Keep to official park paths and out of abandoned mine structures. Trails and South Boundary Road can be seasonally inaccessible by vehicle.

Snow and ice can obscure paths and create steep, slippery river edges. No water-flow sensor or current trail-opening feed is available.

Waterfall appearance depends on rainfall, snowmelt and upstream water. The USGS/NHD waterfall inventory is geographic background data, not a live flow reading. An open park does not guarantee that its loop, parking area or trail is open today.
