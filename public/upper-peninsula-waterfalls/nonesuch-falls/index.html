Is Nonesuch Falls right for your day?

Choose Nonesuch for a mine-history walk with a waterfall reward, not for waterfall height. Leave extra time to read signs and explore the designated historic trail.

Nonesuch Falls is a modest, roughly 10-foot series of rock ledges rather than a broad plunge. This stop pairs nature with copper-mining history.

Do not enter abandoned mine openings or climb masonry. Watch for unstable rock, unbridged wet sections and seasonal closures on South Boundary Road.

Parking is a different location from the waterfall

Use the Nonesuch Mine Trail parking area off South Boundary Road, identified by the Michigan DNR as N46°45.583′ W89°37.194′ (WGS84). The trailhead parking is north of the waterfall and distinct from its GIS point.

Separate verified parking waypoint: Michigan DNR GPS 46.759717, -89.619900. Inspect the signed Nonesuch parking waypoint on a map. Check official entrance signs and current road openings.

The map dot locates the geographic waterfall feature for comparison. It is not a road-routing target. Public access belongs to the signed route. An otherwise open scenic site can still have a temporarily closed road, unstable stairs or changed flow conditions.

Trail and what to expect on foot

Follow the Nonesuch Mine historic walking trail past the old mine ruins toward the Little Iron River. Distances vary by route and viewpoint: allow a short woodland hike rather than assuming the falls are beside the parking area.

Public state-park historic trail reached from a designated South Boundary Road parking area. The trail passes fragile 19th-century mine remains: leave stones, artifacts, foundation walls and old workings undisturbed.

Do not use waterfall height or a straight-line map distance to estimate walking effort. Follow the posted corridor and turn back rather than attempting an improvised shortcut through dense vegetation, river beds or inaccessible structures.

South Boundary Road winter vehicle closure

Winter road decision: Michigan DNR states South Boundary Road closes to vehicles at Headquarters Road south to the Pinkerton Trailhead from December 1 through late spring. The waterfall's normal visitor parking is on the affected park road system; do not plan an ordinary drive-up visit during that closure.

Check Michigan DNR's current park road bulletin before driving. A legal public hiking trail is not proof that its summer visitor parking can be reached by vehicle in winter.
