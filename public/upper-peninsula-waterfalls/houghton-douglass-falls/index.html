Houghton-Douglass Falls

A towering Hammell Creek waterfall framed by volcanic rock; the public site is still being constructed. This is a carefully researched visitor guide, not a live road or river-condition report.

Public visitor approach documented; current opening and road conditions are unverified. DNR says the property is still under development and will officially open/dedicate once all phases are complete; Visit Keweenaw currently describes visitation after Phase 1. DNR cautions that beyond the bridge the trail is rugged with no barriers. Phase 2 overlooks and completed accessible route are scheduled for construction in 2027. Verify posted site-specific restrictions before attempting the undeveloped path.

Before leaving the car

Public arrival and parking
DNR Phase 1 built a dedicated parking area and access drive off M-26, 1.5 miles north of Lake Linden. Use the entrance signed for Houghton-Douglass Falls; the DNR site reference point 47.206425,-88.431575 is an area direction, not a surveyed parking space. Do not use the waterfall's natural GIS marker or historical lower private-land access.
Walking route
From the DNR's completed M-26 site entrance, use the Phase 1 accessible path to the pedestrian bridge over Hammell Creek. BEYOND THE BRIDGE the trail remains rugged and undeveloped, with no completed accessible fall overlook, safety barriers or observation platform. Do not treat a local visitor bureau's approximate 0.5-mile round trip as an ADA-certified trail.
How to decide
If you visit, choose the completed parking-to-bridge portion and assess whether continuing onto the undeveloped path is permitted and safe. This is not yet a completed ADA waterfall overlook.
Current conditions
The parking/bridge may be affected by construction, ice and weather. Phase 2 planned for 2027 is not a live schedule or opening guarantee.

What is actually finished

DNR completed the access road, parking, toilet, bridge and accessible route to the bridge in 2025. The pedestrian bridge crosses Hammell Creek; it does not mean the entire path to the waterfall has become accessible. The final accessible trail, viewing platforms, safety barriers and veterans memorial are planned for the next stage. A visit in 2026 should be judged by the infrastructure that exists, not the future rendering.

Why the two opening messages differ

The Keweenaw visitor bureau says the site is open for visitation following Phase 1. The Michigan DNR says it will officially open and be dedicated once the improvements are complete. These statements address different milestones. Treat the DNR's warnings and on-site closures as governing conditions; the visitor bureau's note should not be used to override work-area restrictions.

The risky last stretch

Past the pedestrian bridge, there is an undeveloped footpath in steep, natural terrain. The DNR explicitly says there are no completed platforms or safety barriers. Mud, cliff edges and slick volcanic rock make a waterfall visit different from a paved bridge walk. Do not promise wheelchair passage beyond the accessible Phase 1 section, and do not improvise an approach from lower private property.

The 2027 improvements

DNR lists Phase 2 construction for 2027, including new accessible viewing facilities. That is a construction target, not a verified opening date. No calendar rollover should cause the guide to automatically mark the site fully open. The next verified agency update must control any status change.
