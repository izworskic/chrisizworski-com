Peavy Falls (dam and historic ledges)

The name survives, but hydroelectric engineering changed what a visitor might see. Neither a nearby fishing area nor a power-station flow number guarantees a visible natural waterfall.

NOT CLEARED FOR AUTOMATED VISITOR NAVIGATION. Hydropower diverted the historic falls; plant flow is not direct falls flow. Raw GIS feature points are not parking lots.

What this location actually is

Michigan GIS #9 locates Peavy Falls on the Michigamme River at 45.990797,-88.209553. We Energies identifies a Peavy Falls hydroelectric plant built in 1943. The World Waterfall Database rates the natural feature unconfirmed, and explains that the original upper drop is diverted by the dam while water can reach a lower ledge through a spillway only when operations allow. This is specialist interpretation, not a verified present-day spill schedule or height survey.

What the official recreation evidence does and does not say

Dickinson County tourism describes a scenic driving/fishing trip to Peavy Falls Dam, and We Energies' Wilderness Shores system includes distinct public recreation sites in the region. Neither source certifies that visitors can park at the dam, enter the utility perimeter, view a natural falls ledge from a legal public overlook or walk from a public lot to the exact GIS falls point. Fishing access somewhere along Peavy Pond is not waterfall-specific visitor clearance.

Flow and current conditions

We Energies/Wisconsin Public Service publishes Peavy Falls headwater level, tailwater level and project 'Total Flow' with approximately ten-minute updates. Those numbers describe hydro project operations and do NOT establish spillway discharge over the former natural falls. In particular, a station 'Total Flow' of zero cannot automatically be called 'dry waterfall,' nor can positive flow be labeled water visibly plunging over an accessible falls. Research reviewed November 2, 2026; no field visit or live gate/trail inspection.
