Margeson Falls

Public land at the falls does not give you a public way to reach it.

PUBLIC ROUTE BLOCKED BY PRIVATE APPROACH. This source record is not a public parking destination, trail map or opening announcement.

What a knowledgeable local would tell you

Michigan DNR's property data identifies a 40-acre state forest parcel around the mapped waterfall. Historical visitor directions, however, describe an approach that begins on private property. A public parcel hidden behind private access is not a signed waterfall destination.

Before you even think about going

Do not attempt private-land approach. Research layer only. A natural waterfall coordinate is not a parking location, a public trail or an invitation to cross unverified land or gated roads.

Six things we have checked

Geographic identity and meaningful waterfall status
Michigan GIS feature #15 locates Margeson Falls at 46.220319,-88.246589 on Margeson Creek northeast of Crystal Falls. Historic Iron County visitor descriptions recognize the named remote cascade but do not establish a signed maintained waterfall trail or safe arrival. Read supporting evidence
Land ownership at the natural point (not visitor route)
Michigan DNR LOTS current state-owned parcel layer directly intersects the natural waterfall point, reporting state-managed Parcel ObjectID 108172, 40 acres, Forest Resources Division, Iron County (parcel description SE1/4 SE1/4; status active). USFS managed parcel and DNR conservation easement point layers have no intersect. This verifies state-managed ownership AT THE WATERFALL, not a public entrance or a lawful corridor through privately owned approach land. Read supporting evidence
Historic or tourism approach versus current public permission
An Iron County area visitor description reports roughly a one-mile approach that starts across private land. No legal public route from a road to the DNR parcel, permission for the private initial segment, signed trailhead, visitor parking or maintained trail could be verified. Do not instruct visitors to drive a private road or cross private parcels based on state ownership of the waterfall alone. Read supporting evidence
Legally signed road approach and waterfall parking
No present verified official signed waterfall visitor lot/turnout and continuous vehicle access to that arrival. Old gated routes, gravel-road shoulders, apartment parking and private entrances do not qualify. Read supporting evidence
Complete lawful walking trail and safe viewpoint
No USDA NFS published trail was found within 1km of the mapped feature. No current complete legal public foot corridor to the natural point was established. Read supporting evidence

What would change this recommendation

Use Iron County/Gogebic parcel/right-of-way and Michigan DNR maps to find any continuous state-land approach from public road without private crossings; verify signed legal parking and a safe footpath. If only a private approach exists, leave research-only until documented public easement/permission. No landowner outreach.
