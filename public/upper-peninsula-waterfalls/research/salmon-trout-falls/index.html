Salmon Trout River Falls: Access and Name Audit

Upper, Twin and Middle Falls lie on different Salmon Trout River branches or mainstem reaches. The Huron Mountain Club, private camps and Commercial Forest use restrictions make geographic pins unsuitable as tourist trailheads.

Research only: no verified public walking route or waterfall-specific parking. Never drive to a raw geographic falls point or infer access from a river or parcel name.

Three distinct waterfalls

Michigan DNR Fisheries Technical Report 88-7 identifies Upper Falls on the WEST Branch half a mile above the East/West junction (state GIS #149; GNIS #2098060 approximately 18m away). It places Middle Falls on the MAINSTEM below that junction (state #151; GNIS #2098058 about 177m away). Twin Falls is another feature on the EAST Branch (state #150; specialist field observation about 40m away), with a separate Hogback Falls farther downstream. They must not be merged into one destination.

Club gates and watershed rights

The DNR describes most of the lower watershed as privately held by the Huron Mountain Club. The club states its roads are gated and patrolled. A 2022 Yellow Dog Watershed monitoring plan documents private HMC access for a separate Lower Dam station and private JM Longyear access for a separate East Branch station. Scientific site access is not permission to hike to the waterfalls; exact current parcel title at each fall has not been independently checked.

Twin Falls and the Commercial Forest trap

A specialist field report updated in 2026 describes a difficult roughly nine-mile trip from a tiny Commercial Forest boundary near Dodge City, avoiding private camps. This is not an official tourist route. The Michigan DNR Commercial Forest Program states that foot access is reserved for hunting, fishing and trapping; other activities require owner permission. That does not automatically allow photography, sightseeing or driving into privately held property. Unofficial roadside parking at a gate, bushwhacking and crossing a nearby Club boundary remain unverified.

Decision: research-only

Each of the three features needs a current parcel owner and land manager, explicitly approved visitor purpose, full legal walking route, designated public arrival/parking, and recent trail/road conditions. Until independently established, no parking GPS, cross-property trail lines or visitor itinerary will be published. Read the state waterfall geographic source for identity only, or choose from established public waterfall guides.
