Warner Falls: a real roadside waterfall, but where can you safely stop?

Warner Falls is easy to recognize from older local tourism descriptions. What is less certain is whether a visitor can legally stop along M-35 and reach a safe viewpoint today. A separate 2026 highway project had been incorrectly connected to the falls; the source record now distinguishes those locations.

RESEARCH ONLY: NOT CLEARED FOR AUTOMATED VISITOR NAVIGATION. The waterfall location is not a parking destination. No present-day authorized roadside pull-off or continuous pedestrian bank access is verified.

One waterfall and two separate highway project descriptions

Warner Falls itself: Michigan GIS #172, Travel Marquette, Michigan's tourism listing and an independent field record identify Warner Falls on Warner Creek south of Palmer. These independent descriptions agree on the feature and its roadside context.

July 2026 MDOT project, north of Palmer: Michigan MDOT's July 1 release describes M-35 reconstruction over a branch of Warner Creek north of Palmer, with a planned mid-August finish. It does not identify the falls south of Palmer, a waterfall visitor turnout or changes to the falls' old trail and guardrail. A shared creek name is not evidence of a shared construction site.

August 2026 MDOT work, other south-of-Palmer culverts: A separate August 12 state notice identifies two culvert replacements near County Roads MT and MP. It does not explicitly locate work at Warner Falls or certify a waterfall turnout. Neither project notice is a present-day visitor parking authorization.

What the tourism guides do and do not establish

Travel Marquette and Michigan.org historically describe viewing the falls from M-35, stopping completely off the roadway past the guardrail, and using a short trail for a closer look. UP Travel also identifies the waterfall and roadside sign. These are useful, location-specific visitor descriptions, but they are not an MDOT site-specific determination of legal stopping, the present shoulder layout or permission to enter adjacent land. Do not use a historic roadside point as a car-navigation target.

The actual roadside-parking rule is more nuanced

Stopping beside M-35 is not categorically forbidden. MDOT says it generally permits parking beside state highways, subject to narrow exceptions. Michigan State Police explains how MDOT/MSP traffic-control orders can create local no-parking restrictions and posted signs. Michigan's vehicle code, MCL 257.672 and 257.674, separately prohibits parking in a traveled lane when an off-road option is possible, on bridges, and where official signs forbid it. These are general rules, not a measurement of usable space or sightlines at this particular curve.

There is no identified state roadside park here. A November 5, 2026 query against the official MDOT roadside-park GIS layer returned no named Warner Falls park or park location within 5 km of the mapped waterfall. That layer does not inventory every legal shoulder or separate scenic viewpoint. Likewise, MDOT's October 29 seasonal park closures cannot automatically be applied to an unnamed highway shoulder.

Land access is a separate question. A historical, third-party-compiled 2023 Marquette parcel polygon intersecting the falls had a residential land-use classification. That does not prove current ownership, a private-property prohibition, or an easement for waterfall visitors. The county's current GIS and assessing service is the proper source for an updated title and bank-access crosscheck; a complete 2026 right-of-way-to-viewpoint route has not been established here.

For a visitor today: Historical tourism directions describe a roadside view and a short trail, and Travel Marquette's 2025 waterfall brochure continues to describe an M-35 stopping approach. But neither that description nor statewide parking law verifies a safe, fully-off-road space at this curve today, any posted restriction, or rights along the steep creek bank. Never stop in a travel lane, on the bridge or where prohibited; avoid an unsafe shoulder and use an established public waterfall trail instead if stopping or walking access is uncertain. We do not supply a speculative parking GPS.

What would qualify this for the public explorer?

Official evidence about the actual M-35 shoulder width, safe sightlines, posted signs and any signed MDOT/MSP no-parking order, together with current parcel/manager proof of the complete creek-bank approach, would change the verdict. A bespoke MDOT-designated parking lot is not a prerequisite if a lawful, demonstrably safe off-road stop already exists. We do not ask visitors to collect the missing evidence; until it is resolved, this is geographically accurate research rather than a recommended visitor route.
