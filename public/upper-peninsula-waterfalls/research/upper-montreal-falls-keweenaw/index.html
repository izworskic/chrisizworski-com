Upper Montreal Falls

A separate higher-country chute upstream from the better-known river-mouth falls, with even less certainty about the last stretch of trail.

What the source evidence supports

Michigan GIS #140 Upper Falls at 47.397970,-87.836302 is 267 metres from a firsthand Upper Montreal site at 47.395630,-87.835510 and about 290 metres from a GNIS-derived Upper Falls record. GIS Lower Montreal #139 is ~705 metres away. A field account also describes a smaller farther-upstream cascade, so the generic 'Upper Falls' name does not itself identify which drop is meant.

Why Upper Montreal needs its own access proof

Visit Keweenaw's current tourism information supports an existing, difficult Lower Montreal approach from Fish Cove. A separate 2024 account attributes an alternative to DNR and The Nature Conservancy, but neither provides a surveyed lawful route from vehicle to the exact Upper Montreal GIS feature. Earlier field accounts describe a fading upstream track and a separate smaller cascade; the state GIS point and specialist site are about 267 metres apart. Do not extend a lower-falls trail or import Lower Montreal's parking point to create an invented Upper Montreal destination.

Existing access and two separate development projects

Fish Cove approach (published, not open-now verified): Visit Keweenaw's visitor guide names Mandan Road to Fish Cove for a difficult Montreal Falls hike; the detailed guide describes a rustic lot and an unmarked, unmaintained shoreline path to Lower Montreal. The 2026–27 brochure still recommends this route. Michigan DNR's 2026 project page links to that existing visitor advice. This is not a guarantee that a particular two-track or lot is open on the day of travel.

2024 DNR/TNC alternative (separate route): Regional reporting says an unnamed forest road off Hoar Lake Loop offered public access across DNR and Nature Conservancy lands, with vehicles stopped before the berm and an approximately 1.3-mile walk. This must not be spliced onto the Fish Cove lot description to invent a single parking point or trail line. Historic access is not a November 2026 gate report.

Two separate projects, only one with a completion announcement: KORC sought DNR permission for new west-bank parking and walking connections in 2026; its proposal is not established as built. Separately, Copper Harbor Trails Club now reports completion of the east-bank Montreal Corridor, while its detailed project page and Michigan DNR's construction description have not caught up. No reviewed operator map or trail-status notice confirms usable parking and a continuous public spur to either named waterfall. Do not mistake a completed long-distance corridor for an opened waterfall-specific trail. Smiths-Fisheries Road remains private.

Map and parking investigation: which mapped trail is actually usable?

Official DNR map versus finished route: Michigan DNR's July 22, 2026 Keweenaw Point Trail map marks the east-bank Montreal River Trail as RTPD Approved, not an as-built trail. The Copper Harbor Trails Club map portal advertises a summer map, but the linked PDF could not be independently retrieved in this source check. Neither supplies a confirmed current trailhead, lawful parking coordinates or an operator-published walk to the individual falls.

Beware the old Trailforks trailhead: The Trailforks Montreal Falls Trail entry maps a 1.9-mile historical route but explicitly marks the Smiths-Fisheries Road approach restricted by a private gate. Its displayed trailhead coordinate is not a public parking recommendation. A separate 23.3-mile Keweenaw Point route ends at Fish Cove; that route does not document the new east-bank waterfall spurs or a driving route to either waterfall. Third-party map data and older route descriptions may lag construction.

Visitor-ready evidence: Visit Keweenaw's published Fish Cove approach names a rustic lot and difficult, unmarked shoreline walk to Lower Montreal. It is a seasonal descriptive guide, not proof of today's open forest road or an authorized walk to the Upper cascade. Until the land manager/operator publishes a signed parking-to-falls route and seasonal restrictions, these geographic research pages provide no automated driving directions or unverified trail lines.

What the new Fish Cove and winter-road evidence does not establish

The August 2022 Visit Keweenaw report documents a constructed Fish Cove gravel parking area; the DNR September 2025 compartment review independently notes the Keweenaw ATV Club/DNR parking partnership and some Mandan Road easements. That materially strengthens the historic Lower Montreal arrival record. It does not prove that a signed, permitted path continues from Fish Cove to the correct Upper Montreal waterfall, whose specialist and state GIS positions remain different.

As of November 5, 2026: DNR's Hoar Lake BK ORV map specifies a seasonal ORV closure beginning November 1, not a blanket passenger-car road closure. The county road commission prohibits parking on county-road and highway shoulders between 2 a.m. and 8 a.m. from November 1 through April 15. Neither proves current Fish Cove lot availability, a legal walking extension to Upper Montreal, or an open and safe winter waterfall visit. The private Smiths-Fisheries Road remains unsuitable for default visitor routing.
