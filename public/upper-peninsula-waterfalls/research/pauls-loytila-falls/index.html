Paul's Falls and Löytilä Falls: one waterfall, two names?

RESEARCH ONLY — DO NOT NAVIGATE TO THIS WATERFALL FROM A MAP PIN. The natural cascade is documented, but no current land-manager-approved parking, all-segments public walking permission, signed trail or reliable motorized arrival has been verified. An isolated waterfall coordinate is not a visitor trailhead.

The misleading location label

UP Travel's Western U.P. waterfall article (February 2024) includes Paul's Falls in its Copper Harbor-oriented guide and describes an approximately six-mile round trip through overgrown logging routes, deer paths and wetland terrain. It also has a named directory entry. The general regional placement is not a verified parking location, foot track or precise county-level feature description.

Independent waterfall identity

Waterfalls of the Keweenaw's Sante River field report explains that the waterfall now presented as Löytilä Falls was previously listed on that site as Paul's Falls. It documents the local Finnish name and the history of the Paul's Falls nickname. This is stronger identity evidence than simply matching a river name.

The specialist identifies the fall as in Houghton County near Toivola, with a natural-feature waypoint 46.96483, -88.69343. That coordinate represents the waterfall itself, not a parking lot, road entrance, permitted trailhead or verified pedestrian viewpoint. Do not infer that Copper Harbor and Toivola refer to separate Paul’s Falls without further independent geographic evidence.

Why there is no approved visitor itinerary yet

  1. Feature identity: The specialist’s documented former-name history supports the Paul's / Löytilä synonym. The state GIS named list has no independently linked exact feature ID for this alias.
  2. Land authority: No parcel-by-parcel landowner permission or DNR/USFS-approved fall corridor found.
  3. Roads and parking: The specialist’s historic narrative relies on branching old logging roads, whose present-day legal driving season, surface and access have not been established. No owner-signed visitor lot.
  4. Full public walking path: No maintained trail, legal easement or checked final riverside approach established. A six-mile old travel account is not a surveyed safe route.
  5. Current conditions: No verified current bridge, road gate, water, wetland, snow or seasonal closure report; do not assume safe conditions.

Visitor decision

This is a geographic identity research entry, not a planned hiking stop. Until a lawful complete arrival and waterfall-viewing route can be documented from authoritative public sources, use the curated waterfall explorer for verified alternative outings. It will remain out of recommended trips, driving directions, the public destination map, nearby suggestions and the indexable waterfall sitemap.
