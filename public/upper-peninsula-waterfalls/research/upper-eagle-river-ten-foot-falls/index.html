Upper Eagle River Falls: the missing Ten-Foot Falls

There are two very different waterfall stops on the Eagle River. The upstream cascade is real, and local tourism promotes it. But a roadside parking reference does not answer who owns the bank, where it is safe to leave the highway, or whether the unmarked walk is permitted.

VISITOR ROUTE NOT FULLY VERIFIED

Tourism sources describe an unsigned M-26 gravel pull-off. MDOT's general policy does not ban most state-highway shoulder parking, but it is not a site-specific authorization to park at any exact spot or cross into adjoining private land. No car-navigation pin or riverbank route is supplied here.

What must be established before this becomes a visitor pin?

  1. Identify the actual waterfall and turnout separately. Resolve the 438 m discrepancy; a map dot does not establish parking.
  2. Verify the pull-off's specific legal status. Check MDOT right-of-way, any traffic control order, and whether a safe vehicle space is provided and currently usable.
  3. Verify public permission for the short walk. Establish the precise land parcel or easement from the roadway to an approved view, if the walk leaves public right-of-way.
  4. Confirm route and season limits. A short unmarked path is not a surveyed, maintained, ADA-accessible trail or an indication of winter plowing and safe footing.
  5. Confirm hazards before a public recommendation. Road curves, fast water and wet volcanic rock can make casual photographs unsafe.

These checks cannot be replaced by requiring visitors to upload photographs, self-report signs, or validate the path for us.

For an Eagle River waterfall stop with a confirmed public viewpoint

Use Lower Eagle River Falls at Eagle River Bridge Park. It has distinct official parking and a pedestrian bridge. Its own seasonal access still requires checking current conditions; it is not the upstream Ten-Foot Falls.
