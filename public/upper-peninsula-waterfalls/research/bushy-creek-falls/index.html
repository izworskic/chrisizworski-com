Bushy Creek Falls: Yellow Dog Access Review

The waterfall is a small creek-mouth drop downstream of the better-known Yellow Dog River cascades. Nearby protected forest is not proof of an open trail to this particular place.

Research only: no verified public walking route or waterfall-specific parking. Never drive to a raw geographic falls point or infer access from a river or parcel name.

The public waterfall route that is verified

The Yellow Dog Watershed Preserve owns a public 688-acre Community Forest with a CR510 bridge parking area and maintained route to its first Hills Falls. It describes roughly three-quarters of a mile to that first drop. This is not a documented trail to Bushy Creek mouth. The Preserve's adopted 2017 forest plan confirms approximately 0.6 mile of Bushy Creek and other small-tributary frontage within the forest, and its Fall/Winter 2023 newsletter (p12) describes a wildlife observation near Bushy Creek Falls in the Community Forest. Those official references strengthen the place association, but still do not supply an approved creek-mouth parking location or an uninterrupted signed public visitor walk.

A separate Bushy Creek property

YDWP also documents a 12-acre Bushy Creek property downstream of the creek confluence. Nothing in its visitor descriptions identifies the precise creek-mouth waterfall as within that tract, nor an uninterrupted trail across every bank. A natural GIS point is not a legal land-title or parking pin.

Why an informal downstream expedition is different

The specialist field account locates tiny Bushy Creek Falls near the Yellow Dog confluence. It says the path fades beyond the fourth Yellow Dog drop and discusses river wading, the north bank and a nearby cabin. That is not an approved public trail, and wading/slippery rock is unsafe to promote as a casual hike. A seasonal Bushy Creek Truck Trail is not automatically drivable or public parking.

What would qualify for a guide

Official landowner parcel evidence at the exact waterfall, approved safe dry footpath and shoreline viewing permission, designated visitor parking, and current road/river conditions. Until then, use the existing Yellow Dog Falls public visitor guide instead of attempting this downstream research point.
