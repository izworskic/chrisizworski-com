Mountain Stream Falls

An exceptional geologic setting behind a private-property boundary.

REPORTED PRIVATE LAND · DO NOT PLAN PUBLIC VISIT. This geographic record is not an approved parking destination, public walking route or live opening report.

What a knowledgeable local would tell you

The feature lies in the Huron Mountains between Mountain Lake and Pine Lake. The World Waterfall Database labels the waterfall Posted and closed to general access, while regional descriptions identify private Huron Mountain Club land. Michigan GIS and a separate USGS-derived hydro feature independently locate the same stream. A broad waterfall directory misplaces an alleged public hike at Sugarloaf Mountain far from this feature.

Before planning a trip

Do not attempt to hike to Mountain Stream Falls or adopt the inaccurate Sugarloaf/M-550 directions.

Six checks behind this decision

Geographic identity and waterfall character
Michigan GIS #127 names Mountain Stream; the independent USGS-derived hydro crosswalk agrees within about 47 metres. World Waterfall Database describes an approximately 25-foot lidar-indicated drop, not a surveyed public viewpoint. Read supporting evidence
Land ownership and visitor restriction
World Waterfall Database explicitly marks the feature Posted/private, closed to general public. A regional source assigns it to Huron Mountain Club lands; this is strong restrictive visitor evidence but is not a new 2026 parcel survey. Read supporting evidence
Competing route credibility
A generic roundup presents a Sugarloaf Mountain trail and unrelated Marquette coordinates; that is not a route into private Huron Mountain territory and conflicts with private-access sources. Read supporting evidence
Authorized public parking
There is no public parking/permission for this feature documented; do not offer mine/club/service roads, alternate coordinates or trailhead maps. Read supporting evidence
Continuous lawful foot route
No publicly authorized waterfall walking trail or vista. Posted/private restrictions defeat visitor publication even if the natural location is correct. Read supporting evidence
Current restrictions and seasonal status
No 2026 public access opening exists in reviewed evidence. Do not interpret general seasonal hiking conditions as a change in property access. Read supporting evidence

What would change this finding

Keep as restricted geographic research. Reconsider only if the relevant owner or land manager publishes a specific lawful public access corridor and clear visitor rules.
