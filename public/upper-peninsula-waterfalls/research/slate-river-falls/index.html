Slate River Falls: the approach still needs proof

The Slate River flows north through the Baraga County forest, turning over steep layers of slate before it tumbles into dark pools near Huron Bay. The waterfall is a real, compelling place. Reaching the right place legally and safely, though, is a different question from finding it on a map.

What must happen before a public visitor route can be offered?

The Nature Conservancy must verify the legal walking route and dry viewpoint; the Road Commission must verify parking and current road conditions; Equalization records must identify the bridge-to-reserve parcel and road right-of-way. No response is assumed.

What The Nature Conservancy protects

The Nature Conservancy's Slate River Forest covers approximately 9,760 acres. TNC says it bought the land in 2021 and protects roughly four miles of the Slate River, with gorges, waterfalls, hemlock and pine forests. Its published rules allow hiking, snowshoeing and photography on the reserve. Vehicles and bicycles, pets, camping and creating new trails are prohibited.

The verified boundary: On October 30, 2026, a live point-in-polygon check against TNC's own public ArcGIS fee-title feature 8370 placed the natural Slate River Falls GIS #119 inside Slate River Forest Reserve, with ownership class Fee Ownership and public-access category Restricted Access. The USGS-located Skanee Road bridge and independent NBI road bridge point are outside the TNC fee polygon. Diagnostic points on the eastern side of the public bridge are inside. The mapped boundary is generalized and is not a property deed, survey, designated parking place or walking easement. This establishes the owner of the natural waterfall, but not the legal transition from road to a safe public viewpoint.

TNC's preserve guidance allows hiking on its restricted-access land while prohibiting motorized vehicles, bicycles, pets and new trails. Its hunting land policy identifies restricted hunting rights on portions of the reserve; these are not blanket bans on ordinary foot travel. Even where public hiking is allowed, the roadside turnaround has not been approved for parking and a river-level view may demand a hazardous ford.

The bridge approach is not the Arvon quarry route

UPtravel and the Lake Superior Circle Tour describe a rough eastern-bank walking route near the Skanee Road bridge, using an informal two-track turnaround and a walk of roughly a half-mile. These sources describe past visitor practice, not a modern signed public trail with certified parking. A National Bridge Inventory-derived bridge listing reports the road bridge as open, but an open bridge is not evidence of legal shoulder parking or permission to use an adjacent logging two-track.

A 2025 Visit Keweenaw feature describes a small pull-off east of the bridge and a gorge-top hiking trail, but does not authorize parking or prove a safe river-level view. An older Baraga County visitor brochure uses the name Slate River Falls for an approach several miles down Arvon Road toward a slate quarry. The independently documented Quartzite Falls and Black Slate Falls are farther upstream near the Arvon approach. Their coordinates and names are not interchangeable with this lower-gorge Slate River Falls location.

The gorge is not a forgiving walking route

A 2006 independent field report says getting a close view involved crossing the river, and the author declined the crossing after heavy rain. Other guides describe wet rock climbs and steep banks. No presently documented non-fording, legally authorized viewing position has been verified. In this gorge, a faint line through the forest is not proof of a maintained public footpath, and a shallow-looking pool is not proof of safe passage.

What would clear the visitor-guide gate

  1. Road-to-owner transition: The falls themselves are confirmed on TNC fee land. Use the official Baraga County GIS parcel map and road-right-of-way records to identify the actual two-track and parking area beyond the public Skanee Road bridge; the generalized TNC polygon does not certify them.
  2. Get the applicable land manager's permission or published easement for the complete route and a no-ford viewing position.
  3. Obtain written or published approval from the Baraga County Road Commission and any adjoining owner for an actual parking place and current road status. Skanee Road flood damage was reported in May 2025; it is not evidence of current passability or closure.
  4. Confirm a current passable walking surface, stable bank viewpoint and seasonal/flow restrictions.

Until each check is supported, this location remains in the opt-in geography research layer, not the visitor itinerary or public XML waterfall sitemap.
