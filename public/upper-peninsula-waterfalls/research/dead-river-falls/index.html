Dead River Falls

Federal 2002 assessment distinguished an UPPCO McClure powerhouse lot then open to public from the upstream informal waterfall trail that entered private property. MBLP Forestville is a separate downstream hydro project. No current private-corridor permission has been established.

Research only. Full public route and parking are not proven.

What we know

FERC 2002 final environmental assessment pp.151-152: UPPCO P-10855 McClure powerhouse on Forestville Road had a gravel lot for about 10 cars then open to the public. The following paragraph explicitly says the 0.5-mile upstream informal path to the waterfalls EXTENDS INTO PRIVATE PROPERTY. McClure's P-10855 and municipal MBLP Forestville P-2589 are different hydropower projects. Recent travel descriptions of a powerhouse trail do not establish a present legal easement or 2026 parking rights. UPPCO publishes McClure hydropower flow and water level data approximately every 10 minutes, but this is NOT measured water flow over each waterfall and NOT a safety/closure signal.

Township trail listing versus current Michigan recreation rights

A January 24, 2022 Marquette Charter Township recreation-plan draft identifies the Dead River Falls Hiking Trail as an established trail on Longyear Realty land, with many of the surrounding properties enrolled in Michigan's Commercial Forest program. It describes general hiking as an allowed use. That is useful historical evidence that the trail is locally recognized, not a present-day landowner authorization or a final legal access determination.

Michigan DNR's current Commercial Forest public-access rules explicitly permit public foot access for hunting, fishing and trapping, but require landowner permission for other activities. Recreational waterfall hiking does not become a public right merely because a parcel appears in the Commercial Forest program. Nor has a current trail easement, complete parcel-by-parcel route, or signed current McClure visitor lot been established. The township draft and historic FERC document therefore do not together satisfy this project's five public visitor-route gates. This distinction does not assert that the site has been officially closed; it means that independent public visitation permission is not verified.

Why the local tourism organization stopped promoting this hike

A more recent local record strengthens the caution. In the February 19, 2025 Marquette Charter Township board packet, the enclosed January 29 Respect Marquette County meeting notes record that Travel Marquette stopped promoting Dead River Falls amid serious erosion and inadequate management of the recreation area. That is a documented tourism-management decision, not a public-land closure notice or permission to use the gorge trail. Together with the historic private-property route and unresolved owner authorization, it means this page must not recommend an access point, walking line, or safe outing.

What still needs verification

Current UPPCO/landowner public visitor access map, exact route easement across private banks, legal signed lot at McClure and current release/closure conditions. Do not use NTN Forestville trailhead, state boat launch or MBLP ownership as substitute.
