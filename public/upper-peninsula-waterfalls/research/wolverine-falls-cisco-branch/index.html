Wolverine Falls

Wolverine Falls is an authentic part of the remote Cisco Branch, but finding a mapped river feature is not the same as establishing a safe destination for a casual visitor.

NOT CLEARED FOR AUTOMATED VISITOR NAVIGATION. Natural-feature coordinates are not visitor parking, a public trail, or a verified safe approach.

What the source evidence establishes

Michigan GIS #28 records Wolverine Falls at 46.405577,-89.348732 without a river label. Michigan DNR's Ontonagon River assessment identifies the falls on the Cisco Branch, and the federal Wild and Scenic Rivers description independently recognizes Wolverine Falls as a notable feature on that branch. American Whitewater includes a 2026 first-hand report placing Wolverine farther upstream than the traditional marked position; a September 2025 solo paddling account describes a substantial Class III drop along the Cisco Branch. Exact contemporary drop-point alignment remains unresolved.

What stops this from being a public waterfall trip

The river is a federally designated Wild and Scenic corridor, but designation does not validate every bank parcel, forest spur, roadside stop, take-out or walking path. American Whitewater's Forest Road 6930 references describe expert paddling put-ins and take-outs, not a certified public waterfall hike. No manager-approved parking-to-viewpoint trail, safe river-bank lookout or fully verified right-of-way has been established.

What would change the verdict

Resolve the correct fall location against GPS-tracked and authoritative river maps; verify Ottawa National Forest/other land ownership, current MVUM road status, parking and a continuous public walking route to a safe viewpoint if one exists.
