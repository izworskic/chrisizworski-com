Au Train Falls: the waterfall is real; the public approach remains unverified

The Au Train River drops across a sweep of sloping rock near an old hydroelectric project. The cascades are not one simple trailhead-to-platform waterfall. Two approaches have appeared in visitor guides, one for upper falls and another for lower falls near utility works. That distinction matters after a major change in federal oversight and property ownership.

The new evidence: June 2026 emergency dam order

Michigan EGLE's June 2, 2026 Emergency Dam Safety Order, dam MI00152, describes significant embankment and levee deficiencies. EGLE concluded that the engineering findings presented a risk of failure in realistic loading conditions, not merely a theoretical maximum flood. The order required UP Hydro to continue drawing down the impoundment to a target 773.43 feet NAVD88 pending remediation, submit a proposed plan by July 2, and apply for a permit by August 31, 2026. These are requirements in a dated order, not a live reservoir gauge or evidence of actual completion.

As of this source review, I could not independently establish whether EGLE later amended or lifted the order, whether a remediation permit was granted, or whether work has begun. The published order does not itself designate the waterfall trails officially closed, but it is serious enough that tourist directions from before the ownership changes should never be presented as a verified visit. A high-hazard dam designation describes consequences if a dam fails; the separate 2026 emergency order addresses identified structural concerns.

What FERC actually decided

On December 29, 2025, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission's final order, Project P-10856-141, terminated the license of UP Hydro's Au Train Hydroelectric Project by implied surrender. The order took effect at the close of business that day. It was not merely a proposal or an unanswered notice, as some older reports still suggest.

The Commission documented outstanding spillway and dam safety problems, the operator's inability to meet directives, bankruptcy, and loss of control over parts of the project. FERC's final order was issued on December 29, 2025; EGLE's later owner letter states November 29, apparently inconsistent with the signed federal order, so the federal primary order controls the license date. The project was classified as having high hazard potential. Existing dam and powerhouse facilities were left in place, and Michigan EGLE became the regulator responsible for the site's dam-safety oversight. A revoked federal license did not demolish a dam, publish new public rights-of-way, or automatically close every forest path.

Who owns the facilities now?

The December 2025 FERC order documents a foreclosure of 18 of 22 former project parcels and subsequent sale to D. Charles Trust Investments, LLC, including the powerhouse and much of the surrounding project buffer. But EGLE's June 2026 letter separately confirms UP Hydro LLC as owner of the north dam, spillway and south levee parcels. These are different parts of a complicated hydropower project, not interchangeable claims that a single company controls every possible waterfall footpath.

Neither document maps a legally approved current visitor parking corridor from Power Dam Road to both the upper and lower cascades. A utility gate, old public recreation requirement under a terminated FERC license, or GIS river coordinate is not a standing permission to cross private property.

Michigan DNR's March 2026 recreation update

In its approved March 12, 2026 Eastern U.P. Citizens Advisory Council minutes, Michigan DNR reported that the power-generating license had ended, and a penstock was severed in January to maintain water flow. Crucially, it also reported that a former hydro-company campground and boat launch were no longer in use following the property sale and failure to obtain a renewed agreement with the new landowner.

Those campground and boat-launch sites should not automatically be treated as the historical upper or lower waterfall parking areas. The DNR minutes do not say that every waterfall approach was closed. They do establish that old recreation arrangements cannot be assumed to survive the 2025 land sale.

Upper and lower cascades have separate access questions

Munising's existing visitor information describes two Power Dam Road parking areas, one for the upper falls and another for the lower falls beyond a gate. This is historical tourism guidance, not owner-signed November 2026 permission for both lots and all connecting footpaths.

A firsthand June 2026 visitor review reports that the upper parking area was overgrown and signed No Trespassing. This is a useful caution, but a single visitor report is not an official closure order, a current posted-sign inspection, or proof that the lower falls route has the same status. Do not cross any posted restrictions or explore locked hydro facility roads.

The township's November 2026 notice about an Army Corps survey along Au Train Lake and the river mouth concerns downstream changes and possible future dam actions, not a surveyed open visitor trail to either falls.

FERC documents that the power station bypassed approximately 0.7 mile of river containing two waterfalls. Recent changes to the penstock, drawdown and control of water make historical photographs an unreliable prediction of today's cascade volume. Neither a basin-level reading nor the power plant's former operating flow certifies safe swimming or wading.

The state GIS waterfall #192 coordinate identifies the natural waterfall itself, not a public parking space. This research page intentionally supplies no driving pin, utility shortcut, unverified walking line or river crossing.

Five visitor access gates, with evidence states

  1. Waterfall identity — verified: Michigan GIS #192 identifies a natural waterfall; FERC describes two cascades in the bypassed river reach. This does not identify a parking point or full path.
  2. Parcel owner/permission — blocked: separate dam and powerhouse/land-buffer ownership is documented, but no current owner-approved public walking easement for either fall was found.
  3. Parking/gates — blocked: historic tourism descriptions are not present-day rights, and a June 2026 report warns of a posted upper-lot restriction. Do not assert the lower lot is either open or closed.
  4. EGLE dam-safety restrictions — unresolved: the June emergency order is official. No authoritative rescission, remediation completion, approved visitor corridor, or current safety buffer was independently established.
  5. Signed walking route and seasonal status — blocked: no checked full parking-to-viewpoint line, winter upkeep, utility-road authorization or verified open-now statement.

Visitor verdict: research-only, access not established. This is neither a claim that the two falls vanished nor an official declaration that every access path is closed. The correct next evidence is written landowner approval for a specific parking-and-trail corridor, combined with EGLE's latest dam order and on-site posted rules.

Source hierarchy

Emergency state order: EGLE, June 2, 2026: dam MI00152. Controls the documented dam-safety directives; its later resolution is unknown.

DNR recreation report: Approved March 2026 minutes. Reports discontinued campground/boat launch and penstock changes, not a waterfall-trail closure order.

Final federal order: FERC termination, Dec 29, 2025. This controls the hydropower-license status.

Legal explanation: January 2026 federal energy law summary describes the transition to state oversight.

Historic visitor routes: Munising visitor bureau describes the upper and lower access traditions, not 2026 owner permission.

Geographic falls record: Michigan waterfall GIS #192 maps a natural feature, not public vehicle access.

Posted restriction clue: June 2026 visitor report describes upper-lot No Trespassing, without official confirmation for the present date or lower falls.

Township notices: Au Train Township publishes the EGLE emergency order and notices a downstream river survey beginning November 2026, not a falls trailhead inspection.

State-dam contact from EGLE order: Michigan EGLE Dam Safety Unit, 989-619-4295. For an on-the-ground trip, ask the current landowner or township to identify the legally permitted visitor parking and path; an EGLE dam engineer regulates dam safety, not necessarily private visitor rights.

Current public visit verdict: Not cleared. Neither official full closure nor permitted arrival established. Last document check: November 3, 2026.
