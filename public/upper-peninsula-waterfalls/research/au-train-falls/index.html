Au Train Falls: the waterfall remains; its visitor access needs a fresh source

The Au Train River drops across a sweep of sloping rock near an old hydroelectric project. The cascades are not one simple trailhead-to-platform waterfall. Two approaches have appeared in visitor guides, one for upper falls and another for lower falls near utility works. That distinction matters after a major change in federal oversight and property ownership.

Research only: no verified current parking or walking route. The dam's former federal hydropower license has been terminated; this is NOT proof that every waterfall viewpoint is closed. It is also NOT permission to drive past a gate, use utility land or wade below the dam.

What FERC actually decided

On December 29, 2025, the Federal Energy Regulatory Commission's final order, Project P-10856-141, terminated the license of UP Hydro's Au Train Hydroelectric Project by implied surrender. The order took effect at the close of business that day. It was not merely a proposal or an unanswered notice, as some older reports still suggest.

The Commission documented outstanding spillway and dam safety problems, the operator's inability to meet directives, bankruptcy, and loss of control over parts of the project. The project was classified as having high hazard potential. Existing dam and powerhouse facilities were left in place, and Michigan EGLE became the regulator responsible for the site's dam-safety oversight. A revoked federal license did not demolish a dam, publish new public rights-of-way, or automatically close every forest path.

Who owns the project now?

The order describes a foreclosure affecting 18 of the 22 project parcels. Stephenson Bank acquired those parcels in May 2025 and later sold them to D. Charles Trust Investments, LLC, including the powerhouse and much of the surrounding project buffer. This establishes an important ownership change, but the order does not identify which present-day waterfall parking clearing or footpath lies on an authorized public parcel. Different upper and lower approaches may cross different title or gate boundaries.

Why old upper and lower falls directions are not enough

Munising's visitor information describes separate upper- and lower-falls parking areas via Power Dam Road, including a lower gravel walk near a gate. Those descriptions show how visitors historically approached the area. They are not a present-day approval from the property owner to use a utility road, park beside a power structure, or pass any closed or signed gate. Water movement below dam facilities may also change without this website receiving a live notification.

The state GIS waterfall #192 coordinate identifies the natural waterfall itself, not a public parking space. This research page intentionally supplies no driving pin, utility shortcut, unverified walking line or river crossing.

Five checks that must clear first

  1. Natural feature identity: verify which upper and lower cascade each visitor route leads toward, without assuming both share the GIS #192 access.
  2. Current owner and public rights: establish who currently controls each actual parking area and gravel trail, plus any recorded public easement.
  3. Current road and gate rules: official visitor permission at both historical Power Dam Road entrances.
  4. Dam-safety and viewing restrictions: current Michigan EGLE and owner notices affecting safe access, reservoir release or work areas.
  5. Seasonal access: current road closure, winter maintenance and posted trail condition information.

Until those questions have documented answers, Au Train remains in the opt-in research layer and is not advertised as a visitor itinerary.

Source hierarchy

Final federal order: FERC termination, Dec 29, 2025. This controls the hydropower-license status.

Legal explanation: January 2026 federal energy law summary describes the transition to state oversight.

Historic visitor routes: Munising visitor bureau describes the upper and lower access traditions, not 2026 owner permission.

Geographic falls record: Michigan waterfall GIS #192 maps a natural feature, not public vehicle access.

Current public visit verdict: Not cleared. Neither automatic closure nor permitted arrival established.

Where to go for a verified waterfall stop

Wagner Falls offers a short developed public approach near Munising. For a forest trail with a documented parking area, see Tibbets Falls. Follow the seasonal cautions in each guide.
