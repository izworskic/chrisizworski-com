Carp River Falls: The right side of the gorge.

Carp River Falls is not a drive-up waterfall and not the same as Morgan Falls. A newer maintained trail offers a documented overlook. The harder question is which route actually fits your group—and whether an old track deserves to be followed at all.

Read the river before you go

There are three separate parts to this day.

First comes a legitimate place to leave your car. Then comes the public trail network and its shared, sometimes narrow paths. The final choice is how you view the waterfall. A safe-sounding route description is not a permission slip to climb down a wet cliff.

01 / ARRIVE

The highway is not the trailhead

Travel Marquette directs visitors to NTN South Trailhead on County Road 553. It explicitly discourages parking across the road on the dirt shoulder because of speed and poor sightlines. From the main lot, use NTN’s signed path to the designated crossing. Keep dogs leashed and watch for riders.

Public approach authority

02 / WALK

Two generations of trail

Older waterfall directions describe a pedestrian Mount Marquette Road approach, Morgan Falls, and then approximately another half mile upstream toward Carp River Falls. The NTN completed the Carp River Falls Trail and Raging River connection in 2024, with a documented waterfall viewing opportunity. These are different descriptions from different years, and we will not merge them into an invented turn-by-turn line.

NTN director's 2024 trail account

03 / LOOK

Let the river do the work

The gorge concentrates water into a multi-part cascade through resistant rock. A managed lookout gives you a waterfall experience without the old scramble to the bottom. Do not cross Morgan Creek or venture over the edge simply because an old guide describes it. Snowmelt and rain add drama but also raise the consequences of a wrong step.

The name trap: two Carp River Falls, one Morgan Falls

The Michigan mapped waterfall near Ishpeming (GIS #163) is not the Marquette gorge waterfall (GIS #169). The latter carries a misleading “Morgan Creek” waterway label in the state geographic source, but matches Travel Marquette’s Carp River Falls location to roughly 18 metres. Morgan Falls (GIS #166) is a separate visitor experience on the creek. A waterfall’s coordinate tells you where water falls, not which road to drive, which bank is public, or whether a gorge descent is safe.

Important: The older Ishpeming Upper Carp River Falls approach has been reported posted No Trespassing near utility property. Do not substitute that destination for Marquette's NTN trails. The updated maintained lookout is supported by 2024 NTN reporting, but its exact signed 2026 link and current conditions must be checked on site or with NTN before a trip.

The five independent checks

Research completed November 1, 2026. Published evidence establishes a public NTN trail system, signed visitor parking and a described waterfall outing; it does not provide live 2026 trail opening, a surveyed complete track or safe gorge-base access.

1. Waterfall identity

Michigan GIS #169 (46.503807, -87.446550) agrees with Travel Marquette's Carp River Falls point (46.50367, -87.44668), while Morgan Falls and Ishpeming Upper Carp are distinct source identities.

2. Authorized trail network

NTN manages the nonmotorized South Trails from a documented public trailhead. The 2024 Carp River Falls Trail and Raging River connection was described by the NTN director, including a viewing location. Current signs and trail uses still govern.

NTN South Trails · 2024 trail completion account

3. Visitor parking and crossing

Travel Marquette identifies the NTN South lot on CR 553 and a safer designated crossing of the highway. Its older falls approach uses vehicle-restricted Mount Marquette Road. No copied or invented parking coordinate appears here.

Published NTN visitor arrival

4. Walking effort and viewpoint

The regional 2025 waterfall brochure rates Carp River Falls hard and describes the older continuation roughly half a mile beyond Morgan Falls. A new managed overlook is documented separately, but a surveyed route from the trailhead to that rail has not been verified and no mileage is asserted for it.

5. Current season and safeguards

NTN provides trail-use guidance and winter grooming, but a general trail network status is not a real-time Carp River Falls verdict. If the current signs conflict with an old online description, the current NTN rules control. Keep away from icy brink and fast water.
