Montreal Falls: the legal way in

At the end of the Keweenaw, a small river works its way through old volcanic rock before reaching Lake Superior. Montreal Falls rewards a careful backcountry outing, not a casual roadside stop. The choice of approach is part of the experience.

Challenging, unmaintained hike. Public Fish Cove approach is documented, but current two-track, parking, shoreline and trail conditions are not verified. Do not drive or walk Smith Fisheries Road: local landowners and the Michigan DNR explicitly identify that approach as trespassing. This is the Keweenaw Montreal River, not Interstate Falls on the Gogebic/Wisconsin border.

Is this waterfall right for your day?

Best fit
Experienced hikers comfortable with rough two-track roads, wet rocks, an unsigned shoreline footpath and limited cellular service. Not an accessible or family-stroller route.
Legal public arrival
From Copper Harbor, follow US-41 east until it becomes Mandan Road. Continue roughly 7.5 miles, then follow Hoar Lake/Fish Cove Road and signs toward Fish Cove. The Visit Keweenaw visitor guide describes a rustic Fish Cove parking area. The former logging tracks are rough; high-clearance vehicles are recommended, and seasonal vehicle access is not guaranteed. Use the signed road destination, never the waterfall map pin for car navigation.
From parking to waterfall
Walk south a short distance to the Lake Superior shore; take the westbound foot-worn shoreline path to Lower Montreal Falls. Visit Keweenaw estimates roughly one mile along shore. The route is not a maintained or signed trail and its precise start, tread and distance are not independently surveyed here.
Alternative public approach
In 2024 reporting about DNR/The Nature Conservancy access, an alternative approach was described via Hoar Lake Loop and an unnamed former forest road, with parking at the road and about 1.3 miles of walking to the river mouth. This is not the same parking lot or measured route as Fish Cove; do not combine the two descriptions or drive past berms. The route is not independently confirmed open today. See the contemporaneous local access report.
Weather and timing
Allow a generous half-day including slow dirt roads, unmaintained shoreline footing, stops and the return. This is an editorial buffer, not an official measured time. Summer and early fall are more realistic than the unverified winter road. Storm waves, spring runoff and wet bedrock can make parts of the route unsafe.

Waterfall location, not parking

What this pin means: an independent waterfall inventory places Lower Montreal Falls near 47.39257°N, 87.84114°W. This is an approximate natural-feature coordinate. It is not a legal visitor arrival, parking lot, trail junction or surveyed viewpoint.

Specialist natural-feature reference provides approximate falls geography but also retains obsolete Smith Fisheries access advice. Do not follow its driving directions. Follow Visit Keweenaw's legal approach instead.

The current landowner/trail improvement picture can change. November 6 DNR Keweenaw Heartlands acquisition update addresses regional public-land transitions, not a surveyed Montreal Falls trailhead.

What you will see: Lower and Upper Montreal Falls

Lower Montreal Falls is the primary public visitor objective, where the river approaches the Lake Superior shore. The terrain is layered bedrock and forest, and the falls can be beautiful after a spell of substantial rain or snowmelt. The shoreline approach is the only visitor itinerary documented in the published Visit Keweenaw directions.

Upper Montreal Falls is a distinct upstream drop described by specialist waterfall inventories. That source places it approximately at 47.39563°N, 87.83551°W, but it does not supply a current land-manager-approved, continuously marked viewpoint route from Fish Cove. Treat an upstream extension as an unverified backcountry option, not a promised part of the hike. No path or crossing is drawn to the upper feature.

Some independent waterfall directories separately list a Middle Montreal Falls. We have not independently confirmed its exact feature identity or a permitted trail to it. That name stays in research, not as a third public trail destination or mapped route. These Keweenaw falls must not be confused with the Wisconsin-border Montreal River near Interstate Falls.

Why the access story matters

For years, informal waterfall descriptions sent visitors down Smith Fisheries Road. Landowners closed this private approach to public travel; Visit Keweenaw and the DNR directed visitors to the Mandan Road/Fish Cove area instead. A trail improvement and future parking project advocated by the Keweenaw Outdoor Recreation Coalition had not secured final permits in public reporting during 2026. A proposed lot or trail is not open visitor infrastructure, and it should not appear as a ready-to-use map route.

By November 2026, state and conservation ownership in the Keweenaw Heartlands was also changing. State announcements discuss new DNR-managed acreage and policy changes, but do not settle the exact legal footprint, passport requirement or current condition of every Montreal Falls access segment. Confirm posted restrictions and your intended public approach; do not assume acquisition alone makes every former private two-track public.

Seasonal decision desk

Spring: River flow can be vigorous, but muddy two-tracks, standing water and slick lakeshore rock may make access worse than the waterfall is worth. Do not assume the road is passable.

Summer: Usually the most sensible season to investigate a dry-weather public approach. Prepare for mosquitoes and limited cell coverage. Flow may be light after drought.

Autumn: Color in the northern hardwoods can be beautiful, though rain, wind and falling temperatures increase shoreline exposure risk.

Winter: No maintained parking, trail grooming or road plowing is established by these sources. This guide does not recommend winter vehicle access or ice travel.
