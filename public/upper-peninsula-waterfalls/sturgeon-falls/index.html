Sturgeon River Gorge Wilderness · Ottawa National Forest

Sturgeon Falls

The Sturgeon River works its way through the quiet forests of the western Upper Peninsula before dropping into a basalt-walled gorge. Here the river narrows, gathers speed and falls into a dark pool beneath the trees. Reaching it means earning the descent and the climb back, not pulling off a highway for a quick photograph.

Public forest wilderness trail and parking documented; current opening and road conditions are unverified. No roads on NCTA's Sturgeon Gorge map are winter-plowed. A second same-name Michigan GIS location sits 6.74 km south of the documented visitor waterfall and must not be used to navigate.

Start at the real wilderness trailhead

Public arrival
The North Country Trail Association's April 2025 map, pages 9–10, marks the Sturgeon River Gorge Wilderness hiking parking area on the Forest Road 2270 access corridor. It is different from the NCT parking area on FR2200. Visit Keweenaw independently identifies a parking area along the FR2270 approach. Stay with current Forest Service signage and map; do not drive to the waterfall's latitude.
Conflicting forest road labels
The NCTA's older 2014 GPS reference table calls the Sturgeon Falls trailhead parking road FR2276, whereas the updated 2025 chapter description and visitor bureau refer to FR2270. These references are not silently treated as a verified modern road name or turn-by-turn route. Current official signage and Forest Service motor-vehicle-use map govern vehicle travel.
The walking route
From the documented wilderness trailhead, walk west/downhill along the established path. NCTA describes an unblazed but followable footpath maintained by Ottawa National Forest, dropping through steep switchbacks. Visit Keweenaw estimates about a mile each way, with a few hundred feet of descent and 2–3 hours for a trip. That is a visitor estimate, not a surveyed GPS trail length.
Don't mistake the spur mileage
The white-blazed half-mile spur on the NCTA map connects wilderness parking with the North Country Trail, not directly with the waterfall. Other NCT segment mileage, including the trail toward FR2200, is also not a Sturgeon Falls parking-to-falls distance.
Difficulty and river
The Forest Service describes a steep and primitive wilderness with few developed trails. Expect rough uneven surfaces, slippery exposed roots or rock after rain and a steep uphill return. River currents and ledges are dangerous, especially in spring runoff. Stay on stable ground; do not ford the river or climb wet cliff-edge rocks for an alternate angle.
Seasonal road decision
NCTA's 2025 map states that no roads on this map are plowed in winter. Its segment guidance also notes spring mud and washouts into early June. There is no live 2026 road clearance, trail closure, river flow or bridge condition report on this page. Confirm forest road permissions, closures and weather before committing to the long forest drive.

Why the gorge deserves the time

Water first broadens across the Sturgeon, then rushes into a narrow chute in the bedrock and drops below a high wooded slope. The quiet on the switchbacks gives way to the river long before you reach the bottom. The federal wilderness description recognizes deep cut valleys, old-growth-associated forest communities, oxbows and terraces. You are visiting a natural river system, not a built waterfall overlook.

This is a good trip for someone who wants a genuine backcountry stop and has enough daylight to travel slowly and turn around if the road, weather or trail changes. It is not a reliable last-minute winter detour, easy-access family boardwalk or swimming destination.

The location discrepancy we found

Three independent sources locate the visitor Sturgeon River Falls at about 46.6424° N, 88.6935° W: NCTA 2014 historical GPS data, Visit Keweenaw and a separate geographic field guide. They broadly agree. But Michigan's source GIS #45 places another named Sturgeon Falls marker at 46.582163° N, 88.684120° W, approximately 6.74 km away. That mismatch is not a hiking distance, and it is not proof that one point is a mistake rather than a second named river feature. The source GIS point is intentionally kept in research-only review; this guide's map uses the corroborated visitor waterfall location, not the disputed state one.

Five independent source checks

Manager
Ottawa National Forest's federal wilderness listing identifies Sturgeon Falls in the designated Sturgeon River Gorge.
Legal public visitor approach
NCTA's 2025 segment and wilderness trail source identifies the public walking area and Ottawa National Forest maintained path. It does not establish today's open condition.
Public parking
NCTA 2025 marks the wilderness visitor lot separately from the FR2200 NCT parking, while its older GPS table uses different road naming.
Trail and effort
The local visitor bureau gives the approximate mile-long descent, steep switchbacks and 2–3-hour outing; the 2025 trail guide identifies the different half-mile NCT connector.
Natural waterfall identity
Historical NCTA points and Visit Keweenaw agree near 46.6424, but Michigan GIS #45 lies ~6.74 km south and remains unlinked.
