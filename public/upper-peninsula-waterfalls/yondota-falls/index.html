Yondota Falls

A few miles beyond Marenisco, the Presque Isle River slips into a sheltered gorge where its current suddenly gathers strength. The trail down from County Road 523 is short, but it leads into a world of cedar shade, rounded boulders and the continuous murmur of water. Instead of one tremendous drop, Yondota offers a chain of rapids, pools and a compact plunge. It is the sort of place that rewards a slower walk, stopping safely along the trail to see how the river changes from one bend to the next.

Federal trail and north-of-bridge trailhead established; current road and parking conditions unverified. The waterfall map point is not a driving destination, and the path climbs on the return.

The quiet Presque Isle gorge

Dark tea-colored water gathers between rock walls, turns white at a narrow plunge, then flows over smaller cascades beneath tall cedars and hemlocks. In spring, the dark river gathers speed through the rock constriction; at lower flow, the channel's shelves and side rapids become more visible. The U.S. Forest Service calls this a series of falls, and taking time to see the successive drops is part of the appeal.

Named trailhead and visitor parking
From US-2 near Marenisco, take County Road 523 north about 3.25 miles to the bridge over the Presque Isle River. Federal recreation directions identify the Yondota trailhead on the north side of the bridge. Independent field reports describe a small west-side turnout beyond the crossing. Follow current signs and park only in a lawful off-road space; do not block the bridge or road. Neither the natural waterfall marker nor an informal shoulder is a parking destination. Road condition and winter clearing are not currently verified.
Walking route and effort
Follow the short river trail from the north-of-bridge trailhead downstream toward the falls. The U.S. Forest Service describes a trail descending beside a series of cascades, with a noticeably uphill return. Independent field visits estimate roughly a quarter mile one way to the main drop. The path is rustic, with uneven ground and wet rock nearby; stay on existing tread and do not wade or scramble along the gorge edge.
When to allow extra time
The trail is short but not flat. Allow 35–90 minutes with photographs and the uphill return. Neither the route nor the river-edge rocks are an accessible boardwalk. Spring runoff, wet leaves and ice make the walk more demanding.
Public land and management
Recreation.gov's official federal facility page identifies Yondota Falls in the Bessemer Ranger District, Ottawa National Forest. The current local forest office is the authority for restrictions, access and any current trail change.
Season and road access
No live October 31, 2026 road or trail opening confirmed. County Road 523 can become a rough forest road and the trailhead may not be plowed or usable in winter.

How to enjoy the river safely

It is tempting to follow the rapids right to the water's lip, but a view from firm ground is enough. Keep to the established trail and banks where footing is secure. Avoid river crossings and steep wet bedrock; the same deep amber current that gives the falls their character can move powerfully through the narrow rock gap.

Where the river goes next

The Presque Isle River continues north through forest and wetlands toward the Porcupine Mountains, where its more famous final series of waterfalls lies closer to Lake Superior. Federal Wild and Scenic River documentation recognizes the river corridor for its geology, wildlife and changing character. At Yondota, that broader wilderness story is visible in miniature.

Before leaving US-2

Consult Ottawa National Forest manager notices and the federal trailhead description. Road surface and closure status can change; the historical 3.25-mile approach is not a real-time opening report. Do not park on the bridge or roadside if no designated turnout is safely available.

Natural waterfall, not vehicle parking

State waterfall #30 is 67 metres from an independent waterfall location. The trailhead is on the north side of the County Road 523 bridge, not at this point.
