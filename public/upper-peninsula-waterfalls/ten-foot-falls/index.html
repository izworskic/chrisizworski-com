Ten Foot Falls: the small Eagle River stop most people drive past

Just west of Phoenix, the Eagle River pushes across a small chain of volcanic-rock ledges. In spring the river widens into a lively cascade; in winter it can shrink to a trickle. This is not the larger Eagle River Falls down the road.

Know which Eagle River waterfall you are visiting

Visit Keweenaw directs travelers from the US-41/M-26 junction at Phoenix along M-26 toward Eagle River for roughly 0.8 mile. Its driving tour describes an unsigned gravel pull-off on the right and the upper Eagle River cascade nearby. The 2023 regional waterfall brochure independently names the same stop, but gives a more approximate location than the specialist falls record.

No driving GPS is supplied. The natural-falls coordinate is not a certified stopping location, and a historic description is not a current road-safety inspection. Only use a visibly lawful, clear off-lane turnout. Treat the nearby flat bank descriptions as historical observations, not approval to cross uncertain land boundaries or scramble wet rocks.

For a reliably designated nearby viewpoint, continue to the separately documented Lower Eagle River Falls pedestrian bridge in the village.

What makes this stop worth finding?

There are two very different Eagle River waterfall experiences. The dramatic Lower Falls in Eagle River village has an established pedestrian bridge. The upper, sometimes called Ten Foot Falls, is a humble blocky drop and stretch of rapids along M-26 near Phoenix. It is more a moment to listen to a spring river than a destination for a long hike.

In normal summer low water the cascade can be slight. The travel bureau's spring tour and older field records agree on the feature, but they do not establish that the historical gravel area is suitable for stopping on a particular day. If it is not clearly safe, skip it and use the public bridge downstream.
