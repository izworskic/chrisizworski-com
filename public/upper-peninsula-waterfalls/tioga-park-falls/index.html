Tioga River · MDOT public park · US-41/M-28 · Baraga County

Tioga Creek Roadside Park Falls

Just off the highway, the Tioga River slips through a strip of grass and spruce, making several small drops among dark river stones. The official roadside park provides a quiet place to stop and hear the water without committing to a difficult wilderness trip. These are the modest park-side cascades, not the larger Tioga Falls several kilometres away near a reported private gate.

Public MDOT park and river access verified; current gate and short creek trail conditions not verified. In late October, roadside parks can close for winter. This is a park-side view only, not authorization to travel to the private-gated natural GIS #46 waterfall.

The waterfall stop that actually has public parking

The roadside park's location and public ownership are independently documented. Michigan DOT calls it Tioga Creek Roadside Park, and its official 2026 notice names it among Baraga County facilities delayed in reopening by lingering spring snow. This confirms a managed public facility, not live opening status today. A Michigan DEQ sampling station names the Tioga River at US-41's state roadside park, independently confirming the watercourse and park meet.

Arrival by road
Arrive at the officially named Tioga Creek Roadside Park on the SOUTH side of US-41/M-28 in Baraga County, west of Three Lakes/Nestoria. Michigan DOT lists Tioga Creek Roadside Park in its 2026 public facilities update, while an independent roadside facility catalog locates the parking/picnic site near 46.57515,-88.33961. That is the PARK location, not the larger, gated Tioga Falls identified by Michigan GIS #46 over two kilometres away. Use the marked park entrance and actual visitor spaces, never roadside shoulder parking.
Public on-foot experience
Within Tioga Creek Roadside Park, start from the established parking and open grass/picnic area by the river and small footbridge. An independent 2015 waterfall field guide records lower cascades visible near the park parking area and a short marked upstream creek trail a few hundred feet to another modest drop. A later independent visitor describes a paved park path becoming a rugged, possibly overgrown footpath with fallen trees. Enjoy the bridge and lower public park-side river views if the upstream side path is blocked; do not bushwhack beyond the park, wade the Tioga River, or seek the unrelated private-gated downstream falls.
Walking effort
Allow 15–45 minutes for the park footbridge, nearby small cascades and time to examine the river. The lower drop can be observed from the park-side river area. A short uphill creek trail may be rough or obstructed; don't promise a precise 0.4-mile maintained loop or a dry cleared path today.
Who operates it?
Michigan Department of Transportation operates public roadside parks, with a 2026 notice explicitly naming this one. This is a seasonal facility, not a permanently open state campground. A park gate, picnic tables or vault toilet shown on a historic map are not proof they are operating today.
When to visit
Spring runoff can give the little cascades more volume; fall color makes the riverbank attractive. MDOT says some parks close in late October; the late-October 2026 gate status has not been verified. Do not stop along the highway or cross a closed gate if the park is shut.

Follow the park, not a stream GPS

From the park parking area, follow the open grassy picnic area to the river's short footbridge and paved path. An independent waterfall observer recorded the lower rapids visible near the parking area and a marked path upstream a few hundred feet to a second small drop. Another photographer later found that trail narrowed and had fallen trees over it. These are field notes, not an active maintained-trail status feed. If the creek path is blocked, enjoy the river from the approved park-side area and turn around.

Two places that happen to share a Tioga Falls name

This is an important geography correction. The Michigan named waterfall database puts Tioga Falls at roughly 46.593805,-88.348735, about 2.2 kilometres north of the roadside park. A field specialist reports a private camp and gate at that farther falls. The roadside park cascades documented near 46.5734,-88.3398 are distinct. **The two should not be joined into one tourist walking route**, nor should directions send you toward the gated upstream site. That is why this guide gives you the designated roadside-park entrance, not a straight-line walk from a waterfall coordinate.

A quiet stretch of the Tioga River

The drops are low and their shapes depend on the season. When the flow is up, water moves noisily over shelves; in drier weather the smaller side channels show dark polished rock. From the public park's grassy opening, the river brings cool air beneath the evergreens, and the roadway begins to recede. It is a good stop for a road trip precisely because it is modest: a river, a few minutes to wander, and a reason to pay attention to this country beyond the windshield.

Plan for the real conditions

Consult MDOT's seasonal notices and roadside park map. If the entrance is closed, do not attempt roadside shoulder parking on a fast highway. Stay within managed public park boundaries, do not wade a swollen river, and do not pursue the private upstream falls along a two-track. Restroom availability, short-path clearing and winter maintenance are not live monitored on this page.

Five checks behind this stop

Land authority
MDOT 2026 official notice lists Tioga Creek Roadside Park as an MDOT managed seasonal site.
Public visitor access
MDOT public roadside parks offer lawful highway-rest visitor areas; this does not apply to downstream private Tioga Falls.
Public parking
Independent roadside park location identifies the MDOT facility and signed highway entrance, not a GIS waterfall or confirmed individual lot space.
River walk and cascade
Independent field observation describes small falls within the public park and short creek path; later reporting warns of overgrowth and obstacles.
Independent geographic check
Michigan DEQ Tioga River sampling at US-41 roadside park confirms the specific stream. GIS Tioga Falls #46 is a different upstream feature with separate access restrictions.

Check seasonal gates. The page does not promise the park is open on October 31 or provide a route beyond the MDOT park property.
