A Newspaper, Not Another Dashboard

The live maps and weather feeds already exist. The Gazette's job is to turn the morning's scattered information into an edition worth reading: one lead, short harbor notes, a levels ledger, weather on the water, and a concrete item to watch tomorrow.

Each issue gets its own permanent, indexable URL and stays in the archive. That makes the Gazette useful both as today's two-minute read and as a dated record of the Great Lakes navigation season.

How the Morning Desk Is Sourced

The edition is assembled automatically from current source data, then checked for factual grounding, stale movements, and repeat leads before publication. Port reports and vessel news older than 48 hours are excluded from the current-news context.

Vessels and ports

BoatNerd passage data and port reports at seven Great Lakes chokepoints. Public AIS can be delayed or incomplete and is never a navigation source.

Water levels

NOAA CO-OPS observations for Superior, Michigan, Huron, Erie, and Ontario.

Marine weather

National Weather Service Great Lakes forecasts, including open-lake wind, wave, and warning context.

Lake reporting

Current Great Lakes environmental reporting and a daily history note, clearly separated from live vessel movements.

Published by Chris Izworski from Bay City on Saginaw Bay. Freighters on the bay and Saginaw River are part of daily life here and the reason the garden project is named Freighter View Farms. The Gazette connects that firsthand view with current maritime data and a permanent daily record.
