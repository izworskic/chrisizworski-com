Why a lake average is not a beach temperature

Lake Michigan is long, deep and shaped by changes in wind and seasonal heating. Its average includes water far offshore as well as near the coast. A sandy shoreline, a sheltered bay and a deep offshore buoy can report very different temperatures on the same day.

ShoreFeel separates measured buoy temperatures from satellite-derived lakewide averages and forecast shoreline weather. If no trustworthy nearby observation exists, the local temperature remains unknown rather than inheriting a convenient number.

How we think about comfort

Beach comfort depends on air, sunshine, cloud cover and wind. Water immersion depends on actual local water temperatures and time in the lake. Wet skin can cool rapidly afterward. These are three experiences, not one universal “feels-like” temperature.
