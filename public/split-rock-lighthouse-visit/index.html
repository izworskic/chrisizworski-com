    MNHS / the buildings

    Walk into a working-era lighthouse.

    General historic-site admission covers the lighthouse, keeper's home, and visitor center, subject to site programming. You're stepping into a 1910 landmark built above one of Lake Superior's most exposed shorelines.

    Not included in a park permit: lighthouse building admission.

    MNHS / the grounds

    See the station from outside.

    A 2026 grounds pass was listed at $8. It lets you experience the historic site's setting without entering the lighthouse or keeper's home. That distinction matters if you come specifically for the historic tower interior.

    Not included: interior lighthouse access.

    DNR / the shoreline

    Look up at the cliff from the lake.

    The famous silhouette makes more visual sense from below: the tower sits high above the rock face. State park access and vehicle permits are managed by Minnesota DNR; that vehicle permit alone does not admit you to the historic buildings.

    Before arriving: verify state park vehicle permit requirements.

    The November 10 ceremony

    29 names. One beacon.

    The Edmund Fitzgerald memorial is not an ordinary afternoon tour. It commemorates the 29 mariners lost in 1975, as well as the Great Lakes' wider maritime history. The lighthouse's beacon is lit as part of the annual observance.

    On the ground

    The practical details that matter.

    Parking: Historic-site visitors park free during operating hours. Outside historic-site hours, MNHS says a state park vehicle permit is required. The two arrangements are not interchangeable.

    Accessibility: There is a paved path from the historic-site lot to the visitor center and marked accessible parking. Don't assume all lighthouse interiors or nearby shoreline trails are step-free.

    Pets: Service animals are permitted at the historic site; pets are not. Drones are prohibited. Current construction may change some programming.
