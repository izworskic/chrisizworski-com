Watch for

Soaring and circling

One eagle holds its broad wings almost level, circles upward, or glides for long stretches without flapping.

What it may mean

Usually travel, hunting, or riding rising air. Soaring by itself is not courtship.

Where to watch

Scan high above rivers, lakes and ridgelines.

Don't assume

A bird circling overhead does not prove a nest is nearby.

Where does the aerial show happen?

The sky is the stage—not one marked spot.

Look across open sky from a lawful public riverfront, wetland overlook or scenic bluff. Territorial and pair flights may occur over broad eagle habitat; none of our map pins is a verified cartwheel venue. We do not map nest or roost locations.

To watch aerial displays

Look for eagles interacting overhead during the local breeding-season window. Watch the treeline and wide sky from a public vantage, without approaching nests.

To watch winter gatherings

Watch open water and feeding corridors from established public viewing areas. Wisconsin DNR suggests early morning or the last hours before dusk for winter feeding and roost movements.

Why the cartwheel explanation is cautious

Cornell describes talon-locked descents as a courtship display; the Smithsonian emphasizes the same behavior as territorial fighting. Audubon and field biologists recognize more than one context. Upper Peninsula · Evidence worth knowing

The UP has real winter eagle concentrations

Your eastern UP route can start with the St. Marys River public shoreline at Rotary Park, Soo Locks Canal Park, or the Lake Superior State University Freshwater Center during its limited open hours. Farther west, explore the Escanaba River North Shore piers or Menominee River public overlooks. Seney is verified eagle habitat, but not a confirmed winter flock location.

A documented feeding aggregation: Michigan Sea Grant reported more than 45 eagles at once near the Dafter Landfill in its January 2023 report, and upwards of 30 in a January 2026 winter report. These are dated historical observations—not live counts or predictions.

Why it matters: eagles can concentrate at food sources such as carrion as well as fish in open river channels. A river-ice model alone would miss this behavior.

Important: not an unrestricted viewing location

The landfill requires operator permission and check-in; access to its active disposal area is prohibited. Do not enter, follow eagle flights to roosts, or expect access based on an old report. For a public-oriented trip, start with the St. Marys River or a managed recreation area.

Field interpretation: The 45+ eagles reported near Dafter were a food-driven aggregation at a restricted facility. It proves winter gatherings need not be tied to freezing rivers. Our public viewing pins are places to observe, not daily predictions or invitations onto private or industrial property. 07 / Read the conditions

A better winter eagle outing

01 · Follow the squeeze

Moving water matters

When still water freezes, eagles may hunt near open river reaches, dams and confluences. A cold forecast alone cannot identify those openings.

02 · Time your visit

Morning and late day

Wisconsin DNR recommends early morning, roughly 8–11 a.m., and the period before dusk. Conditions and feeding activity vary.

03 · Keep your distance

Let wild birds stay wild

Watch from designated public overlooks or your parked vehicle. Never bait eagles, approach roosts or walk onto river ice; leave if birds react to you.

08 / Field questions

Before you set out

When is the best season for winter eagle gatherings?

Many Midwest sites have a winter gathering window from mid-December through early March. River ice, flow and weather can shift the timing. Resident eagles can be visible all year.

Can this page tell me if eagles are there right now?

No. It shows public viewing options, live NWS weather and historical dated surveys at supported locations. An unreported day or missing count cannot be interpreted as zero or as a sighting.

Is open river water guaranteed below every dam?

No. Tailwaters often offer feeding habitat in winter, but hydraulic conditions, ice and access are site-dependent. Check the facility operator before travel.

Why does a bald eagle look brown rather than white-headed?

Young bald eagles can be mottled brown with irregular white patches. The familiar white head and tail develop with maturity. Do not mistake an immature bald eagle for another species based on head color alone.
