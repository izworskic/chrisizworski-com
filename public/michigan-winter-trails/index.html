Munising · Upper Peninsula · snowshoe

Munising Snowshoe Trail

Step away from the road into beech, maple and conifer forest. The National Park Service describes a 2.65-mile loop marked with blue posts.

Make it your dayPark near the end of East City Limits Road, then walk past the wooden fence to the snowshoe route.

Before you go: The snowshoe loop is separate from the groomed ski tracks; check road conditions and park notices. The nearby Munising Falls trail is closed until further notice.

Traverse City · Northern Lower · fat bike

Vasa Winter Sports Singletrack

Wind through the woods east of Traverse City on a winter route created for bikes and other non-ski recreation.

Make it your dayLook for the Winter Sports Singletrack off Supply Road. The DNR describes a 15-kilometer route; check the current groomer report before choosing your ride.

Before you go: The ski pathway and winter singletrack have different uses. Stay off classic ski tracks and avoid riding soft trails when tires leave deep ruts.

Ontonagon · Western Upper Peninsula · ski

Porcupine Mountains winter trails

Lake Superior snow country offers a network of winter trails through the big woods of the Porkies. Choose the scale of your outing before disappearing into the trees.

Make it your dayStart with the park’s winter trail map and pick a route that matches your equipment and experience.

Before you go: Check grooming, road access and the park’s current notices. South Boundary Road is closed to ordinary vehicles in winter.

Near Tawas · Northern Lower · ski

Corsair Ski Trails

In the Huron National Forest, loops wind through pines and rolling country near Silver Creek. This is a place to settle into the rhythm of a ski through the woods.

Make it your dayCompare the Corsair, Silver Valley and Wrights Lake trailheads on the official map; loop length and terrain vary.

Before you go: These trails are managed for skiing during the groomed snow season. Check grooming and access before traveling.

Sleeping Bear Dunes · Northern Lower · snowshoe

Sleeping Bear Heritage Trail

Snow quiets the woods and fields along the lakeshore. Portions of the Heritage Trail are groomed in winter, with space for more than one way to travel.

Make it your dayChoose a groomed segment and check the Friends of Sleeping Bear’s latest report before setting out.

Before you go: Stay out of the ski tracks, check the park’s current pet rules and confirm parking for the segment you choose.

Mears · West Michigan · fat bike

Silver Lake winter dunes

A different kind of winter ride: open dunes, changing sand and views toward Lake Michigan. The landscape in the photo is archival and may look very different when you arrive.

Make it your dayThe DNR lists a fat-tire season from December 15 through March 15. Enter the designated ORV area via the down ramp.

Before you go: Confirm current rules before leaving. No e-bikes; a Recreation Passport is required. Snow, ice and drifting sand can change the ride quickly.

Near Marquette · Upper Peninsula · ski

Blueberry Ridge Pathway

Jack pine woods and seven loops make this a rewarding place to find a ski that fits your day. A lighted loop adds the possibility of an evening outing.

Make it your dayCheck the DNR’s trail map and the recorded grooming report at 906-339-4452 before choosing your loop.

Before you go: Grooming depends on weather. Snowshoers are asked to stay off groomed ski tracks; verify trail conditions and parking before leaving.

A little trail wisdom

The snow is shared. The trails are different.

Fresh snow can make every route look inviting. The trail’s intended use, grooming and current condition tell you what belongs there.

01 / SKIS

Protect the tracks

Those parallel grooves are made for classic skis. Walk or snowshoe beside them where the land manager allows it, and use the designated winter route.

02 / SNOWSHOES

Make room for a slower day

Snowshoes open the door to quiet woods, but deep snow and short daylight can make a familiar distance feel very different. Choose your turnaround before you go.

03 / FAT TIRES

Read the surface

Use routes that welcome bikes. On a soft groomed surface, deep ruts are a sign to turn around and let the trail firm up.

Start with snow depth, then confirm grooming and access with the local trail manager. Snow on a map is not a trail report.

Field judgment

Bring the right plan for the day.

Winter trailheads may be inaccessible when summer roads are unplowed. Never park on snowmobile corridors or drive onto unplowed park roads.

Bring a map, dress in layers, carry water, and tell someone your route and return time. Conditions, daylight and cell service can change quickly. Our markers show destination areas, not directions or proof of access.
