BLUE RIDGE PARKWAY MAP & MILEPOSTS

Use mileposts to understand the Parkway before you build the drive.

The Blue Ridge Parkway runs 469 miles from Rockfish Gap near Waynesboro, Virginia, to Cherokee, North Carolina. NPS directions are milepost-based because GPS addresses do not describe the road especially well. This page gives you the practical anchors; the live planner handles the day-specific route.

THE BIG PICTURE

Five anchors divide the 469-mile road into understandable pieces

MP 0 · ROCKFISH GAPWaynesboro / Afton

The northern terminus begins near Shenandoah National Park. Humpback Rocks is the early landmark before the road settles into Virginia ridge country.

AROUND MP 121Roanoke

Roanoke divides a compact northern drive toward Peaks of Otter from a longer southern run toward Rocky Knob and Mabry Mill.

AROUND MP 291Boone / Blowing Rock

The High Country places Moses Cone, Rough Ridge, Linn Cove Viaduct and Linville Falls within a relatively tight corridor.

AROUND MP 384Asheville

Asheville is a true fork: north climbs toward Craggy Gardens and Mount Mitchell access; south runs toward Mount Pisgah, Graveyard Fields and the Balsams.

MP 469Cherokee

The southern terminus sits beside Great Smoky Mountains National Park. Waterrock Knob and Richland Balsam are the major high-country targets north of town.

HOW TO USE THE MAP

A milepost is not a time estimate

Distance is only the first constraint

NPS notes that about 30 Parkway miles can take roughly an hour before stops. Curves, overlooks and slower traffic can stretch that further.

Out-and-back doubles the road

If you turn around at MP 420 after starting near MP 384, you still need to drive those Parkway miles back unless you exit and use another road.

Closures can split the line

Use the closure page before assuming two mileposts connect today.

Weather follows terrain, not city limits

Use route weather for high-elevation corridors rather than relying only on a gateway-city forecast.

Stops create the trip

Use the best stops guide to understand which landmarks form a coherent cluster.
