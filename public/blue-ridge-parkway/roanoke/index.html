BLUE RIDGE PARKWAY FROM ROANOKE

Roanoke's choice is simple on paper and different in practice.

North puts Peaks of Otter within a compact scenic day. South stretches the drive toward Rocky Knob and Mabry Mill, where Parkway history matters as much as the overlooks. The better route depends on how much time you have and whether today's road and mountain conditions justify the extra miles.

NORTH OR SOUTH

Two route shapes, two different reasons to drive

Roanoke is where scenic efficiency and Parkway history begin pulling in different directions.

NORTH

Peaks of Otter

This is the cleaner short-day choice when you want mountain scenery without committing most of the day to Parkway mileage.

  • Compact route shape from Roanoke
  • Strong scenic payoff when visibility is good
  • Better fit for a two- to four-hour outing
  • Less driving means more margin if conditions change
SOUTH

Rocky Knob → Mabry Mill

South becomes more interesting when history matters enough to earn the extra distance. This is not the route to choose merely because Mabry Mill is famous.

  • Longer Parkway commitment
  • Rocky Knob landscape and recreation
  • Mabry Mill for Parkway cultural history
  • Works better with a larger time budget
THE TIME TEST

When does Mabry Mill actually fit?

About 2 hours

Keep the day close. The compact scenic route usually protects more of your time for the Parkway itself.

About 4 hours

The live engine can compare a fuller Peaks of Otter day with the beginning of the southern historic corridor.

6–8 hours

The longer Rocky Knob and Mabry Mill shape becomes practical if road status and weather support the extra mileage.
