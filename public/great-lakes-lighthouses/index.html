Sources, coverage and how the planner works

Visitor details: Each mapped stop links to its operator, park agency or official tourism visitor reference, reviewed October 18, 2026. Grounds access, tower tours and boat reservations are distinct. There is no live open/closed feed.

Weather: NWS hourly land forecasts and active point alerts, requested only for the selected U.S. stop and cached for ten minutes. Point alerts may not include offshore marine warnings. Port Austin uses a shore/departure-area forecast. Point Clark links to Environment Canada; no U.S. forecast is substituted for Canada. Failed alert checks, active alerts, missing weather and forecasts older than 12 hours suppress the suggested window.

Daylight: Approximate sunrise/sunset calculated from date and latitude/longitude using the sunrise equation; displayed in the selected site’s time zone with daylight saving time. These times do not predict color, visibility or photo composition.

Map: Leaflet, CARTO basemap and OpenStreetMap contributors. Only the 13 curated planning stops are mapped; the larger legacy directory is not fully geocoded. The buttons and visitor information remain usable if basemap tiles fail.

Privacy: Your shortlist is stored in this browser. Shared links contain your selections and date. No account or location permission is required.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about this topic answered from the data.

How many lighthouses are on the Great Lakes?
Counts depend on whether active aids, historic towers, range lights and ruins are included. This directory is a selection of historic structures, not a complete census. The mapped visit planner includes stops on all five Great Lakes, with separate coverage counts.
Which Great Lakes lighthouse is most famous?
Split Rock Lighthouse on Lake Superior in Minnesota is a well-known historic lighthouse and photography destination. Built in 1910 on a 130-foot cliff following the catastrophic 1905 Mataafa Storm, it was decommissioned in 1969 and is now maintained by the Minnesota Historical Society. The November 10 Edmund Fitzgerald memorial lighting ceremony is one of the most moving annual events on the Great Lakes.
Which is the oldest lighthouse on the Great Lakes?
Ohio DNR identifies Marblehead Lighthouse as the oldest lighthouse in continuous operation on the Great Lakes. Fort Gratiot’s surviving 1829 tower is the oldest lighthouse in Michigan. “Oldest surviving” and “oldest continuously operating” are different claims.
What does NRHP mean for lighthouses?
NRHP stands for National Register of Historic Places, the federal government's official list of the nation's historic places worthy of preservation. Lighthouses listed on the NRHP are recognized for their architectural, historical, or cultural significance. NRHP applies to U.S. properties; Canadian heritage designations use separate systems. NRHP listing does not automatically make a lighthouse publicly accessible or fund its preservation.
Can you visit Great Lakes lighthouses?
Many Great Lakes lighthouses are publicly accessible. Some are operated as museums by historical societies. Others are inside state or national parks with organized tours. A number of lighthouses have been converted to bed and breakfasts (Two Harbors Lighthouse in Minnesota and Big Bay Point in Michigan). Some offshore lighthouses are accessible only by charter boat. For the Apostle Islands, check NPS visitor information and authorized transportation operators for lighthouse trips; weather and seasonal availability matter.
What is the tallest lighthouse on the Great Lakes?
Height comparisons depend on whether tower height or focal-plane elevation above water is measured. This legacy directory records tower-height references but is not a verified tallest-lighthouse ranking. Check the relevant operator or USCG Light List for a specific measurement.
