Sleeping Giant Provincial Park · Ontario · Lake Superior

Meet the Giant. Find your own adventure.

The cliffs of Sleeping Giant Provincial Park rise above Lake Superior, beyond boreal forest, secluded beaches and a weathered arch of dark stone. Whether you have a quiet hour or a full day, find a trail worth remembering. The official distances below will help you begin; always check the park’s current notices before setting out.

Six trails, six different stories

The distance beside each trail includes the walk out and back, and on the longer climbs it begins at South Kabeyun parking. For routes without an official walking time, allow generous daylight instead of assuming they are quick outings.

Moderate

Sea Lion Trail

1.5 mi (2.4 km) return

Follow the forest through bare rock to Perry Bay’s pebble shore and the Sea Lion, a weathered diabase arch beside the lake. Ontario Parks suggests about an hour for the return walk; leave time to read the geology panels.

Difficult

Top of the Giant

13.5 mi (21.8 km) return

Walk through the forest, climb onto the Giant’s summit and watch the shoreline unfold far below the cliffs. The full return from South Kabeyun is 13.5 mi (21.8 km); Ontario Parks suggests 6–10 hours. Where cycling is allowed, 8.3 mi (13.4 km) can be ridden and 5.2 mi (8.4 km) walked, with a rack documented at Kabeyun/Talus.

Difficult

Head of the Giant

10.1 mi (16.2 km) return

Beyond Sawyer Bay rises the steepest climb in the park, ending in a wide view from the Giant’s head. The full return is 10.1 mi (16.2 km). Ontario Parks documents a 7.6 mi (12.2 km) cycle and 2.5 mi (4.0 km) walk option, but no rack at the transition.

Difficult

Nanabosho Lookout

10.6 mi (17.0 km) return

Follow the long approach to the Giant’s chest, where Sawyer Bay, Marie Louise Lake and the islands come into view. The 10.6 mi (17.0 km) return can include 7.3 mi (11.8 km) cycling and 3.2 mi (5.2 km) walking on documented sections; no bicycle rack is documented at the foot-only transition.

Moderate

Middlebrun Bay Beach

2.9 mi (4.6 km) return

A path through the woods brings you to Middlebrun Bay’s quiet sand and the sound of cold Lake Superior water. This 2.9 mi (4.6 km) beach return starts at a separate trailhead. Ontario Parks does not publish a full walking time or a complete bicycle approach.

Moderate

Pass Lake Overlook

0.6 mi (1.0 km) return

Climb to an old sandstone lookout above Pass Lake and Thunder Bay, a bluff that once stood as an island when glacial waters were higher. This 0.6 mi (1.0 km) return has its own trailhead; check park information for access and parking.

Let the park maps lead the way

The mapped lines for four trails offer a sense of the land, not turn-by-turn directions. The connection between the mapped South Kabeyun trail and the actual parking entrance is still unverified, so no line has been invented to fill that gap. Follow official park signs for the trail.

Ontario Parks identifies signed South Kabeyun parking on Highway 587 and a bicycle cutoff at Kabeyun/Talus. Bring the right permit, check current alerts and leave time for the walk home. This page does not report live closures or conditions.
