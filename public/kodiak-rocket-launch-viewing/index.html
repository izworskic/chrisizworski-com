ALASKA / KODIAK ISLAND / NARROW CAPE

Rockets at the edge of Alaska. Know where the road ends.

Kodiak's launch range sits beyond green hills and wild Pacific water on Narrow Cape. It is an extraordinary place for a spaceport, but not an open launch-viewing attraction. A scheduled rocket does not mean you can drive past Pasagshak, reach Fossil Beach or approach the pad. Range operations can close the road and surrounding land.

01 / BEFORE YOU HEAD OUT

Can you watch a launch from Narrow Cape?

First find out whether the road and nearby land are accessible for the operation.

PUBLIC LAUNCH-DAY VIEWINGNOT VERIFIED

Alaska Aerospace allows recreation on surrounding land at some times, but its launch facilities are closed to ordinary visitors and nearby areas can close for range safety. We have not verified a public viewing site for any specific launch. Don't make the drive expecting to watch from the gate.

03

Don't invent a place to stand

A shoulder pullout, range gate or unfamiliar beach is not automatically safe or legal for launch viewing. Follow posted restrictions. A park on the map is not permission to watch a rocket from the range.

ROAD-CLOSURE CHECKLIST

The road can close. Check it before you leave.

A published Kodiak Chamber reference lists 1-833-PSCA-321 for Pasagshak Road closures. Verify the contact and current restrictions with Alaska Aerospace before relying on it. We cannot confirm a road is open from this website.

04 / THE SPACEPORT AT NARROW CAPE

A launch range on a wild Pacific headland.

Why rockets launch from Kodiak

The Alaska Aerospace operator describes PSCA as a launch base for polar and sun-synchronous missions over the Pacific. Its location enables launch profiles different from those on Florida's crowded eastern range.

Read the operator's spaceport overview

Some rockets aren't announced in advance

On August 21, 2025, a government rocket launched without advance public announcement, according to local reporting. An empty public calendar therefore means no listing in this feed, not no activity.

Review local reporting

The road rules can change

Alaska DNR authorizes public-safety closures in the Narrow Cape area. Recent local reporting documents concern over road access as spaceport facilities expand; do not treat an old photograph or social post as current access evidence.
