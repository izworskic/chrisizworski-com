ALASKA / KODIAK ISLAND / NARROW CAPE

A rocket on the horizon. A road you may not be able to take.

Kodiak is not Florida. The Pacific Spaceport Complex is an active range, and the road beyond Pasagshak can close for hazardous operations. A published launch time is not permission to drive to the pad, Fossil Beach, or Narrow Cape.

Check range road closures

Before driving toward Pasagshak or Narrow Cape, check Alaska Aerospace announcements, the road-closure hotline and any local notices. No digital “road open” claim is made here.

03

Do not improvise a viewpoint

Do not stop on a road shoulder, approach a gate, enter a safety zone or follow a map pin to the restricted complex. State-park access is not rocket-viewing approval.

Verify the road directly, close to departure.

Kodiak Chamber reporting lists 1-833-PSCA-321 for Pasagshak Road closures. This is a published contact, not a live status feed. Check the number and current closures with the operator before relying on it.

04 / WHY KODIAK IS DIFFERENT

A spaceport in bear country.

The polar-orbit advantage

The Alaska Aerospace operator describes PSCA as a launch base for polar and sun-synchronous missions over the Pacific. Its location enables launch profiles different from those on Florida's crowded eastern range.

Read the operator's spaceport overview

A flight that might not be announced

On August 21, 2025, a government rocket launched without advance public announcement, according to local reporting. An empty public calendar therefore means no listing in this feed, not no activity.

Review local reporting

Access rules may evolve

Alaska DNR authorizes public-safety closures in the Narrow Cape area. Recent local reporting documents concern over road access as spaceport facilities expand; do not treat an old photograph or social post as current access evidence.
