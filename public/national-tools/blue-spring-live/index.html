Are Manatees at Blue Spring Today?

Check Blue Spring State Park manatee conditions today using the latest published manatee count, live spring and St. Johns River temperatures, weather, and visit timing. A recent count tells you what was observed; colder river water relative to the spring helps explain when manatees are more likely to gather in the warm-water refuge. Neither guarantees what you will see when you arrive.

Read the count and its date together

Start with the observation date attached to the manatee count. An older report can describe a previous cold spell without describing today's activity. If the report is missing, treat the count as unknown, not zero. Check the temperature and weather panels separately before making a special trip.

Florida State Parks describes Blue Spring as a winter manatee refuge with approximately 72°F spring water. Manatees gather in that warmer water when surrounding waters cool. That is why the difference between spring and river temperature is more useful planning context than a calendar date alone. Read the park's manatee guidance.

Compare temperatures without confusing the locations

The water page identifies the USGS monitoring stations alongside their readings. The St. Johns River reading near DeLand is regional context; it is not a measurement at every point along the spring run. Check the observation time and location before comparing values. Missing readings should not be interpreted as normal conditions.

Use the water-temperature comparison to understand that difference, then the visit planner to weigh conditions against your available day.

Check access before leaving

A favorable wildlife signal does not establish whether the park has space, whether a particular activity is available, or whether an area is closed. Confirm hours, entry information and current notices with Florida State Parks before you drive. The park's instructions take precedence over this independent planner.

What this page adds

This guide connects the reported wildlife activity, station readings and visit-timing tools into one planning sequence: check the report's age, compare the water conditions, review the weather, then verify park access. It does not provide a live animal census, reserve admission, or predict an exact number of manatees for your visit.
