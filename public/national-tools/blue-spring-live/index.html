Read the dated roll call first

During manatee season, Save the Manatee Club publishes dated Blue Spring morning reports after researchers complete roll call. Blue Spring Live extracts the report date, manatee count and reported river temperature. The date is shown with the count so a prior survey is never presented as a same-day census.

Florida State Parks describes Blue Spring as a winter refuge with water near 72°F, while manatees become vulnerable to prolonged cold exposure below roughly 68°F. That is why the river-to-spring temperature difference remains useful context even when the latest roll call is a day or two old. Read the park's manatee guidance.

Compare temperatures without confusing the locations

The water page identifies the USGS monitoring stations alongside their readings. The St. Johns River reading near DeLand is regional context; it is not a measurement at every point along the spring run. Check the observation time and location before comparing values. Missing readings are shown as unavailable rather than treated as normal conditions.

Use the water-temperature comparison to understand that difference, then the visit planner to weigh conditions against your available day.

Reserve before leaving

Day-use reservations are required at Blue Spring State Park. A favorable wildlife signal does not guarantee entry or establish that every activity is available. Check the official reservation system and current park notices before driving.

What this page adds

This tool connects a dated research roll call, measured water temperatures, recent thermal trends, weather, webcam context and access planning into one decision sequence. It does not provide a live animal census or predict an exact number of manatees for your visit.
