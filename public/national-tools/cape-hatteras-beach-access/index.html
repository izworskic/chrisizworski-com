How to use this before you drive

1. Start with official access

NPS status determines whether a direction is open to ORVs, pedestrian-only, limited or closed. Missing official data never becomes an all-clear.

2. Use tide as timing context

Lower water can leave more beach exposed. It does not measure sand firmness, erosion or overwash and is never labeled a safe-driving guarantee.

3. Check ocean hazards separately

A beach can be open while surf entry is dangerous. High NWS rip-current risk is shown as an ocean warning, not as an invented NPS closure.

4. Read the signs when you arrive

NPS says posted field signs are the most current indication of access. They override this page and any online status that has not caught up yet.

ORV permit: Driving on designated Cape Hatteras ORV routes requires the appropriate NPS permit. Check current permit rules before your trip.
Technical data sources

NPS Cape Hatteras beach-access status is the official eligibility gate. Tide predictions come from NOAA CO-OPS. Surf-zone and coastal hazard context comes from the National Weather Service. NC 12 and ferry links point to NCDOT. The internal ranking is derived and is not an NPS score.

Cape Hatteras beach access questions

Does low tide mean the beach is safe to drive?

No. Tide is only one piece of the day. Sand firmness, erosion, surf, overwash and posted restrictions can differ from what a tide prediction suggests.

Is pedestrian-only the same as closed?

No. We preserve the distinction. A section can be unavailable to vehicles while remaining open to people on foot.

Why can one side of a ramp be open and the other limited?

Resource closures, seasonal routes and beach conditions can affect directions differently. The tool keeps north/south or east/west status separate.

Can this replace the NPS status page?

No. It makes the official information faster to act on. NPS status and field signs remain authoritative.
