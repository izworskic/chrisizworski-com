2026 NYC bridge and tunnel tolls for cars

For a qualifying E-ZPass car, the Port Authority's New York-bound toll is $14.79 off-peak or $16.79 peak. The MTA crossings covered here charge $7.46 for a qualifying car, and four East River bridges have no bridge toll. Manhattan congestion pricing is a separate charge where applicable.

Before you choose a crossing

Bridge toll, Manhattan congestion pricing and live delay are three different costs

A crossing with no bridge toll is not necessarily a toll-free drive into Manhattan. Use the calculator below to compare the actual crossing charge, whether your selected destination enters the Congestion Relief Zone, and the current measured traffic segment. The result depends on vehicle, E-ZPass or mail payment, and departure time.

Which NYC bridges have no crossing toll?

Brooklyn, Manhattan, Williamsburg and Ed Koch Queensboro are untolled East River bridges. Entering local streets in the Manhattan Congestion Relief Zone may still trigger a separate charge.

Which tunnels receive a congestion-zone crossing credit?

Eligible peak-period E-ZPass trips through Lincoln, Holland, Queens–Midtown and Hugh L. Carey tunnels may qualify for an MTA crossing credit. No crossing credit applies in the overnight period. Credits depend on vehicle and payment conditions.

How to read the live times

Real measurements, with the segment scope shown

Port Authority times for the GWB, Lincoln and Holland include crossing approaches and can be compared with the authority's historical travel time. NYC DOT Traffic Management Center links add live measurements for Queens–Midtown, Hugh L. Carey, Brooklyn, Manhattan, RFK and Verrazzano corridors. When enough valid history exists, those NYC DOT readings are compared with an 8-week same-weekday, same-hour average from NYC Open Data. For Queensboro, Williamsburg, Manhattan, RFK and Verrazzano, fixed Mapbox driving-traffic probes fill gaps when official readings are unavailable and compare current conditions with Mapbox's typical travel time. The RFK fallback measures the Manhattan span and FDR approach; the Verrazzano fallback measures the gantry-to-gantry bridge segment. The exact measured segment is shown beside every time.

511NY supplies an additional roadway-event and variable-message-sign layer, refreshed every five minutes and read from a server-side cache. Reported closures, crashes, construction and posted sign messages appear alongside crossing approaches as advisories; event coverage is not a complete closure guarantee and stale reports are labeled. This layer never replaces drive times or toll calculations.

No fake fastest-route claim: these live measurements cover different segment lengths. A 4-minute bridge segment and a 7-minute approach corridor are not automatically comparable door to door. Future departure selections change road-charge math; live traffic always describes current conditions.

What the price includes

Crossing toll + Manhattan congestion charge

Passenger-car and motorcycle estimates use published 2026 toll categories. Truck, bus and multi-axle tolls require facility-specific classification and will show no base quote until that information is verified. The estimate applies the selected payment profile, vehicle class, time period and published crossing-credit rules. Congestion pricing depends on the streets and ramps used after the crossing, so use the destination selector as a first route condition. Fuel, parking and unvalidated approach tolls are not included.
