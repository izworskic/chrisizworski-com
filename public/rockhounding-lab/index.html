Three questions, in order

The engine must clear each stage before showing an opportunity.

  1. Did a meaningful disturbance happen?Compare recent wave forcing to a seasonal site baseline, using direction and period—not height alone.
  2. Have conditions moderated?Check the time since the peak and whether the next forecast period is rebuilding.
  3. Is it sensible to consider a visit?Marine warnings, ice, access changes and insufficient evidence can veto the decision.
What we won't claim: A storm does not guarantee fresh stones. No buoy or offshore model alone establishes safe shoreline access.

A storm doesn't always put more stones on the beach

Local Grand Marais reporting gives us a critical reality check: Lake Superior waves and shore ice can uncover rocks, but they can also carry rocks away or bury them under sand. These are firsthand historical accounts—not a measured 2026 Agate Beach outcome.

Local first-person history · research only
Rocks exposed or redeposited

A March 2012 Grand Marais area shoreline report describes shore ice bringing loose rocks up and leaving them as it melted. Conditions differed by beach segment.

Conclusion: Offshore waves alone tell us a disturbance happened—not whether the right pebbles will be exposed at Agate Beach. New research: where current harbor observations could come from

A recent USACE harbor survey record is now confirmed, while none of these sources establishes measured waves inside Grand Marais Harbor.

  • USACE eHydro SurveyJob — authoritative survey catalog metadata, not a live wave gauge. Live source lookup confirmed 10 Grand Marais survey jobs, with the most recent survey dated July 30, 2026. The original 2026 USACE survey chart is publicly available. The chart documents multibeam soundings in feet relative to IGLD 1985. Original data verified: the 2026 USACE ZIP contains 17,804 numerical XYZ elevations in US survey feet relative to IGLD 1985; these are elevations, not current water depths. New survey coverage check: the surveyed entrance-channel area lies outside both Agate Beach and Bayshore Park research footprints. These soundings cannot establish water depth or wave exposure at either beach. It is not a wave gauge.
  • GRMM4 — nearby observed wind; available station rows do not show measured wave heights.
