Pictured Rocks · Munising · Easy Snowshoe Loop

Snowshoe a quiet Lake Superior hemlock forest

2.65-mile blazed Munising Snowshoe Trail

When Lake Superior's northwest snow bands pile two feet of dry powder into the hills above Munising, the 2.65-mile Munising Snowshoe Trail feels like walking inside a snow globe. Marked with blue posts through quiet hemlock and hardwood ravines, it gives snowshoers a dedicated singletrack loop without ever crossing set cross-country ski tracks.

How I do itPark at the ski-trail lot near the end of East City Limits Road, walk past the wooden fence to pick up the blue snowshoe markers, and take your time under the snow-loaded hemlocks.

Good to know: Keep your snowshoes off the neighboring groomed ski tracks, and remember that the Munising Falls canyon trail remains closed until further notice after its 2025 washout; repairs are estimated for completion in late 2027.

Sleeping Bear Dunes · Empire to Port Oneida · Moderate XC Ski

Glide through Sleeping Bear on cross-country skis

Groomed classic tracks & skate deck on the Heritage Trail

Volunteer groomers from Friends of Sleeping Bear Dunes turn the winding Sleeping Bear Heritage Trail into one of the most scenic Nordic ski corridors in the Midwest. You can glide past the base of the Dune Climb, through the historic white barns of Glen Haven, and out into the rolling farm valleys of Port Oneida.

How I plan itCheck the latest grooming report before you wax or grab your fish-scale classic skis, and stage from the plowed Dune Climb or Glen Haven parking lots off M-109 and M-22.

Good to know: Pets are prohibited on the groomed Heritage Trail from December 1 through March 31, and if you're on snowshoes, walk the outer edge so the parallel classic tracks stay clean.

Porcupine Mountains · Silver City / Ontonagon · Moderate Downhill Ski

Ski 100% natural Lake Superior powder at the Porkies

20 runs, old-growth glades & big-water views

There are no snowmaking guns at the state-run Porcupine Mountains Winter Sports Complex—because Lake Superior dumps more than 200 inches of natural lake-effect snow onto this mountain every winter. From the top of the triple chair, you look straight out across the steel-gray inland sea before dropping into wide cruiser runs or gladed tree skiing.

How I route thereAlways approach the ski area from the east via M-64 through Silver City, and check the mountain's daily lift and snow-base report before making the Western U.P. drive.

Good to know: Never let your GPS route you onto South Boundary Road or west on M-107 toward Lake of the Clouds in winter—both are closed to cars and groomed for snowmobiles.

Silver Lake State Park · Mears · Moderate Fat-Tire Ride

Ride a fat-tire bike across Silver Lake's winter dunes

Dec 15–Mar 15 open-dune fat-bike season

Once the summer dune buggies and trucks pack up for the year, the Michigan DNR opens Silver Lake's 450-acre open dune sea to fat-tire bicycles from December 15 through March 15. When sub-freezing cold locks up the damp sand and snow drifts, you can carve endless lines across the ridges above Lake Michigan.

How I stage the ridePark at the pedestrian lot at 8890 W. Shore Drive, air your tires (minimum 3.5-inch width required) down to 4–5 PSI for float, and enter down the ORV ramp between 8 a.m. and dusk.

Good to know: E-bikes aren't allowed on the winter dunes, you'll need a Michigan Recreation Passport on your vehicle, and stay well back from the Lake Michigan shoreline ice shelves.

Keweenaw Peninsula · Upper Peninsula · Demanding Snowmobile Tour

Ride the Upper Peninsula's deep-snow snowmobile network

3,000+ miles of state-designated trails (Dec 1–Mar 31)

With historic copper-country railroad grades climbing all the way from Houghton and Calumet to Copper Harbor under 250+ inches of annual snow, the Keweenaw and Upper Peninsula snowmobile network is legendary across North America. The official state trail season runs December 1 through March 31 whenever snow base allows.

How to plan a safe loopCheck my Snowmobile Trail Conditions tool and the official DNR interactive trail closure map before trailering north, and make sure your 2026–27 Michigan snowmobile trail permit is affixed to the sled.

Good to know: Many U.P. trails cross generous private timberland easements that only stay open from December 1 to March 31—never ride off the signed corridor and watch for whiteout squalls near Lake Superior.

Sleeping Bear Dunes · Dune Climb (M-109) · Easy Family Outing

Take the family sledding on the lower face of the Dune Climb

Wide, snow-blanketed bowl right off plowed M-109

In summer, climbing the wall of sand above Little Glen Lake means hot sand in your shoes. Once winter snow blankets the lower slope, the National Park Service welcomes sleds, saucers, and snow tubes on the designated Dune Climb sledding bowl—with a plowed parking lot and restrooms right at the bottom.

How I do it with kidsWalk up the lower slope first to check that the sand is fully covered in soft snow (not icy crust), and pack hot cocoa in a thermos for the picnic tables at the base.

Good to know: Stay strictly on the designated lower Dune Climb sledding face—never sled down the steep Lake Michigan coastal bluffs at Pierce Stocking or Empire Bluff, where hidden ice chutes drop 400 feet to the water.

North Muskegon · Muskegon State Park · Moderate Thrill

Slide a real Olympic-designed ice luge track in the dunes

850-foot natural ice luge, wooded skating trail & lighted XC loops

Tucked into the forested Lake Michigan dunes inside Muskegon State Park is one of only four public ice luge tracks in the United States. Designed by three-time Olympian Frank Masley, the 850-foot lower luge track lets everyday visitors learn the steering basics and bank through six real ice curves at up to 30 mph—alongside a quarter-mile ice skating loop through the woods.

How to book itBecause natural ice luge sessions sell out on winter weekends and depend on freezing temperatures, check the Muskegon Luge Adventure Sports Park daily conditions page and reserve your session online before driving.

Good to know: Even if a mid-winter thaw softens the natural ice rink, the park also operates a wheeled luge track and lighted trails through the dunes.

Sand Point · Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore · Moderate Walk

Stand beneath 40-foot blue sandstone ice curtains

Inland cliff seepage formations off Sand Point Road

All winter long, groundwater inside the porous Cambrian sandstone escarpment behind Sand Point Road seeps out into the sub-freezing air and freezes drop by drop into massive curtains of pale blue, white, and iron-tinted gold. Best of all, you reach them on solid ground without ever stepping onto Lake Superior ice.

How I walk inPark in the plowed Sand Point Beach lot at the very end of Sand Point Road, walk back along the road for a quarter to half a mile, and follow the NPS signs uphill into the hardwoods.

Good to know: Never park on the narrow shoulder of Sand Point Road (you will be ticketed or towed), wear microspikes for the uphill climb, and leave pets behind.

Tahquamenon Falls State Park · Paradise · Easy Packed Walk

Watch amber water thunder through Tahquamenon's winter ice

200-foot-wide Upper Falls framed in three-foot snow pillows

Even when eastern Upper Peninsula temperatures plunge twenty degrees below zero, the sheer volume of the Tahquamenon River keeps its root-beer-colored center chute roaring while river mist builds turquoise ice bells and overhangs along the sandstone gorge walls.

How I visitPull into the plowed Upper Falls day-use lot off M-123, slip microspikes over your winter boots for the packed 0.75-mile forest path to the brink, and watch for the park's Saturday evening lantern-lit snowshoe hikes.

Good to know: Stay inside the railed upper overlooks when the lower gorge staircases are gated for ice, and keep boots off the ski-only Lantern Loop.

Sand Point Beach · Munising · Easy Shoreline Walk

Photograph ice-armored driftwood on the Lake Superior shore

Drive-up winter beach inside Pictured Rocks

Sand Point is the only Lake Superior beach in Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore where the road stays plowed for ordinary passenger cars all winter. After a December or January gale, freezing wave spray coats every bleached driftwood log along the shore in clear glass while pancake ice shifts across Munising Bay.

How I pair itWalk the dry sand beach right from the turnaround lot with a telephoto lens or binoculars—on clear mornings you can look straight across the channel to the East Channel Lighthouse and the ice curtains on Grand Island.

Good to know: Keep your boots on the landward beach and never walk out onto wave-built ice shelves or across the bay ice.

Houghton Lake · Roscommon County · Demanding Ice Craft

Learn the fundamentals of Michigan hardwater ice fishing

Walleye, pike & panfish on Michigan's largest inland lake

At more than 20,000 acres and shallow enough to freeze earlier than deep kettle lakes, Houghton Lake has been the beating heart of Michigan ice-shanty culture for generations—especially around January's Tip-Up Town USA festival. Whether you're jigging for bluegills or setting tip-ups for northern pike and walleye, hardwater angling starts with disciplined ice craft.

How to start the right wayBefore you ever step off a launch ramp, stop at a local bait shop in Prudenville or Houghton Lake for same-morning ice thickness and pressure-crack reports, carry a spud bar and wearable ice picks, and review the Michigan DNR Fishing Guide.

Good to know: The marker represents the Denton Township public access area; current parking and ice safety are not verified. Seeing shanties in the distance never guarantees safe ice between you and them—underwater springs, inlets, and wind-driven pressure ridges change ice thickness from yard to yard.

Saginaw Bay & Saginaw River · Bay City · Easy Shore Glassing

Watch wintering bald eagles along Saginaw Bay's open water

Why freeze-up concentrates eagles along rivers and bays

Here in my hometown of Bay City, winter is actually my favorite season to spot bald eagles. As smaller inland ponds and marshes across northern Michigan lock up in ice, dozens of eagles congregate along the open current of the Saginaw River, the warm-water river mouths, and the ice edges around Bay City State Park and Nayanquing Point.

How I glass them without flushing birdsUse your car as a warm, rolling blind at public riverfront pull-offs or scan the cottonwoods from the Tobico Marsh trails with 8x or 10x binoculars.

Good to know: Wintering eagles burn precious calories every time they're forced to fly—stay inside your vehicle or on the trail and never walk toward a perched bird for a closer phone photo.

Belle Isle State Park · Detroit River · Easy Urban Birding

Glass canvasbacks and diving ducks on the Detroit River

One of North America's great winter waterfowl corridors

Because the deep, fast current of the Detroit River almost never freezes solid, vast rafts of canvasbacks, redheads, goldeneyes, common mergansers, and long-tailed ducks spend January and February diving for wild celery right between Belle Isle and the downtown skyline—while freighters and the U.S. Coast Guard cutter *Bristol Bay* work the channel.

How I spend a morning hereDrive across the MacArthur Bridge onto Belle Isle with a Michigan Recreation Passport, park near the Blue Heron Lagoon or south-shore turnouts, and pair a spotting scope with a thermos of coffee.

Good to know: Check my Detroit Birding Today tool before you head down to see which diving ducks and gulls have been logged along the river in the last 48 hours.

Sleeping Bear Dunes · Platte River Campground · Moderate Winter Camp

Camp overnight in the snow at Platte River Campground

Year-round National Park Service winter loop

If you've always wanted to try cold-weather tent camping or winter camper-vanning without hiking five miles into the backcountry first, Sleeping Bear Dunes keeps a loop of its Platte River Campground open year-round—complete with heated modern restrooms and hot showers near the trailhead.

How to do winter camping rightPack a true four-season sleeping pad System (R-value 5.0+ so the frozen ground doesn't pull heat from your body), check Recreation.gov and the NPS winter page for active loop details, and snowshoe the nearby Platte Plains trails by day.

Good to know: Always check the overnight low temperature and Lake Michigan wind-chill forecast before zipping into a tent in January.

Mackinaw City · Headlands International Dark Sky Park · Easy Night Outing

Chase winter constellations and the northern lights at Headlands

600 acres of dark Straits of Mackinac shoreline open 24/7

When a crisp Arctic high-pressure system clears out the lake-effect clouds, winter nights in northern Michigan get dark by 6:00 PM and offer the sharpest, cleanest air of the year. Just west of Mackinaw City, Headlands International Dark Sky Park gives you an unobstructed northern horizon across the Straits of Mackinac—perfect for winter Orion, the Geminids, or a geomagnetic aurora storm.

How I time a dark-sky runCheck my Northern Lights Michigan tool for live KP index, solar-wind Bz, and cloud-cover layers first; bring a red-light headlamp (so you don't ruin your night vision) and chemical hand warmers for your camera batteries.

Good to know: Stay on the designated viewing plaza and shoreline paths—never walk out onto the dark ice ridges of the Straits of Mackinac at night.

Au Sable River Valley · Grayling to Mio · Easy Winter Woods Walk

Walk the quiet white pines along the Au Sable River

Spring-fed dark water framed in snow-covered cedars

Anyone who knows me knows the Au Sable River is my favorite place in Michigan in any season. Because the river is fed by cold, steady groundwater springs through sandy glacial outwash, the main current stays open and dark green all winter—steaming on zero-degree mornings while snow-laden white pines and cedars lean over the bends.

How I spend a winter day in the valleyStart with the plowed 1.25-mile Old Growth loop at Hartwick Pines State Park just off I-75 in Grayling, or pull into a plowed road-end river access along the Mason Tract to listen to the water in complete silence.

Good to know: Admire the river from solid banks and bridges—shelf ice along the Au Sable undercuts quickly over fast spring-fed current.

Presque Isle Park · Marquette · Easy Coastal Walk

Experience Lake Superior's winter power at Marquette's Presque Isle

1.7-billion-year-old dark peridotite cliffs vs. winter rollers

Jutting out into Lake Superior on the north side of Marquette, Presque Isle Park is a 323-acre forested headland designed by Frederick Law Olmsted. In winter, when Peter White Drive closes to cars inside the park loop, locals walk and snowshoe under the pines to watch Lake Superior rollers boom against the dark cliffs of Black Rocks.

How I walk itPark near the entrance pavilion inside the park, pull microspikes over your winter boots, and walk the 2-mile perimeter road loop—staying well inside the tree line when the wind is up.

Good to know: Never step out onto the bare, ice-glazed rock ledges at Black Rocks in winter; one unexpected wave over glare ice leaves zero way back up the cliff.

Silver Lake State Park · Oceana County · November Seasonal Window

Ride horseback along the Lake Michigan shoreline in November

Special Nov 1–30 DNR shoreline equestrian season

Before the December 15 fat-bike season begins, the Michigan DNR opens a special one-month window from November 1 through November 30 allowing equestrian riders to bring their own horses onto the designated Lake Michigan beach corridor at Silver Lake State Park.

How to plan itReview the DNR's official Silver Lake shore-riding map, trailer-staging rules, and registration requirements before hauling horses to Mears in November.

Good to know: This is strictly a November 1–30 late-fall shoreline window for riders bringing their own horses (not a mid-winter rental concession)—check Lake Michigan surf and wind advisories before trailers roll.

My Golden Rule for Michigan Winters

Pick the adventure for the weather you actually have.

The secret to loving a Michigan winter from November through March is flexibility. When the snowbelts are firing, head for the Porkies, Munising's snowshoe woods, or Sleeping Bear's groomed tracks. When a mid-winter thaw hits downstate, pivot to watching diving ducks on the Detroit River, glassing bald eagles along Saginaw Bay, or walking the white-pine banks of the Au Sable.
