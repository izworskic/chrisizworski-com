Lake Superior North Shore / Two Harbors, Minnesota / Field-tested planning principles

GOOSEBERRY FALLS / THE VISIT PLANNER

Follow the river. Choose your falls.

Just off Highway 61, the Gooseberry River tumbles across billion-year-old lava. You can hear the water within minutes of leaving the visitor center. The real question is how far down the gorge you want to follow it.

Only have an hour? Start at the visitor center. The Minnesota DNR recommends 30–45 minutes for Upper and Middle Falls, with a switchback option that avoids the stairs. The three-falls loop takes 45–90 minutes and is not ADA accessible.

03 / FOLLOW THE LANDSCAPE

Four ways to meet the river

Select an outing above; read a little more about what makes this place special here.

30–45 min · switchback option

Upper & Middle Falls

The broad middle ledge introduces the park's ancient volcanic geology. A short walk upstream reveals how the same river has worked through different layers of rock.

45–90 min · uneven sections

Three-falls loop

Walk down into the gorge, where the footbridges place you close to the sound of the water. Look upstream at the falls, then downstream toward the lake.

2 miles · hilly gravel

Fifth Falls

The crowds thin and the river runs through a quieter, more enclosed stretch of forest. Bring the official map and check current access before leaving the main falls area.

Shoreline · separate driving rules

Picnic Flow & Agate Beach

Where the river joins the inland sea, the dark basalt shelves make the geology visible. If you drive from the wayside lot into the park, a vehicle permit is required.

A PLACE SHAPED BY FIRE, ICE & WORK

More than a roadside waterfall.

A billion years ago, lava spread across this part of the Midcontinent Rift. Much later, glaciers and their meltwater helped carve the river gorge. In the 1930s, Civilian Conservation Corps crews built trails, stonework and camp structures that still help visitors explore the park. Follow the route toward Gateway Plaza and the geology becomes a story you can see beneath your feet.

Stay on established trails. The DNR prohibits swimming at Upper Falls, warns of slippery cliffs, and advises keeping children close along the river. Winter ice is never safe to cross. A beautiful waterfall is not a promise of safe access.
