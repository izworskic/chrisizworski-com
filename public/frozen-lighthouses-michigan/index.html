Behind the numbers

What makes a lighthouse freeze?

Four things have to come together

For this watch to show a spray-ice signal, the wind must blow toward the pier at 15 mph or more, the air must be below 32 F, and the water reading must be below 40 F. If one condition is missing, the tool does not show a spray-ice window for that hour.

How we turn weather into a signal

We use the Overland icing model, developed for ice that builds on vessels. It gives us a rough way to compare freezing-spray conditions along the shore, but it does not measure ice on a lighthouse. For readers who want the calculation: PR = Va × (0 − Ta) / (1 + 0.3 × Tw), using wind in meters per second and temperatures in Celsius. Its light, moderate, heavy and extreme categories describe vessel icing, not lighthouse ice thickness.

What the lake keeps to itself

We cannot see every wave at a pier, the ice piled beside it, or exactly how much ice has formed on a tower. Some water temperatures come from a lakewide average rather than the harbor itself. Treat the forecast as a guide to the weather, not a photograph of conditions on shore.

Wind direction matters, too. Where two Coast Guard lights mark a pier, we use the line between them to estimate which winds blow in from the lake. At South Haven and Manistee, we use the generally west-facing shoreline. This is a rough guide to where waves may come from, not a wave forecast. Widespread lake ice may keep waves from reaching the piers, and missing ice readings remain unknown.

Questions from the shoreline

Good questions before a winter visit

Will the St. Joseph lighthouse freeze this week?

The two North Pier lighthouses at St. Joseph are famous for their winter ice. When strong winds push lake water into freezing air, spray can coat the railings and towers. This watch checks the next 72 hours for wind from the lake, wind of at least 15 mph, air below 32 F and water below 40 F. When that weather lines up, the graph shows a possible spray-ice window. It cannot tell us whether ice is there already. A missing or old water reading makes the forecast uncertain. The Visit Southwest Michigan guide linked below points to a beach camera for a visual check. Keep your feet on shore, away from icy or wave-swept piers.

Which Lake Michigan lighthouses get covered in ice?

St. Joseph gets much of the attention, but it is not alone. Strong, cold winds can coat exposed harbor lights at South Haven, Holland's Big Red, Grand Haven, Muskegon, Ludington, Manistee and Frankfort, too. This watch follows nine lights in all, counting both North Pier lights at St. Joseph. Each lighthouse faces the lake a little differently, so the same wind may drive spray toward one pier and away from another. The pins come from the Coast Guard Light List. A forecast shows what weather is possible, not ice already on the tower. Look for a current visual report and view from shore.

What weather makes a Michigan lighthouse freeze?

Cold weather alone is not enough. Spray ice is more likely when waves and wind carry lake water toward a pier while the air is below freezing. For this watch, wind must reach at least 15 mph, the air must be below 32 F, the water reading must be below 40 F, and the wind must blow toward shore. We then use a model originally developed for vessels to compare possible icing conditions. It does not measure ice on the lighthouse or predict an exact thickness. We also cannot see every wave or pocket of ice beside a pier. When an important reading is missing, that hour stays unknown.

How long will lighthouse ice last, and where should I watch it?

Once ice covers a lighthouse, it may linger through a cold spell or melt when rain, warmer air and sunlight return. Our forecast looks for thawing weather, but it cannot tell you the exact day ice will fall away. For a shore view of St. Joseph's North Pier lights, the Visit Southwest Michigan guide recommends Tiscornia Park. It also describes Silver Beach County Park for the South Pier beacon. Check current park access before you go. A strong wind can make wonderful ice and dangerous waves at the same time. Never walk onto an icy or wave-swept pier.
