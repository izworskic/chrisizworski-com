How this works

Each region combines the Michigan DNR's designated snowmobile trail geometry with the DNR's temporary closures layer (an official closure always overrides everything else) and a National Weather Service forecast for that region's hub town. A route score reflects the worst meaningful bottleneck in a region, not an average — one confirmed closure or hazard segment can cap the whole region's condition, the same way one washed-out bridge closes a real route. Grayling–Gaylord is the one region with two verified MISORVA club reports and a live MDOT camera feeding into its score; the other six regions are scored from DNR trail status, DNR closures and weather only, and say so plainly rather than implying club-level coverage that isn't there yet.

Snow depth from NOAA's regional snow analysis is shown on each region's map as context. It is never treated as trail base — wind-packed, sun-crusted or rain-affected snow can sit on a poor trail, and a thin natural snowpack can sit on a well-groomed one.
