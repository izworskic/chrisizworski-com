          How a garden passes its story on

          A season, told in seed.

          Every saved seed carries a little of the summer it grew in. Here is how that story gets written, one flower at a time.

          1. Early summer

            The bloom

            A flower opens and offers two things at once: pollen to give and a place to receive it. What arrives in those few days decides what the seed will carry.

          2. High summer

            The visit

            Bees and wind do not read seed packets. They read botany. Pollen moves easily between plants of the same species and almost never between different ones, whatever the common names suggest.

          3. Late summer into fall

            The seed

            The fruit on the vine looks just as it should. A cross hides inside the seed, where you cannot see it yet. Sweet corn is the one exception gardeners notice right away, in the kernels on the ear.

          4. Next spring

            The telling

            Plant it out and the story comes back. Kept apart, the variety returns as itself. Crossed, it may bring something new. Saving from many plants, not just the best one, keeps the line strong for years.

          A field guide, not a crystal ball.

          Every answer here rests on published horticultural references for species, flowering, pollination and isolation. This explorer does not calculate a percentage chance of crossing, guarantee purity, or predict what a seed will become from its variety name. A separation guideline is not a measured probability of a cross. Bees, wind, the shape of the land and the garden next door all have a say, and a good gardener keeps watching.
