04 / FIELD NOTES

Three rules worth carrying into the garden.

01

Learn the Latin name.

A zucchini and some pumpkins are the same species. A cucumber and a watermelon aren't. Common names can lead you astray.

02

Separate flowering—not just plants.

Crossing needs compatible pollen and receptive flowers at the same time. Distance, timing, bagging, and controlled pollination change the situation.

03

Protect diversity.

Saving from one favorite plant may keep an interesting trait, but long-term preservation also depends on adequate population size and thoughtful selection.

Usually, cross-pollination affects the next generation grown from saved seeds, not the current tomato, squash, or flower's appearance. Corn kernels are a notable exception. Read the University of Minnesota explanation ↗
