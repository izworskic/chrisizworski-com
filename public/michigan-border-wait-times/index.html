Questions travelers ask

Michigan–Canada Border Crossing FAQ

Which Detroit crossing should I take right now?

Use the comparison above after choosing your direction, vehicle and lane. It only calls a crossing shortest when matching official wait reports are available. Also check your starting point, destination, toll, tunnel vehicle restrictions and approach traffic—the lowest border-processing number is not always the shortest total trip.

How often do Michigan border wait times update?

This page checks its official sources every 60 seconds while open. The agency timestamps reveal when CBP or CBSA last changed an underlying report; agency updates are not guaranteed every minute.

Does the reported wait include the toll plaza or highway backup?

No. CBP and CBSA estimates describe border processing for the selected direction and traffic type. This tool deliberately shows Ontario 511 road events and cameras in separate sections.

Which Michigan border crossing is in the Upper Peninsula?

The Sault Ste. Marie International Bridge connects I-75 in Sault Ste. Marie, Michigan, with Highway 17 in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario. It is included here with both official directions, a camera, warnings and tolls.

Are all five crossings open 24 hours?

Each crossing normally operates 24 hours, but closures, lane schedules and temporary restrictions can occur. Check the live port status and operator source before leaving.

What documents do I need to cross between Michigan and Ontario?

Requirements depend on citizenship, age, travel mode and immigration status. U.S. citizens commonly use a passport, passport card, enhanced driver’s license or trusted-traveler card at land borders. Confirm your specific situation with CBP and CBSA.

Can I bring food, alcohol, cannabis or a firearm across the border?

Rules, limits, permits and declaration requirements depend on the item, destination and traveler. Declare goods and check the controlling rules before you leave: CBSA restricted and prohibited goods for entering Canada and CBP Know Before You Go for entering the United States. Cannabis cannot legally cross the Canadian border even where it is otherwise legal; see the Government of Canada border guidance. This tool does not replace customs or firearms advice.

Can NEXUS or FAST be compared with standard lanes?

For U.S.-bound traffic, yes when CBP is actively reporting that lane. Closed, unavailable and unreported dedicated lanes remain unavailable; the tool never substitutes the standard-lane number. CBSA’s public CSV does not separate those lane programs entering Canada.

Can trucks use all three Detroit crossings?

Commercial processing is reported for all three, but the Detroit–Windsor Tunnel has vehicle, hazardous-material and dimensional restrictions. Check the tunnel operator and customs agencies before routing a commercial or oversize vehicle.

Why can a camera look busy when the official wait says “No Delay”?

A camera may show approach traffic, a toll queue or a different direction and lane from the one you selected. “No Delay” is the agency’s border-processing estimate, not a visual traffic count or guarantee.

Does this tool predict the best time to cross?

No. It shows current official reports and, where CBP supplies it, clearly labeled observed-versus-typical hourly context. Weather, incidents, staffing and traffic can change quickly, so this tool does not invent a future crossing recommendation.

How This Border Wait Tool Works

Entering Canada and entering the United States are two different trips reported by two agencies. The tool reads the Canada Border Services Agency report for Canada-bound travelers and the U.S. Customs and Border Protection report for U.S.-bound travelers. It then compares only equivalent direction, vehicle and lane records.

What controls the “shortest wait” answer

  • A crossing must have an official numeric report for the exact selection.
  • A closed port or closed lane cannot be recommended.
  • Equal lowest reports produce a tie.
  • Ontario 511 events, weather warnings and cameras remain context and never alter an official border number.
  • Hourly typical values are historical context only—not a predicted best time.
