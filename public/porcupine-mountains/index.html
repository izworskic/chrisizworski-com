What we still need before publishing hiking routes

The official GPS map identifies unbridged crossings, but those map symbols have not been independently matched to confirmed crossing coordinates. We are comparing exact DNR trail lines to Michigan's river centerlines; an intersection is only a candidate, not evidence of a bridge or ford.

The Escarpment, Government Peak, Lake Superior, and North Mirror Lake trails also have conflicting map and GIS mileages. Those disputes must be resolved before we supply end-to-end trail distances, elevation profiles, or hiking times.

The places that stay with you

Three very different days

Walk into a broad horizon, follow the thunder of a wild river, or rise above the hardwood canopy. Each has different access and effort.

Eastern overlook · M-107

Lake of the Clouds

The entire valley appears at once: a lake held beneath the ridge, hills extending to the horizon, and some of the Midwest's most remarkable forest.

The move: Choose the short overlook approach if you want the view without committing to a wilderness hike. The DNR describes approximately 100 yards of paved or boardwalk approach.

The longer Escarpment Trail is a different, strenuous experience. Road access and parking must be checked before travel.

DNR overlook accessFall-color timing

Western river corridor · County Road 519

Presque Isle waterfalls

The river steps through dark stone in three named falls. The gorge paths, stairs and boardwalks reward a deliberate visit, not three separate roadside stops.

The move: Explore Nawadaha, Manido and Manabezho as one walking area, matching the approach to your comfort with stairs and uneven ground.

The DNR documents an accessible platform for Nawadaha, not universal accessibility across the river corridor. Do not enter the river.

Central forest · South Boundary Road

Summit Peak

The ascent passes through northern hardwoods before rising onto a viewing tower. The experience is as much about the canopy beneath you as the horizon beyond.

The move: Plan for the DNR's half-mile approach and a further stair climb to the viewing platform.

The approach and tower are not equivalent to a step-free scenic overlook. Seasonal vehicle access matters.

Beyond the roadside

The forest has its own story.

The park includes one of North America's outstanding old-growth northern hardwood and hemlock forest landscapes. These are not simply scenic trails: they pass through a rare remnant of the forest that once covered much of the Great Lakes region.

On a longer visit, consider the Union Mine interpretive trail, Mirror Lake country, Big Carp River and Little Carp River corridors, and the Lake Superior shoreline. Their mapped lengths alone do not establish your total out-and-back or loop distance.

Where these decisions come from

The geographic and access facts are drawn from the Michigan DNR. Neither the public GIS field “Open” nor a seasonal road calendar verifies conditions on the travel date. Trail distances, live road status, waterfall flow, and parking availability are deliberately not inferred. Check the park directly before your trip.

  • Michigan DNR — park services, access and current official links
  • DNR visitor guide (2025) — scenic-area approaches, trailer parking, Summit Peak
  • DNR park map — geographic districts, trail inventory and warning symbols
  • DNR public trail GIS — administrative data, not live trail confirmation
  • DNR parking-asset point inventory
  • DNR named recreation/trail reference point inventory
  • DNR designated wilderness and natural history
  • DNR current closure notices

The online tool does not replace the DNR park map, travel notices or backcountry preparation. A Recreation Passport is required for vehicle entry. The main planner is a preliminary itinerary aid, not navigation, emergency advice, official DNR endorsement or a certification of open access.
