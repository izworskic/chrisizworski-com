    How the window works

    Tested before it was shown to you.

    We followed 762 ship passages along the St. Clair River across 36 days of the 2025 season, using public-domain AIS records from NOAA. One third of the days set the typical travel time from each point on the river, a second third set how wide each window needed to be, and the last third tested the result on passages the model had never seen. Only combinations that kept at least three in four ships inside their window are published; the best kept 92%.

    River questions

    St. Clair River ship-watching questions

    Is there a freighter schedule for the St. Clair River?

    No. Freighters move on cargo, weather and traffic, not a timetable. This page estimates when a ship that is already moving along the river should reach your town, from its latest public AIS report and the typical pace of 2025 passages. Ships can stop, anchor or slow, so treat a window as a good time to be at the water.

    How accurate are the windows?

    On days held back from the 2025 model, between 78% and 92% of ships passed inside a window like the one shown when they were up to two hours away. Windows for ships two to six hours away are wider, and only towns whose longer windows passed testing show them.

    Which way do the ships go?

    Downbound, toward Lake Erie ships come from Lake Huron and pass Port Huron first, then Marysville, St. Clair, Marine City and Algonac. Upbound, toward Lake Huron ships pass in the opposite order, coming up from Lake St. Clair.

    Why is there no window for a ship I can see on a map?

    A window is shown only when the ship's latest report is recent, it is moving along the channel and the time to your town falls in a range we tested. Anchored ships, small boats, very old reports and ships too far away are left out on purpose.

    Are there freighters in winter?

    Traffic thins out in winter, especially after the Soo Locks close for their annual maintenance, and the river can stay quiet for days. When nothing is moving the board says so rather than guessing.
