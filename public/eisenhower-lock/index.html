Your visit · researched October 23, 2026

Give the ship time to find you.

A lock visit rewards a little patience. Check the official order of turn before leaving, then allow time to watch the approach, careful entry and change in water level. AIS reports help you see where a vessel was heard; they cannot promise when it will enter the chamber.

The Seaway Visitor Center is at 76 Barnhart Island Road, Massena, New York. Admission is free. The operator lists 9 a.m.–5 p.m., daily from Memorial Day to Labor Day and Wednesday–Sunday outside peak season. Recheck the official page for holidays, closures and changes.

The building has accessible facilities and restrooms. Ask the center about any particular deck-access needs. Between ships, explore the exhibits, theater and ship-handling simulator.

Which way is the ship going?

Upbound means toward Lake Ontario and the Great Lakes. At Eisenhower, that is the rising journey. Downbound means toward Montreal and the Atlantic, descending here toward Snell.

What should I look for?

Watch the ship settle into the chamber, the gates close, and its deck move against the stationary lock wall. Water changes the ship's level; it is not lifted on a mechanical platform.

Where is Snell?

Snell is the other U.S. lock in the Wiley–Dondero Canal. Use Eisenhower's documented public viewing facilities rather than treating Snell's operational grounds as visitor access.

A little river knowledge

Eisenhower Lock questions

Where can I find the Eisenhower Lock ship schedule?

Use the official Seaway order of turn and select Eisenhower Lock. The visitor center also lists 315-769-2422 for a recording of the daily projected lockage schedule. Estimated times can change; this page does not calculate lock arrivals from AIS positions.

Can I watch ships at Eisenhower Lock for free?

The official Seaway Visitor Center lists free admission and public observation decks. Verify current hours and any access restrictions with the operator before driving.

How much does Eisenhower Lock raise a ship?

The Seaway reference booklet describes a lift of about 11.6 metres (38 feet). This is a reference value, not a measurement of the current difference in water levels.

Is the ship on the map next into the lock?

A recent AIS position does not establish the lock queue, entry time or current chamber occupancy. Check the official order of turn for operational transit information.

How this guide reads the river

Open Waters supplies recent public AIS reports from multiple contributors. We screen position ages, deduplicate ship identities and show received reports in five researched areas. Receiver coverage is not a complete traffic census. Official queue information remains at the Seaway source. The models are educational; the charts use dated historical records.
