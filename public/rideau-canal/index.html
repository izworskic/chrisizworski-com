The Rideau Canal in your own time.

Begin beneath Parliament Hill, follow the stone locks through old mill villages, or head into the quiet lakes south of Smiths Falls. Choose how you want to explore, and the field desk will narrow the possibilities.

The canal opened as an alternative military route between the Ottawa River and Lake Ontario. The lakes, rock cuts, masonry and hand-operated gates still tell its construction story.

    The canal is more than a straight line on a map

    Builders used rivers, lakes, excavated cuts, dams and a chain of locks to cross eastern Ontario. Each stone chamber is part of a larger landscape. The tool focuses on understanding that landscape without pretending every visitor is operating a boat.

    How many Rideau Canal locks are there?

    Parks Canada describes 24 lockstations and 47 locks including the Tay Canal. The operational index shows 26 individually named lock locations, including separate upper and lower sites at Nicholsons and Beveridges. Bridges and Colonel By Island are different kinds of entries, not additional locks. Official station index.

    What is a lockstation, and how is it different from a lock?

    A lock is a chamber used to raise or lower boats. A lockstation is the place where one or several chambers are operated. At Jones Falls, for example, four locks form one historic lockstation. Jones Falls profile.

    Can I use this map to navigate a boat or kayak?

    No. Its pins locate official destinations, not a navigable track, depth, obstruction, wave or portage assessment. Use current charts, local conditions and Parks Canada's operational guidance. Parks Canada orientation map.
