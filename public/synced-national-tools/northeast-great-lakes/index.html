Regional rhythm

The reasons to check change with the season.

This network is useful because its three decisions peak for different reasons through the year rather than depending on one short tourism event.

SpringGreat Lakes levels typically enter the seasonal rise while shoreline storm exposure remains important; longer days expand Niagara viewing opportunities and Acadia begins its busier travel season.
SummerShoreline use, boating and property questions are active across the lakes, while long daylight creates broad Niagara rainbow windows and Acadia visitation makes tide timing especially valuable.
FallGreat Lakes wind, seiche and erosion exposure become more consequential as storms strengthen; Niagara daylight shifts lower and Atlantic systems can change Thunder Hole wave energy quickly.
WinterGreat Lakes level trends and shoreline context remain useful even as ice changes local exposure, while Niagara and Acadia tools keep weather and access limitations explicit instead of pretending summer conditions persist.
