Munising • Lake Superior

Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore

A shoreline of mineral-streaked cliffs, sheltered coves and inland waterfalls. The kayak trip is memorable, but a short overlook walk can be just as rewarding.

Before you go: Plan a full day; boat departures and lake conditions can change quickly.

THE MOVEWalk to a shoreline overlook before choosing a boat or kayak trip. The wind, not the photograph, gets the final say.

Copper Harbor • Lake Superior

Keweenaw Peninsula

A dramatic drive north into old-growth pockets, rocky beaches, mining towns and wide-open Superior views. Go for the shoreline and stay for the quiet backroads.

Before you go: Remote stretches warrant fuel, weather and road checks.

THE MOVEMake room for unplanned stops along the shore. The best detours are often a short walk from the road.

Ontonagon • Lake of the Clouds

Porcupine Mountains

Old forest, long ridge walks and the view into the Lake of the Clouds valley make this one of Michigan's finest wilderness landscapes.

Before you go: Trail difficulty varies sharply; check park notices and seasonal road access.

THE MOVECatch the Lake of the Clouds overlook, then trade the busy viewpoint for a stretch of forest trail.

Paradise • Eastern U.P.

Tahquamenon Falls

A broad amber waterfall, forest trails and quieter reaches of the river. Winter brings an entirely different scene, but ice around flowing water requires restraint.

Before you go: Check state-park trail access and winter restrictions before traveling.

THE MOVEHear the Upper Falls before you see them. The quieter trails around the river deserve time too.

Lake Superior • Island wilderness

Isle Royale National Park

An island trip shaped by ferry schedules, weather and time on foot. It rewards slow planning, multi-day hiking and a willingness to let the lake set the pace.

Before you go: Ferry and park operating seasons limit access; not a casual same-day trip.

THE MOVEPlan an island night if you can. Ferry schedules and the lake should shape the whole itinerary.

Grand Marais • Lake Superior

Grand Marais & Grand Sable Dunes

Grand Sable's steep dunes meet the lake beyond a quiet harbor town. Pair a long beach walk with an unhurried evening, and check beach access before leaving pavement.

Before you go: Some scenic approaches are remote and seasonally difficult.

THE MOVESpend time above the dunes, then slow down in Grand Marais instead of treating it as a pass-through.

Marquette • Lake Superior

Marquette & Presque Isle Park

A walkable city surrounded by rocky shoreline, forest paths and Superior overlooks. A good base when you want wilderness during the day and restaurants afterward.

Before you go: Wind and waves change exposed shoreline access.

THE MOVEWalk the exposed Presque Isle coast, then come back into town for a long dinner after the lake air.

Munising • Pictured Rocks

Munising winter ice curtains

On sufficiently cold winters, inland ice formations and shoreline curtains make for an extraordinary visual trip. Formation and safe public viewing require site-specific evidence.

Before you go: Never infer safe ice or formation from temperature alone.

THE MOVEWatch the winter ice develop through dated reports; see it only from permitted, reliably accessible ground.

Empire • Lake Michigan

Sleeping Bear Dunes

Glacial bluffs, dune trails and a huge sweep of blue Lake Michigan. Plan a scenic drive or choose a longer hike; be cautious of steep sand and exposed lakeshore.

Before you go: Bluff-to-water climbs can be hazardous and exhausting.

THE MOVEChoose one overlook and one lake-level stop. That contrast is more memorable than racing through every trail.

Alden • Antrim County

Torch Lake & the Chain of Lakes

Clear blue inland water, wooded roads and excellent paddling nearby. Busy summer sandbars are only one small part of a region worth exploring.

Before you go: Boat traffic and public-launch access matter in peak summer.

THE MOVEFind a quieter public access point beyond the busiest sandbar scene, and keep the day deliberately simple.

M-119 • Emmet County

Tunnel of Trees & Harbor Springs

A narrow, winding tree-lined road above Lake Michigan, with beach stops and villages nearby. Autumn gets the attention, but the shoulder-season drive can feel wonderfully quiet.

Before you go: M-119 has curves and slow traffic; do not rush overlooks.

THE MOVEDrive M-119 slowly. The woodland road matters as much as the view where the trees open up.

Straits of Mackinac

Mackinac Island

Car-free roads, limestone bluffs and ferry arrivals set an entirely different pace. The island works for a day, but early mornings and the interior are worth extra time.

Before you go: Ferry schedules and island businesses are seasonal.

THE MOVELeave the busiest waterfront streets and find the island's quieter interior before the last ferry.

Plan an island visit →Compare ferriesOfficial visitor info ↗

Grayling • Northwoods river country

Au Sable River & Grayling

Cold, clear water, cedar-lined bends and classic canoe travel. Choose a suitable stretch and a shuttle before setting off; fishing and paddling share the river.

Before you go: Use current river conditions and verify access and take-outs.

THE MOVEPick a river stretch for the hours you actually have, and arrange the take-out before launching.

Explore the Au Sable →Paddling field guideOfficial visitor info ↗

Grand Traverse Bay

Traverse City & Old Mission

A base for farm roads, shoreline drives, dunes and tasting-room stops. Make a small itinerary rather than racing across every peninsula.

Before you go: Reserve busy-season experiences; beaches can be windy.

THE MOVEStart at the bay, choose one peninsula, and leave enough room for a late afternoon without an itinerary.

Alpena • Lake Huron

Alpena & Thunder Bay

A quieter Lake Huron coastline with maritime history, lighthouses and shipwreck stories. Ideal for travelers who want big-lake character without the busiest resort corridor.

Before you go: Underwater sites require trained operators; shore viewing is accessible.

THE MOVEExplore the maritime story on shore before you decide how to experience the shipwreck sanctuary.

East Tawas • Lake Huron

Tawas Point & Tawas Bay

A sandy spit, historic lighthouse and important bird migration stop. Make it a slow beach-and-birding day, with an eye on wind off the bay.

Before you go: Migration sightings are observations, not promises.

THE MOVEGive the lighthouse and bird habitat an unhurried morning; conditions and sightings change by the hour.

Ludington • Lake Michigan

Ludington & Nordhouse Dunes

Lighthouse views, dune-country trails and a large stretch of Lake Michigan beach. Build a day around sunset, then add the inland forest paths.

Before you go: Check state-park notices and designated access points.

THE MOVEMake the beach or lighthouse your anchor stop, then let a dune-country trail fill the rest of the day.

Compare beaches →Lake Michigan crossingsOfficial visitor info ↗

Mesick • Manistee National Forest

Manistee River & forest trails

High banks, quiet river sections and long woodland trails. This is a flexible trip: choose a short overlook or spend a weekend on the water.

Before you go: Confirm bridge, parking, shuttle and trail access separately.

THE MOVEChoose between a high-bank walk and a paddling stretch. Either can be a whole day if you don't rush.

Explore river access →Paddling guidanceOfficial visitor info ↗

Bay City • Lake Huron

Saginaw Bay & Bay City

A Great Lakes estuary with birding, ship traffic, wide-open sky and accessible shore. The bayshore rewards repeat visits as wind, water and migration change.

Before you go: Ice coverage is never evidence of safe walking or driving.

THE MOVECome for a shoreline walk, bring binoculars, and see what the sky, birds and shipping lanes offer that day.

Explore the bay →Track freightersOfficial visitor info ↗

Detroit • International river

Detroit Riverfront & Belle Isle

A city-scale Great Lakes adventure: freighters, a riverwalk, islands and changing seasons along a busy shipping corridor.

Before you go: River currents are strong; stay in designated public areas.

THE MOVEWatch the freighters from shore, then explore Belle Isle at a pace that leaves time for the river itself.
