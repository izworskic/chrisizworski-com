WHY THIS ANSWER

We do not turn a nearby gauge into waterfall flow.

The commonly used Snake River gauge is downstream of Shoshone Falls. It is useful context, but it does not directly measure water spilling over the falls. Until the Idaho Power Shoshone discharge field is semantically verified, the official live view remains the direct visual check.

    PLAN YOUR VISIT

    What visitors usually need to know.

    Why can Shoshone Falls look nearly dry?

    The Snake River is heavily managed upstream for irrigation and reservoir operations. Late summer and fall can be minimal, while spring snowmelt and release periods can produce much larger displays.

    When is Shoshone Falls usually strongest?

    Spring is normally the strongest season. The City of Twin Falls says typical spring peaks can be roughly 10,000–12,000 cfs, with wet years higher.

    How much walking is required?

    The closest parking is about 75 feet from the main viewing platform, and multiple viewpoints are ADA accessible. You can make this a very low-walking stop, or extend it with canyon-rim viewpoints and Dierkes Lake trails.

    How long should I budget from I-84?

    Perrine Bridge is about 10 minutes south of I-84, and Shoshone Falls is about another 10 minutes from the bridge. For a quick out-and-back from the interstate, budget roughly 60–75 minutes total: about 20 minutes each way plus 20–35 minutes at the main overlook.

    Where should a first-time visitor go first?

    Drive directly to the Shoshone Falls main overlook. See the waterfall first. If the flow looks weak or you have extra time, add Dierkes Lake in the same recreation complex; Perrine Bridge is the best logical stop as you return toward Twin Falls and I-84.

    Can I camp at Shoshone Falls?

    No. Overnight camping is not allowed at Shoshone Falls or Dierkes Lake.
