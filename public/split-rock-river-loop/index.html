The loop is open.
The bridge isn't.

Split Rock River is one of those hikes where the trail map alone can mislead you. Both reconstructed banks are open—but the former bridge crossing at the upstream end of the loop is still missing. Here's how to choose a hike that doesn't depend on a river ford.

    The decision point

    The open trail is not an open ford.

    At the former bridge location, the temptation is to treat the two trails as one uninterrupted circuit. There is still no bridge. The association identifies an alternate connection and warns that the unbridged river can be impassable. That difference should decide the route before you leave the trailhead.

    Arriving

    Start at the right wayside.

    The Split Rock River Wayside is near milepost 43.5 on Highway 61. Overnight parking is not allowed at that trailhead. Check the signed spur before committing to the east or west side; a trail map is essential for a through-hike.

    Officially grounded answers

    Before you step onto the trail

    Is the Split Rock River Loop open in 2026?

    Both renewed segments are reported open: the east side reopened June 16, 2025, and west side July 10, 2026. That does not mean the original upstream river crossing is bridged. SHTA publishes an alternate near Highway 61. Verify the latest SHTA notice ↗

    Where is the actual detour?

    It is based on signed spur trails, a paved trail and the underpass near Highway 61. Exact junction directions differ northbound versus southbound. Use SHTA's route instructions and map; this tool will not turn a schematic into unverified walking directions.

    How long does the loop take?

    SHTA describes the original loop as roughly 5 miles including its access spur, but that figure is not a measured detour route or a reliable duration. With a short window, choose a partial out-and-back that turns before the unbridged crossing.
