The lighthouse stands on a remote Lake Superior beach with little built shelter. The wide horizon invites careful watching of clouds and waves, but it cannot tell you exactly when weather will change. Check the latest forecast before the drive and reevaluate conditions on shore.

Where does this beach outlook come from?

The air temperature is a location forecast, not a thermometer on the sand. NOAA buoys report open-water conditions at their own coordinates. ShoreFeel does not invent a numerical beach-versus-inland cooling correction without field measurements.

Read this shoreline in layers: a three-day weather forecast for the air, a recent NOAA observation for open-lake water at the station, and Michigan BeachGuard and NWS guidance for potential hazards. Each source tells a different part of the story. None replaces what you find at Crisp Point Beach when you arrive.

Reading the weather together

Start with air comfort and the wind, then look at the waves, water observations and official notices. The Beach Day Score compares day-trip potential, not whether entering the water is appropriate. It is N/A unless the required weather values and both water temperature and wave height are present, with the NOAA observation no more than six hours old. Daily ranking also requires explicit low NWS swim risk and no matched official alert. Open the named sources, judge the station distance, and reassess the posted flag and shoreline conditions when you arrive.
