A note from the shoreline

Why the air can be different at the beach

The Great Lakes store and release heat more slowly than land. Wind crossing the water may carry cooler air into a summer beach or soften the chill on another day. But the direction of the breeze, the shape of the coast, and the time of day all matter.

Data sources, interpretation and limitations

Forecast source: Environment and Climate Change Canada, Meteorological Service of Canada, MSC GeoMet City Page Weather (experimental). ShoreFeel uses the nearest recent city forecast within 60 km of the published park reference, if available. Air temperature is reported in °C and °F. Wind in km/h. City forecast information cannot establish a calibrated air temperature at the waterline. The selected place is represented by an Ontario Parks park gatehouse or identified park coordinate, not an exact beach or parking pin.
