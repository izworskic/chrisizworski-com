What the Beach Day Score means

The 0–100 score answers one narrow question: how promising does this beach look for an ordinary day trip? It combines up to 25 points for air comfort, 20 for dry weather, 15 for wind, 12 for observed water temperature, 13 for observed waves, and 15 for the destination itself.

Recent NOAA observations are used only when they are no more than six hours old. Because offshore stations can be far from a beach, every detail view names the station, its distance, and observation time. Missing lake observations lower confidence instead of being presented as current.

Official notices override the number

An active Michigan BeachGuard closure sets the score to zero. A contamination advisory caps it at 20. A matching National Weather Service beach hazard or rough observed waves also limits the recommendation. Those beaches are not eligible for the daily top picks.

The key truth rule: “No active EGLE alert found” does not mean a recent water sample exists and is not a guarantee of water quality. Check the official record, posted beach signs, changing weather, and your own conditions on arrival.

When the seasonal page turns on

The dedicated Best Michigan Beaches Today ranking turns on automatically every May 15 and pauses after September 15. The core report on this page—search, map, weather, lake observations, and official notice checks—stays live through fall, winter, and spring.

Frequently asked questions

Which Michigan beach is best today?

There is no permanent winner. During summer, today’s top beaches appear above after current official notices and hazards are checked and the eligible locations are scored against the same weather and lake-condition rubric.

Why can water temperature or waves say “not current”?

The report will not pass an old buoy observation off as live. A reading more than six hours old is withheld from the score and displayed as not current.

Can I use this to decide whether to swim?

Use it to compare day-trip conditions, then check BeachGuard, National Weather Service hazards, lifeguard instructions, flags, posted notices, and actual conditions at the shoreline. Great Lakes waves and currents can change rapidly.
