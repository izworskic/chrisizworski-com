A field guide to lake air

Why the forecast can change at the water's edge

The Great Lakes can slow the warming or cooling of air moving across them. On some days, wind coming off cooler water brings a noticeably different feel to the sand. On other days, the wind arrives from the land, and that cooling effect may be absent. Here we report the area's National Weather Service air forecast and wind, without inventing a number for the difference.

Every lake has its own shore shape, water temperature, and exposure. The official water-quality checks are different in Wisconsin, Minnesota, Illinois, Indiana, Ohio, Pennsylvania and New York. Select the park you intend to visit and read its official advisories. The Michigan Beach Report covers Lake Huron and the rest of the Michigan shoreline separately.

Methods and limits

Weather uses National Weather Service hourly forecasts from a separately identified nearby land grid. Temperatures are model forecasts, not measurements on the beach. Wind chill and heat index use the published NWS formulas when their inputs qualify. Each forecast must be recent; otherwise the number is withheld. No NOAA lake buoy is silently substituted for nearshore swimming water. NWS weather, beach-water testing, access rules and posted swimming flags are separate sources.

Sources: NWS public forecast API, NWS lake-breeze science, NOAA offshore observations, and each destination's linked park and beach-health authority. Curated place coverage: November 21, 2026.
