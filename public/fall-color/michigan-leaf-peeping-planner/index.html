Michigan Leaf Peeping Planner

Most color maps answer a question nobody asks: what is peaking today, statewide. The question people actually have is narrower. I live here, I can get away on this weekend, where should I drive? Tell it those two things and it ranks all eight Michigan color regions by how close to peak they should be on your dates and how far you have to go.

Peak timing comes from the same regional model behind the live map, adjusted by recent cool nights, frost, and canopy camera readings when a forecast reaches your dates. Drive times are straight line distance with a road allowance, so treat them as a planning estimate rather than a routing answer.

How the ranking works

Each region carries its own historical peak window, and the U.P. runs roughly two to three weeks ahead of the southern Lower Peninsula. For your date the planner scores how near that region sits to full color, then it penalises distance, because a region at 96 percent four hours away is usually a worse Saturday than one at 88 percent forty minutes away. Regions that will already be bare are pushed down hard, since there is nothing to drive to.

Within the seven day forecast window the score also moves with the weather: a run of cool nights or an early frost pulls color forward, and a warm wet stretch holds it back. Outside that window you are seeing the calendar model alone, which is honest guidance and not a promise.

Once you have picked a region, the individual field guides go deeper on where to actually stand: the Tunnel of Trees, the Porcupine Mountains, Sleeping Bear Dunes, and the statewide peak timeline. If you would rather pick a road than a region, the tour map ranks twelve named scenic drives the same way.
