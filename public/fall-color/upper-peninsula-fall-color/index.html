When does fall color peak in the Upper Peninsula?

Michigan's Upper Peninsula turns first, peaking from late September into mid-October, west to east. The western U.P. around the Porcupine Mountains and Keweenaw is usually first, near the end of September, and the eastern U.P. around Tahquamenon follows about a week later. If you want the earliest fall color in Michigan, the U.P. is where the season opens.

What you'll see

The U.P. is the most concentrated fall color in the state: vast sugar-maple forest, almost no development, Lake Superior as a cold blue backdrop, and waterfalls running through it all. It turns early because it gets the first hard frosts, and it turns intensely. The west is high ground and old-growth hardwood; the east is birch, falls, and the Superior shore.

The drive

Three drives anchor a U.P. color trip: Brockway Mountain Drive on the Keweenaw, the highest paved road between the Rockies and the Alleghenies; M-107 to Lake of the Clouds in the Porcupine Mountains; and M-123 through the Tahquamenon country. M-28 and US-41 connect them across the peninsula, with the Pictured Rocks near Munising in between.

On foot

The Escarpment Trail in the Porkies and the river trail at Tahquamenon are the standout walks. Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore near Munising offers cliff-top trails above Lake Superior with color running to the water's edge.

On the water

The Two Hearted and Presque Isle rivers carry hardwood color down to Lake Superior. Sea-kayak tours along Pictured Rocks run into early fall when the lake allows.

How to time your visit

For the western U.P., plan the last week of September into the first week of October. For the eastern U.P., aim for the first full week to ten days of October. Cold comes off Lake Superior fast, so build in a flexible morning for clear light. This page tracks the whole peninsula; use the live map to see where the color is strongest right now.

Common questions

When is the best time to see fall color in the Upper Peninsula?

The U.P. peaks from late September into mid-October, west to east. The western U.P. around the Porcupine Mountains and Keweenaw is usually first, near the end of September; the eastern U.P. around Tahquamenon follows about a week later.

Where is the best fall color in the Upper Peninsula?

The Porcupine Mountains, the Keweenaw Peninsula with Brockway Mountain Drive, Tahquamenon Falls, and Pictured Rocks are the standout destinations. The west turns first and is known for old-growth maple; the east for birch, waterfalls, and the Lake Superior shore.

Does the Upper Peninsula turn before the rest of Michigan?

Yes. The U.P. gets the first hard frosts and turns one to three weeks ahead of the Lower Peninsula, making it the earliest fall-color destination in the state.

Nearby color, same trip

The Tip of the Mitt and the Straits, just across the Mackinac Bridge, peak about a week after the eastern U.P.
