When do leaves peak in Michigan?

Michigan does not turn all at once. Color opens in the western Upper Peninsula in the last days of September, sweeps down through the northern Lower Peninsula in the second week of October, and finishes in the southeast in the last week of October. If you want one week for the most of the state at once, it is usually the second week of October, when the U.P. still holds and the north of the Mitt has come on.

How color moves across Michigan

Three things set the order. Latitude comes first: the far north cools earliest, so the western U.P. leads and the southeast trails by roughly a month. Elevation pulls the same way, which is why the high country of the Porcupine Mountains and the Keweenaw ridges turn ahead of the lowlands around them. And the Great Lakes work against both, holding warmth into October so that shoreline color near Sleeping Bear and Saugatuck lags the inland woods by several days. The result is a wave that starts at Lake Superior in late September and rolls down to the Ohio line in late October.

What the timing depends on

The calendar gives the shape of the season; the weather gives the exact dates. A warm, mild September delays the turn, sometimes by a week. A run of cold nights and an early frost pulls it forward and deepens the reds, because cool temperatures and bright days drive the sugars that make scarlet. Drought and long gray spells mute the color and can brown the leaves before they ever glow. This is why two Octobers in the same place can peak ten days apart, and why the live readings on the map are a better guide in any given autumn than the averages alone.

Where to go, week by week

Late September into early October

Go north. The Porcupine Mountains and the Keweenaw are at their fullest by the first weekend of October, and Tahquamenon is close behind. The Upper Peninsula as a whole is the place to be.

The second week of October

The heart of the season. The Tunnel of Trees and Mackinac Island peak, Sleeping Bear Dunes comes on over the water, and the Au Sable runs maple and aspen over dark pine. Much of the north is in color at the same moment.

The last ten days of October

The wave reaches home for most of the state's people. Saginaw Bay and central Michigan peak first, then Saugatuck and the southwest, and finally Ann Arbor and the Irish Hills in the southeast.

Into November

After the maples drop, the season softens rather than ends. Tamarack turns gold in the northern bogs, oak and beech hold russet and copper, and the Lake Michigan shoreline keeps color about a week behind the inland woods.

Common questions

When do leaves peak in Michigan?

Michigan does not peak all at once. Color arrives in the western Upper Peninsula in the last days of September, reaches the northern Lower Peninsula in the second week of October, and finishes in the southeast in the last week of October. The single best week for most of the state is the second week of October, when the U.P. is still strong and the northern Lower has come on.

Where is fall color best in early October?

Early October belongs to the north. The western Upper Peninsula and the Tahquamenon country are at or near peak, and the Tip of the Mitt, including the Tunnel of Trees, is turning fast. By the first weekend of October the Porcupine Mountains are usually their fullest.

When does fall color peak in southern Michigan?

The southern third of the state, including Grand Rapids, Kalamazoo, Ann Arbor, and Detroit, peaks in the last ten days of October. Lakeshore towns near Saugatuck can run a few days later still, and oaks across the south hold color into the first week of November.

Does warm weather change when leaves peak?

Yes. A warm, mild September pushes peak later by several days, while a run of cold nights and an early frost can pull it forward and deepen the reds. Cloud and drought dull the color; clear, cool, dry weeks make it vivid. Because the year's weather moves the dates, the live map is a better guide than the calendar in any given autumn.

What turns color after the maples drop?

The season softens rather than ends. Tamarack turns gold in the northern bogs through early and middle November, oak and beech hold russet and copper, and the Lake Michigan shoreline runs about a week behind the inland woods. There is color to find well past the maple rush.
