Michigan Fall Color

Where the leaves are turning, and quiet places to find them.

Michigan's fall color turns from north to south. It usually peaks in late September across the Upper Peninsula and the Keweenaw, through the second and third weeks of October in northern Michigan and the Lower Peninsula, and in the last week of October near Ann Arbor and the southern border. This guide follows the season from live canopy and weather data, with a multi-day forecast and rivers to paddle through the color.

The season

NORTHSOUTH
After the peak, a second wave

When the maples have let go, the season softens rather than ends. Tamarack turns gold in the northern bogs through early and middle November, oak and beech hold their russet and copper, and the Lake Michigan shoreline runs about a week behind the inland woods. There is color to find well past the rush.

On the water

A river is an unhurried way to see the color. You drift beneath the canopy with maple and aspen leaning over both banks and doubled in the water below. Each run carries the live color of its region, so you can see what is turning before you go. Levels and liveries change with the season, so it is worth a call ahead.

When does fall color peak in Michigan?

Michigan's fall color peaks from north to south over about four weeks. The Upper Peninsula turns first, usually in late September and the first week of October. Northern Lower Michigan, including the Tunnel of Trees, Sleeping Bear, and the Grayling and Au Sable country, peaks in the second and third weeks of October. Southern Michigan, including Grand Rapids, Ann Arbor, and the Irish Hills, peaks in the last week of October.

Best places to see fall color in Michigan

The Porcupine Mountains

Old-growth sugar maple above Lake of the Clouds, the first and one of the most intense turns in the state. Peaks late September to early October.

The Keweenaw Peninsula

Brockway Mountain Drive along a ridge over Lake Superior at Michigan's far northern tip. Peaks late September to early October.

Tahquamenon Falls

Gold birch and maple over the amber water of the Upper Falls in the eastern U.P. Peaks the first week or so of October.

The Upper Peninsula

How the whole peninsula turns, west to east, with the standout drives and overlooks. Peaks late September into mid-October.

The Tunnel of Trees

The canopy drive on M-119 from Harbor Springs to Cross Village. Peaks early to mid-October.

Mackinac Island

Color without cars at the Straits, seen by bike along the bluffs. Peaks early to mid-October.

Sleeping Bear Dunes

Hardwood color over dune and Lake Michigan on the Pierce Stocking drive. Peaks the second and third weeks of October.

The Au Sable River

Maple and aspen over dark pine, best by canoe on the Holy Waters near Grayling. Peaks mid-October.

Saginaw Bay and central Michigan

Oak and maple on the bay flats around Bay City and Midland, timed with the waterfowl push. Peaks mid to late October.

Saugatuck and the southwest shore

Late lakeshore color over the Lake Michigan dunes. Peaks the last week of October into early November.

Ann Arbor and the Irish Hills

The last color in the state, oaks holding russet into November. Peaks late October.

Common questions about Michigan fall color

Driving up for colour? Check the rest of the trip before you go.

Where should I go for fall color in Michigan this weekend?

Drive to whichever region is inside its peak window on the date you are travelling, because Michigan turns north to south over about five weeks and there is almost always somewhere at peak between late September and late October. The ordinary sequence runs Western Upper Peninsula around September 28 to October 6, Eastern Upper Peninsula October 1 to 9, the Straits and Tip of the Mitt October 5 to 13, northwest Lower October 8 to 16, northeast Lower October 9 to 17, central Michigan October 14 to 22, southwest October 18 to 26, and southeast Michigan last at October 20 to 28. Those windows shift by up to a week with the season, so check the current regional read before you commit to a long drive. If your own trees have not turned, go north; if they are bare, go south.

When is peak fall color in the Upper Peninsula?

The Western Upper Peninsula peaks first, usually in the last days of September and the first week of October. That covers the Porcupine Mountains, the Keweenaw and the Lake Superior shore around Marquette and Houghton, and it is the earliest color anywhere in Michigan. The Eastern Upper Peninsula around Tahquamenon and Sault Ste. Marie follows about three days later, in the first and second weeks of October. Two things drive the gap: the western U.P. sits higher and further from the moderating effect of the lakes, and its sugar maple stands turn hard and fast once cold nights arrive. Elevation matters within a region too, so ridges and inland hills turn several days before the shoreline below them. A cold, dry, sunny stretch sharpens the color; a warm wet autumn mutes it and drags peak later.

Where is fall foliage peaking in Michigan right now?

Michigan turns roughly north to south over about five weeks, which means that on any weekend from late September to late October some part of the state is at peak even when your own county is not. The sequence runs Western Upper Peninsula around September 28 to October 6, Eastern Upper Peninsula October 1 to 9, the Straits and Tip of the Mitt October 5 to 13, northwest Lower around October 8 to 16, northeast Lower October 9 to 17, central Michigan October 14 to 22, southwest October 18 to 26, and southeast Michigan last at October 20 to 28. Those are ordinary-year windows and shift by up to a week with the season. If your local trees have not turned, drive north; if they are already bare, drive south. Peak holds for roughly a week in any one place once it arrives, and a hard wind or a heavy rain can strip a stand inside a day.

Is there a daily Michigan fall color report?

Yes. A short written report on where the colour stands is published every day from August 20 through November 15, and it is also available as an RSS feed.

Does northern Michigan peak before southern Michigan?

Yes. Color runs north to south across roughly four weeks. The Upper Peninsula leads in late September, northern Lower Michigan peaks in mid October, and the southern border near Ann Arbor and the Irish Hills peaks in the last week of October.

What is the best week to see fall color in Michigan?

For most of the Lower Peninsula north of Grand Rapids, the second and third weeks of October are the surest bet. For the Upper Peninsula, aim for the last week of September into early October. Southern Michigan is best in the final week of October.

When do the tamaracks turn gold?

Tamarack is the last to turn. It goes gold and drops its needles in the northern bogs in early to mid November, a quiet second wave after the maples have fallen.

How does this map know where the color is?

It blends a regional climatology model with Open-Meteo weather and PhenoCam canopy camera readings, plus NASA MODIS canopy greenness whenever a recent composite is available. The canopy readings show how far each region has turned, a run of cold nights pulls the peak earlier, and a short note is written each day through the season.

Can you canoe or kayak through fall color in Michigan?

Yes. Rivers like the Au Sable Holy Waters near Grayling, the Pine near Wellston, the Platte and Crystal near Sleeping Bear, and the Two Hearted in the eastern Upper Peninsula run beneath hardwood canopy. The On the water tab shows the live color along each run.

On light

The gentlest light is the first and last hour of the day, with the sun behind you and the leaves lit from the front. An overcast sky deepens the reds and takes the glare off the water.

How the live color is measured

Each region's color is estimated from its own climatology curve, anchored to PhenoCam canopy camera readings and adjusted by Open-Meteo weather: a run of cold nights and the first frosts pull the peak earlier. A regional climatology model sets the typical timing, and the live readings move it from there. Satellite composites lag real time by up to a couple of weeks, so the weather leads the edge of the season.
