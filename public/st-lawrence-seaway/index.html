Give yourself a front-row seat to a floating elevator. From the public observation decks, compare the ship with a fixed point on the lock wall as its level changes. Then head inside for exhibits and a ship-handling simulator that put the river’s engineering in your hands.

Lock 6 · United States

Dwight D. Eisenhower

Pick a mark on the concrete wall and keep your eye on a railing aboard the ship. As the chamber fills or empties, the ship moves past that fixed reference. That is the trick: change the water level and the floating ship comes along. Upbound rises toward Lake St. Lawrence; downbound descends toward Snell and Montreal.

A little river knowledge

Seaway ship-watching questions

Where can I track St. Lawrence Seaway ships?

Use the regional map for recently received public AIS positions and the official Seaway map and vessel-transit information for source context. Our live reports cover selected areas from Montreal toward Lake Ontario, not the entire Atlantic-to-Great-Lakes waterway.

How many locks are in the St. Lawrence Seaway?

There are seven between Montreal and Lake Ontario and eight in the Welland Canal. Welland connects Lake Ontario and Lake Erie. The Soo Locks provide the separate connection toward Lake Superior.

Where is a good public place to watch a ship in a lock?

Eisenhower Lock in Massena has an official Seaway Visitor Center and observation decks. Iroquois Point is another documented public ship-watching location. Confirm access and current traffic before traveling.

Does no AIS report mean there are no ships?

No. A receiver gap, an older position or a source problem can leave the map without fresh reports. Use the official Seaway vessel-transit information and keep the report timestamps in view.

How this guide reads the river

AIS means Automatic Identification System: equipped ships transmit position reports. Open Waters gathers recent public AIS reports from multiple contributors for this guide. Think of the map as a collection of radio postcards, each with a place and a time. We screen position ages, deduplicate ship identities and show received reports in five researched areas. Receiver coverage is not a complete traffic census. Official queue information remains at the Seaway source. The models are educational; the charts use dated historical records.
