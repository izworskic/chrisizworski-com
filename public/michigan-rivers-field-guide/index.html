Holy Waters & Cedar Bottoms: Michigan’s Blue-Ribbon River Paddling & Fly-Fishing Field Guide

14 Curated Half-Day & Overnight Floats Across the Au Sable, Manistee, Pere Marquette, Pine, Jordan, and Two Hearted Rivers

14 Audited River Floats Live USGS Flow Gauges USFS Permit Rules Hatch Timing & Shuttles

Michigan is blessed with thousands of miles of cold, spring-fed, groundwater rivers that never freeze, never quit, and carry some of the most storied brown trout, brook trout, and steelhead water in North America. But floating a northern Michigan river is not a lazy lake paddle: sweepers lurk on every outside bend, strict federal permits govern wild river corridors like the Pine and Pere Marquette, and knowing whether the river is running clear at 190 CFS or surging in spring runoff dictates whether you bring the family canoe or park the drift boat. Here are 14 field-tested floats curated by a veteran river guide.

Float 01 · Au Sable Mainstream · Grayling

Au Sable River — The Holy Water

Burton's Landing to Wakeley Bridge · 9.0 Miles · 3.5 to 4.5 Hours

Flies-Only · No Kill Classic Au Sable River Boat Water Hex Hatch Legend
Running Clear & Normal
~190 CFS Baseflow USGS 04135700 · Grayling

Pure cold groundwater springs maintain constant temperatures. Gin-clear water over sand and gravel bars; ideal dry-fly wading and traditional wooden riverboat poling.

This is the spiritual birthplace of American trout stream conservation and Trout Unlimited. Known universally among anglers as "The Holy Water," this nine-mile stretch from Burton's Landing downstream to Wakeley Bridge is strictly managed under artificial flies-only regulations. The current is gentle and steady, gliding over golden sand ribbons, clean gravel beds, and expansive dark cedar bottoms that shelter wild brown trout and brook trout.

For paddlers and anglers alike, the etiquette here is legendary: give wading fly-fishers a wide berth on the opposite bank, never drop an anchor in a gravel spawning redd, and keep your paddles quiet. In late June, the Holy Water becomes ground zero for the iconic midnight Hexagenia limbata giant mayfly hatch.

Float 02 · South Branch Au Sable · Roscommon

Au Sable River — South Branch Mason Tract

Chase Bridge to Smith Bridge · 11.5 Miles · 4.5 to 5.0 Hours

Wilderness Sanctuary No Roads · No Cabins George Mason Legacy
Running Clear & Normal
~210 CFS Baseflow USGS 04136500 · Luzerne

Cold spring seeps feed consistent water depth. Excellent floating conditions with deep cutbank pools and gravel riffles under towering white pines.

Gifted to the State of Michigan by auto executive George Mason with the strict legal covenant that it remain wild forever, the Mason Tract is an uninterrupted 4,700-acre wilderness sanctuary. Between Chase Bridge and Smith Bridge, there are zero bridges, zero power lines, and zero riverside cottages—only pristine forest, soaring white pines, and cold cedar bottoms.

The river here twists and turns with energetic grace. Paddlers must navigate fallen cedar logs and deep clay cutbanks. About midway through, the historic Durant Castle site and the quiet Mason Tract Chapel stand hidden in the woods. For fly anglers, the South Branch offers prolific afternoon Sulphur hatches and evening Brown Drake spinner falls.

Float 03 · Lower Au Sable · Oscoda

Au Sable River — Lower River Trophy Run

Foote Dam to Whirlpool Access · 12.0 Miles · 4.5 to 5.5 Hours

Beginner Friendly Big Water · Bald Eagles Migratory Salmon & Steelhead
Running Clear & Normal
~1,350 CFS Baseflow USGS 04137500 · Au Sable

Tailwater release below Foote Dam. Wide channel with smooth, rolling current. No technical tight sweepers, making it forgiving for recreational kayaks and family rafts.

Downstream of Foote Dam—the final hydro barrier before Lake Huron—the Au Sable transforms into a majestic, wide-open coastal river. Flowing through towering high sand banks in the Huron National Forest, this stretch is famous for nesting pairs of bald eagles, playful river otters, and massive migratory runs of king salmon and steelhead pushing upstream from Lake Huron.

Because the channel averages 150 to 200 feet wide, paddlers don't face the hair-raising cedar sweepers that characterize the upper river. It is a peaceful, scenic float suitable for paddlers of all experience levels, ending at the Whirlpool launch just upstream of downtown Oscoda and Lake Huron.

Upper Manistee River — Deward Tract to M-72

Deward Access to Manistee River Bridge · 13.5 Miles · 5.0 to 6.0 Hours

Wild Brook Trout Lumber Ghost Town Technical Cedar Meanders
Running Clear & Normal
~180 CFS Baseflow USGS 04123500 · Grayling

Cold headwater marsh seepage. Crisp, shallow current over white sand and dark gravel riffles. Agility required around fallen cedar logs.

Rising out of the cedar bogs of Antrim and Otsego counties, the Upper Manistee cuts through the haunting pine barrens of the historic lumber ghost town of Deward. This is pure, cold groundwater country—home to vibrant native brook trout and trophy wild browns that thrive in water that rarely exceeds 62°F even in the dog days of July.

Floating this stretch is an intimate, technical journey: the river winds like a serpent through dense cedar overhangs, undercut banks, and tight gravel hairpins. Paddlers should possess solid boat control to avoid strainers. The take-out at the Manistee River Bridge State Forest Campground on M-72 offers walk-in riverside campsites.

Spacious river channel with stable sand and gravel bottom. Smooth, reliable current with ample room to maneuver around occasional woody debris.

For families, recreational paddlers, or anglers looking for an easy, scenic float without heart-pounding whitewater or tight sweeper choke-points, CCC Bridge to Sharon Road is the gold standard of the Manistee watershed. There are numerous sandy points along the way ideal for pulling canoes ashore for lunch or a swim in cold, clean water. For dry-fly anglers, the evening Hendrickson and Isonychia spinner falls along the cedar tree lines provide steady action without demanding combat-casting into logjams.

Lower Manistee River — High Bridge to Bear Creek

High Bridge Access to Bear Creek · 13.0 Miles · 5.0 to 6.0 Hours

Overnight Canoe Camp River Bluff Campsites Salmon & Steelhead Highway
Running Clear & Normal
~1,850 CFS Baseflow USGS 04125550 · Wellston

Substantial volume below Tippy Dam. Strong, deep current with large gravel bars, deep holding holes, and sweeping outside clay banks.

Below Tippy Dam, the Big Manistee River gathers monumental volume and carves through the dramatic glacial moraines of the Huron-Manistee National Forest. Designated as a National Recreation River, this thirteen-mile float from High Bridge to Bear Creek is the premier overnight canoe-camping corridor in the Lower Peninsula.

Scattered along the high forested bluffs are primitive, water-access-only campsites marked by the US Forest Service with fire rings and wilderness latrines. Anglers target chrome-bright migratory steelhead from October through April and heavy king salmon in September. Drift boats, jet boats, and heavily laden touring canoes share this majestic big-water riverway.

Float 07 · Pine River · Wellston

Pine River — Elm Flats to Low Bridge

National Scenic River · 8.5 Miles · 3.0 to 3.5 Hours

STRICT USFS PERMIT REQUIRED Michigan's Fastest Current 7–10 Ft/Mile Gradient
Running Clear & Normal
~275 CFS Baseflow USGS 04125000 · Hoxeyville

Fast, rocky, cold water rushing over cobble and gravel beds. Tight hairpin turns under sheer clay sand cliffs. PFDs mandatory on moving water.

Dropping an astonishing 7 to 10 feet per mile through the Huron-Manistee National Forest, the Pine River is the fastest-flowing river in Michigan's Lower Peninsula. It is a certified National Wild and Scenic River that demands intermediate-to-advanced river reading skills: sharp ninety-degree oxbow bends, standing waves, exposed gravel bars, and sunken clay boulders will test your back-ferrying skills.

IMPORTANT PERMIT RULES: To protect this fragile canyon ecosystem, the US Forest Service enforces a mandatory watercraft permit system from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day. Daily launch slots are strictly capped and must be booked in advance via Recreation.gov. Glass and styrofoam are illegal on the water, and alcoholic beverages are restricted. Fly anglers fish fast pocket water for wild rainbow trout and brown trout.

Float 08 · Pere Marquette · Baldwin

Pere Marquette River — Green Cottage to Upper Branch

National Scenic River · 10.0 Miles · 4.0 Hours

STRICT USFS PERMIT REQUIRED Birthplace of US Brown Trout Flies-Only Water (M-37 to Gleason's)
Running Clear & Normal
~620 CFS Baseflow USGS 04122500 · Scottville

Classic un-dammed free-stone river system. Pure spring-fed flow over dark gravel shoals and deep cedar log jams. Exceptional insect hatches.

In 1884, German brown trout were first released into the waters of the Pere Marquette River, changing American fly fishing forever. Designated as a federal National Wild and Scenic River, the PM flows completely undammed from its headwater forks down to Pere Marquette Lake at Ludington.

Between M-37 and Gleason's Landing, strict artificial-flies-only regulations apply. The river flows over golden gravel riffles and ancient sunken cedar logs. PERMIT ALERT: A USFS watercraft permit is required to launch or float between M-37 and Gleason's from Memorial Day through Labor Day. Drift boat anglers and paddlers alike relish the heavy Hendrickson, Grey Drake, and Hex hatches that bring wild browns to the surface.

Float 10 · Jordan River · East Jordan

Jordan River — Graves Crossing to Rogers Road

Graves Crossing to Rogers Road Access · 8.0 Miles · 3.5 Hours

Michigan's 1st Natural River Gin-Clear Glacial Cold Old-Growth Cedar Cathedrals
Running Clear & Normal
~200 CFS Baseflow USGS 04127800 · East Jordan

Pure groundwater seepage creates astonishing water clarity. Sand and cobble bottom visible through 6-foot pools. Reliable year-round flow.

Designated in 1972 as Michigan's very first Natural Wild and Scenic River, the Jordan River cuts through a majestic, heavily forested glacial valley in the Mackinaw State Forest before emptying into the south arm of Lake Charlevoix. The water here is so pure, cold, and heavily spring-fed that it virtually never freezes, maintaining an icy 48°F temperature at its source.

Floating from Graves Crossing down to Rogers Road (or onward to Webster Bridge) feels like entering an ancient northern temple: giant cedars arch over the water, moss-covered gravel shoals sparkle in the sunlight, and native brook trout dart through deep turquoise pools. Motorized watercraft are strictly forbidden. It is an unforgettable paddle for families and nature lovers.

Pigeon River — Sturgeon Divide to Webb Road

The Big Wild · 7.0 Miles · 3.0 Hours

Wild Elk Country Native Brookies P.S. Lovejoy Legacy
Running Clear & Normal
~85 CFS Baseflow USGS 04128900 · Vanderbilt

Intimate, narrow headwater trout stream. Shallow riffles interspersed with deep cedar root-wad pools. Technical maneuvering required around tight wood.

Set within the 106,000-acre wilderness of the Pigeon River Country State Forest—fondly known across Michigan as "The Big Wild"—the Pigeon River flows through the heart of the largest free-roaming wild elk herd east of the Mississippi. Legendary conservationist P.S. Lovejoy fought to keep this vast tract devoid of commercial developments, and that raw, untamed spirit endures today.

Floating the Pigeon from Sturgeon Divide to Webb Road is a wild, self-reliant undertaking: the river is narrow, dark-bottomed, and cold, with fallen logs and beaver dams requiring agility and occasional short portages. For solitary fly anglers seeking wild brook trout and cautious brown trout without another boat in sight, this is northern Michigan at its most elemental.

Running Clear & Normal

While Hemingway used the name "Two-Hearted" for his famous story because he loved the poetry of the words, literary historians and local guides know that the actual river he fished in 1919 to heal his war-shattered nerves was the Fox River out of Seney. Floating this remote, untrammeled stream today reveals the exact same cold, tea-colored water, deep log-choked pools, and wild brook trout that Nick Adams observed hanging motionless in the gravel current. The paddle terminates at the rustic Fox River State Forest Campground, a tranquil camp nestled beneath tall white pines.

Michigan River Watercraft Permit Rules & Regulations

While most Michigan rivers allow open public access, several of the state’s most pristine National Wild and Scenic river corridors are strictly protected by federal quotas to prevent erosion, overcrowding, and habitat destruction.

Pine River USFS Permits

Mandatory Memorial Day to Labor Day: Every watercraft (canoe, kayak, raft, SUP) launching on the Pine River between Elm Flats and Low Bridge requires an advance daily permit via Recreation.gov. Quotas sell out months in advance. Glass and styrofoam are illegal; PFDs must be worn in moving water.

Pere Marquette Scenic Permits

M-37 to Gleason's Landing: Mandatory US Forest Service watercraft permits are required daily from the Friday before Memorial Day through Labor Day. Only permitted vessels are allowed on the water. Free-flow rules resume downstream of Gleason's Landing.

State Forest Landings

Recreation Passport: Parking personal vehicles at Michigan DNR State Forest campgrounds and boat launches (Au Sable, Upper Manistee, Jordan, Pigeon, Two Hearted) requires a valid Michigan Recreation Passport ($14 resident / $40 non-resident annual sticker).

Cedar Sweeper & Strainer Survival Guide

Northern Michigan cedar rivers are alive with woody debris. Fallen trees, log jams, and sweepers (branches hanging down into the current) create serious entrapment hazards if paddlers panick or make the wrong reaction.

The #1 Rule: Lean Downstream

If your canoe or kayak gets pinned sideways against a cedar sweeper or submerged log, your instinct is to lean away from the obstacle. Do not do this. Leaning upstream allows the water to catch your gunwale and flip the boat instantly beneath the log. Always lean into the obstacle to let water flow under your hull.

Back-Ferrying Technique

Don't paddle straight downstream toward an obstruction trying to outrun it. Point your bow at a 45-degree angle away from the hazard and paddle backwards in reverse. This "back-ferry" uses the river's own current to walk your craft sideways across the river into clear water.

Never Grab Overhead Branches

When drifting under low cedar limbs, never grab the branch to stop yourself. The boat will continue moving out from beneath you, leaving you hanging in the air or flipping the canoe. Duck low into the center of the cockpit, keep your center of gravity down, and let the boat drift clear.

Frequently Asked River Questions

Which Michigan rivers require federal watercraft permits for paddling?

In Michigan, mandatory US Forest Service watercraft permits are enforced on the Pine River and the upper Pere Marquette River (from M-37 to Gleason's Landing) between the Friday before Memorial Day and Labor Day. These permits must be reserved in advance via Recreation.gov to prevent overcrowding and protect sensitive riverbanks. Other rivers like the Au Sable, Manistee, Jordan, and Two Hearted do not require federal launch permits, though a Michigan Recreation Passport is needed for parking at State Forest campgrounds and boat launches.

How do live USGS CFS flow rates affect canoe and drift boat trips?

USGS streamgages measure cubic feet per second (CFS). Running Clear & Normal indicates baseline groundwater flow, offering optimal water clarity and manageable current. High Spring Flow (30% to 50% above median) produces rapid current, submerged boulders, and increased sweeper hazards along outside cutbanks, requiring intermediate boat-handling skills. Low Late-Summer Level exposes shallow gravel bars and riffles, meaning canoes and heavy drift boats may occasionally scrape or require lining through gravel shoals.

When does the iconic Hexagenia limbata mayfly hatch occur on Michigan rivers?

The Hex hatch (giant burrowing mayfly) typically begins in mid-to-late June and runs through early July, starting on warmer lower reaches and progressing northward. Emergence begins at twilight, with the main spinner fall occurring in total darkness between 10:30 PM and midnight. This brings Michigan's largest resident brown trout to the surface along undercut cedar banks.

What is the difference between an Au Sable river boat and a western drift boat?

The classic Au Sable river boat is a long (20 to 24 feet), narrow, flat-bottom wooden craft specifically designed for Michigan's spring-fed, cedar-choked rivers. Guided using a long wooden push pole rather than heavy oars, it navigates tight gravel hairpins silently without disturbing trout. Western-style high-side drift boats (McKenzie style) excel on wide, fast waters like the Lower Manistee and Pine River, but can be cumbersome on narrow upper river meanders.

How does dispersed canoe camping work on the Manistee and Pere Marquette rivers?

On National Forest and State Forest lands, dispersed canoe camping is permitted at designated paddle-in river campsites (look for USFS and DNR numbered site posts). Campers must camp at least 200 feet away from the water where designated sites are not marked, use existing stone fire rings, adhere to seasonal fire bans, pack out all trash, and properly bury or pack out human waste. Glass containers are strictly prohibited on designated wild river corridors.

How do river liveries and vehicle shuttles operate in northern Michigan?

Most established river towns (Grayling, Roscommon, Baldwin, Wellston, East Jordan) feature local liveries that provide full canoe/kayak rentals, spotter vehicles, and vehicle shuttle services (key drops). For personal watercraft owners, liveries or independent shuttle drivers will drive your vehicle from your put-in to your designated take-out for a modest fee while you float.
