Michigan Late-Fall Field Guide · Written by Chris Izworski

After the leaves fall—when the rivers, gales & marshes come alive.

Most people think Michigan's outdoor calendar goes to sleep between the last October color tour and the first real ski weekend in December. They are missing one of the best months of the year. If you pack a thermos and dress for the wind, November gives you thundering Upper Peninsula waterfalls with zero crowds, fresh Lake Superior agates and Petoskey stones rolled up by autumn gales, chrome steelhead in the northern rivers, and thousands of cranes and swans racing the freeze-line.

Three things to know before you pack the truck

Hunter orange in late November, where you can (and can't) pocket beach stones, and respecting November surf.

1. Firearm Deer Season (Nov 15–30): In Michigan, November 15 is practically a state holiday. State forests, national forests, and many state recreation areas are open to hunting. You don't need to stay inside—just throw on a bright blaze-orange hat and vest (and an orange vest for your dog) on forest trails, or pick non-hunting spots like Haehnle Crane Sanctuary and the paved Old Growth loop at Hartwick Pines. 2. The 25-Pound Stone Rule vs. National Lakeshores: On state park beaches and state-owned Great Lakes bottomlands, Michigan EGLE lets you take up to 25 pounds of rocks and fossils a year for personal use. Inside Pictured Rocks and Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshores, federal law prohibits taking a single stone. 3. Gales of November Waves: Never walk out on piers or wet black rock ledges while a gale is blowing—wait until the next morning when the surf drops to hunt the gravel.

FIELD LESSON 01 · WHY NOVEMBER IS THE BEST ROCKHOUNDING MONTH OF THE YEAR

Let the Gales of November do the digging—then walk the beach when the wind lays down

If you walk a famous Lake Superior or Lake Michigan beach in July, you are looking at gravel that three thousand other people have already picked over since breakfast. November is a different world. When a deep autumn low crosses Superior or Northern Lake Michigan, eight-to-fifteen-foot waves act like a conveyor belt: they pull tired summer sand offshore and roll fresh tiers of cobble up onto the beach. The trick is patience. Never go out on the breakwalls or wet rock ledges while the gale is blowing. Watch the buoy wave heights, wait for the wind to drop, and show up the next morning with waterproof boots. On state park beaches and state-owned Great Lakes bottomlands, Michigan EGLE lets you keep up to 25 pounds of stones a year for personal use—just remember that inside Pictured Rocks and Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshores, you have to leave every stone on the beach.

My rule of thumb: Watch the onshore wind over the past 72 hours, wait for the surf to settle, and hunt the wet gravel band right at the waterline.

Eastern U.P. · Paradise & Whitefish Point

Best: Late Oct – Nov 20

Whitefish Point & the Shipwreck Coast: Post-Gale Agates & Fitzgerald Waters

State bottomland collecting (25 lbs/yr) · 17 miles south-southeast of the Edmund Fitzgerald · Late-fall waterbird flight

Standing on the gravel spit at Whitefish Point in early November feels different than any other beach in Michigan. Just 17 miles out in those gray rollers is where the SS Edmund Fitzgerald went down on November 10, 1975, as she tried to reach the shelter of Whitefish Bay. When a northwest gale finally lays down, the waves leave behind fresh tiers of banded Lake Superior agates, red jasper, and after dark under a 365nm UV light, glowing Yooperlites—all while late-season loons, scoters, and snow buntings move past the point.

How I Plan the Day

Check the eastern Lake Superior buoy first. Once a blow settles down, walk the wet waterline gravel at Whitefish Point for agates (within Michigan's 25-lb annual personal limit) and bring binoculars to watch downbound ore boats rounding the point into Whitefish Bay.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

Skip the long sand-and-gravel county road out to Crisp Point if it's raining or snowing—stay on paved Whitefish Point Road and M-123, and drive 25 minutes south to walk the sheltered hemlock path to Upper Tahquamenon Falls.

Good to know before you drive: The indoor Great Lakes Shipwreck Museum buildings close for the season after October 31 (aside from the November 10 memorial bell ringing), so plan on outdoor beach access and dress in full windproof layers.

Eastern U.P. · Paradise (M-123)

Best: All Nov – Dec

Tahquamenon Falls State Park: Roaring Amber Water & Bare-Canopy Canyon Views

Paved M-123 highway access · 200-foot-wide Upper Falls brink · Zero summer tour buses

If you have only seen Tahquamenon in July or during the first week of October, go back in November. Autumn rain soaks the cedar and hemlock swamps that feed the Tahquamenon River, turning the water the color of dark bourbon and sending it roaring over the 200-foot-wide Upper Falls ledge. With the hardwood leaves down, you can see straight through the gorge walls—and when the first Whitefish Bay snow squalls dust the hemlocks, you often have the brink platform almost to yourself.

How I Plan the Day

Start with the 0.75-mile walk through the hemlocks to the Upper Falls brink and gorge steps, then drive four miles east on M-123 to walk the 0.6-mile boardwalk overlooking the twin island cascades at the Lower Falls.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

River mist glazes the wooden gorge stairs early in November—slip microspikes over your boots, skip the long 4–5 mile backcountry River Trail between the falls, and warm up afterward in Paradise or an hour east at the Soo Locks.

Good to know before you drive: The main Upper Falls day-use lot off paved M-123 stays accessible all late fall and winter; during Nov 15–30 firearm deer season, stay on the main waterfall viewing paths rather than wandering deep into adjacent state forest.

Central U.P. · Marquette

Best: Late Oct – Dec

Marquette, Presque Isle & the Dead River: Watching the Gales from Solid Ground

High-ground Lake Superior wave overlooks · Leaf-off Dead River falls · Late-season ore boats loading taconite

When a November low-pressure system spins across Lake Superior, Marquette puts you in the front row—with a real town, hot coffee, and museums five minutes behind you. Because Presque Isle Park faces east and northeast while the rocky headlands catch north swells, you can watch big water from safe upland park pull-offs, watch 1,000-foot lakers load iron ore pellets at the active Upper Harbor LS&I Ore Dock, and then duck inland out of the wind to hike along the Dead River.

How I Plan the Day

Watch the morning surf from the safe interior upland paths at Presque Isle Park and check for loading freighters at the Upper Harbor Marina pull-off, then hike the short leaf-off trail to Wright Street Falls along the Dead River.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

When heavy surf closes the outer Presque Isle ring road, walk the paved downtown Iron Ore Heritage Trail past the historic Lower Harbor Ore Dock, spend an hour inside the Marquette Regional History Center, and grab lunch on Third Street.

Good to know before you drive: Every November, Lake Superior sweeps waves across the dark peridotite ledges at Black Rocks and the harbor breakwalls—admire the surf strictly from high, dry inland viewpoints and never step onto wet rock edges.

Western U.P. · Paulding, Bessemer & Porcupine Mountains

Best: Late Oct – Late Nov

Bond Falls & the Black River Harbor Byway: Six Gorges on One Tank of Gas

100-foot-wide Bond Falls boardwalk + 5 Black River waterfalls ending at a Lake Superior agate beach

In the Western U.P., late fall is a sweet spot right before the 200-inch snowbelt locks the seasonal roads for winter. At Bond Falls near Paulding, the Middle Branch of the Ontonagon River fans out 100 feet wide over stepped dark volcanic rock. An hour west north of Bessemer, County Road 513 (the Black River National Forest Scenic Byway) follows a deep conglomerate canyon past five named waterfalls—Potawatomi, Gorge, Sandstone, Conglomerate, and Rainbow—before emptying right onto a Lake Superior cobble beach at Black River Harbor.

How I Plan the Day

Walk the 0.3-mile boardwalk loop at Bond Falls while the inner lot is still open in November, then drive CR 513 north of Bessemer to hit the Potawatomi/Gorge overlooks and cross the suspension bridge to walk the gravel beach at Black River Harbor.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

Because the Gogebic Range sits at 1,600 feet, early lake-effect snow hits here first—pack microspikes for the steep wooden waterfall stairs, or trade the canyon stairs for early-season Nordic skiing at ABR Trails in Ironwood.

Good to know before you drive: Inside the Porcupine Mountains, M-107 to Lake of the Clouds and South Boundary Road close to cars once winter snow/snowmobile season starts (around Dec 1), and Bond Falls' inner lot is unplowed once deep snow arrives. Wear blaze orange on Ottawa National Forest trails Nov 15–30.

Keweenaw Peninsula · Hancock, Calumet & Eagle River

Best: Late Oct – Late Nov

The Keweenaw Spine: McLain State Park Agates, Roadside Falls & the Seaman Museum

West-facing Lake Superior gravel · 25-lb state park collecting limit · Best indoor mineral museum in the Great Lakes

Jutting 60 miles into Lake Superior, the Keweenaw Peninsula catches every northwest blow head-on. Along M-203 between Hancock and Calumet, F.J. McLain State Park and Calumet Waterworks Park sit right in the path of that fetch—mixing translucent Lake Superior agates with dark volcanic basalt and glowing sodalite stones. Better yet, the Keweenaw has the single best foul-weather backup in northern Michigan: Michigan Tech's A.E. Seaman Mineral Museum in Houghton.

How I Plan the Day

Walk the rocky shoreline at F.J. McLain State Park after a northwest wind event, then drive M-26 up the coast to see Eagle River Falls and Jacobs Falls right from the highway bridges without needing a backcountry hike.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

Spend two hours inside the A.E. Seaman Mineral Museum at Michigan Tech (where you can see what world-class native copper and Lake Superior agates actually look like under gallery lights) and tour Calumet's historic copper-mining district.

Good to know before you drive: High-elevation Brockway Mountain Drive is unplowed once snow flies; stick to plowed US-41 and M-26 in late November, and check if Swedetown Trails in Calumet has rolled its first early snow.

Central U.P. · Munising & Grand Marais

Best: Late Oct – Dec

Munising, Wagner Falls & Grand Marais: Knowing the Line Between Looking & Collecting

Easy 0.1-mile Wagner Falls boardwalk · Look-only NPS beaches at Munising vs. legal agate hunting in Grand Marais

Munising in November is wonderfully quiet after the summer tour boats haul out. Right at the junction of M-28 and M-94, a five-minute walk through old hemlocks brings you to the stepped limestone ledges of Wagner Falls Scenic Site. Down on Munising Bay, Sand Point Beach is a great place to walk among storm-tumbled driftwood—just remember that inside Pictured Rocks National Lakeshore you can only photograph the stones. If you want to fill your pockets with agates, drive east to Agate Beach in Grand Marais or Muskallonge Lake State Park.

How I Plan the Day

Walk the short 0.1-mile boardwalk at Wagner Falls Scenic Site and stroll Sand Point Beach in Munising, then take M-28/M-77 to Grand Marais's township Agate Beach where collecting is legal under the 25-lb state rule.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

Note that the Munising Falls canyon trail remains closed following the 2025 slope washout—instead, walk the wind-sheltered 2.65-mile Munising Snowshoe/Hiking trail in the hemlocks and warm up in downtown Munising.

Good to know before you drive: H-58 between Munising and Grand Marais passes through heavy Lake Superior snow squalls in late November; use state highways M-28 and M-77 if lake-effect bands set up.

Northern Lower · Petoskey & Charlevoix

Best: Late Oct – Dec

Little Traverse Bay & Charlevoix: Why Locals Hunt Petoskey Stones in November

350-million-year-old Hexagonaria coral fossils · Wet cobble beaches at Magnus Park, Petoskey State Park & Fisherman's Island

If you have ever walked Petoskey State Park at noon in July and found nothing but dry gray limestone, try it the morning after a November northwest blow. Autumn waves roll the cobble beds along Little Traverse Bay, and cold November drizzle or spray keeps every stone wet—making the six-sided coral pattern of Petoskey stones and the tiny honeycomb pores of Charlevoix stones jump right out at your feet. Best of all, you are ten minutes from downtown Petoskey's bookstores and fireplaces when your fingers get cold.

How I Plan the Day

Start at Magnus City Park right off US-31 in Petoskey or the Tannery Creek end of Petoskey State Park, or head down to Fisherman's Island State Park south of Charlevoix (where the inner gravel road closes in late fall, leaving miles of quiet shore to walk).

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

Duck into McLean & Eakin Booksellers and Symons General Store in Petoskey's Gaslight District, or spend the afternoon at a fireside tasting room or heated igloo along the Petoskey Wine Region trail.

Good to know before you drive: Personal collecting (up to 25 lbs/year) is legal at Petoskey State Park, Fisherman's Island State Park, and Magnus Park; wear insulated waterproof boots so you can work the shallow wave wash.

Northwest Lower · Empire, Glen Arbor & Frankfort

Best: Late Oct – Dec

Sleeping Bear Dunes & Frankfort: Leaf-Off Bluff Views, Betsie Steelhead & Point Betsie

1.5-mile Empire Bluff boardwalk · Legal stone hunting at Frankfort Public Beach (outside NPS boundaries)

In summer, the humidity over Lake Michigan blurs the horizon. On a crisp 38°F November morning on the Empire Bluff Trail, the air is so clear you feel like you could reach out and touch South Manitou Island and the Point Betsie lighthouse. The beech-maple woods are bare and quiet, late-run coho and fall steelhead are holding in the lower Platte and Betsie rivers, and if you want to hunt beach stones legally, you just step south of the National Lakeshore boundary onto Frankfort Public Beach.

How I Plan the Day

Hike the 1.5-mile round-trip Empire Bluff Trail for the 400-foot overlook above Lake Michigan, check the river mouth at Platte River Point, and walk Frankfort Public Beach by the pier for legal post-storm stone hunting.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

When wind is howling on top of the bluffs (and after Pierce Stocking Scenic Drive closes its gate for winter), walk the sheltered paved Sleeping Bear Heritage Trail through the woods into Glen Arbor and warm up at a Leelanau winery or cidery.

Good to know before you drive: Leave every Petoskey stone where it lies inside Sleeping Bear Dunes National Lakeshore (federal rules prohibit collecting); do your collecting south at Frankfort or north around Charlevoix/Petoskey.

Northwest Lower · Wellston, Brethren & Baldwin

Best: Nov 1 – Dec 20

The Big Manistee & Pere Marquette: Fall Chrome Steelhead & Leaf-Off River Bluffs

Peak November steelhead run below Tippy Dam & on the PM · High-bluff hiking on the Manistee River Trail

For river anglers, November is when the October salmon circus clears out and the hardest-fighting fish in the Great Lakes takes over. Fresh, silver-sided Lake Michigan steelhead push into the Big Manistee below Tippy Dam and into the free-flowing Pere Marquette around Baldwin as the water cools through the 40s. Even if you don't carry a fly rod, hiking the high sandy bluffs of the Manistee River Trail between Red Bridge and Seaton Creek in November gives you sweeping views up and down the river bend that are completely hidden by leaves in summer.

How I Plan the Day

Check the USGS river flow first, then walk the angler access boardwalks below Tippy Dam or the high-bluff overlooks above the Manistee River from Red Bridge or Seaton Creek Campground.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

During November 15–30 firearm deer season, Manistee National Forest sees heavy hunting use—wear a bright blaze-orange vest and hat on any forest trail, or head to coastal Manistee's North Pierhead Riverwalk and Ludington State Park.

Good to know before you drive: Cold November river water is unforgiving; always cinch your wader belt tight, check live CFS discharge before stepping off a gravel bar, and know the flies-only regulations on the Pere Marquette.

Northeast Lower · Grayling, Mio & Oscoda

Best: Late Oct – Dec

The Au Sable Valley: Highbanks Overlooks, Iargo Springs & Hartwick Pines

River Road National Scenic Byway · Steelhead & Atlantic salmon below Foote Dam · Paved 1.25-mile Old Growth Pine Loop

I spend as much time on the Au Sable as anywhere in Michigan, and late fall has a quiet magic here. Along the 22-mile River Road National Scenic Byway west of Oscoda, high sandy bluffs rise 200 feet above the river impoundments at Lumberman's Monument, Highbanks, and the cold natural springs of Iargo Springs. Below Foote Dam, Lake Huron steelhead and Atlantic salmon hold in the tailwater, while up at the headwaters near Grayling, the 160-foot white pines at Hartwick Pines State Park whisper in the November wind long after the oaks have dropped their leaves.

How I Plan the Day

Drive River Road west from Oscoda to walk the Highbanks overlook and descend the wooden boardwalk stairs to the cold-water channels at Iargo Springs, or walk the paved 1.25-mile Old Growth Forest Trail at Hartwick Pines in Grayling.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

Step inside the Michigan Forest Visitor Center at Hartwick Pines (open Wednesday–Sunday in late fall and winter) and note that the separate 3-mile Au Sable River Foot Trail has a closed footbridge—stick to the paved Old Growth loop.

Good to know before you drive: During Nov 15–30 firearm deer season, Huron National Forest lands along River Road are open to hunting (wear blaze orange), while the immediate Old Growth Forest Trail area around the Hartwick Pines Visitor Center is a non-hunting zone.

Saginaw Bay Basin · Bay City & Saginaw

Best: Nov 1 – Dec 15

Home Waters on Saginaw Bay: Tobico Marsh Towers & the Shiawassee Flats

Paved 3.5-mile Tobico Marsh loop & two 30-ft towers · Tundra swans, diving ducks & hunting bald eagles

Living here in Bay City on Saginaw Bay, November is when you look up and realize how big the Great Lakes flyway really is. As shallow marshes up north skim over with ice, tens of thousands of tundra swans, mallards, pintails, and canvasbacks funnel into the coastal wetlands at Bay City State Park's Tobico Marsh and thirty minutes south into the Shiawassee National Wildlife Refuge (where six rivers meet in the Shiawassee Flats)—with bald eagles perched in the cottonwoods watching the flocks.

How I Plan the Day

Walk the level, paved 3.5-mile Lesner Loop to the two 30-foot wooden observation towers overlooking the open lagoon at Tobico Marsh in Bay City State Park, and check the Ferguson Bayou dikes at Shiawassee NWR.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

If a raw northeast wind off Saginaw Bay makes the marsh towers too cold, warm up in downtown Bay City's historic waterfront district or drive 25 minutes south to Frankenmuth's covered bridge and holiday lights.

Good to know before you drive: Shiawassee NWR runs managed deer and waterfowl hunts on specific November days and closes its auto tour for winter—always check the refuge's current schedule before heading to the flats.

Southern Michigan · Jackson County, Chelsea & Detroit River

Best: Oct 25 – Nov 30

Haehnle Crane Sanctuary & the Detroit River: Thousands of Cranes at Dusk

No hunting allowed on Haehnle Sanctuary · 2,000–8,000+ bugling sandhill cranes · Detroit River canvasback rafts

If you live in southern Michigan and want one unforgettable November evening without driving to the bridge, go stand on the grassy hill at Michigan Audubon's Phyllis Haehnle Memorial Sanctuary just northeast of Jackson about an hour before sunset. Wave after wave of Greater Sandhill Cranes—often several thousand birds on a crisp mid-November evening—come bugling out of the surrounding cornfields to drop into Mud Lake Marsh for the night. And because Haehnle is a dedicated wildlife sanctuary, hunting is prohibited on the property even right in the middle of November 15–30 firearm deer season.

How I Plan the Day

Walk the glacial geology trails around the Eddy Discovery Center in Waterloo Recreation Area earlier in the afternoon (wearing blaze orange on state recreation land), then pull into Haehnle Sanctuary on Seymour Road 90 minutes before sunset with binoculars.

If the Gale or Sleet Moves In

Warm up with dinner 20 minutes east in downtown Chelsea or Ann Arbor—or if you are closer to Metro Detroit, head to Humbug Marsh and Lake Erie Metropark on the lower Detroit River to watch massive rafts of diving canvasbacks from the shoreline.

Good to know before you drive: Stay on the designated viewing hill at Haehnle Sanctuary so you never flush roosting cranes from the marsh, leave dogs at home (pets are prohibited on the sanctuary), and bring a folding chair and warm blanket for the sunset watch.
