How the flying odds are built

For flying, the page does not rank days by crowd size. It uses every scheduled domestic departure the Bureau of Transportation Statistics recorded in the seven days before and after Thanksgiving and Christmas Day in 2018, 2019 and 2022–2025 (about 3 million flights; 2020–2021 are left out because airlines flew radically reduced schedules). A flight counts as a major disruption if it was cancelled, diverted, or left 60 or more minutes after its scheduled time.

  • Day and time: for each holiday-relative day and scheduled departure window, the rate is the average of the six seasons, so one terrible year counts once. Departure times matter: on Thanksgiving Sunday, evening departures were disrupted several times as often as morning ones.
  • Your airport: each airport gets a factor, its actual disruptions divided by what the national rates predict for its own mix of days and hours, pulled toward 1.0 so small samples cannot swing the result. A factor is estimated separately for morning, midday and evening departures, and for the holiday you picked.
  • Round trips: outbound and return legs are combined (the chance that at least one is disrupted), using your return airport for the second leg. Ranking is by the six-season average; the strip shows each past season, because a few years (the December 2022 storm, for one) were far worse than typical.
  • Source data: BTS Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance, flight-level records. The page loads a compact summary of them; the build script and method notes are in the project repository (scripts/holiday-travel/, docs/HOLIDAY_TRAVEL_DATA.md).

What it does not know

  • Your airline. Carriers differ a lot, and a meltdown at one (Southwest in December 2022) can dominate a season. Connections add risk: each extra flight is another leg.
  • This year’s weather, staffing or schedule. Past patterns are not a forecast. Christmas falls on a different weekday each year, and the page cannot say how 2026’s weekday layout will play out because it has not happened yet.
  • Security lines. TSA does not publish usable hourly or per-airport data, so no checkpoint wait is estimated. Arrive per your airline’s guidance.
  • Driving. The driving view still uses AAA/INRIX 2025 timing guidance and EIA fuel prices (AAA Thanksgiving 2025, AAA year-end 2025, EIA weekly gasoline, Oct 12, 2026). There is no free route-level traffic history, so driving days are broad estimates, not odds. National checkpoint volumes from TSA are not used.

The travel calendar rolls over automatically; the cited data remains dated until verified newer official figures are added.

Important: these are historical odds, not a promise. Weather, local congestion and cancellations can override every pattern shown here. If it is a long drive, do not depart so early that you are fatigued.
